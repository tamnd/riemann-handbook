## A Hecke projector on potentials

This section turns the semisimplicity of the last section into a projector. It builds a noncommutative polynomial $P$ in the Hecke operators with value $1$ at the eigensystem of a newform such that, after applying $P$, every admitted Hecke operator acts on a potential by its eigenvalue, up to a trivial potential. A potential is a polynomial valued function on the upper half plane whose failure to be invariant is constant, so it is the primitive of a cocycle. The theorem is used in [the Hecke projector for a newform](/entry/hecke-proj-newform/), where the same polynomial $P$ is applied to the Kummer module of Section 6.

Fix $N \geq 1$, a prime $l$, and put $\Gamma = \Gamma_1(N) \cap \Gamma_0(l)$. Let $w \geq 0$ and let $\mathbb{C}[X_0, X_1]_w$ be the homogeneous polynomials of degree $w$. An integer matrix $m$ acts on polynomials by the substitution $p|m = p(m_{00}X_0 + m_{01}X_1,\ m_{10}X_0 + m_{11}X_1)$. Setting $X_0 = X$, $X_1 = 1$ identifies $\mathbb{C}[X_0, X_1]_w$ with $V_w = \mathbb{C}[X]_{\leq w}$ and this action with the slash action of the [Eichler and Shimura decomposition](/entry/exists-per-add-cj-per/).

**Definition 6.5.1 (Potential).** A *potential* of weight $w$ for $\Gamma$ is a map $F : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}[X_0, X_1]_w$ such that, for each $\gamma \in \Gamma$, there is a polynomial $c_F(\gamma)$ with
$$
F(\gamma z)|\gamma - F(z) = c_F(\gamma) \qquad \text{for all } z \in \mathcal{H}.
$$
A potential $F$ is *trivial* if there are $\psi : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}[X_0, X_1]$ and a polynomial $p$ with $\psi(\gamma z)|\gamma = \psi(z)$ for $\gamma \in \Gamma$, $z \in \mathcal{H}$, and $F(z) = \psi(z) + p$ for $z \in \mathcal{H}$.

The map $\gamma \mapsto c_F(\gamma)$ is a cocycle:
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_F(\gamma\delta) &= \bigl(F(\gamma \delta z)|\gamma - F(\delta z)\bigr)\big|\delta + F(\delta z)|\delta - F(z) \\
&= c_F(\gamma)|\delta + c_F(\delta).
\end{aligned}
$$
The Eichler integral of a modular form of weight $w + 2$ is a potential, and its cocycle is the period cocycle. A trivial potential has a coboundary as cocycle, since $c_F(\gamma) = p|\gamma - p$. Conversely, if $c_F(\gamma) = p|\gamma - p$, then $\psi = F - p$ is invariant and $F$ is trivial.

**Definition 6.5.2 (Hecke letters).** The *letters* are the symbols $T_q$ for $q \geq 1$, $U$, and $\langle d \rangle$ for $d \geq 0$. For each $d$ fix $\sigma_d \in \Gamma_0(N) \cap \Gamma_0(l)$ with lower right entry $\equiv d \pmod N$, when one exists. A matrix $m$ acts on functions by $(F|m)(z) = F(mz)|m$, and the letters act by
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_q &\mapsto \sum_{j=0}^{q-1} \Big|\begin{pmatrix} 1 & j \\ 0 & q \end{pmatrix} + \Big|\,\sigma_q \begin{pmatrix} q & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \\
U &\mapsto \sum_{j=0}^{l-1} \Big|\begin{pmatrix} 1 & j \\ 0 & l \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \langle d \rangle \mapsto \big|\,\sigma_d.
\end{aligned}
$$
The *admitted* letters are $T_q$ for primes $q \nmid Nl$, $U$, and $\langle d \rangle$ for $d$ prime to $N$. Let $\mathcal{A}_R$ be the subalgebra of the free $R$-algebra on the letters generated by the admitted letters. An *eigensystem* is a map $\lambda$ from letters to $R$. It extends to an algebra map $\lambda : \mathcal{A}_R \to R$, and the action of letters extends to an action $P \mapsto P_*$ of $\mathcal{A}_R$ on functions.

A polynomial in the letters is a formal word, not an element of a commutative Hecke algebra. The free algebra lets us write down $P$ before we know that the operators commute on a given module.

**Definition 6.5.3 (Semisimple projector on potentials).** Let $\lambda$ be an eigensystem with values in $\mathbb{C}$. We say that $\lambda$ has a *semisimple projector on potentials of weight $w$* if there is $P \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}$ with $\lambda(P) = 1$ such that
$$
(T_* - \lambda(T))(P_* F) \text{ is trivial}
$$
for every potential $F$ of weight $w$ for $\Gamma$ and every admitted letter $T$.

**Theorem 6.5.4 (Hecke projector on potentials at a newform).** Let $k \geq 3$ be odd and let $g$ be a newform of weight $k$, level $M$ and character $\chi$, with coefficients $a_n$. Let $N \geq 5$ with $M \mid N$, let $l$ be a prime with $l \nmid N$, and let $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$ with
$$
\alpha^2 - a_l \alpha + \chi(l) l^{k-1} = 0, \qquad \alpha^2 \neq \chi(l) l^{k-1}, \qquad \alpha^2 \neq \chi(l) l^{k-2}.
$$
Let $\lambda$ be an eigensystem with $\lambda(T_q) = a_q$ for primes $q \nmid Nl$, $\lambda(U) = \alpha$, and $\lambda(\langle d \rangle) = \chi(d)$ for $d$ prime to $N$. Then $\lambda$ has a semisimple projector on potentials of weight $k - 2$.

*Proof.* Let $w = k - 2$, and write $Z^1$, $B^1$ for the cocycles and coboundaries of $\Gamma$ with values in $V_w$.

*Step 1.* The families of the letters $T_q$, $U$ and $\langle d \rangle$ are systems of representatives of double cosets of $\Gamma$. So the letters act on $Z^1$ by the Hecke operators on cocycles, and preserve $Z^1$ and $B^1$. They commute with each other modulo $B^1$.

*Step 2.* By [the semisimplicity theorem at a newform](/entry/hecke-semisimple-at-newform/), a cocycle $z$ with $(T - \lambda(T))^n z \in B^1$ for all admitted letters $T$ satisfies $(T - \lambda(T)) z \in B^1$ for all of them. So on $Z^1/B^1$ the joint generalised eigenspace for $\lambda$ equals the joint eigenspace.

*Step 3.* The space $Z^1$ is finite dimensional. A lemma of linear algebra applies: if commuting operators on a finite dimensional space have a joint generalised eigenspace for $\lambda$ that is an eigenspace, then there is a polynomial $P$ in these operators with $\lambda(P) = 1$ whose image lies in the joint eigenspace. The polynomial is a product of factors $\prod_{\mu}(T - \mu)^{n}/(\lambda(T) - \mu)^n$ over the other eigenvalues $\mu$ of finitely many operators $T$, and only finitely many operators are needed since $Z^1/B^1$ is finite dimensional. So $(T - \lambda(T)) P z \in B^1$ for every admitted $T$ and every $z \in Z^1$.

*Step 4.* The map $F \mapsto c_F$ from potentials to $Z^1$ is linear and commutes with the action of each letter, and so with the action of each $P \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}$: $c_{P_* F} = P c_F$. Hence for a potential $F$ and an admitted $T$,
$$
c_{(T_* - \lambda(T)) P_* F} = (T - \lambda(T)) P c_F \in B^1.
$$
A potential whose cocycle is a coboundary is trivial. $\square$

A potential is a function on all of $\mathbb{C}$, and only its values on $\mathcal{H}$ enter. No holomorphy is asked, so the space of potentials is not finite dimensional. The proof does not need finiteness for potentials, since it works with the cocycles $c_F$.

**Remark 6.5.5 (Formalization).** The statement with the second name is a wrapper under `maxHeartbeats 1000000`. The function $\psi$ of a trivial potential need not be homogeneous. The proof of commutation modulo $B^1$ feeds a lemma a dummy prime $q_0 > Nl$ for pairs of letters without a $T_q$. The linear algebra lemma of Step 3 was read as a statement; the product formula given there is the usual construction.

## Kummer characters and logarithmic derivatives

Let $r$ be a $p^n$-th root of a unit $w$. The Kummer character of $r$ is a class in the first cohomology of a Galois group. This section shows that, up to a bounded power of $p$ and a coboundary, the class depends only on the logarithmic derivative of $w$: the Kummer character of $r$ is the combination $\beta\lambda_\zeta + \alpha\lambda_q$ of the two basic characters, where $d\log w = \alpha\, d\log q + \beta\, d\log \zeta$. We prove this twice. The first theorem works on the arithmetic group $G_n$ for units of the level $n$ coordinate ring. The second works on the geometric group for a unit given as a series in $q_L$. The first theorem is the input of [the cup product formula](/entry/cup-kummer-dlog/) of the next section. The second is fact (C) in the proof of [the local edge congruence](/entry/edge-dlog/).

We keep the notation of [Section 6.11.8](/entry/h1-geometric-level-coboundary/) and [Section 6.12.5](/entry/edge-dlog/): an odd prime $p$, the ring $\mathcal{O}$, a closure $\Omega$ with a tower $(\zeta_m, q_m)$, the groups $G$, $G_L$ and $G_{\mathrm{geo}}$, integral elements, congruences modulo $p^k$, cochains modulo $p^k$, Kummer characters, reparametrizations, the ring $\mathcal{O}_L$ with its embeddings, logarithmic coordinates and evaluations. The method is Fontaine's method of differentials, in a form with explicit constants.

### The arithmetic group

The basic characters at level $n$ are the Kummer characters $\lambda_\zeta$ of $\zeta_{2n}$ and $\lambda_q$ of $q_{2n}$ on $G_n$. So $g(\zeta_{2n}) = \zeta_n^{\lambda_\zeta(g)}\zeta_{2n}$ and $g(q_{2n}) = \zeta_n^{\lambda_q(g)} q_{2n}$ for $g \in G_n$. The element $\zeta_n - 1$ is not a unit, and the theorem allows a power of it in the radicand.

**Theorem 6.13.1 (Kummer characters of units at finite level).** Let $p$ be an odd prime. There is $c \geq 0$ with the following property. Let $\Omega$ be a closure of $\mathcal{O}$ with a tower, $n \geq 1$, and $\iota$ an embedding of $\mathcal{O}_n$. Let $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, let $w$ be a unit of $\mathcal{O}_n$ with logarithmic coordinates $(\alpha, \beta)$, and let $r \in \Omega$ with $r^{p^n} = (\zeta_n - 1)^k\iota(w)$. Let $a$, $\lambda_\zeta$, $\lambda_q$ be Kummer characters of $r$, $\zeta_{2n}$, $q_{2n}$ on $G_n$. Then the cochain
$$
\begin{aligned}
g \mapsto{} & p^c a(g) - p^c\,\iota(\alpha)\,\lambda_q(g) \\
& - p^c\Bigl(\iota(\beta) + \frac{k\,\zeta_n}{\zeta_n - 1}\Bigr)\lambda_\zeta(g)
\end{aligned}
$$
is a coboundary modulo $p^n$ on $G_n$.

The term $k\zeta_n/(\zeta_n - 1)$ is the logarithmic derivative $\zeta\frac{d}{d\zeta}\log(\zeta - 1)^k$ at $\zeta = \zeta_n$. So the theorem reads: the Kummer character of $r$ is $\alpha\lambda_q + \beta_{\mathrm{tot}}\lambda_\zeta$, where $(\alpha, \beta_{\mathrm{tot}})$ are the logarithmic coordinates of the whole radicand.

The proof uses derivations that are defined only up to integral elements. Let $C \subseteq \Omega$ be a subring of integral elements that contains the image of $\mathcal{O}$. A map $D : \Omega \to \Omega$ is a *derivation modulo integral elements on $C$* if $D(x + y) - D(x) - D(y)$, $D(xy) - xD(y) - yD(x)$ and $D(c)$ are integral for all $x, y \in C$ and $c \in \mathcal{O}$. Let $H_\infty$ be the intersection of all $G_m$, and for $r \in \Omega$ let $C_r$ be the ring of integral elements fixed by every element of $H_\infty$ that fixes $r$.

*Proof.* We show that $c = 7$ works.

*Step 1: two derivations.* Assume first that $r$ is integral and that $a$, $\lambda_\zeta$, $\lambda_q$ are as in the statement. A lemma of Fontaine type gives derivations $D_\zeta$ and $D_q$ modulo integral elements on $C_r$ with
$$
D_\zeta(\zeta_n) \equiv \frac{p^4}{p^n}\zeta_n, \quad D_\zeta(q_n) \equiv 0, \quad D_q(\zeta_n) \equiv 0, \quad D_q(q_n) \equiv \frac{p^4}{p^n} q_n,
$$
each up to an integral element, and with a twist rule: for $g \in G_n$ there are $s, t \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that
$$
\begin{aligned}
p^2\Bigl(D_\zeta(g r) &- \bigl(1 + p^n\lambda_\zeta(g) + p^{2n}s\bigr)\, g(D_\zeta r) \\
&- \bigl(p^n\lambda_q(g) + p^{2n}t\bigr)\, g(D_q r)\Bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$
is integral. So $D_\zeta$ is $p^{4}$ times the derivation $\zeta_n \frac{d}{d\zeta_n}$ divided by $p^n$, and the twist rule says how it fails to commute with $g$. The construction goes through almost étale coordinates for the ring generated by $r$ over the level $n$ elements, dual bases for a trace up to $p$, derivations on each level of the tower that glue, and their extension to $C_r$ at the cost of $p^3$.

*Step 2: one Kähler relation.* Let $u, A, B \in \mathcal{O}_n$ with $XY\,du = XA\,dY + YB\,dX$, and let $r^{p^n} = \iota(u)$. Then $r$ is integral. Apply $D_\zeta$ to $r^{p^n} = \iota(u)$. The Leibniz rule gives $p^n r^{p^n - 1}D_\zeta(r)$ on the left. On the right, the Kähler relation and the values of $D_\zeta$ on $\zeta_n$ and $q_n$ give $\iota(B)p^4/p^n$, up to an integral element. So
$$
E_\zeta = p^n r^{p^n - 1}D_\zeta(r) - \iota(B)\frac{p^4}{p^n}, \qquad E_q = p^n r^{p^n - 1}D_q(r) - \iota(A)\frac{p^4}{p^n}
$$
are integral. Now let $g \in G_n$. Then $g(r) = \zeta_n^{a(g)} r$, and the Leibniz rule gives $D_\zeta(g r) \equiv \zeta_n^{a(g)}D_\zeta(r) + a(g)\frac{p^4}{p^n}g(r)$ up to an integral element. Compare this with the twist rule, multiply by $g(r)^{p^n - 1}p^n$, and use $g(r)^{p^n - 1}\zeta_n^{a(g)} = r^{p^n - 1}$ and $g(\iota(v)) = \iota(v)$ for $v \in \mathcal{O}_n$. The result is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&p^6\bigl(\iota(u)\,a(g) - \iota(A)\,\lambda_q(g) - \iota(B)\,\lambda_\zeta(g)\bigr) \\
&\quad \equiv g(x) - x \pmod{p^n}, \qquad x = p^2E_\zeta,
\end{aligned}
$$
and $x$ is integral since $E_\zeta$ is integral.

*Step 3: the theorem.* Choose $s \in \Omega$ with $s^{p^n} = \zeta_n - 1$, and a Kummer character $a_1$ of $s$ on $G_n$. Then $r' = r s^{-k}$ is a $p^n$-th root of $\iota(w)$ with Kummer character $a_0$, and $a \equiv a_0 + k a_1 \pmod{p^n}$. Apply Step 2 to $r'$ with $(u, A, B) = (w, w\alpha, w\beta)$, which satisfies the Kähler relation by the definition of logarithmic coordinates, and divide by the unit $\iota(w)$. Apply Step 2 to $s$ with $(u, A, B) = (X - 1, 0, X)$, since $XY\,d(X - 1) = Y\cdot X\,dX$, and divide by $\zeta_n - 1$. The quotient $p/(\zeta_n - 1)$ is integral, so the division costs one factor $p$. Add the two congruences with the weights $1$ and $k$. This gives the claim with $c = 7$. $\square$

**Remark 6.13.2.** The theorem is a finite level form of the statement that the Kummer map on units is computed by the logarithmic derivative, in the sense of Fontaine's computation of differentials and of the Hodge-Tate decomposition. The constant $c$ measures the failure of the tower to be étale at finite level.

### The geometric group

Over the geometric group $G_{\mathrm{geo}}$ the roots of unity are fixed. Only the character of $q$ survives, and the coefficient of $\lambda_q$ is the logarithmic derivative $w'/w$ in the parameter $q_L$.

**Theorem 6.13.3 (Kummer characters of series).** Let $p$ be an odd prime. There is $c \geq 0$ with the following property. Let $\Omega$ be a closure of $\mathcal{O}$ with a tower, $1 \leq n \leq L$, and $\psi$ a reparametrization at level $L$. Let $H = G_{\mathrm{geo}} \cap G_L$. Let $(c_w, w, w')$ be an evaluation with $w \neq 0$ and $w^{-1}$ integral, and let $r \in \Omega$ with $r^{p^n} = w$. Let $a$ and $\lambda_q$ be Kummer characters of $r$ and of $q_{L+n}$ on $H$. Then
$$
g \mapsto p^c a(g) - p^c\,\frac{w'}{w}\,\lambda_q(g)
$$
is a coboundary modulo $p^n$ on $H$.

The proof uses the module of Kähler differentials $\Omega^1$ of the integral closure $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{int}}$ of $\mathcal{O}$ in $\Omega$, over $\mathcal{O}$. We use the following fact, in the spirit of Fontaine's theorem on the differentials of the ring of integers of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ [Fontaine 1982].

**Proposition 6.13.4 (Torsion differentials).** Let $p$ be an odd prime. There is $c \geq 0$ such that for every closure $\Omega$ with a tower and every $n \geq 1$:

1. if $\eta \in \Omega^1$ satisfies $p^n\eta = 0$, then $p^c\eta = x\, d\log\zeta_n + y\, d\log q_n$ for some $x, y \in \mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{int}}$;
2. if $x\, d\log\zeta_n + y\, d\log q_n = 0$, then $p^c x \in p^{n-1}\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{int}}$ and $p^c y \in p^n\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{int}}$.

Here $d\log\zeta_n = \zeta_n^{-1}d\zeta_n$ and $d\log q_n = q_n^{-1}dq_n$.

*Proof of the theorem.* Take $c_1$ from the proposition. We show that $c = 2c_1 + 1$ works. Write the evaluation as $w = \sum_t e_t\psi(a_t)$ and $w' = \sum_t e_t\psi(\vartheta a_t)$. Since $\psi(q) = q_L$, a lemma on reparametrizations gives $d\,\psi(a_t) = \psi(\vartheta a_t)\, d\log q_L$, and $d\log q_L = p^n\, d\log q_{L+n}$. The element $r$ is integral, since $r^{p^n} = w$ is integral. Choose $p^n$-th roots $y_t$ of $e_t$; they are integral and algebraic over $\mathbb{Z}_p$. Put
$$
\eta = r^{p^n - 1}\,dr - w'\, d\log q_{L+n} - \sum_t \psi(a_t)\, y_t^{p^n - 1}\, dy_t .
$$
By the Leibniz rule, $p^n\eta = dw - w'\,d\log q_L - \sum_t \psi(a_t)\, de_t = 0$. By part 1, $p^{c_1}\eta = x\,d\log\zeta_n + y\,d\log q_n$.

Let $g \in H$. Then $g$ fixes $w$, $w'$, $\zeta_n$, $q_n$, the $y_t$ and the $\psi(a_t)$. It sends $r$ to $\zeta_n^{a(g)}r$ and $q_{L+n}$ to $\zeta_n^{\lambda_q(g)}q_{L+n}$. Since $r^{p^n - 1}dr = w\,d\log r$, this gives $g(\eta) - \eta = \bigl(w\,a(g) - w'\lambda_q(g)\bigr)\, d\log\zeta_n$. Apply $g$ to the expression of $p^{c_1}\eta$ and subtract:
$$
\bigl(g(x) - x - p^{c_1}w\,(a(g) - \tfrac{w'}{w}\lambda_q(g))\bigr)\, d\log\zeta_n + \bigl(g(y) - y\bigr)\, d\log q_n = 0.
$$
By part 2,
$$
p^{c_1}\Bigl(g(x) - x - p^{c_1}w\bigl(a(g) - \tfrac{w'}{w}\lambda_q(g)\bigr)\Bigr) \in p^{n-1}\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{int}}.
$$
Multiply by $p\,w^{-1}$, which is integral and fixed by $g$. With $X = p^{c_1 + 1}w^{-1}x$ this gives $p^{2c_1+1}\bigl(a(g) - \frac{w'}{w}\lambda_q(g)\bigr) \equiv g(X) - X \pmod{p^n}$. $\square$

**Remark 6.13.5 (Formalization).** Both second names are wrappers of the form `first | exact @… | …`. In the first theorem the constant is fixed at $7$ in the proof ([KummerCochainEqDlogCombinationFiniteLevel.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/KummerCochainEqDlogCombinationFiniteLevel.lean#L247)). The derivations of Step 1 come from eight results that we read as statements: almost Galois coordinates, almost dual bases for a trace, glued derivations on the tower levels, the extension of a derivation at the cost of $p^3$, integrality of a dual element, the Kähler relation for derivations modulo integral elements, and two lemmas on the level rings. The proposition on torsion differentials is `GlobalTheory.T.torsion_kaehler_span_dlog_indep`; we read it as a statement and did not open its proof. The sequence $c_w$ of the evaluation plays no role in the second theorem.

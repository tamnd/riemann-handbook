This theorem builds a tower of modular functions over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ whose $j$-function is the classical one. It is restated as [the existence of a modular function tower](/entry/exists-modular-function-tower-algebraic-coefficients/), which [`towerEx`](/entry/tower-ex/) uses. The proof takes the fraction field of a ring of modular functions and checks the axioms of a tower one by one, each with its own lemma.

## Statement

Let $\Omega$ be an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\varepsilon \colon \Omega \to \mathbb{C}$ a ring map. A *tower* over $(\Omega, \varepsilon)$ is the following data.

- **The field.** A field $K$ over $\Omega$ and an $\Omega$-algebra map $\exp \colon K \to \Omega((q^{\mathbb{Q}}))$ to Hahn series with rational exponents (the $q$-expansion).
- **The actions.** A right action of $\mathrm{GL}_2^+(\mathbb{Q})$ on $K$ by $\Omega$-automorphisms, and an action of $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/\mathbb{Q})$ by ring automorphisms that acts on $q$-expansions coefficient by coefficient.
- **The analytic side.** A ring map $\mathrm{an}$ from $K$ to germs of functions on the upper half plane $\mathfrak{H}$. It sends $c \in \Omega$ to the constant $\varepsilon(c)$, is compatible with Möbius transformations, has quotients of holomorphic functions as values, and $\mathrm{an}(f)(z) = \sum_r \varepsilon(\exp(f)_r) e^{2\pi i r z}$ for $\operatorname{Im} z$ large.
- **Level and reciprocity.** Each $f$ is fixed by some $\Gamma(M)$. An upper triangular matrix acts on $q$-expansions in the expected way. If $f$ has level $M$, $s \in \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/\mathbb{Q})$ and $s(\zeta_M) = \zeta_M^d$, then $s$ carries the action of $\gamma \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ on $f$ to the action of any $\gamma'$ congruent modulo $M$ to $\operatorname{diag}(1, d)^{-1}\gamma\operatorname{diag}(1, d)$. The lemma that proves this axiom is named after Shimura reciprocity; compare Shimura, *Introduction to the Arithmetic Theory of Automorphic Functions*, 1971, Chapter 6.
- **The $j$-function.** An element $j_K$ fixed by $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, whose $q$-expansion has integer coefficients, coefficient 1 at $q^{-1}$ and no terms below $q^{-1}$, and that is holomorphic on $\mathfrak{H}$. Each $f \in K$ is algebraic over $\Omega(j_K)$.
- **Integrality and fullness.** The coefficients of each $f$ have bounded denominators. Each meromorphic function of some level that is algebraic over $K$ lies in $K$. Each holomorphic function of level $M$ with $q$-expansions over $\Omega$ at all cusps lies in $K$.

**Theorem.** There is a tower $T$ over $(\Omega, \varepsilon)$ with $\mathrm{an}(j_K) = j = E_4^3/\Delta$.

## Proof

1. `modularFunctionFractionField_nonempty` gives a structure `Frac`. Its field is the fraction field of the ring $R$ generated by the functions $f$ on $\mathfrak{H}$ that are fixed by some $\Gamma(N)$ and such that, for each $\gamma \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ and some $w$, $\Delta^w \cdot (f \circ \gamma)$ has a $q$-expansion with coefficients in $\varepsilon(\Omega)$. `Frac` gives $\exp$, the actions and $\mathrm{an}$.
2. `exists_jInvariant_in_modularFunctionFractionField` puts $j$ in $R$ and gives its $q$-expansion and its invariance.
3. `lv_jT_one_and_jT_smul_eq`, `mdifferentiable_of_hasSomeLevelOver` and `upperHalfPlane_germ_ringHom_and_restrict` show that $j$ is holomorphic on $\mathfrak{H}$.
4. Each remaining axiom is one lemma (see below).

The rest is bookkeeping.

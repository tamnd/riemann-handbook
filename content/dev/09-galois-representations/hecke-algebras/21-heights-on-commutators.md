## Heights on commutators

Let $y$ be an Euler system class for $W$ and $z$ a cocycle of the Tate dual $W^*$ that is a coboundary at $\ell$ and unramified elsewhere. Their cup product is a $2$-cocycle, and when it is a coboundary $du$ modulo $\ell^n$ we can form a *height* function on $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ from $y$, $z$ and $u$. This section proves that the height, multiplied by $\det(W(\sigma) - 1)$, vanishes modulo $\ell^n$ on the commutator subgroup of $H$. The proof is the Euler system argument: it chooses an auxiliary prime $w$ whose Frobenius is close to $\sigma$, uses the tame class of $y$ at $w$, and applies global reciprocity. The theorem is step 4 of the proof of strict vanishing in Section 22.

We keep the notation of Section 18 and Section 20: $W$, $W^*$, $\varepsilon$, $H$, $N$, $\omega$, the ring $\mathcal{O}$ of a coefficient datum, and $a \equiv_n b$ for $|a - b| \leq |\ell^n|$. Let $G_S$ be the Galois group of the maximal extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ unramified outside $S$, and $K_S$ the kernel of $G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to G_S$.

### Cup products and heights

**Definition 6.21.1 (Cup product and height).** For maps $y, z : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^2$ put
$$
(y \cup z)(s, t) = \omega\bigl(y(s), W^*(s)z(t)\bigr).
$$
For $u : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ the *height* of $(y, z, u)$ at $s$ is
$$
\mathrm{ht}(s) = \det\bigl(W(s) - 1\bigr)\,u(s) - \omega\bigl(\mathrm{adj}(W(s) - 1)\,y(s),\, z(s)\bigr),
$$
where $\mathrm{adj}$ is the adjugate matrix.

If $y$ is a cocycle for $W$ and $z$ one for $W^*$, then $y \cup z$ is a $2$-cocycle with values in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell(1)$: $\varepsilon(r)(y\cup z)(s, t) + (y\cup z)(r, st) = (y\cup z)(rs, t) + (y\cup z)(r, s)$. Assume $y \cup z = du$, that is, $(y \cup z)(s, t) = \varepsilon(s)u(t) - u(st) + u(s)$. Fix $s$ with $W(s) - 1$ invertible. On the closed subgroup generated by $s$, $y$ is the coboundary of $x_s = (W(s) - 1)^{-1}y(s)$, and $\mathrm{adj}(W(s) - 1) = \det(W(s) - 1)(W(s) - 1)^{-1}$. So
$$
\mathrm{ht}(s) = \det\bigl(W(s) - 1\bigr)\bigl(u(s) - \omega(x_s, z(s))\bigr).
$$
The bracket compares two trivializations of the cup product on that subgroup: $u$, and the one that comes from $x_s$. So the height is the local invariant of a pair of classes, read at one group element.

### Inputs from the literature

The proof assumes the standard theorems on the Galois cohomology of finite modules over number fields and local fields. They are twelve statements: the Poitou-Tate sequence, finiteness of local and global cohomology, local Tate duality, the local and global Euler characteristic formulas, the orthogonality of unramified classes under local duality, the formula of Greenberg and Wiles, the three local statements again over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, and a local statement on the Bloch-Kato conditions. We call them the *cohomological inputs*. See J. S. Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, Chapter I, and J. Neukirch, A. Schmidt and K. Wingberg, *Cohomology of Number Fields*, Chapters VII and VIII.

### The theorem

**Theorem 6.21.2 (Heights vanish on commutators).** Assume the cohomological inputs. Let $W$ be in normal form for $(e, S, A)$, $\mathcal{O}$ the ring of a coefficient datum that contains the entries of $W$, $S'$ a finite set of places and $n \geq 1$. Let $y$ be a cocycle for $W$ and $z$ a cocycle for $W^*$, both with values in $\mathcal{O}^2$. Let $u : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathcal{O}$ be continuous. Assume:

1. for each prime $q \neq \ell$ there are a place $B$ over $q$ and $x$ with $y(s) = W(s)x - x$ for $s \in I_B$;
2. for each prime $q \neq \ell$ and each place $B$ over $q$ there is $x \in \mathcal{O}^2$ with $z(s) = W^*(s)x - x$ for $s \in I_B$;
3. there is $x \in \mathcal{O}^2$ with $z(s) = W^*(s)x - x$ for $s \in D_A$;
4. $y$ has a tame class at $(w, n, \psi)$, in the sense of Section 18, for each $w \notin S'$ with $\ell^n \mid w - 1$ and each surjective $\psi : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n$ trivial on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_w))$;
5. $u(sa) \equiv_n u(s)$ for all $s$ and all $a \in K_S$;
6. $(y \cup z)(s, t) \equiv_n \varepsilon(s)u(t) - u(st) + u(s)$ for all $s, t$.

Then $\det(W(\sigma) - 1)\,\mathrm{ht}(\sigma) \equiv_n 0$ for every $\sigma$ in the commutator subgroup $[H, H]$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Ord2.ht_vanish_commutator` (also `GlobalTheory.T.cohomology_unramified_lift`, a second name).

We use the following inputs. The first is a form of the Chebotarev density theorem, and the third is global reciprocity for the Brauer group of $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Lemma 6.21.3 (Auxiliary primes).** Let $U$ be an open subgroup of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\sigma \in [\ker\varepsilon, \ker\varepsilon]$. There are a prime $w \notin S \cup S'$, a place $B$ over $w$ and a Frobenius element $\tau \in D_B$ with $\sigma^{-1}\tau \in U$, $\ell^n \mid w - 1$, and such that every prime $v \in S$ is an $\ell^n$-th power modulo $w$.

**Lemma 6.21.4 (Characters of prime conductor).** If $\ell^n \mid w - 1$, there is a surjective $\psi : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n$, trivial on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_w))$, with $\psi(t) = 1$ for some $t \in I_B$ for each place $B$ over $w$, and with $D_B \subseteq \ker\psi$ for each place $B$ over a prime $v \neq w$ that is an $\ell^n$-th power modulo $w$.

**Lemma 6.21.5 (Reciprocity).** Let $\ell$ be odd and $\xi$ be a continuous $2$-cocycle modulo $\ell^n$ with values in $\mathcal{O}(1)$, unramified outside a finite set $T \ni \ell$ of places, and let $w \in T$. If $\xi$ is a coboundary modulo $\ell^n$ on $D_B$ for some place $B$ over each $v \in T \setminus \{w\}$, then it is a coboundary modulo $\ell^n$ on $D_B$ for each place $B$ over $w$.

The second lemma is the character of order $\ell^n$ of $(\mathbb{Z}/w)^\times$. A prime $v$ is an $\ell^n$-th power modulo $w$ exactly when its Frobenius lies in the kernel of this character.

*Proof of the theorem.* Fix $\sigma \in [H, H]$. Since $H \subseteq \ker\varepsilon$, $\sigma \in [\ker\varepsilon, \ker\varepsilon]$, and $\det W(\sigma) = 1$.

*Step 1: a Frobenius near $\sigma$.* The function $F(s) = \det(W(s) - 1)\,\mathrm{ht}(s)$ is continuous. So there is an open subgroup $U$ such that, for $\sigma^{-1}\tau \in U$, $|F(\tau) - F(\sigma)| < |\ell^n|$ and $|\det W(\tau) - \det W(\sigma)| < |2|$. Take $w$, $B$ and $\tau$ from the first lemma. Since $\det W(\tau) = \pm 1$ and $|{-1} - 1| = |2|$, we get $\det W(\tau) = 1$. Since $w \notin S$, $W$ is trivial on $I_B$.

*Step 2: the tame class.* Take $\psi$ from the second lemma. By hypothesis 4, $y$ has a tame class at $(w, n, \psi)$. A lemma on tame classes turns it into $P \in \mathcal{O}$, a unit $u_0$ and a continuous $d : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathcal{O}^2$ with:

- $P \equiv_n u_0\det(W(\tau') - 1)$ for each Frobenius element $\tau'$ at a place over $w$;
- $d(st) \equiv_n d(s) + W(s)d(t) + \psi(s)\,P\,W(s)y(t)$, with $\psi(s)$ read in $\{0, \ldots, \ell^n - 1\}$;
- $d$ vanishes modulo $\ell^n$ on the kernel of $G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to G_{S \cup \{w\}}$, and is an integral coboundary modulo $\ell^n$ on inertia at each $q \in S$ with $q \neq \ell$.

So $d$ trivializes the cup product of $\psi$ with $P\,y$. This is where the derivative of the Euler system enters.

*Step 3: a global $2$-cocycle.* Put
$$
\xi(s, t) = (d \cup z)(s, t) - \psi(s)\,P\,\varepsilon(s)\,u(t).
$$
Using hypotheses 5 and 6, $\xi$ is a continuous $2$-cocycle modulo $\ell^n$, unramified outside $T = S \cup \{w\}$. At the place $A$ over $\ell$, $\psi$ is trivial on $D_A$ (since $\ell \in S$), so $\xi = d \cup z$ there, and it is a coboundary because $z$ is a coboundary on $D_A$ (hypothesis 3). At $v \in S$ with $v \neq \ell$, again $\xi = d \cup z$ on $D_B$, and the cup product of two classes that are unramified at $v$ is a coboundary: this uses the orthogonality of unramified classes among the cohomological inputs. The reciprocity lemma then shows that $\xi$ is a coboundary modulo $\ell^n$ on $D_B$.

*Step 4: the local computation at $w$.* On $D_B$ the representation $W$ is unramified, $\tau$ is a Frobenius with $\det W(\tau) = 1$, the classes $y$ and $z$ vanish on $I_B$ (they vanish on $K_S$, and $I_B \subseteq K_S$), $u$ is invariant under $I_B$ modulo $\ell^n$, and $\psi$ is totally ramified at $w$. A local computation in the tame quotient of $D_B$, which is generated by $\tau$ and by $t \in I_B$, shows that the triviality of $\xi$ on $D_B$ gives $P\,\mathrm{ht}(\tau) \equiv_n 0$.

*Step 5: conclusion.* Since $P \equiv_n u_0\det(W(\tau) - 1)$ with $|u_0| = 1$ and $|\mathrm{ht}(\tau)| \leq 1$, we get $F(\tau) = \det(W(\tau) - 1)\,\mathrm{ht}(\tau) \equiv_n 0$. Since $F(\sigma) \equiv_n F(\tau)$ by Step 1, $F(\sigma) \equiv_n 0$. $\square$

The shape of the argument, a derivative class at an auxiliary prime and the sum of local invariants, is that of the Kolyvagin and Rubin method; see K. Rubin, *Euler Systems*, Chapters 4 and 5.

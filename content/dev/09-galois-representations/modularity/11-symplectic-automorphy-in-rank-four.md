## Symplectic automorphy in rank four

This section proves the symplectic image statement for raw data $D$ in cases II and III: the deformation ring of the residual representation $r_0 = \operatorname{diag}(\theta, \Psi, \alpha, 1)$ is finite in weight zero, has no block diagonal points of the wrong kind, and all its irreducible points are automorphic. [Theorem 8.7.5](/entry/sp4-in0-alt/) takes this from [the potential modularity towers](/entry/pm-exists-of-unramified-adjoint-lifting/) and [the seeds](/entry/fourth-initial-of-prints-h/) of the previous section. The proof is by contradiction. A failure of finiteness gives a point in characteristic $l$ of a special shape, and the promodularity of such points excludes it. We use the raw data of section 7: the characters $\Psi$, $\alpha$, $\beta = \Psi\alpha^{-1}$ and $\theta = \alpha\Psi$, the weights $(e+1, e, 1, 0)$, and the condition that $D$ is good.

**Definition 8.11.1 (Star points).** Let $K$ be a field and $r \colon G \to \mathrm{GSp}_4(K)$ a representation. We say that $r$ is a star point if $r$ is irreducible (its image spans $M_4(K)$), or if $r$ is a block extension: $r$ is block upper triangular for the weight $\lambda = (1, 0, 1, 0)$, the block on the coordinates $\{1, 3\}$ spans $M_2(K)$, and $r$ is not conjugate to a block diagonal representation.

**Definition 8.11.2 (Small pairs).** Let $D$ be good. A small pair for $D$ is a pair $(r, u)$ over the valuation ring $V$ of an algebraically closed field $K$ with these properties. The representation $r$ takes values in $\mathrm{GSp}_4(V)$, reduces to $r_0$, has multiplier $\theta$, is unramified outside the set $T$ of $D$, and is trivial on inertia intersected with $\ker \psi$ at the places of $S_b$. The matrix $u$ is symplectic, and $u r u^{-1}$ is upper triangular at $l$ with diagonal characters $(\theta, \Psi, \alpha, 1)$ on inertia.

**Definition 8.11.3 (Weak purity).** A representation $r$ of $G_F$ is weakly pure if there are an open subgroup $H$ and an integer $M$ with this property: for each Frobenius element $\sigma \in H$ at a power $p^f$ with $p \nmid M$, and all roots $x$ and $y \ne 0$ of the characteristic polynomial of $r(\sigma)$, we have $|\iota x| < p^f |\iota y|$.

For good $D$ we use four conditions.

1. *Finiteness*: for each point of the deformation problem, the $\mathcal{O}$-algebra generated by the coefficients of weight zero is finite.
2. *No endomorphism*: no point is block diagonal with a block on the coordinates $\{0, 2\}$ that spans $M_2$.
3. *Contradiction*: there is no small pair $(r, u)$ over a field of characteristic $l$ such that $r$ is a star point.
4. *Purity and automorphy at points*: every small pair over a field isomorphic to $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ that is a star point is weakly pure; every such pair that is irreducible is automorphic in the sense of the four-dimensional automorphy statement of section 1.

**Theorem 8.11.4 (Finiteness and automorphy from the contradiction).** Assume the cohomology package. Let $D$ be good, fix $\iota$, and assume the contradiction condition and purity and automorphy at points. Then $D$ satisfies finiteness and has no endomorphism, and every irreducible point of $D$ over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ is automorphic.

*Proof.* *Finiteness.* Suppose it fails. Then there are a point over a field of characteristic $l$ and a weight-zero coefficient $x$ with $x^{l^n} \ne x$ for all $n$. A point of this kind gives a small pair $(r, u)$ over a valuation ring. There are three cases for $r$. If $r$ is irreducible, it is a star point, and this contradicts the contradiction condition. If $r$ is diagonal, then all weight-zero coefficients are constant, and this contradicts the choice of $x$. In the remaining case $r$ is block triangular, and a block argument shows that it is a block extension. So $r$ is a star point again, and this is a contradiction.

*No endomorphism.* Suppose a point is block diagonal with a spanning $\{0, 2\}$ block. The block argument gives a block extension $r'$ with the same characteristic polynomials. It is a star point over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$, so it is weakly pure. But a block point has two Frobenius eigenvalues whose absolute values differ by the factor $p^f$, so it is not weakly pure. This is a contradiction.

*Automorphy.* An irreducible point gives a small pair over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ that is irreducible, and automorphy at points applies. $\square$

**Theorem 8.11.5 (Rank four from promodularity in case III).** Suppose that every small star pair in case III is promodular, given the cohomology package, the theorem of Ferrero and Washington, the fit condition, seeds in case III and potential modularity towers. Then the symplectic image statement follows from the cohomology package, the theorem of Ferrero and Washington, the fit condition, seeds in case II, seeds in case III and potential modularity towers.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Cu.modularity_rank_four_of_promodularity_case_three`.

Here a small pair $(r, u)$ is promodular if $r$ is conjugate to the specialization at a point $y$ with $y(J) = 0$ of the universal representation of a potential modularity tower. The theorem of Ferrero and Washington says that the $\mu$-invariant of the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension of an abelian number field is zero (B. Ferrero and L. Washington, *The Iwasawa invariant $\mu_p$ vanishes for abelian number fields*, Ann. of Math. 109 (1979)).

*Proof.* Fix a host, a character $\psi$ and good $D$ of case II or III, and take $\iota$ from the ambient datum. Every small star pair is promodular: in case II by potential modularity towers and the seeds of case II, and in case III by the hypothesis. A promodular point in characteristic $l$ is a local zero of $J$, and the tower has no such point with a star shape, so the contradiction condition holds. A promodular point over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ is weakly pure and automorphic, because the points of the tower are. Then the previous theorem gives the three conclusions. $\square$

**Theorem 8.11.6 (Symplectic image statement).** Assume the cohomology package, the theorem of Ferrero and Washington, seeds in case II, seeds in case III and potential modularity towers. Let $h$ be a host with $h$ big for $t$, let $l \ne 2$, $0 < e < t$, and let $\psi$ be a character of $h$. Let $D$ be good raw data in the case of $(h, e, \psi)$. Then $D$ satisfies finiteness and has no endomorphism, and there is $\iota$ such that every irreducible point of $D$ over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ is automorphic.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Cu.main4_alt` (also `UpperTheory.V.Cu.sp4In_alt`, the same result with the fit condition supplied).

*Proof.* By the previous theorem it is enough to prove promodularity in case III. We do this by a chain reduction. Let $T_0$ be a tower of type four. A seed of case III and the tensor seed construction give a larger tower $T_1$, and for each deep tower $T \ge T_1$ a seed pair $Q$ whose reduction is joined to each residual lift by a chain of points. A chain in rank four moves from one point to the next: if a tower with bounds $C$ has degree $d \ge 30(C+1)^2$, and the ideal $J$ contains the kernel of one point of the chain, then it contains the kernel of the next. So the kernels along the chain all lie over $J$, and the end point is promodular. The fit condition holds by the definition of a fit tower. $\square$

**Remark 8.11.7 (Formalization).** The fit condition `HFit` in [PromodularityCaseThree.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/CuspidalTwists/PromodularityCaseThree.lean#L50) is a hypothesis of several statements, but [`hFit`](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/CuspidalTwists/Sp4In.lean#L42) proves it from the fields of the definition. The theorem `sp4In_alt` in [Sp4In.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/Sp4In.lean#L13) only passes its hypotheses to `main4_alt`. In [CuFinal.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/CuFinal.lean#L44), `main4_alt` has no hypotheses: its statement is an implication between named propositions. The embedding $\iota$ comes from the ambient datum and does not depend on $D$. Weak purity is a strict inequality $|\iota x| < p^f |\iota y|$, not an equality of absolute values, and the name `pureW'` is the identity map. The finiteness proof is in [VWinPm.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/VWinPm.lean#L75).

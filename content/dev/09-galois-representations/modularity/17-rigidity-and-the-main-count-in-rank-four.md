## Rigidity and the main count in rank four

This section proves two inputs of the counts II and III in section 16. The first is rigidity in rank four: an automorphic irreducible point of the symplectic family has no first order deformations other than those that come from conjugation by $\mathfrak{sp}_4$. The second is the main count in rank four, which bounds the length $V_{\mathrm{len}}(e, \psi)$ of case II or case III by $(l N e)^C$. Rigidity comes from a Bloch-Kato vanishing theorem over a CM quadratic field, and that vanishing comes from the adjoint lifting theorem of section 10 applied over a potential modularity tower. The main count uses rigidity at each irreducible piece, together with a Frobenius element at a small prime.

We keep the notation of section 11. Fix an algebraic closure $\Omega$ of $\mathbb{Q}$, an odd prime $l$ and a coefficient ring $\mathcal{O}$ with fraction field in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$. Raw data $D$ consist of a finite set of places $S$, characters $\psi$, $\xi$, $\omega$ of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and an integer $e$, and they give the residual representation

$$r_0 = \operatorname{diag}(\theta, \Psi, \alpha, 1), \qquad \Psi = \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{e} \psi, \quad \alpha = \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} \omega^{-1} \xi, \quad \theta = \alpha \Psi,$$

with Hodge-Tate weights $(e+1, e, 1, 0)$. A point of $D$ is a continuous $r \colon G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to M_4(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l)$ that lifts $r_0$ with the local conditions of $D$. We write $\mathrm{Ctx}_2(D)$ and $\mathrm{Ctx}_3(D)$ for the conditions of case II and case III; each includes the condition that $D$ is good. A character $\chi$ kills $\mu_N$ if it is trivial on $\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_N))$.

**Definition 8.17.1 (Rigid point).** Let $r$ be a point of $D$, and let $\mathbb{D} = \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ be the dual numbers. The point $r$ is rigid if each lift $r' = r + \varepsilon\, r_1$ of $r$ to $M_4(\mathbb{D})$ that satisfies the conditions of $D$ has the form $r_1 = X r - r X$ for some $X \in \mathfrak{sp}_4(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l)$.

A rigid point is an isolated point of the deformation space, up to conjugation. This is the property that makes the trace differences of the main count control the length of a Selmer group: a first order deformation that is not a conjugation would give a tangent direction along which the traces do not move.

**Definition 8.17.2 (Bloch-Kato vanishing).** Let $K$ be a number field and $R$ a continuous representation of $G_K$ on a finite-dimensional $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$-space $W$. We say that Bloch-Kato vanishing holds for $R$ if every continuous cocycle $z \colon G_K \to W$ that is unramified at the places not above $l$ and crystalline at the places above $l$ is a coboundary.

This is the vanishing of the Bloch-Kato Selmer group $H^1_f(K, W)$ of [local Bloch-Kato theory](/learn/padic-hodge/#local-bloch-kato-theory), with the unramified condition at every place away from $l$.

**Definition 8.17.3 (Fit and deep towers, links).** A tower $T$ for $D$ consists of a CM field $K$, a totally real abelian field $F$ and their compositum $L = FK$. The tower is deep if $\psi$ restricted to $G_F$ has order prime to $l$; the fit condition is a list of conditions on the ramification and the degrees of $F$ and $K$. A link of $T$ over a coefficient ring $\mathcal{O}'$ consists of a Hida setting $s$ over $L$ (in the sense of section 10), a level $V$ of $s$, a weight, a residual representation $r$ of rank four for $V$ over $\mathcal{O}'$ with the condition at $v_0$, and the data that identify $r$ with the reduction of $r_0$ restricted to $G_L$.

**Lemma 8.17.4 (Links exist).** Assume the unramified inputs. Let $D$ be raw data and $T$ a fit and deep tower for $D$ with coefficient ring $\mathcal{O}$. Then there is a coefficient ring $\mathcal{O}_1 \supseteq \mathcal{O}$ such that every coefficient ring $\mathcal{O}' \supseteq \mathcal{O}$ with $\mathcal{O}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{O}'$ carries a link of $T$.

*Proof.* We first enlarge the coefficients. The fit condition gives a finite extension in which the characters of $D$ and their reductions take values; this is $\mathcal{O}_1$. Over any $\mathcal{O}'$ that contains $\mathcal{O}_1$ we lift the residual characters of $D$ to characters of $G_L$ with values in $\mathcal{O}'^{\times}$, by the Teichmüller lift. These characters and the set of places of $T$ give a Hida setting $s$ over $L$. The unramified inputs give a level $V$ of $s$ whose set of places is that of $s$. The reduction of $r_0$ restricted to $G_L$ is a direct sum of four characters with values in the residue field; with the lifted characters it is a residual representation of rank four for $V$. Deepness makes the restriction of $\psi$ to $G_F$ of order prime to $l$, so the Teichmüller lift of its reduction is the restriction itself, and the condition at $v_0$ holds. $\square$

**Theorem 8.17.5 (A CM twist with Bloch-Kato vanishing).** Assume the unramified inputs and the adjoint lifting theorem. Let $D$ be raw data in case II or case III, let $N \ge 1$, and assume that $\psi$, $\xi$ and $\omega$ kill $\mu_N$. Let $\iota \colon \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l \to \mathbb{C}$ be an isomorphism and $r$ an irreducible point of $D$ that is automorphic for $\iota$. Then there are a CM field $K \subseteq \Omega$ with $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 2$ and a character $\theta \colon G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l^{\times}$ with these properties.

1. For each $g \in G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, the matrix $r(g)$ lies in $\mathrm{GSp}_4$ with multiplier $\theta(g)$.
2. Let $R$ be a representation of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ on $M_4(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l)$ that extends the adjoint representation of $r|_{G_K}$ with multiplier $\theta|_{G_K}$, in this sense: $R(g) X = r(g) X r(g)^{-1}$ for $g \in G_K$, and there are a complex conjugation $c$ and $P \in \mathrm{GL}_4(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l)$ such that $r(s)^{t} P\, r(c s c^{-1}) = \theta(s) P$ for $s \in G_K$ and $R(c) X = -P^{-1} X^{t} P$. Then Bloch-Kato vanishing holds for $R$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

The representation $R$ of statement 2 is the adjoint representation of the group $\mathcal{G}_4$ of Clozel, Harris and Taylor, built from $r|_{G_K}$ and its polarization.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Z.Win.Dn.exists_cm_field_twist_h1`.

*Proof.* The level conditions on $D$ and the hypotheses on $\mu_N$ give the multiplier $\theta$, and the multiplier condition on points of $D$ gives statement 1. For statement 2 we pass to a tower. Since $D$ is in case II or case III, the theory of section 10 gives a tower $T = (K, F, L)$ for $D$ that is fit and deep. The lemma on links gives a link of $T$ over a large coefficient ring. The conditions of case II or III keep $r$ irreducible on $G_L$ and on $G_K$. The automorphy of $r$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ gives the automorphy of $r|_{G_K}$, and by solvable base change ([base change](/learn/automorphic-forms/#base-change)) of $r|_{G_L}$, since $L/\mathbb{Q}$ is abelian. So $r|_{G_L}$ is an automorphic point of the Hida family of the link, and it is locally generic. The adjoint lifting theorem of section 10 applies at this point: every Bloch-Kato class of the adjoint of $r|_{G_L}$ is a coboundary. Last we go down from $L$ to $K$ and to $\mathbb{Q}$. Restriction from a finite index subgroup is injective on $H^1$ with coefficients in a $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$-space, since the composite with corestriction is multiplication by the index, and restriction preserves the local conditions. So a Bloch-Kato class of $R$ whose restriction to $G_L$ is a coboundary is a coboundary, and Bloch-Kato vanishing holds for $R$. $\square$

**Theorem 8.17.6 (Rigidity in rank four).** Assume the unramified inputs and the adjoint lifting theorem. Let $D$ be raw data in case II or case III, let $N \ge 1$, and assume that $\psi$, $\xi$ and $\omega$ kill $\mu_N$. Let $\iota \colon \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l \to \mathbb{C}$ be an isomorphism and $r$ an irreducible point of $D$ that is automorphic for $\iota$. Then $r$ is rigid.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Th.rigSp4_alt`.

*Proof.* Let $K$ and $\theta$ be as in the [Theorem 8.17.5](/entry/exists-cm-field-twist-h1/), and let $r' = r + \varepsilon r_1$ be a lift of $r$ with the conditions of $D$. Then $z(g) = r_1(g) r(g)^{-1}$ is a cocycle of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with values in $M_4(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l)$ under the adjoint action. The local conditions of $D$ make $z$ unramified away from $l$ and crystalline at $l$, and the multiplier condition makes $z$ take values in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sp}_4$ for the symplectic form $J$ of $r$ (the antidiagonal matrix with entries $1, 1, -1, -1$ from the top right corner to the bottom left corner). Let $\eta$ be the quadratic character of $K/\mathbb{Q}$. Since $K$ is CM, $\eta(c) = -1$ for a complex conjugation $c$. We let $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ act on

$$M_4(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l) = \mathfrak{sp}_4 \oplus \mathfrak{p}, \qquad \mathfrak{p} = \{X : X^{*} = X\},$$

where $X^{*} = J^{-1} X^{t} J$, by the adjoint action on $\mathfrak{sp}_4$ and by $\eta$ times the adjoint action on $\mathfrak{p}$. Call this representation $\rho$. On $G_K$ the character $\eta$ is trivial, so $\rho(g)$ is conjugation by $r(g)$ for $g \in G_K$. Put $P = J\, r(c)^{-1}$. The symplectic condition on $r$ gives $r(s)^{t} P\, r(c s c^{-1}) = \theta(s) P$, and the signs of $\eta(c)$ on the two summands give $\rho(c) X = -P^{-1} X^{t} P$. So $\rho$ is a representation $R$ as in statement 2 of the theorem, and Bloch-Kato vanishing holds for $\rho$. On $\mathfrak{sp}_4$ the representation $\rho$ is the adjoint action, so $z$ is a Bloch-Kato cocycle of $\rho$, and it is a coboundary: $z(g) = \rho(g) Y - Y$ for some $Y \in M_4$. The projection $X \mapsto (X - X^{*})/2$ onto $\mathfrak{sp}_4$ commutes with $\rho$ and fixes the values of $z$. Applied to $Y$ it gives $X_0 \in \mathfrak{sp}_4$ with $z(g) = r(g) X_0 r(g)^{-1} - X_0$ for all $g$. With $X = -X_0$ this reads $r_1 = X r - r X$. $\square$

The point of the twist by $\eta$ is that the adjoint representation of $r$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is not the representation to which the vanishing theorem applies. The vanishing theorem needs the extension of the adjoint of $r|_{G_K}$ on which complex conjugation acts by $X \mapsto -P^{-1} X^{t} P$. The twisted representation $\rho$ is such an extension, and it keeps $\mathfrak{sp}_4$, with the adjoint action, as a direct summand.

**Theorem 8.17.7 (Main count in rank four).** Assume the symplectic image statement of section 11, the cohomology package, the lemmas M, M' and P, the point count in rank four, rigidity in rank four and the corollary X. Assume for each $l$ the two bounds on the Selmer lengths of the symplectic family: an unramified cocycle that is trivial at $l$ is a coboundary, and the length $V(l, e, \psi)$ is finite for $e \ge 1$ and $\psi$ with open kernel. Let $\chi$ be a primitive character. Then there is $C$ with this property. Let $h$ be a host with character $\chi$, let $t$ be such that $h$ is big for $t$, let $l \ne 2$, $0 < e < t$, and let $\psi \in h.X(\mathcal{O}, e)$ be a character in case II or case III. Assume that $\mathcal{O}$ contains the roots of unity of orders $2^{n_{II}}$ and $2^{n_{III}}$. Then

$$V_{\mathrm{len}}(e, \psi) \le (l N e)^{C}.$$

Lean: `main_big`.

*Proof.* The point count gives constants $C_0$ and $A$ with this property. For good raw data $D$ whose characters kill $\mu_N$ and have orders that divide $k$, and a prime $p \ge 5$ with $p \nmid N$ and $p \notin S$, if every irreducible piece is automorphic, there is $\sigma \in G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ such that $\operatorname{tr} r(\sigma) \ne \operatorname{tr} r_0(\sigma)$ for each irreducible piece $r$, and the sum of the terms $-\log |\operatorname{tr} r(\sigma) - \operatorname{tr} r_0(\sigma)|$ is at most $C_0 k (N(e+1))^A \log p$. We take raw data $D$ in the case of $(h, e, \psi)$, with $V_{\mathrm{len}}(e, \psi)$ at most the length $\mathrm{sha}(D)$ of its Selmer group. The symplectic image statement gives finiteness, the absence of endomorphisms and the automorphy of the irreducible pieces for some $\iota$. The lemma P and the cohomology package give a model of $D$. In case II we put $n' = n_{II}$ and $p_1 = 1$; in case III we put $n' = n_{III}$ and $p_1 = p_1(\chi)$. The characters of $D$ then kill $\mu_M$ for $M = l\, 2^{n'} N p_1$. Let $p$ be the least prime with $p \ge 5$ and $p \nmid M$. By Bertrand's postulate, $p \le 2 l N p_1 + 10$. The point count gives $\sigma$. The key inequality

$$\|\mathrm{sha}(D)\| \log l \le 2 \sum_{r} -\log \bigl|\operatorname{tr} r(\sigma) - \operatorname{tr} r_0(\sigma)\bigr|$$

holds, with the sum over a finite set of pairwise non-conjugate irreducible pieces. Its proof uses the model, the lemmas M and M', the local theorems of the cohomology package, the two bounds on Selmer lengths, finiteness, the absence of endomorphisms, and the [Theorem 8.17.6](/entry/rig-sp4-alt/) on rigidity at each piece. Rigidity says that the only first order deformations of a piece are conjugations, which is what allows the length to be bounded by the valuations of the trace differences. With $k$, $N$ and $p$ as above, the right side is at most $(l N e)^{C}$ for a constant $C$ that depends only on $\chi$. Since the length is monotone in the module, the bound for $\mathrm{sha}(D)$ gives the bound for $V_{\mathrm{len}}(e, \psi)$. $\square$

The [Theorem 8.16.5](/entry/sp4-main-alt/) of section 16 removes the hypothesis on roots of unity, by an extension of $\mathcal{O}$ that does not change the length.

**Remark 8.17.8 (Formalization).** The main count is a private theorem in [ConductorAtMapInjectiveCoeff.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/ConductorAtMapInjectiveCoeff.lean#L68), and a second private `main_big` with the same proof and older inputs is in [SymplecticGSp4/ConductorAtMapInjectiveCoeff.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/SymplecticGSp4/ConductorAtMapInjectiveCoeff.lean#L91). Rigidity `RigidAt` is the clause at line 182 of [DecompSigmaOrd.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/SymplecticGSp4/DecompSigmaOrd.lean#L182), and the key inequality is `Raw.Ineq` at line 190 of the same file. Points of $D$ are monoid maps into $M_4$, not into $\mathrm{GL}_4$. In [RigSp4.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/RigSp4.lean#L185) the name `hAd` of the adjoint lifting hypothesis is reused at line 185 for the vanishing statement, and lines 15 to 23 hold three empty sections. The proof of the lemma on links builds the Hida setting, the weight and the residual representation, but the existence statement uses only the level from the unramified inputs ([TLinkEx.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/TLinkEx.lean#L30)). We state the fit condition, the conditions of case II and III, the point count and the lemmas M, M', P and X in words and do not unfold them; we also do not unfold the step from $L$ to $K$ (`adDown`), which we read as the restriction argument above.

This chapter proves the statement of [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/) that the main proof needs: each regular algebraic polarized cuspidal representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$, for a CM field $E$, has a continuous semisimple $\ell$-adic Galois representation $\rho_\pi$ that matches $\pi$ at the places of $E$. The main line starts from a nice Hecke tower of smooth projective varieties. It cuts the [[étale cohomology|7YX76G0838]] of the tower by a system of Hecke eigenvalues and takes a semisimple graded part, a Galois piece. Then it proves the local properties of the piece: the Eichler-Shimura relation at good places away from $\ell$, crystallinity above $\ell$ by the comparison theorem of Faltings, the Hodge-Tate jumps from Hodge cohomology through the Hodge-Tate decomposition, and a crystalline Eichler-Shimura relation for the Frobenius of $D_{\mathrm{cris}}$. With a tower of Kuga-Sato varieties over unitary Shimura varieties and the holomorphic Lefschetz formula, these local steps give the Galois row in weight one, and a theta lift for a unitary dual pair carries it to the general case. Sections 13 to 16 make the plans that feed the Kuga-Sato tower: good models of Hecke correspondences, unitary data, frames and vectors, and test functions with a spectral property. Section 17 proves local-global compatibility at places of depth zero by base change along cyclic extensions, and Section 18 joins the parts into the Galois row. Sections 19 to 22 prove local-global compatibility at the other places: Shintani lifts and mixed components after base change, abelian CM extensions with prescribed splitting, compatibility for plain toral components from spherical places, and compatibility on a class of places. Sections 23 to 25 build the Galois representation as a limit: trace limits at $\ell$, a representation of dimension $a$ cut out of a larger one, and limits of Frobenius traces along congruences. Section 26 is the main line, which joins these results. Sections 27 to 32 prove its last inputs: the cutting of a representation with lacunary Hodge-Tate jumps, the density of forms with generic Satake parameters, families of forms near a large highest weight, the change of the weight at a place above $\ell$, congruences at generic places, and the congruence statement for odd rank.

From Volume 1 the chapter uses [étale cohomology](/learn/geometry/#tale-cohomology) with its finiteness, base change and correspondence action, [decomposition groups, inertia and Frobenius](/learn/galois-representations/#decomposition-groups-inertia-and-frobenius), [the cyclotomic character and Tate modules](/learn/galois-representations/#the-cyclotomic-character-and-tate-modules), [period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings), [crystalline, de Rham and potentially semistable representations](/learn/galois-representations/#crystalline-de-rham-and-potentially-semistable-representations), [the Weil group](/learn/galois-representations/#the-weil-group), and [the Galois representation of an automorphic representation](/learn/galois-representations/#the-galois-representation-of-an-automorphic-representation). Its top theorem is the step [the Galois row from a theta lift](/step/galois-representations-of-theta-lift/), and its geometric part is the step [GeoOne holds](/step/geo-one-holds/). Through these it gives the hypothesis [Row0b](/step/row0b-def/) of the step [the Galois row holds](/step/galois-row-holds/), which the main proof uses for [every character](/step/every-character/).

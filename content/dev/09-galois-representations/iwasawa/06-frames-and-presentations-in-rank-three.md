## Frames and presentations in rank three

This section sets up the deformation theory that the propagation argument of the next section uses. A *frame* packs the Galois data of a CM field: a profinite group with a complex conjugation, a finite set of local groups, the cyclotomic character and a residual character $\Psi$. We prove that the Galois data of the window step give a frame with good cohomological bounds and many places. Then we study the universal ring of polarized ordinary lifts of rank three of the diagonal representation $\Psi \oplus 1 \oplus \Psi^{-1}$. At a point where the lift is not upper triangular, we find an ideal whose minimal primes are large and connected in codimension one, and on which each component is of one of two known types. The frame result feeds [Theorem 8.1.6](/entry/vwin-of-hypotheses-h-e-alt/), and the ideal feeds [Theorem 4.7.5](/entry/gd-ok/). The background is in [deformation functors](/learn/deformations-patching/#deformation-functors) and [local rings and dimension](/learn/commutative-algebra/#local-rings-and-dimension).

### Frames

**Definition 4.6.1 (Frame).** Let $l$ be a prime and $k$ a field. A *frame* over $k$ consists of:

1. a profinite group $\Gamma$, a character $\delta : \Gamma \to \{\pm 1\}$ and an element $c \in \Gamma$; put $\Gamma_0 = \ker \delta$;
2. a finite set $V$ and, for $v \in V$, a profinite group $G_v$, a homomorphism $\mathrm{loc}_v : G_v \to \Gamma_0$, a subgroup $I_v \subseteq G_v$ and an integer $d_v \geq 0$;
3. a finite set $\mathrm{Inf}$ and elements $c_w \in \Gamma$ for $w \in \mathrm{Inf}$;
4. characters $\varepsilon : \Gamma \to \mathbb{Z}_l^\times$ and $\Psi : \Gamma \to k^\times$, and an element $g_0 \in \Gamma_0$.

Put $d = \sum_v d_v$, let $\omega : \Gamma \to k^\times$ be $\varepsilon$ modulo $l$, and let $g^* = c g^{-1} c^{-1}$ for $g \in \Gamma_0$.

The model is the Galois group of a CM field $L$ over its totally real subfield $L^+$: $\delta$ cuts out $L$, $c$ is a complex conjugation, $V$ is a set of places of $L^+$ with decomposition groups $G_v$, inertia groups $I_v$ and local degrees $d_v$, the $c_w$ are the complex conjugations at the infinite places, and $\varepsilon$ is the cyclotomic character.

**Definition 4.6.2 (Good frame).** Let $C_0 \geq 0$ and let $k$ have characteristic $l$. A frame is *good of bound $C_0$* if $l$ is odd, $k$ is finite, $\Gamma_0$ is open, $c^2 = 1$, $\delta(c) = -1$, the maps $\mathrm{loc}_v$ and $\varepsilon$ are continuous, $\ker \Psi$ is open, $\Psi(c) = 1$, $\Psi(g_0)^2 \neq 1$, $\# \mathrm{Inf} = d$, $c_w^2 = 1$ and $\delta(c_w) = -1$ for all $w$, and these conditions hold:

1. (genericity) $\omega \neq 1$ on $\Gamma_0$; $\omega \Psi^{j} \neq 1$ on $\Gamma_0$ for $j = \pm 1$; $\omega \Psi^j \delta \neq 1$ on $\Gamma_0$ for $j = \pm 2$; $\Psi^2 \circ \mathrm{loc}_v \neq 1$ for each $v$; and $\omega|_{\Gamma_0}$ is not a power of $\Psi|_{\Gamma_0}$;
2. (classes) the groups $H^1$ of $\Gamma$ with coefficients $k(\Psi^{-1}\delta)$ and $k(\Psi^{-2}\delta)$, with the strict local conditions at $V$, are nonzero;
3. (local structure) $\Psi \circ \mathrm{loc}_v$ is trivial on $I_v \cap \ker(\varepsilon \circ \mathrm{loc}_v)$; the $l$-part of the abelianized inertia at $v$ is generated by $d_v$ elements of $I_v$, all but one of them in this kernel; and the abelian $l$-quotients of $\Gamma_0$ that kill all the inertia groups (and their conjugates by $c$) have bounded exponent;
4. (Euler characteristic) for every finite field $K$ of characteristic $l$, every continuous representation $W$ of $\Gamma$ over $K$ with no nonzero $\omega$-equivariant linear form, and every family of subspaces $b_v \subseteq W$ stable under $G_v$,
$$\dim H^2 + \dim H^0 + \sum_v d_v \dim b_v \leq \dim H^1 + \sum_w \dim \ker(\rho(c_w) - 1),$$
for the cohomology of the cone of $W$ relative to the local subspaces $b_v$;
5. (bounds) the rank of $H^1(\Gamma, k(\Psi^{-2}\delta))$ with strict local conditions and the rank of $H^1(\Gamma_0, k(\Psi^{-1}))$ with strict local conditions at $V$ and $cVc$ are at most $C_0$, and the rank of $H^1(\Gamma, k)$ with relaxed local conditions is at most $1 + C_0$.

The frame is *big for $C_0$* if $3 C_0 + 7 < d$ and $5 < d_v$ for every $v$.

The inequality in (4) is the form of the global Euler characteristic formula that the deformation theory needs: tangent spaces are large when the local terms $d_v \dim b_v$ are large. Bigness says that there are many local degrees of freedom compared with the global classes counted by $C_0$.

**Theorem 4.6.3 (Existence of frames).** Assume the cohomological inputs on global Galois cohomology. Let $l$ be a prime, let $O$ be a local ring whose residue field $k$ has characteristic $l$, and let $\eta : G_\mathbb{Q} \to \{\pm 1\}$, $\Psi : G_\mathbb{Q} \to O^\times$, $\sigma \in G_\mathbb{Q}$, a place $B$ of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ over $l$, $M \geq 1$ and an open subgroup $H_b$ satisfy the generic conditions of the window step: $l$ is odd, $k$ is finite, $2l \mid M$, $\sigma$ is a complex conjugation, $\eta$ is the character of an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant supported on $M$ and is unramified at $l$, $\bar\Psi(\sigma) = 1$, $\bar\Psi$ is a power of $\omega$ on inertia at $l$, and the further conditions of genericity. Then for every $Q \neq 0$ there are a CM field $L$, an element $g_1 \in G_\mathbb{Q}$, a frame $\Phi$ over $k$ attached to $L$ and these data, integers $C_0$ and a prime $w$, such that $\Phi$ is good of bound $C_0$ and big for $C_0$, $w \nmid M$, $w \equiv 1 \pmod l$, $w \equiv 1 \pmod Q$, the decomposition groups at $w$ lie in the kernels of $\eta$, $\bar\Psi$ and $\omega$, and the subgroup of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ attached to $L$ acts on the $w$-th roots of unity through $l$-th powers.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Fr.frame_ex`.

*Proof.* Choose the open subgroup $H_0$ of index two that splits the places over $M$ like $\eta$, and the witness prime $w \equiv 1 \pmod Q$ by Chebotarev's theorem. The class rank of the field cut out by $\ker \eta \cap \ker \bar\Psi \cap \ker \omega$ inside the layers of the tower is bounded by a constant $C_0$ that does not depend on the layer. Put $m = 3 C_0 + 7$, so that $m < l^m$. Let $U$ be the intersection of $H_0$, the subgroup acting on $\mu_w$ through $l$-th powers, $H_b$ and the $m$-th layer of the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_l$-extension, and let $L$ be its fixed field, a CM field. The decomposition group at $l$ has relative index at least $l^m$ in $U$, so the local degree $d_v$ at each place over $l$ is at least $l^m > 3C_0 + 7$. Choose $s$ in the decomposition group at $B$ and in $U$ with $\bar\Psi(s)^2 \neq 1$; it lies in $\ker \eta$ and in $G_L$. The frame $\Phi$ is the frame of $L$ linked at $s$. Its good properties come from the generic conditions, from the nontrivial classes for $\bar\Psi^{-1}\eta$ and $\bar\Psi^{-2}\eta$, from the Euler characteristic formula and from the class rank bound, which passes from $\mathbb{Q}$ to $L^+$ by restriction. Bigness follows from $d_v \geq l^m$ and $d \geq d_v$. $\square$

### Ordinary polarized representations

Fix a good frame over the residue field $k$ of a complete discrete valuation ring $O$, and let $n \in \{2, 3\}$. Put $w_3(i) = 1 - i$ for $i = 0, 1, 2$, and $w_n(i) = n - 1 - 2i$ otherwise. Let $J$ be the antidiagonal matrix of the polarization.

**Definition 4.6.4 (Ordinary polarized representation).** Let $K$ be a commutative ring. An *ordinary polarized representation* of rank $n$ over $K$ is a homomorphism $r : \Gamma_0 \to M_n(K)$ with characters $\psi_{v,i} : G_v \to K^\times$ for $v \in V$ and $i < n$, such that $r(g^*) = J \, r(g)^{T} J$, the matrix $r(g_0) - r(g_0^*)$ is diagonal, and for each $v$ and all $h_0, \dots, h_{n-1} \in G_v$,
$$\prod_{i = 0}^{n-1} \big( r(\mathrm{loc}_v h_i) - \psi_{v,i}(h_i) \big) = 0 .$$

The product condition is the ordinary condition: over a field it says that $r|_{G_v}$ has a full flag with diagonal characters $\psi_{v,0}, \dots, \psi_{v,n-1}$ in this order. The *residual representation* is the diagonal one, $g \mapsto \mathrm{diag}(\Psi(g)^{w_n(i)})$. For $n = 3$ this is $\Psi \oplus 1 \oplus \Psi^{-1}$.

**Definition 4.6.5 (The space of lifts).** The *space* of rank $n$ is a complete local noetherian $O$-algebra $R$ with an ordinary polarized representation $\rho$ of rank $n$ that lifts the residual representation and is universal for such continuous lifts. Let $B \subseteq R$ be the closure of the $O$-algebra generated by the products of entries of total weight zero and by the values of the $\psi_{v,i}$, let $A \subseteq R$ be the $B$-algebra generated by the entries of $\rho$, and let $u$ be $\rho$ with values in $A$.

For a representation $\rho$ over $K$ we use three ideals: $\mathrm{tri}^+(\rho)$, generated by the entries $r(g)_{ij}$ with $j < i$; $\mathrm{tri}^-(\rho)$, generated by those with $i < j$; and $e(\rho)$, generated by those with $w_n(i) - w_n(j)$ odd. A prime $P$ is *off-triangular* if it contains neither $\mathrm{tri}^+$ nor $\mathrm{tri}^-$. For $n = 3$, $P$ is of *type TI* if it is off-triangular and $e(\rho) \not\subseteq P$, and of *type TE* if it is off-triangular and $e(\rho) \subseteq P$. A TE prime is *thick* if, modulo $P$, there is a second representation that agrees with $\rho$ on the entries of even weight difference, kills the entries $(1,0)$ and $(1,2)$, is ordinary for the same characters, and has no fixed line through the middle basis vector.

For $n = 3$ the entries with odd weight difference are the four entries next to the diagonal. Modulo a TE prime the representation is a sum of the middle character and a rank two piece on the outer basis vectors.

**Definition 4.6.6 (Localization).** Let $x$ be a prime of $A$. A *localization at $x$* is a finite unramified extension $O'$ of $O$ with a complete local $O'$-algebra $R_x$ and a map $\iota : A \to R_x$ whose maximal ideal pulls back to $x$, universal for maps out of $A$ with the same residues. Put $\rho_x = \iota \circ u$.

### The even presentation

Let $\sigma$ be an involution of $R_x$ over $O'$ that acts on each entry $\rho_x(g)_{ij}$ by the sign $(-1)^{w_3(i) - w_3(j)}$ and fixes the image of $B$. Its eigenspaces give a grading by $\mathbb{Z}/2$.

**Definition 4.6.7 (Even presentation).** An *even presentation* of $R_x$ consists of a surjection $p : O'[[X_1, \dots, X_a, Y_1, \dots, Y_g]] \to R_x$ with even variables $X$ and odd variables $Y$, even relations $F_1, \dots, F_b$ and odd relations $G_1, \dots, G_g$ generating $\ker p$, such that $a = b + 3d + 1$, all linear coefficients of elements of $\ker p$ lie in the maximal ideal of $O'$, each entry $\rho_x(g)_{ij}$ is the image of an element of parity $w_3(i) - w_3(j)$, the image of $B$ is even, and $e(\rho_x)$ is generated by the images of the odd variables $Y_j$.

**Theorem 4.6.8 (Even presentations).** Let the frame be good of bound $C_0$, let $x$ be a prime of $A$ for the space of rank three with $e(u) \subseteq x$, and let $R_x$ be a localization at $x$. Then $R_x$ has an even presentation.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Fr.epres`.

*Proof.* Since $e(u) \subseteq x$, conjugation by $\mathrm{diag}(1, -1, 1)$ preserves the lifts at $x$, and by universality it gives the involution $\sigma$, with the properties above. The residue field has odd characteristic, so $2$ is a unit and every element splits into its even and odd parts. Take a minimal presentation of $R_x$ compatible with the grading: the even and odd variables span the even and odd parts of the tangent space, and the even and odd relations span the even and odd parts of the obstruction space. The tangent and obstruction spaces are $H^1$ and $H^2$ of the even part ($j = 0$) and the odd part ($j = 1$) of the adjoint representation, with the ordinary local conditions. Apply the Euler characteristic inequality of the frame to each part. For the even part, each local subspace $b_v$ has dimension at least $4$ and the fixed space of each $c_w$ has dimension at most $1$; for the odd part the numbers are $2$ and $2$. Since $\# \mathrm{Inf} = d$, this gives
$$\begin{aligned} \dim H^1_{\mathrm{even}} - \dim H^2_{\mathrm{even}} &\geq 4d - d - \dim H^0 , \\ \dim H^1_{\mathrm{odd}} - \dim H^2_{\mathrm{odd}} &\geq 2d - 2d . \end{aligned}$$
So the number of even variables exceeds the number of even relations by at least $3d + 1$ after the correction by the constants, and the number of odd relations is at most the number of odd variables. Adding redundant even relations and odd relations gives the exact counts $a = b + 3d + 1$ and $g$ odd relations. The odd variables generate $e(\rho_x)$ because the odd entries generate it and each is an odd series, hence in the ideal of the $Y_j$. $\square$

### Sharp ideals

**Definition 4.6.9 (Chained ideal).** Let $I$ be an ideal of a ring $R$ and $D \geq 0$. Then $I$ is *chained in dimension $D$* if $\dim R/P \geq D + 1$ for every minimal prime $P$ over $I$, and any two minimal primes over $I$ are joined by a chain $P = P_0, P_1, \dots, P_s = Q$ of minimal primes with $\dim R / (P_{t-1} + P_t) \geq D$ for each $t$.

**Definition 4.6.10 (Sharp ideal).** Let $x$ be a prime of $A$ for the space of rank three and $R_x$ a localization. An ideal $I$ of $R_x$ is *sharp* if: $I = 0$ when $e(u) \not\subseteq x$; $I \subseteq P$ for each prime $P$ with $e(\rho_x) \not\subseteq P$; $I$ is chained in dimension $3d + 1$; and each minimal prime over $I$ is of type TI or is thick.

**Theorem 4.6.11 (Sharp ideals in rank three).** Let the frame be good of bound $C_0$ and big for $C_0$. Let $x$ be a prime of $A$ for the space of rank three with $\mathrm{tri}^+(u) \not\subseteq x$, and let $R_x$ be a localization at $x$. Then $R_x$ has a sharp ideal.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Fr.sharp3`.

*Proof.* First, every minimal prime $P$ over an ideal $I$ chained in dimension $3d+1$ is off-triangular. The ideal $\mathrm{tri}^+(\rho_x)$ is the unit ideal because $\mathrm{tri}^+(u) \not\subseteq x$. The quotient by $\mathrm{tri}^-(\rho_x)$ has dimension less than $3d$, since upper triangular lifts have fewer degrees of freedom and the frame is big. If $P$ contained $\mathrm{tri}^-$, then $3d + 2 \leq \dim R_x/P < 3d$, which is absurd.

Case $e(u) \subseteq x$. Take an even presentation. Each odd relation $G_i$ is odd with no linear term, so $G = M Y$ for a $g \times g$ matrix $M$ of series. Let $I$ be the ideal generated by $p(\det M)$. Its preimage is generated by $\ker p$ and $\det M$, that is by $b + 1$ series in $a + g$ variables over $O'$, of which $g$ define the ideal $(MY)$. Since $\mathrm{adj}(M) M Y = \det(M) Y$, the preimage contains $\det(M) Y_j$ for all $j$, and the ring is cut out by $b + 1$ equations on the locus $Y = 0$ union the locus $\det M = 0$. By Krull's height theorem every minimal prime has dimension at least $1 + a + g - g - (b + 1) = 3d + 1 + 1$, and by Hartshorne's connectedness theorem for complete intersections the minimal primes are chained in codimension one. For a prime $P$ with $e(\rho_x) \not\subseteq P$ some $p(Y_j) \notin P$, and $\det(M) p(Y_j) = 0$ gives $p(\det M) \in P$. Each minimal prime over $I$ is off-triangular; if it contains $e(\rho_x)$ it is thick by the construction of the thick representation from $M$, and otherwise it is of type TI.

Case $e(u) \not\subseteq x$. Take $I = 0$. A presentation of $R_x$ with $N = N' + 3d + 1$ variables and $N'$ relations exists by the same Euler characteristic count without grading. Then $R_x$ is a quotient of a regular ring of dimension $N + 1$ by $N'$ elements, so every minimal prime has dimension at least $3d + 2$, and the connectedness theorem gives the chain condition. No prime of $R_x$ contains $e(\rho_x)$, since such a prime would pull back to a prime below $x$ containing $e(u)$. So every minimal prime is of type TI. $\square$

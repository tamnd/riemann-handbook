## Propagation through components in rank three

This section proves that, in the space of rank three lifts, every prime of type TI lies over a fixed ideal of the base ring, if one seed component does and the same is known in rank two. The argument is a local-to-global propagation. Goodness spreads from one irreducible component to the next through closed points where the components meet. At each closed point, the [sharp ideal](/entry/sharp3/) of the previous section controls how the components meet, and the hypotheses on the local flip control how goodness passes through thick components. The result is the rank three step of the [window theorem](/entry/vwin-of-hypotheses-h-e-alt/), and it uses the [frames](/entry/frame-ex/) of the previous section. The background is in [local rings and dimension](/learn/commutative-algebra/#local-rings-and-dimension) and [local and global deformation rings](/learn/deformations-patching/#local-deformation-problems-and-global-deformation-rings).

We keep the notation of the previous section: a frame over $k$, good of bound $C_0$ and big for $C_0$, a space of rank $n$ with rings $B \subseteq A$, the representation $u$ over $A$, and the map $\pi$ from primes of $A$ to primes of $B$, $\pi(p) = p \cap B$.

### Good primes

Let $\Lambda \subseteq B$ be the ideal generated by the maximal ideal of $O$ and by the elements $\psi_{v,i}(h) - 1$ for $h$ in the inertia at $v$ with $\Psi(h) = 1$. An ideal $M$ of $B$ is *of finite $\Lambda$-type* if the maximal ideal of $B$ lies in the radical of $M + \Lambda$. Fix such an ideal $M$.

**Definition 4.7.1 (Good prime).** A prime $p$ of $A$ is *good* if $M \subseteq \pi(p)$. The space is *all good* for $M$ if every prime of type TI is good.

The base locus of the theory is the set of primes $p$ of $A$ with $\pi(p)$ containing the maximal ideal of $B$ and $\mathrm{tri}^+(u) \not\subseteq p$. These are the primes over the closed point of $B$ at which the lift is not lower triangular. Write $\mathrm{Core}$ for this set and $\mathrm{Cl}$ for its closed points.

We use four hypotheses on the rank three space.

1. (local good primes) At each closed point $x \in \mathrm{Cl}$ and each localization $R_x$, if $p$ is a prime of type TI over a good prime that is *nice* (its quotient is a domain with the expected residual behaviour), then every prime of $R_x$ below $p$ is good.
2. (the flip) Goodness passes along a chain of thick primes from one TI prime to another, through the intersections of the chain.
3. (seed) There are a prime $\xi$ of $A$ of type TI and a prime $Z$ of $B$ with $M \subseteq Z \subseteq \pi(\xi)$ and $\dim B/Z = 1 + 3d$.
4. (rank two) The rank two space is all good for an ideal $M_2$ of finite $\Lambda$-type.

The seed is the place where the propagation starts. The rank two hypothesis controls the TE primes, since modulo a TE prime the lift has a rank two piece.

### Global and local data

**Definition 4.7.2 (Global data).** *Global data* on a noetherian topological space $X$ are subsets $\mathrm{TI}$, $\mathrm{Good}$, $\mathrm{Core}$, $\mathrm{Cl}$ and $\mathrm{EE}$ of $X$. They are *good* if:

1. $\mathrm{Cl} \subseteq \mathrm{Core}$ and $\mathrm{Core}$ is preconnected;
2. every closed set that meets $\mathrm{Core}$ meets $\mathrm{Cl}$;
3. $\mathrm{Core}$ lies in the closure of $\mathrm{Cl} \setminus \mathrm{EE}$;
4. every irreducible component that meets $\mathrm{TI}$ meets $\mathrm{Core}$;
5. at each $x \in \mathrm{Cl}$, if $Y$ and $Y'$ are TI components through $x$ and $Y \subseteq \mathrm{Good}$, then $Y' \subseteq \mathrm{Good}$;
6. each point of $\mathrm{Cl} \setminus \mathrm{EE}$ lies on a TI component;
7. some TI component lies in $\mathrm{Good}$.

**Lemma 4.7.3 (Global propagation).** If global data on a noetherian space are good, then $\mathrm{TI} \subseteq \mathrm{Good}$.

*Proof.* Call a TI component good if it lies in $\mathrm{Good}$. Let $U$ be the union of the good TI components and $U'$ the union of the other TI components; both are closed, since $X$ has finitely many components. By (5) a point of $\mathrm{Cl}$ cannot lie on a good and on a bad TI component. By (6) and (3), each point of $\mathrm{Core}$ is a limit of points of $\mathrm{Cl}$ on TI components, so $\mathrm{Core} \subseteq U \cup U'$, and by (2) and (5) the sets $U \cap \mathrm{Core}$ and $U' \cap \mathrm{Core}$ are disjoint. Since $\mathrm{Core}$ is preconnected, one of them is empty. By (7) and (4), $U \cap \mathrm{Core}$ is nonempty, so $U' \cap \mathrm{Core}$ is empty, and by (4) there is no bad TI component. Each TI point lies on a TI component, so it is good. $\square$

**Definition 4.7.4 (Local data).** On the spectrum of a localization $R_x$ the *local data* are the integer $D = 3d + 1$, the sets of primes of type TI and of type TE, the good primes (those whose image in $B$ contains $M$), the nice primes, the thick primes and the sharp ideal $I$. The local data give the *conclusion* that if some good TI prime $P$ has $\dim R_x/P \geq D$, then every TI minimal prime is good. The local propagation lemma says that the conclusion holds when the local data satisfy the local form of the hypotheses: goodness goes up and TI goes down along specialization, nice primes are good and of known type, the components of the sharp ideal are chained in dimension $D$, the dichotomy holds, and the hypotheses (1) and (2) hold.

### The global data are good

The global data of the space are the spectrum of $A$ with these sets: $\mathrm{TI}$ is the set of primes $p$ at which $u$ is of type TI, $\mathrm{Good}$ is the set of good primes, $\mathrm{Core}$ and $\mathrm{Cl}$ are as above, and $\mathrm{EE}$ is the set of primes containing $e(u)$.

**Lemma 4.7.5 (The global data are good).** Let the frame be good of bound $C_0$ and big for $C_0$. Let $M$ be of finite $\Lambda$-type and assume the hypotheses (1), (2) and (3). Let $I_3$ be an ideal of $B$ of finite $\Lambda$-type with $I_3 \subseteq \pi(\xi)$ for every prime $\xi$ of $A$ of type TE. Then the global data of the space of rank three are good.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Pr.gd_ok`.

*Proof.* At each closed point $x \in \mathrm{Cl}$ choose a localization $R_x$ and a sharp ideal $I$, by the Theorem on sharp ideals. The local data satisfy the local hypotheses: (1) and (2) hold by assumption, the dichotomy and the chain condition hold by sharpness, and the thick primes are controlled by $I_3$. So the local conclusion holds at $x$.

The ring $A$ is noetherian. The connectedness theorem for the rank three space shows that $\mathrm{Core}$ is nonempty and preconnected, and that it lies in the closure of $\mathrm{Core} \setminus \mathrm{EE}$. The ring $A$ is of finite type over the complete local ring $B$, so it is Jacobson over the closed point: every closed set that meets $\mathrm{Core}$ meets $\mathrm{Cl}$, which gives (2). The same property, applied to an open neighbourhood of a point of $\mathrm{Core} \setminus \mathrm{EE}$, gives (3).

For (4), a TI component has a minimal prime of type TI. Its image in $B$ specializes to the maximal ideal, and the lift is not lower triangular on it, so the component meets $\mathrm{Core}$.

For (5), let $x \in \mathrm{Cl}$ and let $Y, Y'$ be TI components through $x$. The localization at $x$ is a chart: each component through $x$ is the closure of the image of a minimal prime $P$ of $R_x$, and $P$ is of type TI if the component is. Since $Y$ is good, $P$ is good; it is a minimal prime over the sharp ideal, so $\dim R_x/P \geq 3d + 2 \geq D$. The local conclusion makes the minimal prime $P'$ of $Y'$ good. The set of good primes is closed, so $Y' \subseteq \mathrm{Good}$.

For (6), let $x \in \mathrm{Cl} \setminus \mathrm{EE}$. The sharp ideal at $x$ is zero, so the minimal primes of $R_x$ are minimal primes over $I$. By the dichotomy each is TI or thick, and a thick prime contains $e(\rho_x)$, so its image would lie in $\mathrm{EE}$ below $x$. So all minimal primes at $x$ are TI and $x$ lies on a TI component.

For (7), the seed gives a closed point $x$ over $Z$ and a prime $P$ of $R_x$ of type TI over $\xi$, with $\dim R_x / P = \dim B/Z = 1 + 3d$ after the shift by the dimension of the residue field, and $M \subseteq Z \subseteq \pi(P)$. Since $3d + 1 \leq 1 + 3d + 1$, the local conclusion at $x$ applies, and the TI component through $P$ is good. $\square$

### The rank three theorem

**Theorem 4.7.6 (Propagation in rank three).** Let the frame be good of bound $C_0$ and big for $C_0$. Let $M_2$ be an ideal of finite $\Lambda$-type for the space of rank two, and suppose that the space of rank two is all good for $M_2$. Let $M$ be an ideal of finite $\Lambda$-type for the space of rank three, and assume the hypotheses (1), (2) and (3) for $M$. Then:

1. every prime of $A$ of type TI is good for $M$;
2. there is an ideal $I$ of $B$ of finite $\Lambda$-type with $I \subseteq \pi(p)$ for every off-triangular prime $p$ of $A$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Pr.components_good_rank_three`.

*Proof.* The rank two result, applied to the rank two piece of the lift modulo a TE prime, gives an ideal $I_3$ of $B$ of finite $\Lambda$-type with $I_3 \subseteq \pi(\xi)$ for every TE prime $\xi$. By the Lemma, the global data are good, and the Lemma on global propagation gives (1).

For (2), put $I = M \cap I_3$. Let $J$ be a prime of $B$ that contains $I + \Lambda$. Then $J$ contains $M$ or $I_3$, so it contains the radical of $M + \Lambda$ or of $I_3 + \Lambda$, hence the maximal ideal of $B$. So the maximal ideal lies in the radical of $I + \Lambda$. Let $p$ be off-triangular. If $e(u) \subseteq p$, then $p$ is of type TE and $I \subseteq I_3 \subseteq \pi(p)$. Otherwise $p$ is of type TI and $I \subseteq M \subseteq \pi(p)$ by (1). $\square$

The classical model of this argument is the connectedness method for deformation rings: if a deformation ring is connected in codimension one and one component is automorphic, then every component is, provided automorphy passes through the intersections of components.

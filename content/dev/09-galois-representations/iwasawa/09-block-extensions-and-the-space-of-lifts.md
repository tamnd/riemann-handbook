## Block extensions and the space of lifts

This section proves two results for four-dimensional representations. The first is a lattice construction of Ribet type: a lift of the reducible representation $r_0$ that is a sum of two blocks of rank two can be replaced by a nonsplit extension of one block by the other, with the same characteristic polynomials and the same local conditions. The second is the existence of the space of lifts for a four-dimensional frame: a ring over a complete local base with a universal pair of a representation and local data, whose base is generated by the coefficients of characteristic polynomials. The block extension feeds [Theorem 8.11.4](/entry/fin0-no-end-all-irr-of-good-con-purity-aut/), and the space of lifts feeds [Theorem 8.11.6](/entry/main4-alt/). The background is in [determinants and residually reducible representations](/learn/deformations-patching/#determinants-and-residually-reducible-representations) and [matrices](/learn/algebra/#matrices).

### Blocks

Index the basis of a four-dimensional space by $0, 1, 2, 3$ and give the indices the weights $\lambda = (1, 0, 1, 0)$. The *A-block* is the pair of indices $\{0, 2\}$ and the *B-block* is the pair $\{1, 3\}$. A matrix $X \in M_4$ is *block* if $X_{ij} = 0$ whenever $\lambda_i \neq \lambda_j$.

**Definition 4.9.1 (Block extension).** Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $r : \Gamma \to M_4(R)$ a homomorphism. Then $r$ is a *block extension* if:

1. $r(g)_{ij} = 0$ whenever $\lambda_j < \lambda_i$, that is, whenever $i$ is in the A-block and $j$ in the B-block;
2. the B-blocks $r(g)_{\{1,3\},\{1,3\}}$ for $g \in \Gamma$ span $M_2(R)$;
3. there is no matrix $Y$, with $Y_{ij} = 0$ unless $\lambda_i < \lambda_j$, such that $(1 - Y) r(g) (1 + Y)$ is block for all $g$.

So $r$ is an extension of the representation on the A-block by the representation on the B-block, the B-block is absolutely irreducible, and the extension does not split. Since $Y^2 = 0$ for such $Y$, the matrix $1 - Y$ is the inverse of $1 + Y$.

### Pairs over the algebraic closure

We keep the good symplectic data of the previous section: $r_0 = \mathrm{diag}(\theta, \Psi, \alpha, 1)$, the set $T = \{l\} \cup S_b$ and the element $s$ in the decomposition group at $l$. An *ambient field* is an algebraically closed field $K$ of characteristic zero with a valuation ring $V$ and compatible maps from the rings of integers of the finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_l$ into $V$.

**Definition 4.9.2 (Kolyvagin pair).** A *pair* for the data and an ambient field is a representation $r : G_\mathbb{Q} \to \mathrm{GSp}_4(V)$ and an element $u \in \mathrm{GSp}_4(V)$ such that:

1. $r$ is defined over a discrete valuation ring inside $V$;
2. $r \equiv r_0$ modulo the maximal ideal, $r$ has multiplier $\theta$, $r$ is unramified outside $T$, and for $p \in S_b$ it is trivial on the part of the inertia at $p$ on which $\psi$ is trivial;
3. $u$ has multiplier $1$, the conjugate $u^{-1} r u$ is upper triangular on the decomposition group at $l$, its diagonal is congruent to $(\theta, \Psi, \alpha, 1)$, and equal to it on the inertia at $l$.

The element $u$ gives the ordinary flag at $l$.

**Theorem 4.9.3 (Block extensions).** Assume the cohomological inputs: the local conditions, Poitou-Tate duality, the global finiteness, the global Euler characteristic formula, the orthogonality of unramified classes and the Greenberg-Wiles formula. Let the symplectic data be good, and let $(r, u)$ be a pair such that $r(g)$ is block for all $g$ and the A-blocks of $r$ span $M_2(K)$. Then there is a pair $(r', u')$ such that $r'$ is a block extension over $K$ and
$$\det(X - r'(g)) = \det(X - r(g))$$
for all $g \in G_\mathbb{Q}$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Ws.bl`.

*Proof.* Two steps below, the existence of the cocycle and the span of the B-block, give the classical reasons. The Lean proof reaches them through its named inputs, which we did not unfold. First, the pair descends to a finite extension: there are a complete discrete valuation ring $A$ inside $V$, finite over $\mathbb{Z}_l$, and a pair $(r_1, u_1)$ over $A$ whose image in $V$ is $(r, u)$. Let $U \subseteq M_4$ be the set of matrices supported on the entries $(i,j)$ with $\lambda_i < \lambda_j$, with $i$ in the B-block and $j$ in the A-block. Since $r$ is block, conjugation by $r$ preserves $U$, and $U \cong \mathrm{Hom}(A\text{-block}, B\text{-block})$ as a module for $G_\mathbb{Q}$.

The cohomological inputs give a nonzero cocycle $z : G_\mathbb{Q} \to U$ for this action such that: $z$ takes values in $\varpi U$ for a uniformizer $\varpi$ of $A$, $z(c) = 0$ for the complex conjugation $c$, $z$ vanishes on the kernel of $r$ at the places away from $l$, the class of $z$ is not a coboundary over $K$, and there is $y \in U$ with $z$ compatible with the flag of $u$ at $l$ through $y$. The class exists by the Greenberg-Wiles formula: the Selmer group of $U$ with the ordinary local conditions is larger than its dual, because the A-block and the B-block have different weights at $l$.

Put $r'(g) = (1 + z(g)) r(g)$ and $u' = (1 - y) u$. Since $z$ is a cocycle and $U^2 = 0$, $r'$ is a homomorphism. It is congruent to $r$, hence to $r_0$, since $z$ is divisible by $\varpi$. Its multiplier is $\theta$, as $1 + z(g)$ is symplectic of multiplier $1$, and it is unramified outside $T$ and trivial on the required inertia at $S_b$ by the conditions on $z$. The flag condition for $u'$ follows from the condition on $z$ through $y$. So $(r', u')$ is a pair.

The entries of $r'$ with $i$ in the A-block and $j$ in the B-block are those of $r$, hence zero, so (1) holds. The B-block of $r'$ is the B-block of $r$, and its span is $M_2(K)$ by the dual of the spanning hypothesis on the A-block under the symplectic form. If some $Y$ made $r'$ block, then $z$ would be the coboundary of $Y$, which is excluded; this gives (3). Finally $z(g) r(g)$ is strictly block triangular with respect to the blocks of $r(g)$, so $r'(g)$ and $r(g)$ have the same characteristic polynomial. $\square$

The classical source of this construction is Ribet's proof of the converse of Herbrand's theorem, in which a lattice in an irreducible representation is chosen so that its reduction is a nonsplit extension. Here the extension is made directly by a cocycle.

### Four-dimensional frames

**Definition 4.9.4 (Four-dimensional frame).** Let $O$ be a ring and $\Gamma$ a compact totally disconnected group. A *four-dimensional frame* over $O$ consists of an index set of places with subgroups $G_v$ and $I_v$ of $\Gamma$, sets $\mathcal{M}$ and $N$ of elements, characters $\chi_0, \dots, \chi_3 : \Gamma \to O^\times$, a character $\nu$ and an element $g_0 \in \Gamma$. A *point* over a ring $R$ is a homomorphism $r : \Gamma \to M_4(R)$ with elements $n_v$ for the places.

The frame is *standard* for a prime $l$ and a map $a : O \to \Lambda$ to a complete discrete valuation ring $\Lambda$ with finite residue field of characteristic $l$, if: the reduction of the frame is good, that is, $\chi_i \chi_{3 - i} = \nu$, the $\chi_i$ are trivial on $N$, their values at $g_0$ are distinct with unit differences, $2$ is a unit, and the characters are locally constant; the characteristic is $l$; the maps are continuous; the open subgroups have finitely many $\mathbb{Z}/l$-characters trivial on the normal closure of $N$; and the conditions on closedness, on the places and on $\mathcal{M}$ hold.

**Definition 4.9.5 (Space of lifts).** A *space* for a standard frame and $a$ consists of:

1. a complete local ring $B$ over $\Lambda$ and a $B$-algebra $A$ of finite type, with $B \to A$ injective;
2. a continuous point $(P, n)$ over $A$, with $A$ generated over $B$ by the entries of $P$;
3. a continuous map $\mathrm{cp} : \Gamma \to B[X]$ lifting the characteristic polynomials of $P$ and reducing to $\prod_i (X - \chi_i)$;
4. elements of $B$ lifting the values of the $\chi_i$;
5. the condition that $B$ is generated by the coefficients of $\mathrm{cp}$ and these elements;
6. the universal property: every lift of the residual point factors through $A$;
7. the map to the polynomial ring over the residue field.

So $B$ is the ring of pseudo-characters with the given local data, and $A$ is the ring of lifts over it. This is the shape of the space used in rank three in the Section on frames.

**Theorem 4.9.6 (Existence of the space of lifts).** Let $\Lambda$ be a complete discrete valuation ring with finite residue field, and let the frame be standard for $l$ and $a : O \to \Lambda$. Then the frame has a space.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Ws.spaceEx`.

*Proof.* The finiteness condition on characters is the $\Phi$-condition of Mazur, so the universal ring $R$ of lifts of the residual point exists and is noetherian. The torus of diagonal matrices acts on $R$, and since the values of the $\chi_i$ at $g_0$ are distinct with unit differences, this action gives a grading of $R$ with a weight presentation, and the universal point is homogeneous. Let $B$ be the closure of the part of weight zero. It contains the coefficients of the characteristic polynomials, which are invariant under conjugation, and it is generated by them and by the lifts of the characters, because every monomial of weight zero in the entries is a combination of traces of products by the distinctness at $g_0$. Let $A$ be the $B$-algebra generated by the entries of the universal point. It is of finite type over $B$, the map $B \to A$ is injective, and the universal property of $R$ gives the universal property of $A$. The continuity of $\mathrm{cp}$ follows from that of the point. $\square$

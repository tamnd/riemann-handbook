This volume proves the results on Galois cohomology, Galois representations and modularity that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. It proves [local class field theory](/learn/class-field-theory/#local-class-field-theory) and the local Euler characteristic formula, the global reciprocity law and Poitou-Tate duality with restricted ramification. It attaches to each regular algebraic polarized cuspidal representation of $\mathrm{GL}_a$ over a CM field a [Galois representation](/learn/galois-representations/#the-galois-representation-of-an-automorphic-representation) that matches it at the places, from the cohomology of Shimura varieties and a theta lift. The second half proves a bound for the lengths of Tate-Shafarevich groups of cyclotomic twists, the window bound, by the method of Ribet in higher rank: [Iwasawa theory](/learn/galois-cohomology/#iwasawa-theory), the [Euler system of Kato](/learn/galois-cohomology/#euler-systems-and-kato), Hecke algebras, [deformation rings](/learn/deformations-patching/#deformation-functors) and patching, and modularity theorems in ranks two to four. Volume 1 gives the definitions and recalls these statements. This volume proves them.

The proof uses the volume in two of its chapters. [The Galois row chapter](/proof/galois-row/) takes its top statement from Chapter 3: the step [the Galois row from a theta lift](/step/galois-representations-of-theta-lift/) and its geometric part [GeoOne holds](/step/geo-one-holds/) give the hypothesis [Row0b](/step/row0b-def/) of the step [the Galois row holds](/step/galois-row-holds/). The row is used for [EveryCharacter from the Galois row](/step/every-character/), which also takes the Euler characteristic formula of Chapter 1 and the reciprocity law and first inequality of Chapter 2. The main theorem of complex multiplication and the base case of Poitou-Tate duality enter [EveryCharacter from Row0b](/step/every-character-of-row/). [The window bound chapter](/proof/vwin/) uses Chapters 4 to 8 through the step [VWin from the rank n inputs](/step/v-window-of-depth-zero-residual-bc-cm/), which gives [VWin from Row0b](/step/vwin-of-row/). Chapter 8 also uses the [base change for unitary groups](/step/base-change-n/), the [published inputs](/proof/literature/) and [the CM inputs](/step/cm-abelian-inputs-from-literature/) of the proof.

Chapter 1 proves local class field theory for finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and the local Euler characteristic formula. It builds the Lubin-Tate tower and computes the reciprocity map on it, [Theorem 1.2.4](/entry/reciprocity-lubin-tate/). The existence theorem says that every subgroup of finite index of $K^\times$ is a norm group, [Theorem 1.4.6](/entry/existence/), and the universal norms are trivial, [Corollary 1.4.7](/entry/eq-one-of-forall-mem-norm-grp/). Gluing the finite layers gives local reciprocity in characteristic zero, [Theorem 1.5.6](/entry/artin-reciprocity-char-zero/). The last two sections prove Tate's formula for a finite module $M$ of order invertible in $K$: the coprime case by Kummer theory, [Corollary 1.6.6](/entry/euler-char-coprime/), and the general case by the ramification filtration, [Theorem 1.7.7](/entry/euler-char-galois/). The proof does not use local duality.

Chapter 2 proves the global results. It computes the Herbrand quotient of the idele class group, [Theorem 2.1.3](/entry/cyc-herbrand-idele-class-group/), proves the first inequality, [Theorem 2.1.4](/entry/index-range-idele-class-norm/), and the global reciprocity law, [Theorem 2.2.2](/entry/idele-symbol-diagonal-and-ker-le/). Section 3 proves the main theorem of complex multiplication, [Theorem 2.3.2](/entry/main-theorem-complex-multiplication/). Sections 4 to 7 prove Poitou-Tate duality for the group $G_S$: the base case, [Theorem 2.5.7](/entry/gs-base-case/), the vanishing of compact cohomology in high degrees, [Theorem 2.6.10](/entry/gs-tate-compact-cohomology-eq-zero/), and the hypotheses with real places, [Theorem 2.7.10](/entry/gs-tate-poitou-tate-hypotheses/). Section 8 bounds the unramified cokernel of a lattice with Hodge-Tate data, [Theorem 2.8.5](/entry/u7/). The last sections give Selmer presentations, [Theorem 2.9.2](/entry/one-matrix-of-galois-cohomology/), and a ray class character all of whose powers prime to $\ell$ are good twists, [Theorem 2.10.5](/entry/twist-all-powers-of-galois-cohomology-class-field/).

Chapter 3 proves the Galois row. It cuts the [étale cohomology](/learn/geometry/#tale-cohomology) of a Hecke tower of smooth projective varieties by a system of Hecke eigenvalues, so that Galois pieces exist, [Theorem 3.1.6](/entry/rep-ex-holds/). Then it proves the local properties of a piece: the Eichler-Shimura relation, [Theorem 3.2.6](/entry/es-step-holds/), crystallinity above $\ell$, [Theorem 3.3.4](/entry/crys-step-of/), the bijective Hodge-Tate map, [Theorem 3.4.4](/entry/ht-bij/), the Hodge-Tate jumps, [Theorem 3.5.3](/entry/jump-step-holds/), and the crystalline Eichler-Shimura relation, [Theorem 3.6.1](/entry/geo-side-of/). With Kuga-Sato varieties over unitary Shimura varieties and the holomorphic Lefschetz formula, [Theorem 3.18.1](/entry/weight-one-geometric-realization/), these give the Galois row in weight one, [Corollary 3.7.4](/entry/geo-one-holds/). The row itself is [Theorem 3.7.5](/entry/galois-local-global-strong-rec-parameter/), and a theta lift carries weight one to it, [Theorem 3.18.6](/entry/galois-local-global-strong-of-geometry-lift/). Section 17 proves compatibility at depth zero, [Theorem 3.17.12](/entry/local-global-compat-at-iwahori-fixed-of-unlinked/). The later sections build the representation as a limit along congruences. Their main line is [Theorem 3.27.18](/entry/core-all-of/), with the cutting, [Theorem 3.28.7](/entry/div-set-holds/), the density of generic forms, [Theorem 3.29.9](/entry/dense-gen-holds/), and the congruence statement for odd rank, [Theorem 3.33.5](/entry/cong-fin-odd-of-ex/).

Chapter 4 proves the Iwasawa theory inputs of the window bound. The theorem of Ferrero and Washington bounds the $l$-rank of class groups in cyclotomic towers, [Theorem 4.1.11](/entry/ferrero-washington/), and gives the part at $2$, [Theorem 4.2.7](/entry/thm-bab-of-lemma-k/). For odd $l$, Kolyvagin's induction with Gauss sums bounds a Selmer group by a Stickelberger value, [Theorem 4.4.4](/entry/ml-count/), which is a generalized Bernoulli number, [Theorem 4.5.5](/entry/v-char/). Then the chapter builds frames for ordinary lifts of rank three, [Theorem 4.6.3](/entry/frame-ex/), and shows that the components are good, [Theorem 4.7.6](/entry/components-good-rank-three/). It constructs models of symplectic deformation rings, [Theorem 4.8.4](/entry/ex-model/), with the trace inequality of the Ribet method, [Theorem 4.8.5](/entry/galois-rep-ineq-of-all-irreducible-nonvanishing/), and models for unitary groups, [Theorem 4.8.7](/entry/exists-model/). It ends with block extensions, [Theorem 4.9.3](/entry/bl/), and the space of lifts in rank four, [Theorem 4.9.6](/entry/space-ex/).

Chapter 5 proves the Euler system results of the window bound. The logarithmic derivative of a zeta class made from Siegel units is a coefficient of a product of two Eisenstein series of weight one, [Theorem 5.2.8](/entry/law/), also for diamond translates, [Theorem 5.3.2](/entry/twist/). Section 4 builds Kato's norm-compatible system of zeta classes, [Theorem 5.4.4](/entry/k4-top/), and Section 5 proves with Coleman power series that its class does not vanish on inertia at $\ell$, [Theorem 5.5.10](/entry/p-locu/). Ordinary cocycles of the adjoint of an automorphic representation of dimension 2 to 4 are coboundaries, [Theorem 5.6.8](/entry/ord-van2/). The last section builds Hilbert modular forms of level one congruent to one, [Theorem 5.7.5](/entry/hilbert-one/).

Chapter 6 collects the Hecke side of the Kato argument for newforms of odd weight. It builds a standard tower of modular functions, [Theorem 6.1.5](/entry/tower-exists/), proves the Eichler and Shimura decomposition, [Theorem 6.3.5](/entry/exists-per-add-cj-per/), and the semisimplicity of the Hecke action at a newform, [Theorem 6.4.3](/entry/hecke-semisimple-at-newform/). It proves the Eichler and Shimura relation modulo prime powers, [Theorem 6.6.5](/entry/eichler-shimura-frobenius-quadratic-mod-prime-pow/), and builds the Hecke projector for a newform, [Theorem 6.7.5](/entry/hecke-proj-newform/). Rankin values give a nonvanishing ordinary eigenfunctional, [Theorem 6.9.10](/entry/eig-f-value-ne-zero/), from an ordinary Eisenstein measure, [Theorem 6.10.5](/entry/chapter-m-holds/). Part 2 computes the edge class of two Siegel units, [Theorem 6.12.7](/entry/pair-edge-dlog/), and cup products of Kummer classes, [Theorem 6.14.3](/entry/cup-kummer-dlog-mod/). It glues an Euler system class, [Theorem 6.18.6](/entry/middle-glue2/), transfers nonvanishing, [Theorem 6.19.2](/entry/nonvanishing-transfer/), proves the Greenberg and Wiles inequality, [Theorem 6.23.2](/entry/gw-ineq/), and kills the Bloch-Kato Selmer group, [Theorem 6.24.4](/entry/es-vanish/).

Chapter 7 proves the results on deformation rings and patching, with the stability and counting results that the modularity argument takes from $K$-theory and locally symmetric spaces. Complexes of split unimodular sequences are exact in a stable range, [Theorem 7.1.4](/entry/split-seq-exact-at-of-two-mul-add-stable-range-le/), which gives homological stability of $\mathrm{GL}_n$, [Theorem 7.2.2](/entry/k-stability-main/). The chapter bounds the rank of odd homology of arithmetic groups, [Theorem 7.5.5](/entry/b-star-odd/), regularizes cochains near the cusp, [Theorem 7.6.4](/entry/g2/), and proves an abstract count for rank three rings with a torus action, [Theorem 7.7.4](/entry/abstract-count/). Sections 8 to 10 prove the first order and obstruction bounds at Taylor-Wiles level, [Theorem 7.8.2](/entry/b1-ar/) and [Theorem 7.9.3](/entry/b2-ar/), and choose auxiliary elements, [Theorem 7.10.2](/entry/choice/). The last sections give a presentation, [Theorem 7.11.3](/entry/pres/), the core patching theorem, [Theorem 7.12.2](/entry/pa-core/), and show that automorphy implies the core properties, [Theorem 7.13.1](/entry/p-from-aut/).

Chapter 8 proves the automorphic part of the window bound. The row gives the window bound, [Theorem 8.1.10](/entry/vwin-of-row/), through Lemma K and four counts, [Theorem 8.16.7](/entry/of-four-alt/). For the first count, two Bloch-Kato Selmer groups vanish, [Theorem 8.2.9](/entry/kato-two-w-of-prints-h-of-r-t-of-quad-bc2/), from the unramified zeta theorem, [Theorem 8.3.7](/entry/zeta-ur/), the Hodge-Tate law of smoothed zeta classes, [Theorem 8.4.2](/entry/k5-top/), and the norm relation at a tame prime, [Theorem 8.6.3](/entry/kato-norm-relation-tame-prime/). The chapter proves automorphic induction from $\mathrm{GL}_2$ to $\mathrm{GL}_4$, [Theorem 8.8.7](/entry/ind-cs/), the symplectic image statement, [Theorem 8.11.6](/entry/main4-alt/), adjoint automorphy, [Theorem 8.13.9](/entry/adjoint-automorphy-w-of-pure-weight-adjoint-lift/), and the modularity of Hida families in rank three, [Theorem 8.19.4](/entry/modularity-n-of-printed-mult-comp/). Then come Lemma K, [Theorem 8.15.3](/entry/lemma-k/), the main count in rank four, [Theorem 8.17.7](/entry/main-big/), and Counts V and I, [Theorem 8.21.6](/entry/count-v-of-printed-bound-k-reduced-unitary-lift/) and [Theorem 8.22.3](/entry/count-i-of-printed-bound-k-kato-automorphy/). The extended hypotheses rest on quadratic base change, [Theorem 8.23.11](/entry/quad-bc2/), descent from imaginary quadratic fields, [Theorem 8.24.7](/entry/desc-s/), and the literature, [Theorem 8.25.5](/entry/published-hypotheses-from-patching-alt/).

The chapters use each other in one direction. Chapters 1, 2 and 3 cite no other chapter of the volume and can be read in any order. Chapter 6 feeds Chapter 5: it gives the edge congruence of Theorem 5.1.6 and a hypothesis of Theorem 5.5.10. Chapters 4 to 7 all feed Chapter 8. Chapter 4 gives the window bound from four counts and the main count in rank four, Chapters 5 and 6 give the zeta theorem and the Hodge-Tate law, and Chapter 7 gives Lemma K and the count in case V. Chapter 8 also assumes the Galois row of Chapter 3, in Theorem 8.1.10. So a reader can take Chapters 1 to 3 first, then Chapter 6, then Chapter 5, then Chapters 4 and 7 in either order, and Chapter 8 last.

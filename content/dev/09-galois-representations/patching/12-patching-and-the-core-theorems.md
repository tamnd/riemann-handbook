## Patching and the core theorems

This section puts the pieces together. The presentation of the previous section gives a dimension statement for each completion of the universal ring at a point. The patching argument of Taylor and Wiles, in the form of Kisin, gives a module over a power series ring whose support has large dimension at each point. Together they show that an ideal $J$ cut out by automorphic forms lies in every prime of the right kind below a chain of points. The core patching theorem also feeds the [vanishing step](/entry/vwin-of-hypotheses-h-e-alt/) of the modularity chapter directly, and the next section applies it to the universal ordinary determinant ring of a CM field. For the background see [Taylor-Wiles-Kisin patching](/learn/deformations-patching/#taylor-wiles-kisin-patching) and [automorphy lifting for unitary groups](/learn/deformations-patching/#automorphy-lifting-for-unitary-groups).

### Stable and non-semisimple primes

Let $(B, D)$ be a pair over $\mathcal{O}$, with $D$ a determinant of dimension $n$ of $\Gamma_0$. Two kinds of primes of $B$ matter.

The determinant is *discretely stable* at a prime $Q$ if there are $g_1, \dots, g_{n^2}$ and $h_1, \dots, h_{n^2}$ in $\Gamma_0$ with

$$\det\bigl(\operatorname{tr} D(g_i h_j)\bigr)_{i,j} \notin Q.$$

Over a field this says that the traces of $D$ span a space of dimension $n^2$, so the reduction of $D$ at $Q$ is the determinant of an absolutely irreducible representation.

Let $I$ be a set of elements of the group algebra $B[\Gamma_0]$. A prime $Q$ is *non-semisimple for $I$* if there are a field $\kappa$ and a ring map $B \to \kappa$ with kernel $Q$ such that the quotient of $\kappa[\Gamma_0]$ by the two-sided ideal generated by the image of $I$ is not a semisimple ring.

### The Galois input at a completion

The three conditions of the previous section have versions over $A$ itself. Let $x$ be a point with ring $A = K[[t]]$ and a basis $\kappa_1, \dots, \kappa_g$ of odd classes, and let $h \colon \Gamma \to \Gamma_{\mathrm{base}}$ be a morphism of global data.

1. $\mathrm{K}_1(D_0)$: any family $z_1, \dots, z_e$ in $\mathrm{Sel}_1$ such that $\sum_i c_i z_i \in \mathrm{Bd}_1$ with $c_i \in A$ forces all $c_i = 0$ has $e \le D_0$.
2. $\mathrm{K}_2$: for any $g + 1$ families $z_{i,1}, \dots, z_{i,g}$ of even Selmer cocycles there is $c \in A^{g+1}$, $c \ne 0$, and $\Psi \in \mathrm{Sel}_2$ whose $\epsilon'_j$ component is $\sum_i c_i z_{i,j}$ for each $j$.
3. $\mathrm{K}_3$: for each odd Selmer cocycle $z$ there are $s$ and $c_j \in A$ with $t^s z - \sum_j c_j \kappa_j$ an odd coboundary.

**Theorem 7.12.1 (Galois input at a completion).** Let $l \ne 2$. Let $x$ be a point of rank $n$ with a basis $\kappa$ of odd classes, $(B, D)$ a pair over $\mathcal{O}$, and $f$ an ordinary point at level $h$. Let $\hat B$ be a local ring that is the completion of $B$ at $\ker f$, and let $D_0 \in \mathbb{N}$. Assume $\mathrm{K}_1(D_0)$, $\mathrm{K}_2$ and $\mathrm{K}_3$. Let $Z$ and $Q$ be primes of $\hat B$ such that $D$ is discretely stable at $Z$, $\dim \hat B / Z \ge 1 + D_0$, and either $D$ is discretely stable at $Q$ or $Q$ is non-semisimple for the kernel of the point. Then $Z \subseteq Q$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Pa.galOut`.

*Proof.* Extend $f$ to $r_F \colon \hat B \to F$ with kernel the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$. The theorem on generalized matrix algebras gives a generalized matrix representation over $\hat B$, with module $N$. A further lemma gives a complete discrete valuation ring $\mathcal{O}'$ mapping onto $F$ through $\hat B$, so we have a context. The conditions $\mathrm{K}_1$ to $\mathrm{K}_3$ over $A$ give the rank bound $D_0$, the lifting condition and the spanning condition over $F$: a family over $F$ with bounded denominators becomes a family over $A$ after multiplying by a power of $t$. The presentation theorem now gives $X_1, \dots, X_{D_0}$, generators $m_1, \dots, m_g$ of $N$ and coefficients $c$.

Suppose first that $a = n$. Then $N = 0$, so the ideal $I_N$ is zero and $\mathfrak{m} \subseteq (X_1, \dots, X_{D_0}) + \mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}'}\hat B + \mathfrak{m}^2$. Since $\hat B$ is complete, the map $\mathcal{O}'[[Y_1, \dots, Y_{D_0}]] \to \hat B$ with $Y_a \mapsto X_a$ is onto. The source is a regular local domain of dimension $1 + D_0$. The preimage of $Z$ is a prime with quotient of dimension at least $1 + D_0$, so it is the zero ideal. Hence the kernel of the map is zero, $\hat B$ is a domain, and $Z = 0 \subseteq Q$.

Suppose now that $a < n$. A lemma on stable primes shows that if $D$ is discretely stable at a prime $P$, then either $a = n$ or $I_N \not\subseteq P$. So $I_N \not\subseteq Z$. For $Q$, either $I_N \not\subseteq Q$ by the same lemma, or $Q$ is non-semisimple and then the annihilator of $N$ lies in $Q$ by item 6 of the generalized matrix representation. A dimension count on the algebra $\hat B \oplus N$, which is a quotient of a power series ring over $\mathcal{O}'$ in $D_0 + g$ variables, with the $g$ relations of the presentation, now gives $Z \subseteq Q$. We give this last step in outline only. $\square$

### Patching

A *Taylor-Wiles group* for a global datum $\Gamma$ consists of an open normal subgroup $H$ on which $\eta$ is trivial and $\varepsilon \equiv 1 \bmod l$, an integer $q$, elements $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_q \in G'$, and for each $N$ a global datum $\Gamma_N$ with a morphism to $\Gamma$ and elements $\phi_{N,j}, \tau_{N,j}$. The $\phi_{N,j}$ approach the $\sigma_j$, the $\tau_{N,j}$ generate the kernel of $\Gamma_N \to \Gamma$ up to the maximal pro-$l$ quotient of $H$, and $\tau_{N,j}$ is conjugated by $\phi_{N,j}$ to a power with exponent $\equiv 1 \bmod l^N$. The group is *pro-$l$* if the relevant subgroups $\mathrm{PL}(H)$ and their pullbacks to the $\Gamma_N$ are trivial.

A *Taylor-Wiles system* with variables $\sigma$ and $D$ weight variables for a pair $(B, D)$ and an ideal $J$ consists of:

1. an $\mathcal{O}$-algebra map $\lambda \colon \Lambda \to B$, where $\Lambda = \mathcal{O}[[\sigma \sqcup \{1, \dots, D\}]]$, sending the weight variables to the twisted values $c_k\psi_{v_k,i_k}(\gamma_k) - 1$ of the ordinary characters;
2. a $B$-module $M$ that is finite and free over $\Lambda$ through $\lambda$, with $\operatorname{ann}_B M \subseteq p$ if and only if $J \subseteq p$, for every prime $p$ of $B$;
3. for each $N$, a complete local ring $R_N$ with a pair over the residual datum of $\Gamma_N$, a module $M_N$ that is free over a quotient of $\Lambda[[\{1, \dots, q\} \times \{1, \dots, n\}]]$ by an augmentation ideal at level $N$, and maps relating $R_N$ and $M_N$ to $B$ and $M$, with the Hecke relations at the Taylor-Wiles places.

This is the data that automorphic forms provide. A *point at level $D_0$* is a point $x$ with a basis of odd classes, eigenvalues $\alpha$ with $\mathrm{Eig}(\alpha)$, the bounds $\mathrm{B}_1(D_0)$ and $\mathrm{B}_2$, and an $\mathcal{O}$-algebra map $f \colon B \to A$ under which $D$ and the ordinary characters go to those of $\rho$. The residual datum is *adapted* if the residual characters $\bar\chi_i$ are trivial on $H \cap \Gamma_0$ and the residual inertia groups are those of the global datum.

For primes $p_0, \dots, p_h$ of $B$ the *chain conclusion* for $J$ says: if $e_0, \dots, e_{h-1}$ are primes, non-semisimple for the kernel of $D$, with $e_i \subseteq p_i \cap p_{i+1}$, and if there is a prime $q_1$ with $D$ discretely stable at $q_1$ and $J \subseteq q_1 \subseteq p_0$, then every prime $q \subseteq p_h$ at which $D$ is discretely stable or non-semisimple contains $J$.

**Theorem 7.12.2 (Core patching theorem).** Let $l \ne 2$. Let $T$ be a pro-$l$ Taylor-Wiles group for $\Gamma$, $(B, D)$ a pair over $\mathcal{O}$ with adapted residual datum, $J$ an ideal of $B$, and a Taylor-Wiles system for $J$ with one variable $\lambda_0$ and $D$ weight variables. Assume that the values of the pair together with $\lambda(\lambda_0)$ topologically generate $B$ over $\mathcal{O}$. Let $x_0, \dots, x_h$ be points at level $D + nq$ such that $\mathcal{O} \to K_i$ is onto and $f_i(\lambda(\lambda_0)) = t$ for each $i$. Then the chain conclusion holds for $J$ and $\ker f_0, \dots, \ker f_h$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Pa.core`.

*Proof.* We give the steps.

1. Since $T$ is pro-$l$, the groups $\Gamma_N$ have a limit $\Phi$: a compact group with a nonprincipal ultrafilter on $\mathbb{N}$ and maps to every $\Gamma_N$, which approximate a map to a global datum. Patching the rings $R_N$ and modules $M_N$ along the ultrafilter gives a ring $R_\infty$ over $S_\infty = \Lambda[[\{1, \dots, q\} \times \{1, \dots, n\}]]$ and a module $M_\infty$ that is finite and free over $S_\infty$.
2. Let $B'$ be the ring obtained from $B$ by adjoining the roots of the characteristic polynomials at the $\sigma_j$. The patched ring maps onto $B'$ after killing the patching variables. Since the eigenvalues of each point split, each $\ker f_i$ lifts to a prime $P_i$ of $B'$, and the chain lifts with it.
3. A general patching lemma, applied to the free module $M_\infty$ and the annihilation relations of the patched modules, gives at each point a completion of the patched ring and an ideal in it, the support of the patched module. Every minimal prime of this ideal has a quotient of dimension at least $1 + D$. At primes that contain the patching variables, the support ideal lies in a prime exactly when the annihilator of $M$ lies in its contraction. A prime above the support ideal at one point, lying below the next point of the chain, moves to a prime above the support ideal at the next point with the same contraction. The dimension of the weight ring at each point, which uses $f_i(\lambda(\lambda_0)) = t$, enters here.
4. The conditions $\mathrm{B}_1(D + nq)$ and $\mathrm{B}_2$ of each point give $\mathrm{K}_1(D + nq)$, $\mathrm{K}_2$ and $\mathrm{K}_3$ for the limit global datum at that point.
5. The Galois input at a completion applies at each point. With the moving property it carries the support ideal from the stable prime $q_1$ below $p_0$, across the non-semisimple primes $e_i$, to $q$ below $p_h$.
6. Discrete stability and non-semisimplicity transfer between $B$ and the patched ring, and $J$ is the support ideal of $M$. So $J \subseteq q$. $\square$

### Removing the extra variable and the pro-$l$ condition

**Theorem 7.12.3 (Core patching theorem after base change).** Let $l \ne 2$. Let $T$ be a pro-$l$ Taylor-Wiles group, $(B, D)$ a pair with adapted residual datum, $J$ an ideal of $B$, and a Taylor-Wiles system for $J$ with no extra variable and $D$ weight variables. Assume that the values of the pair topologically generate $B$ over $\mathcal{O}$. Let $x_0, \dots, x_h$ be points at level $D + nq$ whose residue fields all have the same number of elements. Then the chain conclusion holds for $J$ and $\ker f_0, \dots, \ker f_h$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Pa.coreBC`.

*Proof.* Let $k$ be the common residue field size. There is a complete discrete valuation ring $\mathcal{O}'$, finite and free over $\mathcal{O}$ with residue field of $k$ elements, generated over $\mathcal{O}$ by one element $\alpha$ that is a root of a monic polynomial $P$ over $\mathcal{O}$, such that $P$ splits over every finite field of $k$ elements. Put $B' = (\mathcal{O}' \otimes_{\mathcal{O}} B)[[s]]$. The residual datum, the pair and the Taylor-Wiles system base change to $\mathcal{O}'$, and the system over $B'$ has one extra variable, which maps to $s$. The values of the new pair and $s$ generate $B'$. The chain of primes lifts to $\mathcal{O}' \otimes_{\mathcal{O}} B$. Since $P$ splits over $K_i$ and $K_i$ has $k$ elements, each $f_i$ extends to $f'_i \colon B' \to A_i$ with $\mathcal{O}' \to K_i$ onto and $f'_i(s) = t$. Apply the core patching theorem over $\mathcal{O}'$. Discrete stability and non-semisimplicity transfer between $B$ and $B'$, so the conclusion descends to $B$. $\square$

**Theorem 7.12.4 (Core patching theorem in general).** The same statement holds without the assumption that $T$ is pro-$l$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Pa.raw`.

*Proof.* There is a quotient of the global datum, with a pro-$l$ Taylor-Wiles group, through which $T$ factors. Since the residual datum is adapted, the residual characters are trivial on $H \cap \Gamma_0$, and so on the subgroup $\mathrm{PL}(H)$ that is killed. The residual datum, the pair and the Taylor-Wiles system then descend to the quotient, the descended datum is adapted, and the values of the old pair are among the values of the new one, so generation still holds. Each point descends with the same kernel and residue field, and the non-semisimple primes are the same. Apply the previous theorem to the quotient. $\square$

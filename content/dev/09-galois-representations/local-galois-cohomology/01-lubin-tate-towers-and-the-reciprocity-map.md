## Lubin-Tate towers and the reciprocity map

This section sets up the objects of the next section. A nonarchimedean local field $K$ has a reciprocity map, built from its unramified extensions and the norm, in the style of abstract class field theory. A uniformizer $\pi$ gives a tower of totally ramified abelian extensions, generated by the torsion points of a [[Lubin-Tate formal group laws|77QT4B80V8]] group. The next section computes the reciprocity map on this tower. The key input is a descent lemma, proved at the end of this section: a twisted Frobenius fixes a root of an Eisenstein polynomial for the uniformizer $u\pi$.

Throughout, $K$ is a nonarchimedean local field with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, residue field of $q$ elements and normalized valuation $\operatorname{ord}_K$. Let $\Omega$ be a separable closure of $K$, with the unique extension of the valuation of $K$, and let $G = \operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/K)$ with its Krull topology ([profinite groups](/learn/galois-representations/#profinite-groups)). For an open subgroup $U$ of $G$ we write $\Omega^U$ for its fixed field, and for a field $L$ between $K$ and $\Omega$ we write $G_L$ for the subgroup of $G$ that fixes $L$ pointwise.

### The degree map

**Definition 1.1.1 (degree map).** For $\sigma \in G$ and $n \geq 1$, let $a_n(\sigma) \in \mathbb{Z}/n$ be the class with $\sigma(\xi) = \xi^{q^{a}}$ for each root of unity $\xi$ of order dividing $q^n - 1$, for $a$ in the class $a_n(\sigma)$. These classes are compatible, and the *degree map* is the continuous homomorphism
$$d : G \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}, \qquad d(\sigma) = (a_n(\sigma))_{n \geq 1}.$$

Lean: `GroundTheory.LocalField.unramifiedDegree` (also `GroundTheory.LocalField.unramifiedCoord`).

The roots of unity of order prime to $p$ generate the maximal unramified extension $K^{\mathrm{nr}}$ of $K$ in $\Omega$. So $\sigma$ has degree $1$ exactly when $\sigma$ acts on $K^{\mathrm{nr}}$ as the arithmetic Frobenius, and the kernel of $d$ is the inertia group $G_{K^{\mathrm{nr}}}$ ([decomposition groups, inertia and Frobenius](/learn/galois-representations/#decomposition-groups-inertia-and-frobenius)). For an open subgroup $V$ of $G$, the index of $d(V)$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the residue degree $f_V$ of $\Omega^V$ over $K$.

### The reciprocity map

The multiplicative group $\Omega^\times$ is a discrete $G$-module. For open subgroups $V \leq W$ of $G$ with $V$ normal in $W$, the norm $N_{W/V} : (\Omega^V)^\times \to (\Omega^W)^\times$ is $a \mapsto \prod_{c \in W/V} c(a)$, the field norm of $\Omega^V$ over $\Omega^W$. We take $W = G$, so $\Omega^G = K$.

**Definition 1.1.2 (Frobenius lift; reciprocity map).** Let $U$ be an open normal subgroup of $G$, with fixed field $L_U = \Omega^U$. A *Frobenius lift* is an element $\sigma \in G$ with $d(\sigma) = f$ a positive integer. For a Frobenius lift $\sigma$, let $\Sigma_\sigma$ be the fixed field of the closure of the subgroup generated by $\sigma$ and $U \cap \ker d$. The *reciprocity map* of $L_U/K$ is the homomorphism
$$r_U : G/U \to K^\times / N_{L_U/K}(L_U^\times)$$
that sends the class of a Frobenius lift $\sigma$ to the class of $N_{\Sigma_\sigma/K}(\pi_\Sigma)$, with $\pi_\Sigma$ a prime element of $\Sigma_\sigma$. Each class of $G/U$ contains a Frobenius lift, the value does not depend on the choices, and $r_U$ is a homomorphism.

Lean: `GroundTheory.LocalField.Abstract.ClassFieldData.reciprocity` (also `GroundTheory.LocalField.Abstract.ClassFieldData.FrobeniusLift`, `GroundTheory.LocalField.Abstract.frobeniusClosure`, `GroundTheory.LocalField.localClassFieldTheory`).

This is the reciprocity map of Neukirch's abstract class field theory for the pair $(G, \Omega^\times)$, with the degree map $d$ and the valuation $\operatorname{ord}_K$ on $K^\times$. Its construction needs the class field axiom: for each cyclic extension $F'/F$ inside $\Omega$ of finite degree, the Tate groups satisfy $\#\hat H^0(\operatorname{Gal}(F'/F), F'^\times) = [F' : F]$ and $\#\hat H^{-1}(\operatorname{Gal}(F'/F), F'^\times) = 1$. This holds for local fields.

Lean: `GroundTheory.LocalField.classFieldAxiom_units`.

The only property of $r_U$ that the next section uses is its value on Frobenius lifts of degree $1$.

**Proposition 1.1.3 (value on a Frobenius lift).** Let $U$ be an open normal subgroup of $G$ and $\sigma \in G$ with $d(\sigma) = 1$. Let $\Sigma$ be the fixed field of the closure of $\langle \sigma \rangle (U \cap \ker d)$, and let $y \in \Sigma^\times$ have valuation $1$ in $\Sigma$. Then $r_U(\sigma U)$ is the class of $N_{\Sigma/K}(y)$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.LocalField.Abstract.ClassFieldData.reciprocity_mk_eq_norm`.

*Proof.* The degree of $\sigma$ is $1$, so $\sigma$ is a Frobenius lift, and the value of $r_U$ on its class is the class of the norm of a prime element of $\Sigma$, by the definition. An element of valuation $1$ in $\Sigma$ is a prime element. $\square$

### The Lubin-Tate tower

**Definition 1.1.4 (Lubin-Tate series).** Let $\pi$ be a uniformizer of $K$. A *Lubin-Tate series* for $\pi$ is a power series $f \in \mathcal{O}_K[[X]]$ with constant term $0$, coefficient of $X$ equal to $\pi$, and $f \equiv X^q \pmod{\pi}$ coefficientwise.

Lean: `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.IsLubinTateSeries`.

**Example 1.1.5.** The polynomial $g_\pi(X) = \pi X + X^q$ is a Lubin-Tate series for $\pi$.

By the theorem of Lubin and Tate in [Volume 1](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws), each Lubin-Tate series $f$ gives a formal group law $F_f$ over $\mathcal{O}_K$ with $f \in \operatorname{End}(F_f)$, and endomorphisms $[a]_f$ for $a \in \mathcal{O}_K$ with $[a]_f(X) \equiv aX$ modulo degree $2$ and $[\pi]_f = f$. The elements of $\Omega$ of positive valuation form an $\mathcal{O}_K$-module $F_f(\mathfrak{m}_\Omega)$ under the law $x +_F y = F_f(x, y)$, with $a \cdot x = [a]_f(x)$. Each $\tau \in G$ acts on it by $x \mapsto \tau(x)$, and this action is $\mathcal{O}_K$-linear, since the series have coefficients in $\mathcal{O}_K$.

**Definition 1.1.6 (Lubin-Tate torsion and fields).** For $n \geq 0$, the module of $\pi^n$-torsion points is
$$F_f[\pi^n] = \{x \in F_f(\mathfrak{m}_\Omega) : [\pi^n]_f(x) = 0\},$$
and the *Lubin-Tate field* $L_n$ is $K(F_f[\pi^n])$, the field generated over $K$ by these points.

Lean: `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.lubinTateTorsion`, `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.lubinTateField` (also `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.lubinTateGalPointMap`).

The field $L_n$ depends only on $\pi$ and $n$, not on $f$, because two Lubin-Tate series for $\pi$ give isomorphic formal groups over $\mathcal{O}_K$. For $n \geq 1$ the extension $L_n/K$ is totally ramified of degree $(q - 1) q^{n-1}$, and $\operatorname{Gal}(L_n/K) \cong (\mathcal{O}_K/\pi^n)^\times$, where $a$ acts on $F_f[\pi^n]$ by $[a]_f$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.lubinTateField_eq`, `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.finrank_lubinTateField`, `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.lubinTateGalEquiv`.

For $f = g_\pi$ the torsion points are roots of polynomials. Let $g_\pi^{(n)}$ be the $n$-fold composite of $g_\pi$, with $g_\pi^{(0)} = X$, and put
$$P_{\pi, m}(X) = \pi + \bigl(g_\pi^{(m)}(X)\bigr)^{q - 1}.$$
Then $g_\pi^{(m+1)} = g_\pi^{(m)} \cdot P_{\pi, m}$, so the roots of $P_{\pi, m}$ are the points of $F_{g_\pi}[\pi^{m+1}]$ not killed by $\pi^m$.

**Lemma 1.1.7 (Eisenstein generators).** The polynomial $P_{\pi, m}$ is monic of degree $(q - 1) q^m$ and Eisenstein at the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_K$. For each Lubin-Tate series $f$ for $\pi$ there is a root $l \in \Omega$ of $P_{\pi, m}$ with $L_{m+1} = K(l)$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.ltEisenstein_monic`, `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.isEisensteinAt_ltEisenstein`, `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.exists_lubinTateField_eq_adjoin_eisenstein`.

*Proof.* Modulo $\pi$, $g_\pi^{(m)}$ is $X^{q^m}$, so $P_{\pi, m}$ is $X^{(q-1)q^m}$ modulo $\pi$, and its constant term is $\pi$. So it is monic and Eisenstein. The field $L_{m+1}$ does not depend on $f$, so take $f = g_\pi$. A generator of the cyclic $\mathcal{O}_K/\pi^{m+1}$-module $F_{g_\pi}[\pi^{m+1}]$ generates $L_{m+1}$, since the other points are $[a](l)$ with $a \in \mathcal{O}_K$, and these are power series in $l$ with coefficients in $\mathcal{O}_K$. A generator is not killed by $\pi^m$, so it is a root of $P_{\pi, m}$. $\square$

### A fixed root

The next lemma is the heart of the computation. Let $u \in \mathcal{O}_K^\times$, so $u\pi$ is a second uniformizer. Write $[a]$ for the endomorphisms of $F_{g_\pi}$.

**Lemma 1.1.8 (fixed root of the twisted Eisenstein polynomial).** Let $\sigma \in G$ with $d(\sigma) = 1$, let $m \geq 0$, and let $l \in \Omega$ be a root of $P_{\pi, m}$ with $\sigma(l) = [u^{-1}](l)$. Then there is $x \in \Omega$ such that

1. $x$ is a root of $P_{u\pi, m}$;
2. $\sigma(x) = x$;
3. each $\tau \in G$ that fixes $K^{\mathrm{nr}}$ pointwise and fixes $l$ also fixes $x$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.LocalField.exists_fixed_root_ltEisenstein`.

*Proof.* Let $B$ be the completion of the ring of integers of $K^{\mathrm{nr}}$. It is a complete discrete valuation ring with algebraically closed residue field, $\pi$ is a prime element of $B$, and the Frobenius of $K^{\mathrm{nr}}$ extends to an automorphism $\varphi$ of $B$ over $\mathcal{O}_K$ with $\varphi(b) \equiv b^q$ modulo the maximal ideal.

Step 1 (a fixed root over $B$). Let $A = B[X]/(P_{\pi, m})$ and let $\bar X$ be the class of $X$. Let $T$ be the ring endomorphism of $A$ that acts by $\varphi$ on $B$ and sends $\bar X$ to $[u^{-1}](\bar X)$. This is well defined, because $[u^{-1}]$ maps the roots of $P_{\pi, m}$ to roots. The key lemma of Lubin-Tate theory over $B$ gives a power series $\theta \in B[[X]]$ that is an isomorphism of formal groups from $F_{g_\pi}$ to $F_{g_{u\pi}}$, with $\theta^\varphi = \theta \circ [u]$. This is the lemma that Neukirch uses to show that the Lubin-Tate fields of two uniformizers have the same compositum with $K^{\mathrm{nr}}$ (see the Notes and references). Put $z = \theta(\bar X) \in A$. Then $z$ is a root of $P_{u\pi, m}$, since $\theta$ maps primitive $\pi^{m+1}$-torsion to primitive $(u\pi)^{m+1}$-torsion, and
$$T(z) = \theta^\varphi\bigl([u^{-1}](\bar X)\bigr) = \theta\bigl([u][u^{-1}](\bar X)\bigr) = z.$$

Step 2 (finite order). The automorphism $\sigma$ permutes the finitely many roots of $P_{\pi, m}$ in $\Omega$, so $\sigma^N(l) = l$ for some $N \geq 1$. Then $[u^{-N}](l) = \sigma^N(l) = l$. Since $P_{\pi, m}$ is the minimal polynomial of $l$, the series $[u^{-N}](X) - X$ lies in the ideal of $B[[X]]$ generated by $P_{\pi, m}$, and so $T^N(\bar X) = \bar X$.

Step 3 (descent to a finite unramified extension). The classes $1, \bar X, \dots, \bar X^{e-1}$, with $e = (q-1)q^m$, form a basis of $A$ over $B$. Since $T^N$ fixes $\bar X$, it acts on coordinates in this basis by $\varphi^N$. From $T^N(z) = z$, the coordinates of $z$ are fixed by $\varphi^N$, so they lie in the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_E$ of the unramified extension $E$ of degree $N$ of $K$ in $\Omega$. So $z$ comes from an element of $\mathcal{O}_E[X]/(P_{\pi, m})$, and this ring maps to $\Omega$ by $X \mapsto l$. Let $x$ be the image of $z$.

Step 4 (the three properties). The map $\mathcal{O}_E[X]/(P_{\pi, m}) \to B[X]/(P_{\pi, m})$ is injective, so the relation $P_{u\pi, m}(z) = 0$ gives (1). The automorphism $\sigma$ acts on $\mathcal{O}_E$ as $\varphi$, because $d(\sigma) = 1$, and sends $l$ to $[u^{-1}](l)$. So $\sigma$ acts on the image of $\mathcal{O}_E[X]/(P_{\pi, m})$ as $T$ acts on $A$, and $T(z) = z$ gives (2). An element $\tau$ that fixes $K^{\mathrm{nr}}$ and $l$ fixes the coordinates of $z$ in $E \subseteq K^{\mathrm{nr}}$ and fixes $l$, so it fixes $x$. This is (3). $\square$

**Remark 1.1.9 (Formalization).** The degree map is defined by a choice: if no class $a$ with the stated property exists, the coordinate is set to $0$ ([MaximalUnramified.lean](lean:GroundTheory/LocalFields/MaximalUnramified.lean#L131)). The class always exists, so this default is never used. The fixed root lemma is stated for a field $\Omega$ that is separably closed, algebraic and Galois over $K$, with a valuation that extends that of $K$ ([Descent.lean](lean:GroundTheory/LocalFields/LubinTateReciprocity/Descent.lean#L325)); its proof needs a raised heartbeat limit. The isomorphism $\theta$ of Step 1 is `GroundTheory.LocalField.FormalGroup.exists_iso_lubinTateFormalGroup` ([OverUnramified.lean](lean:GroundTheory/LocalFields/FormalGroups/OverUnramified.lean#L296)), stated for any complete discrete valuation ring $B$ over $\mathcal{O}_K$ with algebraically closed residue field and a Frobenius lift $\varphi$. The isomorphism $\operatorname{Gal}(L_n/K) \cong (\mathcal{O}_K/\pi^n)^\times$ is chosen by `Classical.choose` from an existence statement.

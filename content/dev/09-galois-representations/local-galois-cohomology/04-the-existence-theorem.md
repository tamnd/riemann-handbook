## The existence theorem

We keep the setting of the previous section: $K$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ inside $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, and $N_L = N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. This section proves the existence theorem of local class field theory: each subgroup of finite index of $K^\times$ is the norm group of a finite abelian extension. The proof follows the classical route through Kummer theory and the norm limitation theorem. Its corollary, that an element of $K^\times$ which is a norm from every finite abelian extension is $1$, gives the injectivity of the Artin map in the next section.

### Kummer extensions

**Lemma 1.4.1 (Kummer count).** Let $n \geq 1$, and assume that $K$ contains all $n$-th roots of unity in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$. Then the number of $\chi \in X(G_K)$ with $n\chi = 0$ is $[K^\times : (K^\times)^n]$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.card_char_torsion`.

*Proof.* A character with $n\chi = 0$ takes values in $\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z} \cong \mu_n$, so these characters form $\operatorname{Hom}(G_K, \mu_n) = H^1(G_K, \mu_n)$. By Kummer theory this group is isomorphic to $K^\times/(K^\times)^n$, which is finite. $\square$

**Lemma 1.4.2 (the maximal abelian extension of exponent $n$).** Let $n \geq 1$, and assume that only finitely many $\chi \in X(G_K)$ satisfy $n\chi = 0$. Then there is a finite abelian extension $L$ of $K$ such that $\chi$ is trivial on $L$ if and only if $n\chi = 0$. If $K_0 \subseteq K$ and $\sigma(K) \subseteq K$ for all $\sigma \in G_{K_0}$, then $\sigma(L) \subseteq L$ for all $\sigma \in G_{K_0}$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.exists_maxExp` (also `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.maxExp_stable`).

*Proof.* Let $G_L$ be the intersection of the kernels of the finitely many $\chi$ with $n\chi = 0$. It is open and contains the commutators. A character trivial on $L$ factors through $G_K/G_L$, which embeds in a product of copies of $\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}$, so $n\chi = 0$. For the last claim, $\sigma \in G_{K_0}$ permutes the characters of $G_K$ of order dividing $n$ by conjugation, so it maps $G_L$ to itself. $\square$

**Lemma 1.4.3 (the norm group of a Kummer extension).** Under the hypothesis of the Kummer count, let $L$ be the extension of the previous lemma. Then $N_L = (K^\times)^n$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.normGrp_maxExp` (also `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.card_trivOn`).

*Proof.* Let $a \in K^\times$. For $\chi$ trivial on $L$ we have $n\chi = 0$, so $(a^n, \chi) = n(a, \chi) = (a, n\chi) = 0 = \chi(1)$. So $1$ is a reciprocity element for $a^n$, and $a^n \in N_L$ by the reciprocity isomorphism of the previous section. On the other side, $[K^\times : N_L] = [L : K]$ is the number of characters trivial on $L$. These are the $\chi$ with $n\chi = 0$, and by the Kummer count there are $[K^\times : (K^\times)^n]$ of them. A subgroup of $N_L$ of the same finite index is equal to $N_L$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.4.4 (norm limitation).** Let $L \supseteq K$ be finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with $G_L$ normal in $G_K$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ solvable. Let $E \subseteq L$ be the maximal abelian subextension, that is, $G_E = G_L [G_K, G_K]$. Then $N_{L/K}(L^\times) = N_{E/K}(E^\times)$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.normGrp_eq_of_soluble` (also `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.exists_maxAb`).

*Proof.* Induction on $[L : K]$. If $L = K$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ has a normal subgroup with a nontrivial cyclic quotient, since it is solvable. Its fixed field $M$ is a cyclic extension of $K$ with $M \neq K$, and $\operatorname{Gal}(L/M)$ is solvable. The induction hypothesis for $L/M$ reduces the claim to the case where $L/M$ is abelian and $M/K$ is cyclic. The inclusion $N_{L/K}(L^\times) \subseteq N_{E/K}(E^\times)$ holds since $E \subseteq L$. For the other one, let $\sigma \in G_K$ restrict to a generator of $\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)$, and let $a = N_{E/K}(y)$ with $y \in E^\times$. Then $M \subseteq E$, and $b = N_{E/M}(y)$ satisfies $a = N_{M/K}(b)$. Let $g \in G_M$ be a reciprocity element for $b$ on $L/M$. Since $b$ is a norm from $E$, $g$ fixes $E$, so $g \in G_L [G_K, G_K]$. As $G_K/G_M$ is cyclic and generated by $\sigma$, this gives $g \equiv \sigma h \sigma^{-1} h^{-1} \bmod G_L$ for some $h \in G_M$. Let $h$ be a reciprocity element for $c \in M^\times$. Then $\sigma h \sigma^{-1}$ is a reciprocity element for $\sigma(c)$, so $b^{-1}\sigma(c)c^{-1}$ has the reciprocity element $g^{-1}\sigma h\sigma^{-1}h^{-1} \in G_L$ and lies in $N_{L/M}(L^\times)$. Since $N_{M/K}(\sigma(c)c^{-1}) = 1$, we get $a = N_{M/K}(b) \in N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. $\square$

### The existence theorem

**Theorem 1.4.5 (norm groups inside $n$-th powers).** Let $n \geq 1$. There is a finite abelian extension $E$ of $K$ with $N_E \subseteq (K^\times)^n$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.exists_normGrp_le_pow`.

*Proof.* Let $M = K(\mu_n)$ be the field generated over $K$ by the $n$-th roots of unity. It is a finite abelian extension of $K$. By the Kummer count over $M$, only finitely many characters of $G_M$ are killed by $n$. Let $L$ be the maximal abelian extension of $M$ of exponent $n$, from the lemma. Since $M/K$ is Galois, $L$ is stable under $G_K$, so $G_L$ is normal in $G_K$. The group $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ is an extension of the abelian group $\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)$ by the abelian group $\operatorname{Gal}(L/M)$, so it is solvable. Let $E \subseteq L$ be the maximal abelian subextension of $L/K$. By norm limitation, $N_E = N_{L/K}(L^\times)$.

Let $x = N_{L/K}(y)$ with $y \in L^\times$. By the transitivity of the norm, $x = N_{M/K}(N_{L/M}(y))$. By the lemma on the norm group of a Kummer extension over $M$, $N_{L/M}(y) = w^n$ for some $w \in M^\times$. So $x = N_{M/K}(w)^n \in (K^\times)^n$. $\square$

**Theorem 1.4.6 (existence theorem).** Let $H \leq K^\times$ be a subgroup of finite index. Then there is a finite abelian extension $L$ of $K$ with $N_{L/K}(L^\times) = H$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.existence`.

*Proof.* Let $n = [K^\times : H]$. Then $a^n \in H$ for each $a \in K^\times$, so $(K^\times)^n \subseteq H$. The [Theorem 1.4.5](/entry/exists-norm-grp-le-pow/) on norm groups inside $n$-th powers gives $E$ with $N_E \subseteq (K^\times)^n \subseteq H$. The lemma on subgroups above a norm group, in the previous section, gives $L \subseteq E$ with $N_L = H$. $\square$

**Corollary 1.4.7 (universal norms are trivial).** Let $a \in K^\times$ with $a \in N_L$ for every finite abelian extension $L$ of $K$. Then $a = 1$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.DiscreteGroups.LocalClassField.eq_one_of_forall_mem_normGrp`.

*Proof.* For $i \geq 0$ let $U_K^{(i)} = \{x \in U_K : |x - 1| \leq p^{-i/e_K}\}$. If $\pi$ is a uniformizer, then $|\pi| = p^{-1/e_K}$, so $U_K^{(i)} = 1 + \pi^i \mathcal{O}_K$. These subgroups are open, and their intersection is $\{1\}$.

Assume $a \neq 1$. Choose $i$ with $a \notin U_K^{(i)}$ and a uniformizer $\pi$. Put $m = |v_K(a)| + 1$ and
$$H = U_K^{(i)} \cdot \langle \pi^m \rangle.$$
The group $H$ is open, since it contains $U_K^{(i)}$. It has finite index, since an open subgroup of $K^\times$ that contains an element of positive valuation has finite index. By the [existence theorem](/entry/existence/), $H = N_L$ for a finite abelian $L$, so $a \in H$ by assumption. Write $a = u\pi^{mk}$ with $u \in U_K^{(i)}$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then $v_K(a) = mk$, and $|v_K(a)| < m$ forces $k = 0$. So $a = u \in U_K^{(i)}$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Remark 1.4.8 (Formalization).** The existence theorem asks only that $H$ has finite index, while the classical statement asks that $H$ is open and of finite index. Both agree here: a subgroup of finite index $n$ contains $(K^\times)^n$, which is open since $K$ has characteristic $0$. The group $U_K^{(i)}$ is defined with the absolute value of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ ([ValUnitsF.lean](lean:ReciprocityTheory/ClassFieldTheory/LocalClassField/ValUnitsF.lean#L19)).

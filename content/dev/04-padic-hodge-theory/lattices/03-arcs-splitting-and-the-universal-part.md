## Arcs, splitting, and the universal part

The lifting along arcs cuts the letters $\mathbb{Z}/n$ into arcs that end at given cuts, and treats one arc at a time. Two statements carry it in case A. The *splitting* writes a finite class of $T_2$, after a power of $p$, as a sum of classes attached to Wach vectors, one for each cut. The *universal part* gives, at each cut, a basis in which every such Wach vector has coordinates with controlled denominators. This section defines arc data and proves both statements from admitted inputs on Wach modules. They are used in [the class split along arcs](/entry/class-split-along-arcs/) and in [the universal part in case A](/entry/universal-part-case-a/).

We use the [Wach datum](/entry/theorem-of-local-lemmas/) of the previous section, with $\Lambda$, $\varphi$, $\pi_0$, $q = \pi_0 + p$, $\Delta$, and the Wach modules $N(T) \subseteq D(T)$. Recall that $x \in D(T)$ is a *Wach vector at $c$* if $e_c x = x$, $x$ is fixed by $\Delta$, $x$ has finite poles, and $\gamma x - x \in \pi_0 N(T)$.

**Definition 8.3.1 (Kinds of letters).** A *kind* is one of: *thin*, *up* of size $b$, or *down* of size $b$, with $b \in \mathbb{N}$. Up and down kinds are *thick*. For $G_0, S, \eta \in \mathbb{N}$ the *window* of a kind is
$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{win}(\text{thin}) &= [-G_0, G_0], \\ \mathrm{win}(\text{up } b) &= [b, b + S] \cup [-\eta, \eta], \\ \mathrm{win}(\text{down } b) &= [-b - S, -b] \cup [-\eta, \eta]. \end{aligned}$$
A thick kind of size $b$ is *admissible* for $\eta$ if $\eta < b$; the thin kind is always admissible.

**Definition 8.3.2 (Arc datum).** An *arc datum* $A$ on $\mathbb{Z}/n$ consists of numbers $G_0, S, \eta, h, j \in \mathbb{N}$, a kind $\mathrm{ty}(i)$ for each letter $i$, a finite set of *cuts* $C \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/n$, and a length $\ell_c \in \mathbb{N}$ for each letter $c$. The arc of a cut $c$ is the set of letters $c, c-1, \dots, c - \ell_c + 1$. The arc datum is *good* if:

1. $C$ is not empty and $j \ge 1$;
2. for each $c \in C$: $1 \le \ell_c \le n$, $c - \ell_c \in C$, no $c - d$ with $1 \le d < \ell_c$ is a cut, and $j \le \ell_c$;
3. every kind is admissible for $\eta$, $G_0 \le h$, $1 \le h$, and $\mathrm{size}(\mathrm{ty}(i)) + S \le h$ for every thick letter $i$;
4. either the $j$ letters $c + 1, \dots, c + j$ after each cut are thin, or $j = 1$, all letters are thick, and $\mathrm{size}(\mathrm{ty}(c+1)) + S \le G_0$ for each cut $c$.

So the arcs of the cuts are disjoint and cover $\mathbb{Z}/n$. A lattice $T$ is *in the window* of $A$ if $T$ is crystalline and the jumps of $T$ at each letter $i$ lie in $\mathrm{win}(\mathrm{ty}(i))$.

Attach to $A$ and the prime $p$ the numbers
$$M = \Bigl\lfloor \frac{2h}{p - 1} \Bigr\rfloor + 1, \qquad \sigma = \Bigl\lceil \Bigl( 2 G_0 + \frac{M}{p^{j-1}} \Bigr) \frac{p}{(p-1)^2} + 1 \Bigr\rceil,$$
and to each cut $c$ the element
$$\Lambda_c = \prod_{s = 0}^{\ell_c - 1} \varphi^s(q)^{H_s} \in \Lambda,$$
where $H_s = G_0$ if the letter $c - s$ is thin or $\ell_c - s \le j$, and $H_s = h$ otherwise. For a letter $a$ and $e \in \mathbb{N}$ let $P_{a,e}(T)$ be the additive group generated by the elements $u \varphi^e(x)$ with $u \in \Lambda^\Delta$ and $x \in N(T)$ in the part of letter $a$ and fixed by $\Delta$. The arc condition at $c$ on a Wach vector $w$ is $\Lambda_c w \in P_{c - \ell_c, \ell_c}(T)$: the vector $w$, multiplied by $\Lambda_c$, comes from the start of the arc by $\ell_c$ steps of Frobenius.

**Theorem 8.3.3 (Splitting along arcs in case A).** Assume the admitted inputs for the splitting and for the arc coordinates, the freeness of Wach lattices, the exactness of $D_{\mathrm{cris}}$ on strict sequences, the property of Wach modules that kills quotients, the Fontaine core statement in case A, and the bound on core jumps. Let $\ell \ge 5$ be a prime, $(K, L)$ good at level $1$ in case A, and take the Wach datum at $(\ell, K, L)$. Let $A$ be a good arc datum and let $T_2$ be in the window of $A$. Let $Y$ be a crystalline lattice, let $c \in Z^1(T_2)$, and let $T_2 \xrightarrow{\iota} Y \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathbf{1}$ be the extension defined by $c$: the sequence is exact, and there is $y \in Y$ with $\pi(y)$ generating $\mathbf{1}$ and $\iota(c(g)) = g y - y$ for all $g$. Let $r \in \mathbb{N}$ be such that, at each cut $c'$, $p^r$ moves every $\Delta$-invariant element of $N(\mathbf{1})$ at the letter $c'$ into the image of $N(Y)$ under $\pi$. Then there are, for each cut $c'$, a Wach vector $w_{c'}$ of $T_2$ at $c'$ and a class $x_{c'} \in H^1(T_2)$ such that:

1. $w_{c'}$ satisfies the arc condition at $c'$, and the pole condition at $c'$ for every horizontal basis at the start of the arc;
2. $x_{c'}$ is the class of $p^\sigma \varphi^j(w_{c'})$ minus a truncation term;
3. $p^{r + 2\sigma} [c] = \pm \sum_{c' \in C} x_{c'}$ in $H^1(T_2)$, with one sign for the whole sum.

*Proof.* First we get the refined level zero condition modulo $p$. It follows from the admitted arc inputs together with a membership criterion, which comes from the freeness of Wach lattices, the properties P3, P4, P5 and P9 of Wach lattices, and the exactness of $D_{\mathrm{cris}}$. With this condition, the splitting is the splitting theorem from exactness. That theorem uses the admitted inputs for the splitting, property P6, the conclusion on $H$-classes, the unit classes, the property that kills quotients, the Fontaine core statement, and the shape of the split at each cut. The shape comes from the arc inputs, the level zero condition modulo $p$ (from the arc inputs, P4 and P9), its refined form, the bound on core jumps and the exactness of $D_{\mathrm{cris}}$ on strict sequences. $\square$

The universal part is a statement about a single cut. We give its content; the full statement is [the universal part in case A](/entry/universal-part-case-a/). Fix a good arc datum $A$, a number $m$, a lattice $T_2$ in the window of $A$ such that at every thick letter $T_2$ has at most $m$ jumps in $[-\eta, \eta]$, and a cut $c$ with $\ell = \ell_c$ and $c' = c - \ell$. Let $\rho_c$ be the number of thick letters on the arc of $c$. Then the universal part gives a horizontal basis $b_1, \dots, b_d$ at $c'$ and a set $R$ of at most $m \rho_c$ indices such that:

1. outside $R$ the coordinates of the basis lie in the up steps of the filtration at the up letters of the arc;
2. at each down letter the filtration step $-\eta$ is spanned by the coordinates in $R$;
3. every Wach vector $w$ at $c$ that satisfies the arc condition and the pole condition has coordinates $f_k$ in the basis, after multiplication by $p^\sigma$, with denominators that divide products of $\varphi^s(q)$ fixed by the kinds of the letters on the arc;
4. the families of coordinates supported on $R$ are generated, modulo $\pi_0$, by at most $m \rho_c (\ell + 1)$ families;
5. every family of coordinates that satisfies the invariance condition comes from a Wach vector at $c$ with the arc condition.

A second part says that coordinates with the numerators and denominators fixed by the kinds give elements of the Wach module along the arc.

**Proposition 8.3.4 (Universal part from the window condition).** Assume the saturation property of Wach modules for arc lifting, the killing at levels, the condition that $\gamma$ keeps the uniformizer, the arc coordinates theorem, property P9 of Wach lattices, the conclusion on $H$-classes, and the equality $D_{\mathrm{dR}} = D_{\mathrm{cris}}$ for all local data. Assume also the window killing condition and the generator condition at every $(\ell, K, L)$ in case A with $\ell \ge 5$. Then the universal part in case A holds.

*Proof.* At each $(\ell, K, L)$ in case A with $\ell \ge 5$, the ring of $L$ is a discrete valuation ring, and property P9 gives the ring condition: the Wach module is described by the uniformizer polynomial. The ring condition, P9, the conclusion on $H$-classes, the first part of the arc coordinates theorem and the window killing condition give the restriction of sections. The same inputs, with the restriction of sections in place of the window killing condition, the condition on $\gamma$ and the stability of $\varphi^s(q)$ under the ring condition, give the sections statement. The universal part follows from the sections statement, the generator condition, $D_{\mathrm{dR}} = D_{\mathrm{cris}}$ and the inputs above, by the theorem on sections. $\square$

For a letter $a$, $u, w \in \Lambda$ and $f \in E$, say that $f$ *has fraction $u / w$ at $a$* if $w f = e_a u y$ for some $y \in \Lambda^\Delta$.

**Definition 8.3.5 (Inverse level condition).** The Wach datum satisfies the *inverse level condition* if, in case A with $p \ge 5$, the following holds for every letter $a$, every $s \in \mathbb{N}$, every finite $T \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ with $s \notin T$, and all exponents $e_t$. Put $w = \prod_{t \in T} \varphi^t(q)^{e_t}$. If $f \in E$ has fraction $1/w$ at $a$, and $p^\kappa(\gamma f - f)$ has fraction $\varphi^s(q)/w$ at $a$ for some $\kappa$, then there are $\kappa' \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x \in \mathcal{O}$ such that $p^{\kappa'} f - e_a x$ has fraction $\varphi^s(q)/w$ at $a$.

In words: if $\gamma$ fixes $f$ modulo $\varphi^s(q)$, then $f$ is a constant modulo $\varphi^s(q)$, up to a power of $p$.

**Proposition 8.3.6 (Universal part from inverse levels).** Assume the inputs of the last proposition except the window killing condition and the generator condition, and assume the inverse level condition at every $(\ell, K, L)$ in case A with $\ell \ge 5$. Then the universal part in case A holds.

*Proof.* At each $(\ell, K, L)$ in case A with $\ell \ge 5$, P9 gives the ring condition and the tool algebra, and with them the stability of $\varphi^s(q)$ and a rational core statement. These, the killing at levels, $v(p) = 1$ and the inverse level condition give the window killing condition. The same inputs give the generator condition, by the theorem on universal generators of units. Then apply the last proposition. $\square$

**Theorem 8.3.7 (Universal part in case A).** Assume the saturation property of Wach modules for arc lifting, the killing at levels, the condition that $\gamma$ keeps the uniformizer, the arc coordinates theorem, property P9 of Wach lattices, the conclusion on $H$-classes, and $D_{\mathrm{dR}} = D_{\mathrm{cris}}$ for all local data. Then the universal part in case A holds.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.UnusedSlot80.universal_general_statement_holds`.

*Proof.* By the last proposition it suffices to prove the inverse level condition at each $(\ell, K, L)$ in case A with $\ell \ge 5$. The ring of $L$ is a complete discrete valuation ring in which $p$ is not a unit. Property P9 gives the ring condition, and with it the tool algebra. The rational level statement for the ring condition then gives the inverse level condition. $\square$

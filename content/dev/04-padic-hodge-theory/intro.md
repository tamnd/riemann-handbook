This volume proves the results of $\ell$-adic Hodge theory that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. They are about representations of the Galois group $G_K$ of a finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, and about the lattices in them. The volume builds the [period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings), compares the étale cohomology of smooth proper models with their Hodge, de Rham and crystalline cohomology, and attaches Breuil-Kisin, Wach and prismatic Dieudonné modules to lattices. It then proves the $p$-adic monodromy theorem, the integrality theorem of Bhatt and Scholze, and a local theory of Bloch-Kato type with explicit constants. Volume 1 gives the definitions of [crystalline, de Rham and potentially semistable representations](/learn/galois-representations/#crystalline-de-rham-and-potentially-semistable-representations) and states many of these results. This volume proves them.

The proof uses the volume in three places. The first is [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/). Its steps [GeoOne holds](/step/geo-one-holds/) and [the Galois row holds](/step/galois-row-holds/) need the period rings, the comparison theorems for models, and the control of Hodge-Tate jumps in limits of lattices. Chapters 1 to 3 supply these inputs. The second place is the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/) with its branch [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/). It needs Hodge-Tate periods of abelian schemes, the de Rham property of Hecke characters, prismatic Dieudonné modules, the comparison of crystalline and de Rham cohomology, and the monodromy theorem. Chapters 4 to 6 supply these. The third place is the local part of the theta lifts: the step [EveryCharacter from theta integrals and local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/) and the step [the hypotheses at the third place of the unitary group](/step/unitary-group-third-place-hypotheses-def/). They need bounds on Selmer groups and on the lifting of cohomology classes along maps of lattices. Chapters 6 to 8 supply these bounds.

Chapter 1 builds $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$, $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ twice, from two definitions of the ring of integers of $\mathbb{C}_\ell$, and proves that the two models are isomorphic with all their structure, [Theorem 1.1.10](/entry/compat-f/). Period data tied to the first model are then tied to the second, [Theorem 1.2.4](/entry/loc-tied6/). The chapter ends with forty basic properties of $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$, [Theorem 1.3.4](/entry/period-ring-base-base/).

Chapter 2 proves the comparison theorems for a smooth projective model over the ring of integers of $K$. It computes Hodge cohomology by Čech complexes on the site of thickenings, [Theorem 2.1.9](/entry/hodge-iso-natural-cech/), and builds the Hodge-Tate map, [Theorem 2.3.6](/entry/hodge-tate-of-etale-side/). The map is bijective, which gives the Hodge-Tate decomposition and the dimension formula $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} V_n = \sum_a \dim_K \mathrm{Hdg}(a, n)$, [Theorem 2.4.6](/entry/ht-bij-of/). For the crystalline side, the chapter builds a period map with values in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, [Theorem 2.5.6](/entry/pmap/), and proves a local comparison on a small chart, [Theorem 2.6.9](/entry/local-of/). The later sections prove descent for tensor functors, [Theorem 2.8.8](/entry/des-tens/), the existence of level systems, [Theorem 2.9.7](/entry/lev-sys-ex/), and the finite length of Witt vector cohomology, [Theorem 2.10.3](/entry/witt-cohomology-finite-length/).

Chapter 3 writes Breuil-Kisin modules of lattices in semistable representations as frames: matrices over $W(C^\flat)$ with a Frobenius of bounded poles. Its main result gives a frame whose height is bounded by the Hodge-Tate jumps alone, [Theorem 3.4.6](/entry/exists-frame-sst/). Since the bound does not depend on the lattice, the frames of a tower of lattices have a limit, [Theorem 3.5.9](/entry/limit-frame-exists/). So a representation covered by semistable representations with fixed jumps is de Rham with the same jumps, [Theorem 3.7.6](/entry/closed-of-cover/).

Chapter 4 has two parts. The first builds period data for abelian schemes over $A_{\mathrm{inf}}/(\xi^2)$, [Theorem 4.2.1](/entry/abelian-period-datum-main/), and bounds the colength of their Hodge-Tate periods at split places, [Theorem 4.5.6](/entry/colength-bound-split-place-chart/), and at unramified places, [Theorem 4.6.2](/entry/unramified-companion-chart-characters/). The second proves that the $\ell$-adic character of an algebraic Hecke character is de Rham at the places above $\ell$, with the expected Hodge-Tate jumps, [Theorem 4.8.6](/entry/field-of-leaves/). The proof uses the periods of Lubin-Tate characters, [Theorem 4.7.7](/entry/has-periods-main/).

Chapter 5 also has two parts. The first works with a $p$-divisible group over the ring of integers of an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. It builds an adapted basis of quasi-logarithms, [Theorem 5.1.6](/entry/adapted-basis/), lifts the period matrices to $A_{\inf}$, [Theorem 5.5.1](/entry/window-lift/), and constructs the prismatic Dieudonné module with its Hodge filtration, [Theorem 5.6.6](/entry/prismatic-dieudonne-hodge-at-of-has-datum/). The second compares crystalline and de Rham cohomology in degrees one and two for a smooth scheme over a ring with a nilpotent divided power ideal, [Theorem 5.10.8](/entry/crystalline-smooth-lift-of-relations/).

Chapter 6 is the longest. Its first part bounds the congruence length of a point of a Hecke algebra by a Bloch-Kato Selmer group and local terms, [Theorem 6.2.2](/entry/selmer-bound-at-point-main/). The local terms come from Wach lattices, through the twisted torsion class theorem, [Theorem 6.7.5](/entry/aux-of-bloch-kato-tate-duality-euler-char/). The second part proves the integrality theorem of Bhatt and Scholze: crystalline lattices come from prismatic F-crystals, [Theorem 6.13.4](/entry/bhatt-scholze-integral/). The third part proves the $p$-adic monodromy theorem, that de Rham representations are potentially semistable, [Theorem 6.15.4](/entry/de-rham-potentially-semistable/). The proof reduces to a statement on matrices, constructs Frobenius eigenvectors in $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^+$ with prescribed residues, and uses Hyodo's theorem, [Theorem 6.24.2](/entry/hyodo-statement/), and Sen's theorem on finite inertia in weight zero, [Theorem 6.27.5](/entry/sen-admissible-representation-finite-inertia/). The last section computes the Harish-Chandra restriction of a product of cuspidal characters of finite general linear groups, [Theorem 6.28.3](/entry/res-cusp-prod-main/).

Chapter 7 is about Galois cohomology at one prime with coefficients in crystalline lattices. Its first part proves the exact local conditions: the admissible classes of a twisted crystalline lattice are the classes of the Bloch-Kato subgroup $H^1_f$, [Theorem 7.5.3](/entry/twisted-lattice-cohomology-theorem-top/). The proof uses local Tate duality, the Euler characteristic formula and an operator $\psi$ on $(\varphi, \Gamma)$-modules, [Theorem 7.2.4](/entry/lemma-psi/). The same part derives Theorems B and C on surjections of lattices, [Theorem 7.7.5](/entry/local-lifting-theorem-b-of-bloch-kato-tate-duality-euler-char/) and [Theorem 7.8.6](/entry/prv-twenty-five-of-bloch-kato-tate-duality-euler-char/). The second part works with Breuil-Kisin modules over a tame field. It proves an integral Taylor formula for their connection, [Theorem 7.12.6](/entry/int-conn-taylor-main/), relates the jumps of the Breuil-Kisin filtration to the Hodge-Tate jumps, [Theorem 7.13.6](/entry/enstable-and-jumps/), and lifts finite classes along surjections of good lattices, [Theorem 7.15.2](/entry/lift-f-of-is-good/). This gives Theorem T, [Theorem 7.16.3](/entry/theorem-tclosing-main/), and Theorem A, [Theorem 7.17.5](/entry/theorem-a-closing/).

Chapter 8 studies Galois-stable lattices in crystalline representations, mostly over unramified $K$. For a surjection $T_1 \to T_2$ of lattices, every finite class of $T_2$ lifts to $T_1$ after multiplication by a power of $p$ that depends only on the Hodge-Tate jumps. The chapter proves the local Theorem B, [Theorem 8.1.10](/entry/thm-b-at/), the local Theorem C, [Theorem 8.2.7](/entry/theorem-of-local-lemmas/), the lifting along arcs in case A, [Theorem 8.3.7](/entry/universal-general-statement-holds/), and Theorem E in its final form, [Theorem 8.14.1](/entry/theorem-e-final-form/). It also proves a colength bound from frames for every constant $C \ge 160$, [Theorem 8.16.8](/entry/integrality-from-metric-of-large-constant/), and collects the local theory with explicit constants, [Theorem 8.20.2](/entry/local-theory-of-admitted/).

Chapters 1 to 5 do not use each other, so a reader can take them in any order. Chapters 6 to 8 form one block. Chapter 8 supplies the local Theorems B, C and E and the export bound to Chapter 7, and the lifting constants to Chapter 6. Chapter 7 uses the clauses on Wach lattices, the twisted torsion class theorem, the lifting constants and the integrality theorem of Bhatt and Scholze from Chapter 6. So a reader of the block can take Chapter 8 first, then Chapter 6, then Chapter 7.

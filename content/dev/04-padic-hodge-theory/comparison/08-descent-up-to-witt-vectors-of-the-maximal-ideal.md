## Descent up to Witt vectors of the maximal ideal

This section proves the descent hypothesis of the local comparison, [Theorem 2.6.9](/entry/local-of/). The three functors in that hypothesis are $W(A)/\xi$, $A_{\mathrm{cris}}(A)$ and the filtration steps of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}(A)$, all modulo $\ell^n$, on perfect algebras $A$. We show that each of them has descent from perfect algebras to test algebras, up to each element $s$ of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ whose Witt coordinates all lie in the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$ ([Theorem 2.8.11](/entry/out/)). The proof has three layers. First, the good systems of powers of [Theorem 2.7.9](/entry/powers-good/) give descent for the functors $N \otimes A$ with $N$ killed by a power of $\pi$, up to each root $r_k$. Second, five structural facts reduce the three functors to these tensor functors by short exact sequences. Third, the five facts hold. The [geometric steps](/entry/steps-holds/) use the result through the local comparison.

We keep the notation of the previous section. So $\pi \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$ has $\pi^\sharp = \ell$, $r_k = \pi^{1/\ell^k}$, and $A_{\mathrm{inf}} = W(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat)$ (see [[Witt vectors|6N3X2QX4W9]]). We write $\xi = \ell - [\pi] \in A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ and $[r_k]$ for the Teichmüller lift of $r_k$.

**Definition 2.8.1 (Perfect algebras and three functors).** A *perfect algebra* is a perfect $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$-algebra $A$ that is $\pi$-adically complete and on which $\pi$ is a nonzerodivisor. A perfect algebra is a *test algebra* if it is algebraically closed and of valuation type for $\pi$. For a perfect algebra $A$ and $n \geq 0$ put:

1. $U(A) = W(A)/\xi$ and $U_n(A) = U(A)/\ell^n$, with $\theta : W(A) \to U(A)$ the quotient map;
2. $A^0_{\mathrm{cris}}(A)$ the $W(A)$-subalgebra of $W(A)[1/\ell]$ generated by the elements $\xi^k/k!$, and $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}(A) = A^0_{\mathrm{cris}}(A)/\ell^n$;
3. $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n(A)$ the ideal of $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}(A)$ generated by the images of $\xi^k/k!$ for $k \geq r$.

So $U_n$ is a functor to $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$-modules, and $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$ and $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n$ are functors to $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$-modules. For $A = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$ these are $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}/\ell^n$, $A_{\mathrm{cris}}/\ell^n$ and its filtration.

**Definition 2.8.2 (Descent to test algebras).** Let $F$ be a functor from perfect algebras (or from perfect $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$-algebras) to modules over a ring $R$, and $s \in R$. Then $F$ has *descent up to $s$* if for each $A$ the augmented Čech complex of $F$ over the category of maps from $A$ to test algebras is exact up to $s$ in every degree, in the sense of section 6.

**Definition 2.8.3 (Witt vectors of the maximal ideal).** An element $s \in A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ lies in $W(\mathfrak{m}^\flat)$ if none of its Witt coordinates is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$.

Since $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$ is local, this is the kernel of the map from $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ to the Witt vectors of the residue field. The lift $[r_k]$ lies in $W(\mathfrak{m}^\flat)$, since $\pi$ is not a unit. If $x - 1$ is not a unit, then $[x] - 1$ lies in $W(\mathfrak{m}^\flat)$, since its image in the Witt vectors of the residue field is $[1] - 1 = 0$. So $\mu = [\varepsilon_0] - 1$ lies in $W(\mathfrak{m}^\flat)$.

Four general lemmas on descent are used. Let $j$ be the inclusion of test algebras.

**Lemma 2.8.4 (Induction on degree).** Let $F$ be a functor to $R$-modules and $I$ an ideal of $R$ with $I \subseteq (x^N : x \in I)$ for every $N$. If every object $A$ has a system of powers that is good for $F$ and $I$, then $F$ has descent up to each $s \in I$.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.P.induct`.

*Proof.* By induction on the degree $n$ of the augmented Čech complex over the points of $A$. In degree $n$ one compares the complex for $A$ with the complexes for $B$ and its powers $K_m$, whose points extend those of $A$. Exactness up to $s$ at the Čech complex of $B$ over $A$ and the induction hypothesis for the $K_m$ give exactness in degree $n$ up to a product of elements of $I$. The condition on $I$ writes $s$ as a combination of products of this form, so exactness holds up to $s$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.8.5 (Full and faithful change of category).** Let $G$ be a full and faithful functor and $F$ a functor on its target. If $F$ has descent along $G \circ j$ up to $s$, then $F \circ G$ has descent along $j$ up to $s$.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.D.des_ff`.

*Proof.* Since $G$ is full and faithful, the maps from $A$ to $j(t)$ are in bijection with the maps from $G(A)$ to $G(j(t))$. So the two augmented complexes are isomorphic. $\square$

**Lemma 2.8.6 (Ideal of descent).** Let $S \subseteq R$ be a set such that for each $a \in S$, either $F$ has descent up to $a$, or $a$ kills $F(A)$ for every $A$. Then $F$ has descent up to each element of the ideal generated by $S$.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.D.des_span`.

*Proof.* If $a$ kills all values of $F$, then $a$ kills the cohomology of the augmented complex, so $F$ has descent up to $a$. The set of $s$ such that a complex is exact up to $s$ is an ideal. $\square$

**Lemma 2.8.7 (Short exact sequences).** Let $0 \to F_1 \to F_2 \to F_3 \to 0$ be a sequence of functors that is exact at each object and compatible with the scalars. If $F_1$ and $F_3$ have descent up to $a$, then $F_2$ has descent up to $a^3$. If $F_2$ and $F_3$ have descent up to $a$, then $F_1$ has descent up to $a^3$.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.D.des_ses`.

*Proof.* The augmented Čech complexes form a short exact sequence of complexes. A diagram chase in its long exact sequence, with three uses of exactness up to $a$, gives exactness of the middle complex, or of the left complex, up to $a^3$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.8.8 (Descent for tensor functors).** Assume that $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$ is a valuation domain, that each nonzero element divides some $\pi^M$, and that each non-unit is divisible by some $r_k$. Let $N$ be an $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$-module with $\pi^M N = 0$ for some $M$. Then the functor $A \mapsto N \otimes A$ on perfect algebras has descent up to $r_k$, for every $k$.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.D.des_tens`.

*Proof.* Let $I$ be the ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$ generated by the $r_k$. For each $N$ we have $I \subseteq (x^N : x \in I)$: since $r_{k+N}^{\ell^N} = r_k$ and $N \leq \ell^N$, we get $r_k = r_{k+N}^{N} \cdot r_{k+N}^{\ell^N - N}$. By [Theorem 2.7.9](/entry/powers-good/), every perfect $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$-algebra has a system of powers that is good for $N \otimes -$ and $I$. The lemma on induction on degree gives descent up to each element of $I$ for $N \otimes -$ on perfect $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$-algebras. The functor from perfect algebras to perfect $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$-algebras is full and faithful, so the lemma on change of category gives the claim. $\square$

**Definition 2.8.9 (Structural facts).** The *structural facts* are the following five statements.

1. *Tilt.* $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$ is a valuation domain, each nonzero element divides some $\pi^M$, and each non-unit is divisible by some $r_k$.
2. *Level one.* There are an $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$-module $N$ and $M$ with $\pi^M N = 0$, and additive isomorphisms $\tau_A : N \otimes A \cong A_{\mathrm{cris},1}(A)$, natural in $A$, with $\tau_A(a_0 x) = a\, \tau_A(x)$ for $a \in A_{\mathrm{inf}}$, where $a_0$ is the first Witt coordinate of $a$.
3. *Sequence.* For each $n$ there are injective additive maps $\alpha_A : A_{\mathrm{cris},1}(A) \to A_{\mathrm{cris},n+1}(A)$ with $\alpha_A(y) = \ell^n y$ on representatives $y \in A^0_{\mathrm{cris}}(A)$, whose image is the kernel of the reduction $A_{\mathrm{cris},n+1}(A) \to A_{\mathrm{cris},n}(A)$.
4. *The ring $U$.* $\ell$ is a nonzerodivisor on $U(A)$, and for $x \in W(A)$, $\theta(x) \in \ell U(A)$ if and only if $x_0 \in \pi A$.
5. *Graded pieces.* Each $z \in \mathrm{Fil}^r_n(A)$ is congruent modulo $\mathrm{Fil}^{r+1}_n(A)$ to $x \cdot \xi^r/r!$ for some $x \in W(A)$, and $x \cdot \xi^r/r! \in \mathrm{Fil}^{r+1}_n(A)$ if and only if $\theta(x) \in \ell^n U(A)$.

**Theorem 2.8.10 (Descent from the structural facts).** Assume the structural facts. Let $s \in W(\mathfrak{m}^\flat)$ and $n \geq 0$. Then $U_n$ has descent up to $s$, and $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$ and $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n$ (for each $r$) have descent up to the image of $s$ in $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.D.out_of_rin`.

*Proof.* 1. *Reduction to the roots.* By fact 1, each $s \in W(\mathfrak{m}^\flat)$ lies in the ideal of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ generated by $\ell^n$ and the lifts $[r_k]$. The element $\ell^n$ kills $U_n$, $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$ and $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n$. By the lemma on the ideal of descent, it is enough to prove descent up to each $[r_k]$.

2. *The functor $U_n$.* Since $W(A)/\ell = A$ and $\xi \equiv -[\pi]$ modulo $\ell$, fact 4 gives a natural isomorphism $U_1(A) \cong A/\pi A = (\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat/\pi) \otimes A$, under which $a \in A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ acts through $a_0$. So [Theorem 2.8.8](/entry/des-tens/) gives descent of $U_1$ up to $[r_k]$, whose first coordinate is $r_k$. For $n + 1$, multiplication by $\ell^n$ is injective on $U_1$ since $\ell$ is a nonzerodivisor on $U(A)$, and gives the exact sequence $0 \to U_1 \to U_{n+1} \to U_n \to 0$. As in step 3 below, the lemma on short exact sequences and $[r_{k+2}]^3 \mid [r_k]$ give descent of $U_{n+1}$ up to $[r_k]$.

3. *The functor $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$.* Induction on $n$. For $n = 0$ the functor is zero. For $n = 1$, fact 2 identifies $A_{\mathrm{cris},1}$ with $N \otimes A$, and $[r_k]$ acts as $r_k$, so [Theorem 2.8.8](/entry/des-tens/) gives descent up to $[r_k]$. For $n + 1$, fact 3 gives the exact sequence
$$0 \to A_{\mathrm{cris},1} \xrightarrow{\ \alpha\ } A_{\mathrm{cris},n+1} \to A_{\mathrm{cris},n} \to 0.$$
By induction both outer terms have descent up to $[r_{k+2}]$, so by the lemma on short exact sequences the middle term has descent up to $[r_{k+2}]^3$. Since $[r_{k+2}]^3$ divides $[r_k] = [r_{k+2}]^{\ell^2}$, the middle term has descent up to $[r_k]$.

4. *The functor $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n$.* By fact 5, the map $x \mapsto x \cdot \xi^r/r!$ induces a natural isomorphism from $U_n$ onto $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n/\mathrm{Fil}^{r+1}_n$. Induction on $r$. For $r = 0$, $\mathrm{Fil}^0_n = A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$ and step 3 applies. For $r + 1$, the exact sequence $0 \to \mathrm{Fil}^{r+1}_n \to \mathrm{Fil}^r_n \to U_n \to 0$, the induction hypothesis, step 2 and the second form of the lemma on short exact sequences give descent of $\mathrm{Fil}^{r+1}_n$ up to $[r_{k+2}]^3$, hence up to $[r_k]$.

This gives descent up to each element of $W(\mathfrak{m}^\flat)$ for the three functors. $\square$

**Theorem 2.8.11 (Descent up to Witt vectors of the maximal ideal).** Let $s \in W(\mathfrak{m}^\flat)$ and $n \geq 0$. Then $U_n$ has descent up to $s$, and $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$ and $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n$ (for each $r$) have descent up to the image of $s$ in $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.D.out`.

*Proof.* The five structural facts hold. Fact 1 holds because $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$ is a valuation ring of rank one with value group $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\pi$ of positive valuation. Fact 2 is the computation of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ modulo $\ell$ by base change from $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$. Fact 3 holds because $A^0_{\mathrm{cris}}(A)$ has no $\ell$-torsion. Facts 4 and 5 are computations in $W(A)$ with $\xi = \ell - [\pi]$. Then [Theorem 2.8.10](/entry/out-of-rin/) applies. $\square$

With $s = \mu$ this is the hypothesis of [Theorem 2.6.9](/entry/local-of/), so the local comparison on a small chart holds with no hypothesis.

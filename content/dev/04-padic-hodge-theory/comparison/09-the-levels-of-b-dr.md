## The levels of the de Rham period ring

The period map of section 5 ([Theorem 2.5.5](/entry/crystalline-de-rham-first-chern-exists/)) was built one level at a time. At level $m$ it reads a crystalline class as a map from the dual of de Rham cohomology to $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+/t^m$. Section 5 took two inputs as given: a good level system that is compatible with $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, and a $\rho$-system on it. This section proves both, and so completes the construction of the period map. The level system is concrete: level $k$ is the quotient $B_k = B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+/t^k$, with a subring $A_k$ generated by the image of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ and by divided powers of $\xi/\ell^M$. The $\rho$-system then comes from a general realization principle: a compatible family of crystalline classes over $A_{\mathrm{cris}}/\ell^n$ lifts, up to a power of $\ell$, to one class over $A_k$, and its image over $B_k$ has unique coordinates in a basis of de Rham cohomology.

We keep the notation of section 5. So $(A_{\mathrm{cris}}, B_{\mathrm{dR}}, B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+, t, i, o)$ are the period data of $K$, with the action of $G_K$, and $\xi = \ell - [\pi]$ generates the kernel of $\theta$ on $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ (see the Background on [period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings)). In $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ the elements $\xi$ and $t$ generate the same ideal.

**Definition 2.9.1 (Level).** A *level of order $m$* consists of:

1. a $K$-algebra $B$ with a ring map $p : B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ \to B$, an ideal $I_B$ of $B$ and divided powers on $I_B$;
2. an $\mathcal{O}_K$-algebra $A$ with an ideal $I$ and divided powers on $I$, a ring map $\iota : A \to B$, and a ring map $\lambda : A_{\mathrm{cris}} \to A$;
3. actions $\sigma_A$ and $\sigma_B$ of $G_K$ on $A$ and $B$ by ring maps.

The level is *good* if $m \geq 1$ and the following hold.

1. *Quotient.* $p$ is surjective, its kernel is $t^m B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, and it is the given map on $K$.
2. *Subring.* $\iota$ is injective and a map of divided power rings, $I = \iota^{-1}(I_B)$, $I_B = \iota(I) B$, $\iota$ is the inclusion on $\mathcal{O}_K$, and $\iota \circ \lambda = p \circ i$ on $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$.
3. *Galois.* The actions commute with $p$, $\iota$ and $\lambda$, fix $\mathcal{O}_K$ and $K$, and respect the divided powers.
4. *Filtration.* The $r$-th divided power ideal of $I_B$ is $p(t)^r B$.
5. *Lattice.* For each $s \geq m$, the ring $A$ with $I$ has the lattice properties at $s$: $I$ is prime, the divided power ideals are finitely generated, $A$ is $\ell$-adically complete, any two elements are comparable by divisibility modulo $I$, every element outside $I$ divides a power of $\ell$ modulo $I$, a closure condition for the divided power ideals in the $\ell$-adic topology holds, and a torsion freeness condition holds.

A good level is a pair $A \subset B$ in which $B$ is a truncation of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and $A$ is a divided power ring close to the image of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. The lattice properties make $A$ behave like a valuation ring of mixed characteristic.

**Definition 2.9.2 (Level system).** A *level system* is a sequence $L_0, L_1, \ldots$ in which $L_m$ is a level of order $m + 1$, with transition maps $A_{m+1} \to A_m$ and $B_{m+1} \to B_m$. It is *good* if each $L_m$ is good, the transition maps commute with $p$, $\iota$, $\lambda$, $\mathcal{O}_K$, $K$, the divided powers and the actions of $G_K$, and if for every compatible family $(y_m)$ with $y_m \in B_m$ there is a unique $x \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ with $p(x) = y_m$ for all $m$.

A level is *compatible with $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$* if $\lambda(\xi) \in I$, and $a - \lambda(w) \in I$ whenever $a \in \mathcal{O}_K$, $w \in A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ and $\theta(w) = a$.

**Definition 2.9.3 (The standard levels).** Fix $M \geq 0$. For $k \geq 0$ let $B_k = B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+/t^k$ with the image $\bar{\xi}$ of $\xi$, and let $\eta = \bar{\xi}/\ell^M$. Let $A_k \subset B_k$ be the subring generated by the image of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ and the elements $\eta^i/i!$. Let $I_B = (\bar{\xi})$, with the divided powers $x \mapsto x^n/n!$ (which exist since $B_k$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra), and let $I = A_k \cap I_B$. The *lattice hypotheses* for $M$ say that the images of $\mathcal{O}_K$ and of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ lie in $A_k$, that $A_k$ is closed under the divided powers of $I_B$, that $G_K$ preserves $t^k B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and $A_k$, and that the transition maps $B_{k+1} \to B_k$ take $A_{k+1}$ into $A_k$.

So the standard level of order $k$ is $A_k \subset B_k$, with $\lambda$ and $\sigma$ induced from $A_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and from the action on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. The constant $M$ makes room for the divided powers of $\xi$ in $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$: these are the elements $\xi^i/i!$, while $A_k$ holds $\xi^i/(\ell^{Mi} i!)$.

**Lemma 2.9.4 (Divided powers over a field of characteristic zero).** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero, $\iota : A \to B$ and $\iota' : A' \to B'$ ring maps, with $B'$ a $K$-algebra, and $I \subseteq A$, $I' \subseteq A'$ ideals. Let $J = \iota(I) B$ and $J' = \iota'(I') B'$ carry divided powers $\delta$ and $\delta'$. If $s : A \to A'$ and $g : B \to B'$ are ring maps with $g \iota = \iota' s$ and $s(I) A' \subseteq I'$, then $g$ is a map of divided power rings from $(B, J, \delta)$ to $(B', J', \delta')$.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.HT.dp_of_char`.

*Proof.* The ideal $J$ is generated by the elements $\iota(a)$ with $a \in I$, and $g(\iota(a)) = \iota'(s(a))$ lies in $\iota'(I') B' = J'$. So $g(J) \subseteq J'$. For $x \in J$ we have $n!\, \delta_n(x) = x^n$, so $n!\, g(\delta_n(x)) = g(x)^n = n!\, \delta'_n(g(x))$. Since $B'$ is a $K$-algebra and $K$ has characteristic zero, $n!$ is a unit in $B'$, and $g(\delta_n(x)) = \delta'_n(g(x))$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.9.5 (Standard levels are good).** Assume the lattice hypotheses for $M$, and let $k \geq 1$. Then the standard level of order $k$ is good.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.HT.lev_good`.

*Proof.* We check the conditions of a good level one by one. Injectivity of $\iota$ and $I = \iota^{-1}(I_B)$ hold by construction, since $A_k$ is a subring and $I$ is defined as a preimage. The map $\iota$ respects divided powers because $A_k$ is closed under them. The Galois action on $B_k$ respects divided powers by the lemma on fields of characteristic zero. We take as given the other conditions (the kernel $t^k$, the image of $I$, the filtration, the lattice properties, the Galois compatibilities), as facts on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and on $A_k$ under the lattice hypotheses. $\square$

**Theorem 2.9.6 (The standard system is good).** Assume the lattice hypotheses for $M$. Then the levels $L_m = (A_{m+1} \subset B_{m+1})$, with the transition maps induced by $B_{m+2} \to B_{m+1}$, form a good level system.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.HT.levSys_good`.

*Proof.* Each $L_m$ is good by the previous theorem. The transition maps are induced by the quotient maps of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, so they commute with $p$, $\lambda$, $\mathcal{O}_K$, $K$ and $\iota$ by construction. The transition on $B$ respects divided powers by the lemma on fields of characteristic zero. We take as given that the transition on $A$ respects divided powers, the compatibility with $G_K$, and the uniqueness of $x \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ for compatible families, which holds because $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is $t$-adically complete and separated. $\square$

**Theorem 2.9.7 (Existence of a level system).** Let $K$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. There is a good level system whose levels are all compatible with $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.HT.levSys_ex`.

*Proof.* We take as given that there is $M$ such that the lattice hypotheses hold. The bound on $M$ comes from the integers of $K$: the images of $\mathcal{O}_K$ must lie in $A_k$. The standard system for this $M$ is good by the previous theorem. We also take as given that $\lambda(\xi)$ and $a - \lambda(w)$ lie in $I$ for each level, so each level is compatible with $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$: both elements map to zero under $\theta$. $\square$

To build a $\rho$-system on a level system, we use an abstract form of the period map at one level.

**Definition 2.9.8 (Chain).** A *chain* over rings $\Lambda \to A \to B$ and a field $K \to B$ consists of: $\Lambda$-modules $S_n^j$ with transition maps $S_{n+1}^j \to S_n^j$; $A$-modules $Z_n^j$ and $Z^j$, with maps $r_n : S_n^j \to Z_n^j$ (semilinear along $\Lambda \to A$) and reductions $Z^j \to Z_n^j$; $B$-modules $W^j$ with maps $b : Z^j \to W^j$; and $K$-vector spaces $V^j$ with maps $\mu : V^j \to W^j$. It is *good* if $\ell$ is a unit in $B$, each $V^j$ is finite-dimensional, every $w \in W^j$ has unique coordinates $g_s \in B$ with $w = \sum_s g_s \mu(b_s)$ for a basis $(b_s)$ of $V^j$, the maps commute with the transitions, every compatible family $x = (x_n)$ in $S^j$ has a class $z \in Z^j$ and $e$ with $\mathrm{red}_n(z) = \ell^e r_n(x_n)$ for all $n$, and $b(z) = 0$ when all reductions of $z$ vanish.

A *realization* of a compatible family $x$ is a $K$-linear map $f : (V^j)^* \to B$ such that for some $e$ and $z$ as above, $\ell^e \sum_s f(b_s^*)\, \mu(b_s) = b(z)$ for every basis.

For a smooth model $\mathfrak{X}$, a level $L_m$ and $s \geq 0$ there is a chain with: $S_n^j$ the crystalline cohomology of $\mathfrak{X}$ over $A_{\mathrm{cris}}/\ell^n$; $Z^j$ and $Z_n^j$ the cohomology of thickenings over $A_m$ (and modulo $\ell^n$) whose $s$-th divided power ideal vanishes; $W^j$ the same over $B_m$ for the generic fibre; and $V^j = H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$. Then $\rho(x, m, f)$ is defined to mean: $f$ is a realization of $x$ in this chain for all large $s$.

**Theorem 2.9.9 (Existence of a $\rho$-system).** Let $(L_m)$ be a good level system whose levels are compatible with $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. Then the relation $\rho$ above is a $\rho$-system for smooth models, with tautological lines and the de Rham first Chern class of the line on the generic fibre.

Lean: `CrystallineSemistableComparison.Pb.Kap.Mo.rhoSys_ex`.

*Proof.* We take as given six facts on realizations in a good chain: a compatible family has a realization; the realization is unique; realizations add and commute with scalars; they are functorial along maps of good chains; and they respect cup products and the unit class.

1. *Good chains.* For a smooth model, take a finite cover by étale charts of dimensions $d_\alpha$. We take as given that for $s \geq \max_\alpha d_\alpha + m + 1$ the chain at level $m$ and $s$ is good. The map from $A_{\mathrm{cris}}/\ell^n$ to $A_m/\ell^n$ that defines $r_n$ comes from the compatibility of $L_m$ with $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$.
2. *Existence and uniqueness.* A good chain gives a realization $f$ at one $s$. For larger $s'$ there is a map of chains from $s'$ to $s$ that is the identity on $V$ and $W$, so a realization at $s'$ is one at $s$, and uniqueness at $s$ shows that the realization does not depend on $s$.
3. *Linear structure.* Additivity, linearity over $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ through $\iota \lambda$ and over $K$ follow from the facts on realizations.
4. *Levels, Galois and pullbacks.* The transition $L_{m+1} \to L_m$, the action of $g \in G_K$, and the pullback along a map of models give maps of good chains. Functoriality gives the compatibility with transitions, with $G_K$ and with pullback.
5. *Filtration.* We take as given the following: if $x$ lies in filtration step $n \leq m$ and $\varphi$ vanishes on $\mathrm{Fil}^{r+1}$, then $f(\varphi)$ lies in the $(n - r)$-th divided power ideal of $B_m$. Its proof is a chart computation at level $m$.
6. *Cup product and unit.* These are the corresponding facts on realizations, applied with the cup product of the chain.
7. *First Chern classes.* We take as given, from a chart computation, that the de Rham first Chern class $c$, read as the map $\varphi \mapsto \varphi(c)$, is a realization of a crystalline first Chern class. By uniqueness, $f(\varphi) = \varphi(c)$.

These are the conditions of a $\rho$-system. $\square$

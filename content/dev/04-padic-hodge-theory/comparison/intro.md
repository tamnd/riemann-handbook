This chapter proves the comparison theorems of $\ell$-adic Hodge theory that [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/) needs for smooth projective models over the ring of integers of a finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. It first computes Hodge cohomology on the thickening site by Čech complexes of differential forms on a coordinate cover, and it shows that the top power $c^d$ of the de Rham first Chern class of a tautological line is not zero. With these inputs it builds the Hodge-Tate map, which pairs étale cohomology with Hodge cohomology and takes values in $\mathbb{C}_\ell$. It proves that the map is injective, then that it is bijective, and it deduces the dimension formula $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} V_n = \sum_a \dim_K \mathrm{Hdg}(a, n)$. For the crystalline comparison it builds a period map that pairs crystalline classes over $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ with de Rham classes and takes values in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, and it proves the local comparison between the thickening site and the perfectoid site on a small étale chart, up to a bounded power of $\mu$. The later part of the chapter proves the descent statement and the level systems that these sections take as inputs, and it passes from a chart to a model.

From Volume 1 the chapter uses [period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings), [crystalline, de Rham and potentially semistable representations](/learn/galois-representations/#crystalline-de-rham-and-potentially-semistable-representations), [the cyclotomic character and Tate modules](/learn/galois-representations/#the-cyclotomic-character-and-tate-modules), [étale cohomology](/learn/geometry/#tale-cohomology), and [de Rham cohomology](/learn/geometry/#de-rham-cohomology-and-deformations). Its results feed four theorems of the chapter on Galois representations: [the Hodge-Tate bijection](/entry/ht-bij/), [the Hodge-Tate comparison for smooth projective models](/entry/hodge-tate-comparison-smooth-projective/), [the Hodge-Tate period matrices for good models](/entry/ht-spec-good/), and [the geometric steps](/entry/steps-holds/). Through them it enters the step [GeoOne holds](/step/geo-one-holds/) of the Galois row.

## Free divided power thickenings

The last four sections of the chapter compare the crystalline cohomology of a smooth scheme with the de Rham cohomology of a smooth lift, in degrees one and two. This section sets up the objects that the comparison works with: thickenings, their levels and their divided power de Rham complexes. It proves that a thickening at a bounded level has a free extension by finitely many divided power variables, and that the extension does not change the divided power de Rham complex in degrees zero, one and two. The next section uses the free extension to build coproducts with the thickenings of an affine lift.

We use divided powers on ideals, divided power morphisms and sub divided power ideals in the usual sense; see [rings, ideals and modules](/learn/algebra/#rings-ideals-and-modules). For forms and the de Rham complex see [de Rham cohomology and deformations](/learn/geometry/#de-rham-cohomology-and-deformations). All rings are commutative.

**Definition 5.7.1 (Level).** Let $B$ be a ring and $\delta$ a divided power structure on an ideal $J$ of $B$. For $N \geq 0$ let $\mathrm{fil}_N(\delta)$ be the ideal spanned by the products $\delta_{k_1}(a_1) \cdots \delta_{k_r}(a_r)$ with $r \geq 0$, all $a_l \in J$ and $k_1 + \cdots + k_r \geq N$. We say that $\delta$ is *at level* $N$ if $\mathrm{fil}_N(\delta) = 0$. We say that $\delta$ is *nilpotent* if every such product of total degree at least $N$ is $0$, for some $N$.

So $\delta$ is nilpotent if and only if it is at some level. We have $\mathrm{fil}_0(\delta) = B$ and $\mathrm{fil}_1(\delta) = J$, so only the zero ring is at level $0$. A divided power morphism maps $\mathrm{fil}_N$ into $\mathrm{fil}_N$, so a target at level $N$ kills $\mathrm{fil}_N$ of the source.

**Definition 5.7.2 (Thickening).** Let $R$ be a ring, $\kappa$ a divided power structure on an ideal $I$ of $R$, and $t : Y \to \mathrm{Spec}\, R$ a scheme over $R$. A *thickening* $T$ of $Y$ consists of an $R$-algebra $B_T$, an ideal $J_T$ of $B_T$ with a nilpotent divided power structure $\delta_T$ such that $R \to B_T$ is a divided power morphism, a ring $B_{T,0}$ with a surjective ring map $\pi_T : B_T \to B_{T,0}$ whose kernel is $J_T$, and a morphism $x_T : \mathrm{Spec}\, B_{T,0} \to Y$ such that $t \circ x_T$ is the spectrum of $\pi_T$ composed with $R \to B_T$. A *morphism* $f : T \to T'$ consists of an $R$-algebra map $f : B_T \to B_{T'}$ that is a divided power morphism and a ring map $f_0 : B_{T,0} \to B_{T',0}$, with $f_0 \circ \pi_T = \pi_{T'} \circ f$ and $x_T \circ \mathrm{Spec}(f_0) = x_{T'}$.

Since $\pi_T$ is surjective, $f_0$ is fixed by $f$, and two morphisms are equal when their ring maps are equal. We say that $T$ is at level $N$ when $\delta_T$ is.

**Definition 5.7.3 (Divided power de Rham complex).** Let $T$ be a thickening and $B = B_T$. Let $\Omega^\bullet_{B/R}$ be the exterior algebra over $B$ of the module of differentials $\Omega^1_{B/R}$. Let $\mathcal{R}_T$ be the ideal of $\Omega^\bullet_{B/R}$ spanned by the one forms $d(\delta_{n+1}(a)) - \delta_n(a)\, da$ with $a \in J_T$ and $n \geq 0$. Put $K^0(T) = B$ and $K^j(T) = \Omega^j_{B/R} / (\Omega^j_{B/R} \cap \mathcal{R}_T)$ for $j \geq 1$. The differential $d$ passes to the quotients and gives a complex
$$K^0(T) \to K^1(T) \to K^2(T) \to K^3(T),$$
and a morphism of thickenings gives a map of complexes. We say that a morphism $f : T \to T'$ is a *quasi-isomorphism in low degrees* if the induced map $H^j(K^\bullet(T)) \to H^j(K^\bullet(T'))$ is bijective for $j = 0, 1, 2$.

The identity is a quasi-isomorphism in low degrees, and so is a composite of two of them.

**Definition 5.7.4 (Free thickening).** Fix $N$. Let $u : T \to F$ be a morphism of thickenings and $\tau = (\tau_l)_{l \in L}$ a family of elements of $B_F$. We say that $(u, \tau)$ is *free at level* $N$ if $F$ is at level $N$, every $\tau_l$ lies in $J_F$, and for each thickening $S$ at level $N$, each morphism $a : T \to S$ and each family $c$ of elements of $J_S$ indexed by $L$, there is a unique morphism $g : F \to S$ with $g \circ u = a$ and $g(\tau_l) = c_l$ for all $l$.

**Theorem 5.7.5 (One free variable).** Let $R$, $I$, $\kappa$ and $t : Y \to \mathrm{Spec}\, R$ be as above. Let $n \geq 0$ and let $T$ be a thickening of $Y$ at level $n + 1$. Then there are a thickening $F$, a morphism $u : T \to F$ and an element $\tau \in B_F$ such that $(u, \tau)$ is free at level $n + 1$ and $u$ is a quasi-isomorphism in low degrees.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PDForm.exists_isFree_one_qAt` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_freeCrystallineThickening_oneElement_level`, a wrapper).

*Proof.* Put $B = B_T$ and $J = J_T$. We take as given these results.

- (a) There is a *divided power polynomial algebra* $P$ over $B$: a $B$-algebra with a $B$-basis $e_0, e_1, \ldots$ such that $e_0 = 1$ and $e_m e_k = \binom{m+k}{m} e_{m+k}$.
- (b) For a ring map $f : B \to S$ and elements $c_0, c_1, \ldots$ of $S$ with $c_0 = 1$ and $c_m c_k = \binom{m+k}{m} c_{m+k}$, there is a ring map $G : P \to S$ that extends $f$ with $G(e_k) = c_k$. There is a $B$-algebra map $\varepsilon : P \to B$ whose kernel is the ideal $A$ spanned by the $e_k$ with $k \geq 1$.
- (c) Put $J^+ = JP + A$. There is a divided power structure $\eta$ on $J^+$ that is *standard*: $B \to P$ is a divided power morphism and $\eta_k(e_1) = e_k$ for all $k$.
- (d) If $G : P \to S$ is a ring map, $\delta_S$ is a divided power structure on an ideal $J_S$, $c \in J_S$, $G$ restricted to $B$ is a divided power morphism, and $G(e_k) = \delta_{S,k}(c)$ for all $k$, then $G$ is a divided power morphism from $\eta$.
- (e) For $N \geq 1$, $\mathrm{fil}_N(\eta)$ is a sub divided power ideal. The quotient by a sub divided power ideal has a divided power structure on the image ideal, the quotient map is a divided power morphism, and a ring map out of the quotient is a divided power morphism if its composite with the quotient map is one. If a divided power morphism $f : P \to P'$ maps the ideal of $P$ onto a set that spans the ideal of $P'$, then $\mathrm{fil}_N$ of the target lies in the ideal spanned by $f(\mathrm{fil}_N)$.
- (f) A truncated Poincaré lemma: if $T$ is at level $n+1$, then $B \to P / \mathrm{fil}_{n+1}(\eta)$ induces bijections on $H^0$, $H^1$ and $H^2$ of the complexes above.

1. *The thickening.* Put $K = \mathrm{fil}_{n+1}(\eta)$, $B_F = P/K$ and $J_F = J^+ B_F$, with the quotient structure $\delta_F$ of (e). By (e) the ideal $\mathrm{fil}_{n+1}(\delta_F)$ lies in the ideal spanned by the image of $K$, so it is $0$, and $F$ is at level $n + 1$. So $\delta_F$ is nilpotent. The map $R \to B \to P \to B_F$ is a composite of divided power morphisms.
2. *The reduction.* For $p \in P$ we claim that $\varepsilon(p) \in J$ if and only if $p \in J^+$. If $\varepsilon(p) \in J$, write $p = \varepsilon(p) + (p - \varepsilon(p))$; the second term is in the kernel $A$ of $\varepsilon$. Conversely $\varepsilon$ maps $JP$ into $J$ and kills $A$. Since $K \subseteq \mathrm{fil}_1(\eta) = J^+$, the map $\pi_T \circ \varepsilon$ passes to $B_F$, and its kernel is $J_F$. Put $B_{F,0} = B_{T,0}$, let $\pi_F$ be this map and $x_F = x_T$. Let $u$ be the map $B \to B_F$, with $u_0$ the identity. Put $\tau = e_1 \bmod K$, which lies in $J_F$.
3. *Existence of maps.* Let $S$ be at level $n + 1$, $a : T \to S$ and $c \in J_S$. The elements $c_k = \delta_{S,k}(c)$ satisfy the conditions of (b), so there is $G : P \to S$ that extends $a$ with $G(e_k) = \delta_{S,k}(c)$. By (d), $G$ is a divided power morphism. So $G(K)$ lies in $\mathrm{fil}_{n+1}(\delta_S) = 0$, and $G$ factors through a ring map $g : B_F \to B_S$, which is a divided power morphism by (e). Put $g_0 = a_0$. For $p \in P$, the element $p - \varepsilon(p)$ lies in $J^+$, so $G$ maps it into $J_S$, and $\pi_S(G(p)) = \pi_S(a(\varepsilon(p))) = a_0(\pi_T(\varepsilon(p)))$. So $g$ is a morphism with $g \circ u = a$ and $g(\tau) = \delta_{S,1}(c) = c$.
4. *Uniqueness.* Let $g'$ be another such morphism, and let $H$ be $g'$ composed with $P \to B_F$. Then $H$ agrees with $G$ on $B$. Since $H$ is a divided power morphism, $H(e_k) = H(\eta_k(e_1)) = \delta_{S,k}(c) = G(e_k)$. The $e_k$ span $P$ over $B$, so $H = G$ and $g' = g$.
5. *Cohomology.* By (f), $u$ is a quasi-isomorphism in low degrees. $\square$

**Theorem 5.7.6 (Finitely many free variables).** Let $R$, $I$, $\kappa$ and $Y$ be as above, let $n, m \geq 0$ and let $T$ be a thickening of $Y$ at level $n + 1$. Then there are a thickening $F$, a morphism $u : T \to F$ and elements $\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_m \in B_F$ such that $(u, \tau)$ is free at level $n + 1$ and $u$ is a quasi-isomorphism in low degrees.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PDForm.FreeFin.exists_free` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_freeThickening_level_finitelyMany_elements`, a wrapper).

*Proof.* We use induction on $m$. For $m = 0$ take $F = T$ and $u$ the identity; the empty family is free, since a morphism $g$ with $g \circ \mathrm{id} = a$ is $a$. For the step, take $(u, \tau)$ for $m$ elements. Then $F$ is at level $n + 1$, so the last theorem gives $(u', \tau')$ free at level $n + 1$ with one element, $u' : F \to F'$. We claim that $u' \circ u$ with the family $\tau', u'(\tau_1), \ldots, u'(\tau_m)$ is free at level $n + 1$. The elements lie in $J_{F'}$, since $u'$ is a divided power morphism. Let $S$, $a$ and $c_0, c_1, \ldots, c_m$ be given. Freeness of $(u, \tau)$ gives $g_1 : F \to S$ with $g_1 \circ u = a$ and $g_1(\tau_l) = c_l$. Freeness of $(u', \tau')$ gives $g : F' \to S$ with $g \circ u' = g_1$ and $g(\tau') = c_0$. Then $g$ has the required properties. If $g'$ also has them, then $g' \circ u'$ has the properties of $g_1$, so $g' \circ u' = g_1$, and then $g' = g$. The composite $u' \circ u$ is a quasi-isomorphism in low degrees as a composite of two. $\square$

The quasi-isomorphism condition is `IsQ` in [PullbackBijectiveDegreesOneTwo.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/PullbackBijectiveDegreesOneTwo.lean#L63); its six fields are injectivity and surjectivity on $H^0$, $H^1$ and $H^2$. The divided power polynomial algebra is an abstract structure with a basis, and its existence is a separate result; we read the statements of the inputs (a) to (f) only. Both theorems are stated at level $n + 1$, which loses nothing since only the zero ring is at level $0$. The basis lemma used for uniqueness in the first file sits in a different namespace from the theorem of that file.

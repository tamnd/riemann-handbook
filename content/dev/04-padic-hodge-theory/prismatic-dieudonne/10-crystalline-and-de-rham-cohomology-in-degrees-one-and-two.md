## Crystalline and de Rham cohomology in degrees one and two

This section compares the crystalline cohomology of $X_0$ with the de Rham cohomology of the smooth lift $X$, in degrees one and two. An affine lift gives a relation between crystalline and de Rham classes. The first theorem shows that this relation is defined everywhere, is surjective and detects zero. The second theorem turns it into a linear isomorphism that respects cup products, agrees with the comparison map in degree one and is natural in the datum. The comparison is an input of the step on characters ([step](/step/every-character/)), through the files on abelian schemes.

We keep the notation of the last three sections. Fix a crystalline datum $D$, with ring $R$, ideal $I$, divided powers $\kappa$ and morphisms $s : X \to \mathrm{Spec}\, R$, $t : X_0 \to \mathrm{Spec}\, R$ and $i : X_0 \to X$. Let $\mathrm{Th}$ be the category of thickenings of $X_0$, let $O$ be the functor $T \mapsto B_T$ on $\mathrm{Th}$, and let $\mathcal{A}$ be the category of affine opens of $X$, with one morphism $U \to V$ when $V \subseteq U$. We use the cohomology of a complex over a category from [the last section](/entry/lift-restriction-bijective-low-degrees/).

**Definition 5.10.1 (Crystalline cohomology).** For $j = 1, 2$ the *crystalline cohomology* of $D$ is $H^j(\mathrm{Th}, O)$, the cohomology of the category $\mathrm{Th}$ with values in $O$. The *cup product* $H^1(\mathrm{Th}, O) \times H^1(\mathrm{Th}, O) \to H^2(\mathrm{Th}, O)$ sends the classes of cocycles $a$ and $b$ to the class of the cocycle $(\varphi, \psi) \mapsto \psi(a_\varphi)\, b_\psi$ on composable pairs $T \xrightarrow{\varphi} T' \xrightarrow{\psi} T''$.

**Definition 5.10.2 (De Rham cohomology).** For an affine open $U$ of $X$ let $O(U)$ be its ring of sections. The modules $\Omega^j_{O(U)/R}$ for $j = 0, 1, 2, 3$, with the exterior derivative, form a complex $\Omega^\bullet$ of functors on $\mathcal{A}$. For $j = 1, 2$ the *de Rham cohomology* of $X$ is $H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(X) = H^j(\mathcal{A}, \Omega^\bullet)$. A total $1$-cochain is a pair $w = (w^0, w^1)$, with $w^0_a \in O(V)$ for each morphism $a : U \to V$ and $w^1_U \in \Omega^1_{O(U)/R}$ for each $U$. The Lean code defines a cup product $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X) \times H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X) \to H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$.

**Definition 5.10.3 (Comparison relation).** Let $\Lambda$ be an affine lift of $D$, with thickenings $L(U)$ and isomorphisms $e_U : O(U) \to B_{L(U)}$. Let $K^\bullet$ be the divided power de Rham complex on $\mathrm{Th}$. A total $1$-cochain $k$ of $K^\bullet \circ L$ on $\mathcal{A}$ and a total $1$-cochain $w$ of $\Omega^\bullet$ *correspond* if $k^0_a = e_V(w^0_a)$ for each morphism $a : U \to V$ and $k^1_U$ is the class of $e_U(w^1_U)$ in $K^1(L(U))$ for each $U$. Correspondence of total $2$-cochains is defined in the same way, component by component. Let $c \in H^1(\mathrm{Th}, O)$ and $h \in H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$. The pair $(c, h)$ is in the *comparison relation* $\mathrm{Rel}^1_\Lambda$ if there are a total $1$-cocycle $k$ of $K^\bullet$ on $\mathrm{Th}$, a $1$-cocycle $f$ of $O$ on $\mathrm{Th}$ and a total $1$-cocycle $w$ of $\Omega^\bullet$ on $\mathcal{A}$ such that $c = [f]$, $h = [w]$, the first component of $k$ is $f$, and the restriction $L^* k$ of $k$ along $L$ corresponds to $w$. The relation $\mathrm{Rel}^2_\Lambda$ on $H^2(\mathrm{Th}, O) \times H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$ is defined in the same way.

**Theorem 5.10.4 (The comparison relations detect zero).** For every crystalline datum $D$, every affine lift $\Lambda$ of $D$ and $j = 1, 2$:

1. for every $c \in H^j(\mathrm{Th}, O)$ there is $h \in H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$ with $(c, h) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_\Lambda$;
2. for every $h \in H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$ there is $c \in H^j(\mathrm{Th}, O)$ with $(c, h) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_\Lambda$;
3. if $(c, h) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_\Lambda$, then $c = 0$ if and only if $h = 0$.

*Proof.* We give the proof for $j = 1$. We take as given three results.

- (a) The map $H^1(\mathrm{Th}, K^\bullet) \to H^1(\mathrm{Th}, O)$ that forgets the forms is bijective.
- (b) The restriction map $H^1(\mathrm{Th}, K^\bullet) \to H^1(\mathcal{A}, K^\bullet \circ L)$ is bijective ([theorem](/entry/lift-restriction-bijective-low-degrees/)).
- (c) For each total $1$-cochain $k$ of $K^\bullet \circ L$ there is exactly one total $1$-cochain $w$ of $\Omega^\bullet$ that corresponds to $k$, and for each $w$ there is exactly one such $k$. If $k$ and $w$ correspond, then $k$ is a cocycle if and only if $w$ is a cocycle, and $k$ is a coboundary if and only if $w$ is a coboundary.

Correspondence is compatible with differences: if $k$ corresponds to $w$ and $k'$ corresponds to $w'$, then $k - k'$ corresponds to $w - w'$. This is clear from the definition, since each $e_U$ is additive.

1. *Key step.* Let $k$ be a total $1$-cocycle of $K^\bullet$ on $\mathrm{Th}$, with first component $k^0$. Then $k^0$ is a coboundary of $O$ if and only if $L^* k$ is a coboundary. Indeed, the class $[k]$ maps to $[k^0]$ under the map of (a) and to $[L^* k]$ under the map of (b). Both maps are injective, so $[k^0] = 0$ if and only if $[k] = 0$, if and only if $[L^* k] = 0$.
2. *Every $c$ has a partner.* Write $c = [f]$ with $f$ a $1$-cocycle of $O$. By the surjectivity in (a) there is a total $1$-cocycle $k$ of $K^\bullet$ whose class maps to $[f]$. So $k^0 - f$ is a coboundary and $[k^0] = c$. By (c) there is $w$ that corresponds to $L^* k$, and $w$ is a cocycle since $L^* k$ is a cocycle. Then $k$, $k^0$ and $w$ show that $(c, [w]) \in \mathrm{Rel}^1_\Lambda$.
3. *Every $h$ has a partner.* Write $h = [w]$ with $w$ a total $1$-cocycle of $\Omega^\bullet$. By (c) there is $k_0$ that corresponds to $w$, and $k_0$ is a cocycle. By the surjectivity in (b) there is a total $1$-cocycle $k$ of $K^\bullet$ on $\mathrm{Th}$ with $[L^* k] = [k_0]$, so $L^* k - k_0$ is a coboundary. By (c) there is a cocycle $w_1$ that corresponds to $L^* k$. Then $L^* k - k_0$ corresponds to $w_1 - w$, so by (c) the cochain $w_1 - w$ is a coboundary and $[w_1] = h$. Put $c = [k^0]$. Then $k$, $k^0$ and $w_1$ show that $(c, h) \in \mathrm{Rel}^1_\Lambda$.
4. *Zero is detected.* Let $(c, h) \in \mathrm{Rel}^1_\Lambda$, with $k$, $f$ and $w$ as in the definition. Then $c = [k^0]$ and $h = [w]$. By step 1, $c = 0$ if and only if $L^* k$ is a coboundary. Since $L^* k$ corresponds to $w$, by (c) this holds if and only if $w$ is a coboundary, that is, if and only if $h = 0$.

For $j = 2$ the proof is the same, with total $2$-cochains in place of total $1$-cochains and with the degree two parts of (a), (b) and (c). $\square$

**Definition 5.10.5 (Pullback of crystalline classes).** Let $(I, \kappa)$ and $(I', \kappa')$ be divided power ideals of rings $R$ and $R'$, and let $t : Y \to \mathrm{Spec}\, R$ and $t' : Y' \to \mathrm{Spec}\, R'$ be schemes. Let $\sigma : R \to R'$ be a divided power morphism and $\psi : Y' \to Y$ a morphism with $t \circ \psi = \mathrm{Spec}(\sigma) \circ t'$. Then there is a functor from the thickenings of $Y'$ to the thickenings of $Y$. It keeps the ring $B_T$, its ideal and its divided powers, it makes $B_T$ an $R$-algebra through $\sigma$, and it replaces $x_T$ by $\psi \circ x_T$. Composition with this functor gives a $\sigma$-semilinear *pullback* $\psi^*$ from the cohomology of the thickenings of $Y$ with values in $O$ to that of $Y'$, in degrees one and two.

**Definition 5.10.6 (Morphisms of data).** Let $D$ and $D'$ be crystalline data. A *morphism* from $D$ to $D'$ consists of a ring map $\sigma : R \to R'$, a morphism $\varphi : X' \to X$ with $s \circ \varphi = \mathrm{Spec}(\sigma) \circ s'$, and a morphism $\varphi_0 : X'_0 \to X_0$ with $i \circ \varphi_0 = \varphi \circ i'$, such that $\sigma$ is a divided power morphism from $(I, \kappa)$ to $(I', \kappa')$ and $t \circ \varphi_0 = \mathrm{Spec}(\sigma) \circ t'$. The last two conditions give the pullback $\varphi_0^* : H^j(\mathrm{Th}, O) \to H^j(\mathrm{Th}', O)$. On the de Rham side, let $\mathcal{P}$ be the category of pairs $(U', U)$ of affine opens $U'$ of $X'$ and $U$ of $X$ with $U' \subseteq \varphi^{-1}(U)$. The maps $O(U) \to O(U')$ given by $\varphi$ pull back a class $h \in H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$ to a class over $\mathcal{P}$, and the projection $(U', U) \mapsto U'$ restricts a class $h' \in H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(X')$ to a class over $\mathcal{P}$. We say that $h'$ is a *pullback* of $h$ if these two classes over $\mathcal{P}$ are equal.

**Definition 5.10.7 (Comparison map in degree one).** Let $H^1_{\mathrm{cris}}(X/R)$ be the cohomology with values in $O$ of the category of thickenings of $X$ over $R$, where $R$ carries the zero ideal with its divided powers. The Lean code defines an $R$-linear *comparison map* $\mathrm{cmp} : H^1_{\mathrm{cris}}(X/R) \to H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$. It evaluates a cocycle on the trivial thickenings $\mathrm{Spec}\, O(U) \to X$ of the affine opens $U$. If $\mathrm{id}_R$ is a divided power morphism from the zero ideal to $(I, \kappa)$ and $s \circ i = t$, then the pullback $i^* : H^1_{\mathrm{cris}}(X/R) \to H^1(\mathrm{Th}, O)$ is defined.

**Theorem 5.10.8 (Crystalline and de Rham cohomology).** There are, for every crystalline datum $D$, $R$-linear isomorphisms
$$e_1(D) : H^1(\mathrm{Th}, O) \to H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X), \qquad e_2(D) : H^2(\mathrm{Th}, O) \to H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$$
with the following three properties.

1. For every $D$ and all $a, b \in H^1(\mathrm{Th}, O)$, $e_2(D)(a \cup b) = e_1(D)(a) \cup e_1(D)(b)$.
2. For every $D$ such that $i^*$ is defined, as in the last definition, and every $c \in H^1_{\mathrm{cris}}(X/R)$, $e_1(D)(i^* c) = \mathrm{cmp}(c)$.
3. For every morphism $(\sigma, \varphi, \varphi_0)$ from $D$ to $D'$ and every $c \in H^1(\mathrm{Th}, O)$, the class $e_1(D')(\varphi_0^* c)$ is a pullback of $e_1(D)(c)$. The same holds for $e_2$ and every $c \in H^2(\mathrm{Th}, O)$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.SmoothLift.crystallineSmoothLift_of_relations`.

*Proof.* We take as given five results. Every crystalline datum has an affine lift. For every affine lift $\Lambda$ of $D$ and $j = 1, 2$, the relation $\mathrm{Rel}^j_\Lambda$ respects sums and scalars: $(a, h), (b, h') \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_\Lambda$ imply $(a + b, h + h') \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_\Lambda$, and $(a, h) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_\Lambda$ implies $(r a, r h) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_\Lambda$ for $r \in R$. If $(a, h), (b, h') \in \mathrm{Rel}^1_\Lambda$, then $(a \cup b, h \cup h') \in \mathrm{Rel}^2_\Lambda$. If $i^*$ is defined, then for every $c \in H^1_{\mathrm{cris}}(X/R)$, $(i^* c, \mathrm{cmp}(c)) \in \mathrm{Rel}^1_\Lambda$. For a morphism $(\sigma, \varphi, \varphi_0)$ from $D$ to $D'$, affine lifts $\Lambda$ of $D$ and $\Lambda'$ of $D'$, and $j = 1, 2$, if $(c, h) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_\Lambda$ and $(\varphi_0^* c, h') \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_{\Lambda'}$, then $h'$ is a pullback of $h$.

1. *A relation gives an isomorphism.* Let $M$ and $N$ be $R$-modules and let $P \subseteq M \times N$ be a relation that is defined on all of $M$, surjective onto $N$, compatible with sums and scalars, and such that $(c, h) \in P$ implies that $c = 0$ if and only if $h = 0$. Then there is an $R$-linear isomorphism $e : M \to N$ with $e(c) = h$ if and only if $(c, h) \in P$. Indeed, if $(c, h), (c, h') \in P$, then $(0, h' - h) \in P$ by the compatibility with sums and with the scalar $-1$, so $h = h'$. So $P$ is the graph of a map $e$, which is $R$-linear by the same compatibility. If $e(a) = e(b)$, then $(a - b, 0) \in P$, so $a = b$. The surjectivity of $P$ gives that of $e$.
2. *Choice of lifts.* For each $D$ choose an affine lift $\Lambda_D$. By the theorem above and the first two inputs, step 1 applies to $\mathrm{Rel}^1_{\Lambda_D}$ and $\mathrm{Rel}^2_{\Lambda_D}$. It gives isomorphisms $e_1(D)$ and $e_2(D)$ such that $(c, e_j(D)(c)) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_{\Lambda_D}$ for all $c$, and such that $(c, h) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_{\Lambda_D}$ implies $h = e_j(D)(c)$.
3. *Cup products.* Since $(a, e_1(D)(a))$ and $(b, e_1(D)(b))$ are in $\mathrm{Rel}^1_{\Lambda_D}$, the third input gives $(a \cup b, e_1(D)(a) \cup e_1(D)(b)) \in \mathrm{Rel}^2_{\Lambda_D}$. By step 2 this is property 1.
4. *Degree one.* The fourth input gives $(i^* c, \mathrm{cmp}(c)) \in \mathrm{Rel}^1_{\Lambda_D}$. By step 2, $e_1(D)(i^* c) = \mathrm{cmp}(c)$.
5. *Naturality.* We have $(c, e_j(D)(c)) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_{\Lambda_D}$ and $(\varphi_0^* c, e_j(D')(\varphi_0^* c)) \in \mathrm{Rel}^j_{\Lambda_{D'}}$. The fifth input gives property 3. $\square$

## Notes and references

The chapter feeds two parts of the proof. The period matrices, their lift to $A_{\inf}$ and the prismatic Dieudonné functor prove [the theorem on prismatic Dieudonné modules](/entry/prismatic-dieudonne-hodge/), a literature input of the proof ([step](/step/cm-abelian-inputs-from-literature/)). The [comparison of crystalline and de Rham cohomology](/entry/crystalline-smooth-lift-of-relations/) in degrees one and two is used in the files on abelian schemes, below the step on characters ([step](/step/every-character/)). The treatment here differs from the sources: the cohomology is that of a category of thickenings, not of the crystalline site, only degrees one and two are compared, and the comparison goes through a chosen affine lift.

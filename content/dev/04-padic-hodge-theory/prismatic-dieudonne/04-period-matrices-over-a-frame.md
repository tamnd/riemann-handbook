## Period matrices over a frame

This section proves that a $p$-divisible group $G$ over $O$ such that $G$ and its dual are connected has, over every frame over $A_{\inf}$, a Frobenius matrix $U$ and two period matrices $Y$ and $G_m$ with a list of relations. The relations say that $Y$ and $G_m$ are inverse to each other up to $\mu$, that they are compatible with Frobenius through $U$, and that their reductions under $\theta$ are the Hodge-Tate map and the map $\mathrm{dlog}$. The next section lifts these matrices from the frame to $A_{\inf}$ itself ([Theorem 5.5.1](/entry/window-lift/)). Together with the reduction to connected groups ([Theorem 5.6.3](/entry/has-datum-of-conn-dual-conn/)), this gives the Hodge-Tate data from which the prismatic Dieudonné functor is built ([Theorem 5.6.6](/entry/prismatic-dieudonne-hodge-at-of-has-datum/)), and through it the theorem on prismatic Dieudonné modules ([Theorem 2.8.5 of Volume 12](/entry/prismatic-dieudonne-hodge/)).

We keep the notation of the last three sections. A $p$-divisible group $G$ is *connected* if each Hopf algebra $A_n$ is a local ring. Let $\varepsilon$ be a primitive system and $\mu = [\varepsilon] - 1$. The Weil pairing of $T_p G$ with $T_p G^\vee$ takes values in $T_p \mu_{p^\infty}$, and $\varepsilon$ is a generator of this module; this identifies the pairing with a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-valued pairing $\langle t, \chi \rangle$. Let $\mathrm{HT} : T_p G \to \omega_{G^\vee}$ be the Hodge-Tate map and $\mathrm{dlog} : T_p G^\vee \to \omega_G$ the map that sends a character to the pullback of $dT/T$; see [the cyclotomic character and Tate modules](/learn/galois-representations/#the-cyclotomic-character-and-tate-modules).

**Definition 5.4.1 (Period of a quasi-logarithm).** Let $(B, Q, \theta)$ be a frame for $p$ with $B$ and $Q$ algebras as in the frames over $A_{\inf}$, let $c$ be a chart of $G$, let $f$ be a series over $Q$ in $d$ variables, let $t \in T_p G$ and $y \in B$. For each $n$, the component $t_n$ of $t$ is an $O$-point of $\mathrm{Spec}\,A_n$, and it gives the elements $t_n(x_{n,i})$ of $O$. We say that $y$ is a *period* of $f$ at $t$ if:

1. for each $n$ and each $z \in B^d$ with $\theta(z_i) = t_n(x_{n,i})$ for all $i$, the truncations of $f$ evaluated at $z$ converge $p$-adically in $Q$ to a value $f(z)$;
2. for each such $n$, $z$ and value, $p^n f(z) - y$ lies in $p^n B$.

So $y$ is the limit of $p^n f(z_n)$ for lifts $z_n$ of the coordinates of $t_n$. Since $p^n t_n = 0$, this is the usual way to read off a period from a quasi-logarithm.

**Definition 5.4.2 (Nilpotent modulo the maximal ideal).** Let $\theta : B \to O$ and $\sigma : B \to B$ be ring maps and $M$ a square matrix over $B$. The matrix $M$ is *nilpotent modulo the maximal ideal* for $\theta$ and $\sigma$ if there is $N \geq 0$ such that for every vector $v$ over $B$, no entry of $\theta(\Phi^N(v))$ is a unit of $O$, where $\Phi(v) = M \sigma(v)$.

**Theorem 5.4.3 (Period matrices over a frame).** Let $p$, $C$, $v$ and $O$ be as in the first section, and let $\varepsilon \in O^\flat$ be a primitive system. Let $G$ be a $p$-divisible group over $O$ of height $h$ such that $G$ and $G^\vee$ are connected. Let $(B, Q, a, \theta, \sigma, s, \tau)$ be a frame over $A_{\inf}$ for $\varepsilon$. Then there are $d, k \geq 0$, a square matrix $U$ over $B$ of size $d + k$, a matrix $Y$ over $B$ with rows indexed by $\{1, \ldots, d\} \sqcup \{1, \ldots, k\}$ and $h$ columns, a matrix $G_m$ over $B$ with $h$ rows and columns indexed like the rows of $Y$, a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-basis $t_1, \ldots, t_h$ of $T_p G$, elements $\chi_1, \ldots, \chi_h$ of $T_p G^\vee$, an $O$-basis $w_1, \ldots, w_d$ of $\omega_G$ and an $O$-basis $l_1, \ldots, l_k$ of $\mathrm{Lie}(G^\vee)$, such that, with $\Delta = \mathrm{diag}_{d,k}(p, 1)$ and $\Delta' = \mathrm{diag}_{d,k}(1, p)$:

1. $d + k = h$, and $U$ is invertible;
2. $\sigma(Y) = \Delta\, U^{T}\, Y$;
3. $U \Delta\, \sigma(G_m^{T}) = p\, G_m^{T}$;
4. $G_m Y = a(\mu) \cdot 1_h$;
5. $\theta(Y_{(1,i), m}) = 0$ for all $i$ and $m$, and $\theta((G_m)_{m, (2,j)}) = 0$ for all $m$ and $j$;
6. $U \Delta$ and $(U^{T})^{-1} \Delta'$ are nilpotent modulo the maximal ideal for $\theta$ and $\sigma$;
7. $\langle t_m, \chi_{m'} \rangle = 1$ if $m = m'$ and $0$ otherwise;
8. $\theta(Y_{(2,j), m}) = l_j(\mathrm{HT}(t_m))$ for all $j$ and $m$;
9. $\mathrm{dlog}(\chi_m) = \sum_i \theta((G_m)_{m, (1,i)})\, w_i$ for all $m$.

*Proof.* We take as given the results named below, and we use the theorems of the first three sections.

1. *Chart and basis over $O$.* Since $G$ is connected, it has a chart $c$ of some dimension $d$, and $d + \mathrm{rank}\,\mathrm{Lie}(G^\vee) = h$. The triple $(O, C, \mathrm{id})$ is a frame for $p$. The theorem on [adapted bases](/entry/adapted-basis/) with $E = C$ gives $k$, a basis $e^0$ of quasi-logarithms for $\mu_c$ over $C$ that is adapted, whose first block consists of logarithms, and whose second block consists of Lie series for a basis $l_1, \ldots, l_k$ of $\mathrm{Lie}(G^\vee)$. So $d + k = h$.
2. *Lift to the frame.* Since $\theta$ is surjective, the law $\mu_c$ has a lift $\mu'$ along $\theta$. There is a ring map $\Theta : Q \to C$ compatible with $\theta$. The basis $e^0$ lifts to a basis $e$ of quasi-logarithms for $\mu'$ over $Q$ that is adapted for $\theta$ and maps to $e^0$ under $\Theta$. This lift uses the theorem on [symmetric cocycles](/entry/symmetric-cocycle-is-coboundary-mod/) with $N = J = \ker \theta$.
3. *Frobenius.* There is a matrix $U$ such that $\varphi_s(e_i) - \sum_l (U\Delta)_{l,i}\, e_l$ is integral for all $i$, and has coefficients in $pB$ for $i$ in the first block. The theorem on [the Frobenius matrix](/entry/frob-matrix-is-unit/) gives that $U$ is invertible. Since $G$ is connected, $U \Delta$ is nilpotent modulo the maximal ideal. Since $G^\vee$ is connected and $U$ is invertible, so is $(U^{T})^{-1} \Delta'$. This gives parts 1 and 6.
4. *Bases of Tate modules.* The module $T_p G$ is free of rank $h$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p$; let $t_1, \ldots, t_h$ be a basis. The pairing is perfect, so there is a dual family $\chi_1, \ldots, \chi_h$ for it. This gives part 7.
5. *The matrix $Y$.* Each $e_l$ has a unique period $Y_{l,m}$ at $t_m$. Periods are additive and $B$-linear in $f$, the period of an integral series with constant term $0$ is $0$, and the period of $\varphi_s(f)$ is $\sigma$ of the period of $f$. Apply this to $\varphi_s(e_i) - \sum_l (U\Delta)_{l,i} e_l$. Uniqueness of periods gives $\sigma(Y_{i,m}) = \sum_l (U\Delta)_{l,i} Y_{l,m}$, which is part 2.
6. *The matrix $G_m$.* For each $\chi_m$ there is a quasi-logarithm $f_m$ over $Q$ such that $\varphi_s(f_m) - p f_m$ is integral and such that the period of $f_m$ at $t$ is $a(\mu \langle t, \chi_m \rangle)$ for each $t$; it also has a form attached to it. Since $e$ is a basis, there is a unique vector $g_m$ over $B$ with $f_m - \sum_l g_{m,l} e_l$ integral. Put $(G_m)_{m,l} = g_{m,l}$. The periods of $f_m$ at $t_{m'}$ give $\sum_l g_{m,l} Y_{l,m'} = a(\mu) \langle t_{m'}, \chi_m \rangle$, which is part 4 by part 7. Both $\sum_i (U\Delta)_{l,i} \sigma(g_{m,i})$ and $p\, g_{m,l}$ are coefficient vectors of $p f_m$ modulo integral series, since $\varphi_s(f_m - \sum g_{m,l} e_l)$ is integral. Uniqueness in the basis gives part 3.
7. *Reduction by $\theta$.* The period of a quasi-logarithm over $C$ at $t$ is the value of its tangent vector on $\mathrm{HT}(t)$. The first block of $e^0$ consists of logarithms, whose tangent vector is $0$, and the second block has tangent vectors $l_j$. Since the periods of $e^0$ are the images $\theta(Y)$, this gives part 5 for $Y$ and part 8. The image of $f_m$ under $\Theta$ is a logarithm over $O$ with integral error, and a logarithm has no component on the second block. So $\theta(g_{m,(2,j)}) = 0$, and the linear part of the image of $f_m$ is the vector $\theta(g_{m,(1,i)})$. The forms $dx_1, \ldots, dx_d$ of the chart are an $O$-basis $w$ of $\omega_G$, and $\mathrm{dlog}(\chi_m)$ is the form given by the linear part of $f_m$. This gives part 5 for $G_m$ and part 9. $\square$

The matrices $Y$ and $G_m$ play the roles of the two comparison maps between $T_p G \otimes A_{\inf}$ and the Dieudonné module, with $\mu$ as the defect. Part 2 says that $Y$ is the matrix of a Frobenius equivariant map, and part 4 says that $G_m$ inverts it up to $\mu$.

**Remark 5.4.4 (Formalization).** The theorem is in [HasPeriodIntegral.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/HasPeriodIntegral.lean#L95), with the uniqueness and linearity of periods at lines 48 to 86. Periods are `Chart.HasPeriod` and nilpotence is `IsNil` in EqZeroOfForallPowDvd.lean. The results we take as given are `GlobalTheory.T` statements, among them `exists_pDivGroupFormalChart_of_connected`, `chartDim_add_finrank_dual_lie_eq_height`, `isTorsionFreePDThickeningFrame_valuation_integers`, `exists_formalGroupLawLift_of_surjective`, `exists_lift_adapted_basis_quasi_logarithms_frame`, `exists_frobenius_matrix_quasiLogarithm_basis`, `frobenius_nilpotent_mod_maximal_connected_pDivGroup`, `isNil_dual_frobenius_alt`, `exists_unique_period_quasiLogarithm_pDivGroup`, `quasiLogarithm_period_frobenius_twist`, `exists_quasiLogarithm_of_dualCharacter`, `logarithm_integers_adapted_basis_no_lie_components`, `pdDivGroupChart_forms_basis_and_differential` and `quasiLogarithm_period_eq_tangent_hodgeTate`; we read their statements, not their proofs. The conclusion holds for every frame over $A_{\inf}$, and the statement does not say that such a frame exists; existence is the separate theorem `GlobalTheory.BRing.exists_bFrame` in ExistsBFrame.lean (line 203), and the two are combined with the lift of the next section in HasDatumBiconnOf.lean (line 10). The elements $\chi_m$ are only asked to be dual to the $t_m$; that they form a basis follows from the perfectness of the pairing, but the statement does not say it.

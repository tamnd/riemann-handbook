## Period data over the $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ tower

Let $G$ be a smooth commutative group of relative dimension $g$ over $A_{\mathrm{inf}}/(\xi^2)$. This section defines a *period datum* of $G$ and proves that $G$ has one when $[p]$ is finite and flat, the integral points are $p$-divisible with $p^{2g}$ points of order $p$, and the Weil pairing on these points is nondegenerate. A period datum is a compatible system of $p$-power torsion points $\tau_i(n)$, with two matrices of periods: the Hodge-Tate periods $H$, read from the pairing of $\tau_i(n)$ with tangent vectors, and the periods $B$ of lifts of $\tau_i(n)$ to $A_{\mathrm{inf}}/(\xi^2, p^{n+1})$. They satisfy the period relation $B^{\mathsf T} H - H^{\mathsf T} B = cE$, where $E$ is the Gram matrix of the Weil pairing and $c = \zeta_p - 1$. The theorem is the core of [the theorem on abelian schemes](/entry/abelian-period-datum-main/) in the next section. Through [the proposition on Hopf orders](/entry/htd-of-dat/) in Section 3, a period datum gives the Hodge-Tate datum on which the length bounds of Sections 5 and 6 rest.

Throughout the chapter $p$ is a prime and $C$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ with a valuation $v : C \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$, written multiplicatively. A ring $\mathcal{O}$ with a map $\mathcal{O} \to C$ is *the ring of integers of $v$* if the map is injective with image $\{x : v(x) \leq 1\}$. Then $\mathcal{O}$ is a local domain. In this section we also assume that $p$ is not a unit in $\mathcal{O}$ and that $\mathcal{O}$ is $p$-adically complete and separated. We use the [period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings) of the Background: the tilt $\mathcal{O}^\flat$, the ring $A_{\mathrm{inf}} = W(\mathcal{O}^\flat)$ of [[Witt vectors|6N3X2QX4W9]] and the map $\theta : A_{\mathrm{inf}} \to \mathcal{O}$ with $\theta([x]) = x^\sharp$.

### The tower

Fix $\varepsilon \in \mathcal{O}^\flat$ such that $\zeta_p = (\varepsilon^{1/p})^\sharp$ is a primitive $p$-th root of unity. Put
$$\xi = \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} [\varepsilon^{1/p}]^i, \qquad \nu = [\varepsilon^{1/p}] - 1,$$
so that $\xi \nu = [\varepsilon] - 1$ and $\theta(\xi) = 0$. Let $\Lambda = A_{\mathrm{inf}}/(\xi^2)$. Then $\theta$ induces a surjection $\bar\theta : \Lambda \to \mathcal{O}$, and $\Lambda$ is a local ring. All rings below are local $\Lambda$-algebras, and all maps are local homomorphisms of $\Lambda$-algebras.

**Definition 4.1.1 (Thickening tower).** A *thickening tower* over $\Lambda$ consists of a local $\Lambda$-algebra $\mathcal{O}$, local $\Lambda$-algebras $R_n$ and $R_n'$ for $n \geq 0$, maps $\pi_n : R_n' \to R_n$ and $q_n : \mathcal{O} \to R_n$, elements $\xi_n, c_n' \in R_n'$, an element $c \in \mathcal{O}$, units $\hat\zeta_n \in R_n'$ and units $\zeta_n \in \mathcal{O}$, with these properties:

1. $\pi_n$ is surjective with kernel $\xi_n R_n'$, $\xi_n^2 = 0$, and each $a$ with $a \xi_n = 0$ lies in $\xi_n R_n'$;
2. $p^{n+1} = 0$ in $R_n'$, and an element $a \in \mathcal{O}$ with $q_n(a) = 0$ for all $n$ is $0$;
3. $\pi_n(c_n') = q_n(c)$, $\hat\zeta_n^{p^{n+1}} = 1 + c_n' \xi_n$ and $q_n(\zeta_n) = \pi_n(\hat\zeta_n)$;
4. there are transition maps $R_{n+1}' \to R_n'$ and $R_{n+1} \to R_n$ that commute with $\pi$ and $q$ and map $\xi_{n+1}$ to $\xi_n$, and each compatible family $(x_n) \in \prod_n R_n$ is $(q_n(a))_n$ for some $a \in \mathcal{O}$.

So $\pi_n$ is a square-zero thickening, and $\hat\zeta_n$ is a lift of the root of unity $\zeta_n$ whose $p^{n+1}$-th power is $1$ up to a multiple of $\xi_n$.

**Example 4.1.2 (The $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ tower).** Take $R_n = \mathcal{O}/p^{n+1}$ and $R_n' = \Lambda/p^{n+1}$, with $\pi_n$ induced by $\bar\theta$ and $q_n$ the reduction. Let $\xi_n$ and $c_n'$ be the images of $\xi$ and $\nu$, and put $c = \theta(\nu) = \zeta_p - 1$, which is not $0$. Let $\hat\zeta_n$ be the image of $[\varepsilon^{1/p^{n+1}}]$ and $\zeta_n = \theta([\varepsilon^{1/p^{n+1}}])$. Then $\zeta_n$ is a primitive $p^{n+1}$-th root of unity, $\zeta_{n+1}^p = \zeta_n$, and $\hat\zeta_n^{p^{n+1}} = [\varepsilon] = 1 + \nu\xi$ in $R_n'$. The kernel of $\theta$ is generated by $\xi$, and this gives the conditions on $\pi_n$. We call this tower the *$A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ tower*. Its ring $\mathcal{O}$ is a domain, because $\mathcal{O} \to C$ is injective.

### Groups with a tangent space and a biextension

We work with a group functor on local $\Lambda$-algebras that has the two structures we need: a tangent space that describes it on square-zero thickenings, and a biextension that gives the Weil pairing ([duality and the Poincaré bundle](/learn/abelian-varieties/#duality-and-the-poincar-bundle)).

**Definition 4.1.3 (Group with tangent space and biextension).** A *framed group* $\mathcal{B}$ over $\Lambda$ consists of:

1. a functor $R \mapsto G(R)$ from local $\Lambda$-algebras to abelian groups;
2. a free $\Lambda$-module $\mathfrak{g}$ of finite rank, the *tangent module*;
3. for each surjection $\pi : R \to S$ with square-zero kernel $I$, an injective homomorphism $\mathrm{tg}_\pi : \mathfrak{g} \otimes_\Lambda I \to G(R)$, natural in $\pi$, whose image is the kernel of $G(\pi)$, with $G(\pi)$ surjective;
4. for each $R$ and $x, y \in G(R)$, a set $P(x, y)$ with a simply transitive action of $R^\times$, natural in $R$, with partial group laws $P(x, y) \times P(x', y) \to P(x + x', y)$ and $P(x, y) \times P(x, y') \to P(x, y + y')$ and trivializations $P(0, y)$ and $P(x, 0)$, subject to the axioms of a biextension of $G \times G$ by $\mathbb{G}_m$.

Let $N \geq 1$ and let $x, y \in G(R)$ with $Nx = Ny = 0$. Take $u \in P(x, y)$. The $N$-fold sums of $u$ in the first and in the second variable lie in $P(Nx, y) = P(0, y)$ and $P(x, Ny) = P(x, 0)$, so they are $l_1$ and $l_2$ times the trivializations, with $l_1, l_2 \in R^\times$. The unit $e_N(x, y) = l_1/l_2$ does not depend on $u$. This is the *Weil pairing* of the biextension.

**Definition 4.1.4 (Hodge-Tate period).** A *dual-number thickening* of $R$ is a local algebra $D$ with maps $\pi : D \to R$ and $\sigma : R \to D$ with $\pi\sigma = \mathrm{id}$, and an element $\eta \in D$ with $\eta^2 = 0$, $\ker \pi = \eta D$ and $\sigma(r)\eta = 0$ only for $r = 0$. Let $y \in G(R)$ with $Ny = 0$, and let $h : \mathfrak{g} \to R$ be $\Lambda$-linear. Then $h$ is a *Hodge-Tate period* of $y$ at level $N$ if for each $w \in \mathfrak{g}$
$$e_N\bigl(\mathrm{tg}_\pi(w \otimes \eta), \sigma(y)\bigr) = 1 + \sigma(h(w))\,\eta \quad \text{in } D^\times.$$

So $h(w)$ is the derivative of the Weil pairing of $y$ with the infinitesimal point of direction $w$. For an abelian scheme this is the Hodge-Tate map $T_p G \to \omega$ of the Background ([the Hodge-Tate sequence](/learn/formal-groups/#dieudonne-modules-and-the-hodge-tate-sequence)), composed with the polarization.

### Hodge-Tate data and period data

Let $\mathrm{Ix} = \{1, \dots, g\} \sqcup \{1, \dots, g\}$, a set of $2g$ indices, and let $O_e$ be a commutative $\mathbb{Z}_p$-algebra with an involution $\sigma$.

**Definition 4.1.5 (Hodge-Tate data).** Let $R$ be a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-algebra and $c_p \in R$. *Hodge-Tate data* for $(O_e, \sigma, c_p)$ consist of a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-algebra map $\rho : O_e \to M_{\mathrm{Ix}}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, a ring map $\rho_L : O_e \to M_g(R)$, matrices $P \in M_{\mathrm{Ix}}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ and $P_L \in M_g(R)$, a matrix $E \in M_{\mathrm{Ix}}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ and matrices $H, B \in M_{g \times \mathrm{Ix}}(R)$, such that:

1. $E^{\mathsf T} = -E$, $E$ has zero diagonal, $\det E \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, and $\rho(a)^{\mathsf T} E = E \rho(\sigma a)$ for all $a$;
2. $P$ and $P_L$ are idempotents, $P^{\mathsf T} E = E P$, and $P$ and $P_L$ commute with $\rho$ and $\rho_L$;
3. $H \rho(a) = \rho_L(\sigma a)^{\mathsf T} H$ for all $a$, and $H P = P_L^{\mathsf T} H$;
4. $B^{\mathsf T} H - H^{\mathsf T} B = c_p E$.

Condition 4 is the period relation. It says that the alternating form $E$, scaled by $c_p$, is the commutator of the two period matrices.

**Definition 4.1.6 (Period datum).** Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a framed group over $\Lambda$ with $\mathfrak{g}$ of rank $g$, and take a thickening tower with $\mathcal{O}$ a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-algebra. A *period datum* of $\mathcal{B}$ for $(O_e, \sigma, c_p)$, with $c_p \in \mathcal{O}$, consists of:

1. a formal chart: a local algebra $K$ with a map $\mathcal{O} \to K$, a formal group law $F$ of dimension $g$ over $\Lambda$, a basis $(w_k)$ of $\mathfrak{g}$, and for each surjection $R \to K$ with nilpotent kernel a bijection from $\ker(G(R) \to G(K))$ to $\mathfrak{m}_R^g$ that carries the group law to $F$, is natural, and sends $\mathrm{tg}(w \otimes i)$ to the coordinates of $w$ times $i$;
2. a thickening $W \to K$ of $\Lambda/(p)$-type with $p^2 = 0$ in $W$, a Teichmüller map from a perfect ring of characteristic $p$, and the Witt addition formula, such that $G(W) \to G(K)$ is surjective;
3. finitely many finite free $\mathcal{O}$-algebras $A_i$ with points $y_i \in G(A_i)$, $p y_i = 0$, that represent the $p$-torsion of $G$ over $\mathcal{O}$-algebras, with functions separated by the $\mathcal{O}$-points, and $\sum_i \operatorname{rank} A_i = \#G(\mathcal{O})[p]$;
4. an action of $O_e$ on $G(S)[p]$, natural in $S$, with a $\Lambda$-linear action on $\mathfrak{g}$ that matches it on tangent vectors;
5. Hodge-Tate data $(\rho, \rho_L, P, P_L, E, H, B)$ over $\mathcal{O}$;
6. points $\tau_i(n) \in G(\mathcal{O})$ for $i \in \mathrm{Ix}$, $n \geq 0$, with $p^{n+1}\tau_i(n) = 0$ and $\tau_i(n) = p\,\tau_i(n+1)$, such that $t \mapsto \sum_j t_j \tau_j(0)$ is a bijection $(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\mathrm{Ix}} \to G(\mathcal{O})[p]$;
7. $\Lambda$-linear maps $h_i : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathcal{O}$, with $q_n h_i$ a Hodge-Tate period of $q_n(\tau_i(n))$ at level $p^{n+1}$ for a fixed system of dual-number thickenings of the $R_n$, and $H_{ki} = h_i(w_k)$;
8. elements $b_i \in \mathfrak{g} \otimes \mathcal{O}$ such that for each $n$ some lift $\tilde\tau \in G(R_n')$ of $q_n(\tau_i(n))$ has $p^{n+1}\tilde\tau = \mathrm{tg}_{\pi_n}(\xi_n w)$ with $w \in \mathfrak{g} \otimes R_n'$ reducing to $b_i$, and $B_{ki}$ is the $k$-th coordinate of $b_i$;
9. for all $i, j, n$ there is $k \in \mathbb{N}$ with $q_n(E_{ij}) = k$ and $e_{p^{n+1}}(\tau_i(n), \tau_j(n)) = \zeta_n^k$;
10. $a \cdot \tau_j(0) = \sum_k \rho(a)_{kj}\,\tau_k(0)$, and $\rho_L(a)$ is the matrix of $a$ on $\mathfrak{g}$ in the basis $(w_k)$;
11. $q_0$ is surjective with kernel $p\mathcal{O}$, and $\zeta_0 = -1$ if $p = 2$.

So $E$ is the matrix of the Weil pairing on the basis $\tau$, column $i$ of $H$ is the Hodge-Tate period of $\tau_i$, and column $i$ of $B$ is the period of a lift of $\tau_i$ through the thickening by $\xi$.

### The theorem

**Theorem 4.1.7 (Period data exist).** Take the $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ tower. Let $G$ be a commutative group scheme over $\Lambda$, and let $\mathcal{B}$ be a framed group with a bijection $e_R : G_{\mathcal{B}}(R) \to G(R)$ for each local $\Lambda$-algebra $R$, additive and natural in $R$. Assume:

1. $G$ is smooth of relative dimension $g$, and $[p] : G \to G$ is finite and flat;
2. $G_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{O})$ is $p$-divisible, and $\#G_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{O})[p] = p^{2g}$;
3. if $x \in G_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{O})[p]$ and $e_p(x, y) = 1$ for all $y \in G_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{O})[p]$, then $x = 0$.

Let $\mathcal{O}$ be a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-algebra, let $O_0$ be a commutative ring with an involution $\bar{\ }$, and for $a \in O_0$ let $\mathrm{Ad}(a)$ be an endomorphism of $G_{\mathcal{B}}$ over $\mathcal{O}$-algebras with an adjoint $\mathrm{Ad}(a)^*$ for the biextension, that is natural bijections $P(\mathrm{Ad}(a)x, z) \cong P(x, \mathrm{Ad}(a)^* z)$ compatible with the group laws. Assume $\mathrm{Ad}(a)^* = \mathrm{Ad}(\bar a)$, and that $\mathrm{Ad}(1) = 1$, $\mathrm{Ad}(a + b) = \mathrm{Ad}(a) + \mathrm{Ad}(b)$ and $\mathrm{Ad}(ab) = \mathrm{Ad}(a)\mathrm{Ad}(b)$. Then there is a period datum $D$ of $\mathcal{B}$ for $O_e = \mathbb{Z}_p \otimes O_0$, $\sigma = 1 \otimes \bar{\ }$ and $c_p = c$, such that:

1. $P = 0$ and $P_L = 0$;
2. $\mathrm{Ad}(a)$ acts on tangent vectors through $\rho_L(1 \otimes a)$: $\mathrm{Ad}(a)\,\mathrm{tg}(w_l \otimes i) = \mathrm{tg}\bigl(\sum_k w_k \otimes \rho_L(1 \otimes a)_{kl}\, i\bigr)$;
3. $\mathrm{Ad}(a)\,\tau_j(n) = \sum_k \bigl(\rho(1 \otimes a)_{kj} \bmod p^{n+1}\bigr)\tau_k(n)$ for all $j$ and $n$.

*Proof.* The ring $\Lambda$ is local, and $\mathcal{O}$ is a domain. We build the parts of $D$ in order. Choose $p^\flat \in \mathcal{O}^\flat$ with $(p^\flat)^\sharp = p$. The thickening is $W = A_{\mathrm{inf}}/(p^2, [p^\flat])$, a lift of $\mathcal{O}/p = \mathcal{O}^\flat/p^\flat$ with $p^2 = 0$, and its Teichmüller map comes from $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$. Since $G$ is smooth, its group law in coordinates near the origin is a formal group law $F$ of dimension $g$ ([formal group laws](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws)), and this gives the formal chart. Smoothness also gives the lifting property: $G(W) \to G(K)$ is surjective, because the kernel of $W \to K$ is nilpotent. Since $[p]$ is finite and flat, its kernel $G[p]$ is a finite flat group scheme, and its coordinate ring over $\mathcal{O}$ splits into finitely many finite free local factors $A_i$. These represent the $p$-torsion; the construction uses the embedding of $\mathcal{O}$ in the algebraically closed field $C$. The dual-number thickenings of the $R_n$ are built directly. The points $\tau_i(n)$ come from the hypotheses on $p$-divisibility and on the number of $p$-torsion points: a basis of $G(\mathcal{O})[p]$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ is lifted step by step through division by $p$. The action of $\mathrm{Ad}$ on $\mathfrak{g}$ is its differential at the origin, and it is a ring map in $a$. Last, the Tate matrices: $\rho$ is the matrix of $\mathrm{Ad}$ on the $p$-adic Tate module in the basis $\tau$, $E$ is the matrix of the Weil pairings, $H$ and $B$ are the Hodge-Tate periods and the lift periods of the $\tau_i$. The pairing is alternating with zero diagonal, and its determinant is a unit by nondegeneracy (hypothesis 3). The relation $\rho(a)^{\mathsf T}E = E\rho(\sigma a)$ is the adjunction $\mathrm{Ad}(a)^* = \mathrm{Ad}(\bar a)$. The period relation is the identity $c\,E_{ij} = \langle h_j, b_i \rangle - \langle h_i, b_j \rangle$ between the Weil pairing and the two kinds of periods, where $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ is the pairing of $\mathfrak{g}^\vee$ with $\mathfrak{g} \otimes \mathcal{O}$; it is a theorem on the biextension over the thickenings $R_n'$, which we use as given here. The projectors are taken to be $0$, so their conditions hold. The last condition of the datum holds because $\zeta_0 = \theta([\varepsilon^{1/p}]) = \zeta_p$ is $-1$ when $p = 2$. $\square$

**Remark 4.1.8 (Formalization).** The projectors $P$ and $P_L$ of the Hodge-Tate data are always $0$ in this chapter, so their conditions hold trivially. The ring $\mathcal{O}$ of the theorem carries two structures: it is the ring of integers of $v$, and it is the carrier of the $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ tower. The framed group is defined in [SqZeroOfSpan.lean](lean:ReciprocityTheory/DualLattices/SqZeroOfSpan.lean#L56); we have listed its biextension axioms only in outline. The thickening $W$ of item 2 of the period datum is a structure with about fifteen conditions, which we have summarized. The proof cites a chain of results, each a theorem of its own in the development: the existence of the Witt thickening, of the formal chart, of the representing algebras of $G[p]$, of the compatible basis, and of the Tate matrices.

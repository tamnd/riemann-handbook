## Local Galois cohomology and exact local conditions

This section sets up the objects of the chapter: crystalline representations and their jumps, the finite part $H^1_f$ of Galois cohomology, lattices, and *admissible* classes. It then states the *exact local conditions*. They say that a class is admissible exactly when it is in $H^1_f$, or zero, or unramified, according to the coefficients. The main theorem of the section reduces the exact local conditions, in the form that the third-place series uses, to three statements on admissible classes at one prime. Sections 4 and 5 prove these statements.

Fix a prime $\ell$ and an algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$. For a finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, let $G_K$ be its Galois group, $I_K \subseteq G_K$ the subgroup generated by the elements $\sigma$ with $|\sigma(x) - x| < 1$ for all $|x| \le 1$ (the inertia subgroup), and $K_0$ the intersection of $K$ with the maximal unramified extension. We fix *period data* for $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ in the sense of the chapter on period rings, tied to a model as in [the transport of ties](/entry/loc-tied6/). They give for each $K$ a ring $B$ with a Frobenius $\varphi$, a ring $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ with a filtration $(\mathrm{Fil}^a)_{a \in \mathbb{Z}}$, an injection $\iota : B \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, and actions of $G_K$ (see [period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings)). Let $L$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ (the coefficient field), $\mathcal{O}$ the integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ in $L$, and $\lambda \in \mathcal{O}$ an irreducible element.

We say that $(K, L)$ is in *Case A* if $K$ is unramified over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, $\ell \ne 2$, $K \subseteq K_0(\mu_\ell)$, and each $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-embedding of $K_0(\mu_\ell)$ into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ has image in $L$. In Case A we fix the family of embeddings $\tau_i : K_0 \to L$, indexed by $i \in \mathbb{Z}/n$ with $n = [K_0 : \mathbb{Q}_\ell]$, that the Wach data of the period data provide.

**Definition 7.1.1 (Crystalline representation and jumps).** Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $L$-vector space with an $L$-linear action $\rho$ of $G_K$. Put $D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V) = (V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} B)^{G_K}$. Then $V$ is *crystalline* if $\dim_L D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V) = [K_0 : \mathbb{Q}_\ell] \cdot \dim_L V$. For a $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-embedding $\tau : K \to L$, let $D_{\mathrm{dR},\tau}(V)$ be the set of $x \in (V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} B_{\mathrm{dR}})^{G_K}$ with $(1 \otimes c)x = \tau(c)x$ for all $c \in K$, and put $\mathrm{Fil}^a_\tau = D_{\mathrm{dR},\tau}(V) \cap (V \otimes \mathrm{Fil}^a)$. The *multiplicity* of $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ at $\tau$ is
$$m_\tau(V, a) = \dim_L \mathrm{Fil}^a_\tau - \dim_L \mathrm{Fil}^{a+1}_\tau .$$
For a multiset $U$ of integers and $i \in \mathbb{Z}/n$, $V$ *has jumps $U$ at $i$* if $m_\tau(V, a)$ is the multiplicity of $a$ in $U$ for each $a$ and each $\tau$ that restricts to $\tau_i$ on $K_0$.

The integers $a$ with $m_\tau(V,a) > 0$ are the jumps of the Hodge filtration in the $\tau$-component. Up to the sign convention of the filtration on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, they are the Hodge-Tate weights of $V$ (see [crystalline representations](/learn/padic-hodge/#crystalline-and-semistable-representations)).

**Definition 7.1.2 (Finite cocycles and $H^1_f$).** A *continuous cocycle* is a continuous map $c : G_K \to V$ with $c(gh) = c(g) + g\,c(h)$. A coboundary is a map $g \mapsto gx - x$. A cocycle $c$ is *finite* if there is $y \in V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} B$ with $c(g) \otimes 1 = g y - y$ for all $g$. The quotient of the finite cocycles by the coboundaries is $H^1_f(K, V)$.

So a class is finite when it becomes a coboundary after the scalars are extended to $B$, that is, when the extension of the trivial representation by $V$ that it defines is again crystalline. This is the definition of Bloch and Kato (see [local Bloch-Kato theory](/learn/padic-hodge/#local-bloch-kato-theory)).

**Definition 7.1.3 (Lattices and their cohomology).** A *lattice* in $V$ is an $\mathcal{O}$-submodule $T \subseteq V$ that is stable under $G_K$, finitely generated and free over $\mathcal{O}$, and spans $V$ over $L$. The *lattice cocycles* are the continuous cocycles with values in $T$, the lattice coboundaries are the maps $g \mapsto gx - x$ with $x \in T$, and $H^1(K, T)$ is their quotient. The *lattice finite cocycles* are the lattice cocycles that are finite, and $H^1_f(K, T) \subseteq H^1(K, T)$ is their image.

Now we fix the numerical data of a local situation. The letters $i \in \mathbb{Z}/n$ index the embeddings $\tau_i$.

**Definition 7.1.4 (Top data).** *Top data* of rank $r$ consist of functions $h, \eta : \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{N}$, a type $\mathrm{ty}(i) \in \{G, \mathrm{Bd}\}$ and an integer $w_i$ for each letter $i$, an integer $t \ge 1$, and multisets $\mathrm{low}_i$ and $\mathrm{cl}_i$ of integers, with these properties for each $i$. Put $U_i = \mathrm{low}_i + \mathrm{cl}_i$ and $B_i = \{w_i, w_i + 1, \dots, w_i + t - 1\}$.

1. $h_i \ge 2$, $\eta_i \ge 1$, and $U_i$ has $r$ elements.
2. Each element $u$ of $U_i + B_i$ satisfies $1 \le u \le h_i - 1$.
3. If $\mathrm{ty}(i) = G$: the elements of $\mathrm{low}_i + B_i$ are in $[1, \eta_i]$, and the elements of $\mathrm{cl}_i$ are larger than $\eta_i$.
4. If $\mathrm{ty}(i) = \mathrm{Bd}$: the elements of $\mathrm{low}_i + B_i$ are in $[h_i - \eta_i, h_i - 1]$, and the elements of $\mathrm{cl}_i$ are smaller than $h_i - \eta_i$.

Let $\tilde U_i = U_i + B_i$, let $d_i$ be the number of elements of $U_i$ that are smaller than $w_i$, and put $d = \sum_i d_i$. Let
$$s_i = \Big\lfloor \sum_{j \ge 0} \frac{h_{i+j+1}}{\ell^{j+1}} \Big\rfloor .$$
The data satisfy *T1* if $\eta_i + 4 s_i + 6 < \ell$ for all $i$, *T2* if $|u - \beta| > s_i + 2$ for all $u \in \mathrm{low}_i$ and $\beta \in B_i$, and *T3* if for all $u \in \mathrm{cl}_i$ the residue $u$ (type $G$) or $h_i - u$ (type Bd) is not congruent modulo $\ell$ to any integer $y$ with $-2s_i - 2 \le y \le \eta_i + 2s_i + 3$.

**Definition 7.1.5 (Galois data and the standing hypotheses).** *Galois data* consist of $\lambda \in \mathcal{O}$, a representation $V_0$ of $G_K$ over $L$ with a lattice $T_0$, and two characters $\chi_1, \varepsilon : G_K \to \mathcal{O}^\times$. Put $\chi_c = \chi_1 \varepsilon^{-(c-1)}$ for $c \ge 1$. The *standing hypotheses* are: $\ell \ge 5$, $(K, L)$ is in Case A, $n = [K : \mathbb{Q}_\ell]$, the map $i \mapsto \tau_i$ is a bijection onto the embeddings of $K_0$, $\tau_{i+1} \circ \sigma = \tau_i$ for the Frobenius $\sigma$ of $K_0$, and $\lambda$ is irreducible. The Galois data are of *top type* for top data of rank $r$ if $\dim_L V_0 = r$, $V_0$ is crystalline with jumps $U_i$ at each $i$, $\chi_1$ is continuous and $L(\chi_1)$ is crystalline with jumps $\{w_i\}$ at each $i$, and $\varepsilon$ is the cyclotomic character.

For a representation $W$ of $G_K$ on an $\mathcal{O}$-module $M$ and a finite $\mathcal{O}$-module $X$, let $M \otimes X$ carry the twisted action $g(m \otimes x) = \chi_1(g)^{-1} gm \otimes x$, and let $H^1(K, M \otimes X)$ be the locally constant cocycles of this action modulo the coboundaries. For such a cocycle $z$ and $a$ with $\lambda^a X = 0$, the *extension* $E_a(z)$ is $(M \otimes X) \times \mathcal{O}/\lambda^a$ with
$$g(m, y) = \big(g m + \chi_1(g)\, y\, z(g),\ \chi_1(g)\, y\big),$$
where $g m$ is the untwisted action $g \otimes 1$. It is the twist by $\chi_1$ of the extension of $\mathcal{O}/\lambda^a$ by the twisted module that $z$ defines. So it is an extension of $\mathcal{O}/\lambda^a(\chi_1)$ by $M \otimes X$.

**Definition 7.1.6 (Admissible representation and admissible classes).** Fix top data and Galois data. A representation $V$ of $G_K$ with a lattice is *full* if $V$ is crystalline with jumps $\tilde U_i$ at each $i$, and for every lattice $T$ of $V$ the semisimplification of $T/\lambda T$ is that of $T_0/\lambda T_0 \oplus \bigoplus_{c=1}^{t} \mathcal{O}/\lambda(\chi_c)$. A representation $\pi$ of $G_K$ on an $\mathcal{O}$-module $M$ is *admissible* if $M$ is finite and $\pi \cong T/T_2$, compatibly with the actions, where $T_2 \subseteq T$ are lattices in a finite direct sum of full representations. A class in $H^1(K, M \otimes X)$ is *admissible* if for some $a \ge 1$ with $\lambda^a X = 0$ it is the class of a cocycle $z$ with $E_a(z)$ admissible. We write $\mathrm{adm}(M, X)$ for the set of admissible classes.

Let $H = H^1(K, T_0(\chi_1^{-1}))$ and $H_f = H^1_f(K, T_0(\chi_1^{-1}))$. Let $A(X)$ be the space of all maps $G_K \to T_0 \otimes X$ modulo the coboundaries of the twisted action. The *tensor map* $H \otimes X \to A(X)$ sends $[c] \otimes x$ to the class of $g \mapsto c(g) \otimes x$. The *cocycle map* is the injection $H^1(K, T_0 \otimes X) \to A(X)$. The *finite Selmer group* $H^1_f(X)$ is the set of classes whose image in $A(X)$ lies in the image of $H_f \otimes X$.

**Definition 7.1.7 (The three statements at one prime).** Let $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)$ be the classes in $H^1(K, \mathcal{O}(\chi_1) \otimes X)$ of cocycles that vanish on $I_K$. For all $K$ and $L$, all top data with T1, T2 and T3, and all Galois data of top type that satisfy the standing hypotheses, consider:

1. *(Statement at one prime.)* $T_0(\chi_1^{-1}) \otimes \mathcal{O}/\lambda$ has no nonzero invariants and its $H^2$ is zero. $H$ is free of rank $rn$. $H_f$ has a complement in $H$ and has rank $d$. For each finite $X$, the tensor map is injective with the same image as the cocycle map, and $\mathrm{adm}(T_0, X) = H^1_f(X)$.
2. *(Twisted characters.)* For $2 \le c \le t$ and each finite $X$, $\mathrm{adm}(\mathcal{O}(\chi_c), X) = \{0\}$.
3. *(The first character.)* For each finite $X$, $\mathrm{adm}(\mathcal{O}(\chi_1), X) = H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)$.

The third-place series uses the exact local conditions in a cocycle form, with the numerical data written in another way. We state that form.

**Definition 7.1.8 (The top core).** Let $(K, L)$ be in Case A. Fix $t \ge 1$, functions $h, w, \eta : \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{N}$, a type $\mathrm{typ}(i) \in \{\text{true}, \text{false}\}$, multisets $U_i$, a representation $\rho$ of $G_K$ on $V$ with a lattice $T_0$, and characters $\chi_c : G_K \to \mathcal{O}^\times$ for $c \ge 1$. The *top hypotheses* are:

1. $t \ge 1$, and the *position hypotheses*: for each $i$, the elements of $U_i$ are in $[1, h_i - 1]$, $1 \le w_i$, $w_i + t - 1 \le h_i - 1$, $w_i + t - 1 \le \eta_i$ if $\mathrm{typ}(i)$ is true, $h_i - \eta_i \le w_i$ if it is false, $\eta_i + 4s_i + 6 < \ell$, the elements of $U_i$ in the window ($[1, \eta_i]$ if true, $[h_i - \eta_i, h_i - 1]$ if false) are at distance more than $s_i + 2$ from $B_i$, and the elements outside the window avoid the residues of T3;
2. $\rho$ is continuous and crystalline with jumps $U_i$ at each $i$;
3. for $1 \le c \le t$: $\chi_c = \chi_1 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-(c-1)}$ with the cyclotomic character, $\chi_c$ is continuous, $L(\chi_c)$ is crystalline, and its only jump over $\tau_i$ is $w_i + c - 1$, with multiplicity 1.

For a lattice $T$ and finite $X$, let $G_K$ act on $T \otimes X$ by $g \mapsto \chi_1(g)^{-1} (g \otimes 1)$. A *cocycle* is a locally constant $z : G_K \to T \otimes X$ with $z(gh) = z(g) + g z(h)$. It is a coboundary if $z(g) = gm - m$ for some $m$, and *unramified* if this holds for $g \in I_K$. The cocycle is *admissible in the quotient sense* if there is $a_0$ such that for every $a \ge a_0$ with $\lambda^a X = 0$, the module $((T \otimes X) \times \mathcal{O})/(0 \times \lambda^a \mathcal{O})$ with the action $g(p, y) = (gp + y \chi_1(g) z(g), \chi_1(g) y)$ is isomorphic, compatibly with the actions, to $T_1/T_2$ for lattices $T_2 \subseteq T_1$ in a finite product of representations that are continuous, crystalline with jumps $\tilde U_i$ at each $i$, and whose characteristic polynomials are congruent modulo $\lambda$ to $\det(X - \rho(g)) \prod_{c=1}^{t} (X - \chi_c(g))$. The *top core* is the statement: if $\ell \ge 5$, then for all data with the top hypotheses and every finite $X$:

- (a) for every $\rho'$ with $\rho'(g) = \chi_1(g)^{-1} \rho(g)$ and every lattice $T_0'$ of $\rho'$ with the same underlying module as $T_0$, a cocycle $z$ in $T_0 \otimes X$ is admissible if and only if $z(g) = \sum_j c_j(g) \otimes x_j + (gm - m)$ with finitely many lattice finite cocycles $c_j$ of $T_0'$, elements $x_j \in X$ and some $m$;
- (b) for $2 \le c \le t$ and each lattice $T_c$ of $L(\chi_c)$, a cocycle in $T_c \otimes X$ is admissible if and only if it is a coboundary;
- (c) for each lattice $T_1$ of $L(\chi_1)$, a cocycle in $T_1 \otimes X$ is admissible if and only if it is unramified.

**Theorem 7.1.9 (Exact local conditions).** Assume that the period data are tied to a model, and fix Wach data. Assume the three statements at one prime: the statement at one prime, the statement on twisted characters and the statement on the first character. Then for every $(K, L)$ in Case A and every irreducible $\lambda \in \mathcal{O}$, the top core holds for the embeddings $\tau_i$ of the Wach data, the element $\lambda$ and the inertia group $I_K$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.exactLocalConditions_topCore`.

*Proof.* This is [Theorem 7.10.1](/entry/top-core-of-typings/) applied to the tie, the Wach data and the three statements. That theorem builds top data of rank $r$ and Galois data of top type from the top hypotheses, shows that admissibility in the quotient sense is admissibility of the class of $E_a(z)$, and then reads part (a) from statement 1, part (b) from statement 2 and part (c) from statement 3. $\square$

**Remark 7.1.10 (Formalization).** The statement at one prime is called `ErrorExponentStatement` in Lean; it has no error exponent in it. The section uses two notions of admissibility: one by lattice quotients of products of full representations with a congruence of characteristic polynomials ([TableLineDegreeTwo.lean](lean:Formalism/SharpRestriction/LevelLetterCocycles/TableLineDegreeTwo.lean#L93)), and one by sums of full representations with a condition on residual semisimplifications ([MkMemAdmissibleAtIffCocycle.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/MkMemAdmissibleAtIffCocycle.lean#L609)). The standing hypotheses ask $n = [K : \mathbb{Q}_\ell]$ while the embeddings are those of $K_0$; in Case A the two degrees agree. The wrapper sets `maxHeartbeats` to 1000000 for a one-line proof.

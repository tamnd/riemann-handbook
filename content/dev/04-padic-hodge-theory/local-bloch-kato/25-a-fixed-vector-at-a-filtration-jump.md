## A fixed vector at a filtration jump

This section proves the first lemma in the proof of [the theorem on filtration jumps](/entry/jumps-of-m-main/). Let $i$ be a jump of the filtration of a $\varphi$-module $P$ that is compared with a lattice $T$. The lemma gives a vector of $V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ that is fixed by $G_{K_\varpi}$ and lies in $\mathrm{Fil}^i$ but not in $\mathrm{Fil}^{i+1}$. The second lemma of that proof then reads $i$ as a Hodge-Tate jump of $V$. So the lemma is the step that carries a jump from the side of $\varphi$-modules to the side of Galois representations.

We keep the notation of [the section on monodromy and jumps](/entry/jumps-of-m-main/): a prime $\ell$, integers $a, e \ge 1$ with tame descent data, a system of roots $\varpi$ with tame field $K_\varpi$, a finite extension $L$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}$, $E = X^e + \ell$, a $\varphi$-module $P$ of rank $\iota$ with lattice $M$ and filtration $\mathrm{Fil}^i P$, the disc ring $R$ and $M_R$, $N = X \nabla_P$, and the horizontal vectors $\xi(d)$ for constant vectors $d$. As in [the section on the Galois action](/entry/gb-sub-b-mem-fil-zero/), $\mathcal{M}[1/\ell]$ is the divided power lattice with $\ell$ inverted and $\iota_B : \mathcal{M}[1/\ell] \to B_{\mathcal{O}}^\iota$ is the map to crystalline periods. Let $T$ be an $\mathcal{O}$-lattice in an $L$-representation $V$ of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$, $j$ an identification of $P$ with $T$, and $j_B : B_{\mathcal{O}}^\iota \to B_{\mathcal{O}} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}} T$ a crystalline comparison for $(P, j)$ with both of its properties.

**Definition 7.25.1 (The $E$-adic completion).** Let $\widehat{A}$ be the completion of $\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{O}}[1/\ell]$ for the ideal generated by $E$, let $\widehat{E}$ be the image of $E$ in $\widehat{A}$, and let $\widehat{A}[1/\widehat{E}]$ be the localization. There is a subring of $L[[X]]$, with $E$ inverted, that maps to $\widehat{A}[1/\widehat{E}]$; we do not unfold it. For a vector $v$ with entries in $L[[X]]$, let $\widehat{v}$ be the family of images of its entries $v_{\tau, i}$ in $\widehat{A}[1/\widehat{E}]$, where an entry outside the subring is given the value $0$. For $j \in \mathbb{Z}$, a family $w = (w_{\tau, i})$ of elements of $\widehat{A}[1/\widehat{E}]$ *lies in $\widehat{E}^j \widehat{A}$* if there is $N \ge 0$ with $j + N \ge 0$ such that each $\widehat{E}^N w_{\tau, i}$ is the image of $\widehat{E}^{j+N} x$ for some $x \in \widehat{A}$.

The ring $\widehat{A}$ is isomorphic, as an $L$-algebra, to the ring of power series over $L[X]/(E)$. We do not unfold this isomorphism. For $c \in L[X]/(E)$ let $\kappa(c) \in \widehat{A}$ be the image of the constant series $c$, and for a family $c = (c_\tau)$ indexed by $\mathbb{Z}/a$ let $\kappa(c) = (\kappa(c_\tau))_\tau$. So $\kappa$ is a choice of constants in the complete ring $\widehat{A}$.

Fix standard period data for $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, with de Rham ring $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, its subring $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and the filtration $\mathrm{Fil}^j(V \otimes B_{\mathrm{dR}})$. The data give ring maps $c_B : B_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ and $c_D : B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, and the roots $\varpi$ give an $\mathcal{O}$-algebra map $\iota_D$ from the families $(s_\tau)_{\tau \in \mathbb{Z}/a}$ of elements of $\widehat{A}$ to $\mathcal{O} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell} B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. We do not unfold $c_B$, $c_D$ or $\iota_D$.

We use the theorem of Fontaine from [the section on monodromy and jumps](/entry/jumps-of-m-main/) and six further results. We read their statements, not their proofs.

**Theorem 7.25.2 (A map to de Rham periods).** There is an additive map $X : B_{\mathcal{O}} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}} T \to V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ such that $X((o \otimes b) \otimes x) = (o x) \otimes c_B(b)$ for all $o \in \mathcal{O}$, $b \in B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ and $x \in T$.

**Theorem 7.25.3 (Action of de Rham coefficients).** There is an additive map $A$ from $\mathcal{O} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell} B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ to the additive endomorphisms of $V \otimes B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ such that $A(o \otimes b)(v \otimes c) = (o v) \otimes c_D(b)\, c$ for all $o \in \mathcal{O}$, $b \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, $v \in V$ and $c \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}$.

**Theorem 7.25.4 (Equivariance).** Let $X$ be as in the first theorem. Then $X(g b) = g X(b)$ for all $g \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$ and $b \in B_{\mathcal{O}} \otimes T$, where $g$ acts on both factors.

**Theorem 7.25.5 (Constants commute with the Galois action).** Let $A$ be as in the second theorem. For every family $c$ of elements of $L[X]/(E)$, every $g \in G_{K_\varpi}$ and every $y \in V \otimes B_{\mathrm{dR}}$,
$$g\, A\big(\iota_D \kappa(c)\big)(y) = A\big(\iota_D \kappa(c)\big)(g y).$$

**Theorem 7.25.6 (A horizontal vector at a jump).** Let $\ell \ne 2$, and assume $E N(M) \subseteq M_R$. Let $i$ be a jump of $P$. Then there are $n \ge 0$, constant vectors $d_1, \dots, d_n$ and families $c_1, \dots, c_n$ of elements of $L[X]/(E)$ such that $\xi(d_k) \in \mathcal{M}[1/\ell]$ for every $k$, and the family
$$w = \sum_{k=1}^n \kappa(c_k)\, \widehat{\xi(d_k)}$$
lies in $\widehat{E}^i \widehat{A}$ but not in $\widehat{E}^{i+1} \widehat{A}$.

**Theorem 7.25.7 (Comparison of filtrations).** Assume the theorem of Fontaine. Let $\ell \ne 2$, and let $X$ and $A$ be as in the first two theorems. Let $n \ge 0$, let $s_1, \dots, s_n$ be families of elements of $\widehat{A}$, let $m_1, \dots, m_n \in \mathcal{M}[1/\ell]$, and let $j \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then $\sum_k s_k\, \widehat{m}_k$ lies in $\widehat{E}^j \widehat{A}$ if and only if
$$\sum_{k=1}^n A\big(\iota_D(s_k)\big)\Big(X\big(j_B(\iota_B m_k)\big)\Big) \in \mathrm{Fil}^j(V \otimes B_{\mathrm{dR}}).$$

The last theorem says that, on vectors that come from $\mathcal{M}[1/\ell]$, the $E$-adic order on the side of $\varphi$-modules equals the order for the filtration of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ after the comparison $j_B$. This is the classical picture in which $E$ goes to a generator of the kernel of $\theta : B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ \to \mathbb{C}_\ell$, so that powers of $E$ match the filtration of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$.

**Theorem 7.25.8 (A fixed vector at a filtration jump).** Assume the theorem of Fontaine. Let $\ell \ne 2$, let $P$, $T$, $j$ and $j_B$ be as above for standard period data, and assume $E N(M) \subseteq M_R$. Let $i$ be a jump of $P$. Then there is $x \in V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ such that:

1. $g x = x$ for every $g \in G_{K_\varpi}$;
2. $x \in \mathrm{Fil}^i(V \otimes B_{\mathrm{dR}})$;
3. $x \notin \mathrm{Fil}^{i+1}(V \otimes B_{\mathrm{dR}})$.

*Proof.* Fix the maps $X$ and $A$ of the first two theorems. By the theorem on horizontal vectors at a jump, which uses $\ell \ne 2$ and the hypothesis on $E N$, there are $n$, $d_1, \dots, d_n$ and $c_1, \dots, c_n$ with $m_k = \xi(d_k) \in \mathcal{M}[1/\ell]$ such that $w = \sum_k \kappa(c_k)\, \widehat{m}_k$ lies in $\widehat{E}^i \widehat{A}$ and not in $\widehat{E}^{i+1} \widehat{A}$. Put
$$x = \sum_{k=1}^n A\big(\iota_D \kappa(c_k)\big)\Big(X\big(j_B(\iota_B m_k)\big)\Big).$$

Apply the comparison of filtrations with $s_k = \kappa(c_k)$. With $j = i$ it turns $w \in \widehat{E}^i \widehat{A}$ into $x \in \mathrm{Fil}^i$; this is part 2. With $j = i + 1$, if $x$ were in $\mathrm{Fil}^{i+1}$, then $w$ would lie in $\widehat{E}^{i+1} \widehat{A}$, which is false; this is part 3.

For part 1, let $g \in G_{K_\varpi}$. The action of $g$ on $V \otimes B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is additive, so it is enough to show that $g$ fixes each term. Fix $k$ and put $u_k = j_B(\iota_B m_k)$. Then
$$\begin{aligned} g\, A\big(\iota_D \kappa(c_k)\big)\big(X(u_k)\big) &= A\big(\iota_D \kappa(c_k)\big)\big(g X(u_k)\big) \\ &= A\big(\iota_D \kappa(c_k)\big)\big(X(g u_k)\big) \\ &= A\big(\iota_D \kappa(c_k)\big)\big(X(u_k)\big). \end{aligned}$$
The first equality is the theorem on constants, since $g \in G_{K_\varpi}$. The second is the equivariance of $X$. The third is the horizontality of $j_B$: since $\xi(d_k) \in \mathcal{M}[1/\ell]$ and $g \in G_{K_\varpi}$, the element $u_k = j_B(\iota_B \xi(d_k))$ is fixed by $g$. $\square$

The proof does not use the identification $j$ directly. It enters only through the first property of the crystalline comparison, which the comparison of filtrations needs. The hypothesis on $E N$ is only used to find the horizontal vectors; [the theorem on monodromy and jumps](/entry/enstable-and-jumps/) shows that it holds for every crystalline comparison.

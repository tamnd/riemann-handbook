## The top core from the three statements

This section proves the top core of Section 1 from the three statements at one prime. The top core is stated with lattice quotients and characteristic polynomials, while the three statements are stated with the admissible classes of a local situation. So the proof first builds a local situation from the top hypotheses, and then translates each part of the top core into a statement on admissible classes. The result is the proof of [the exact local conditions](/entry/exact-local-conditions-top-core/).

We keep the notation of Sections 1 and 4. Fix a prime $\ell$, period data for $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ with $A_{\inf}$-data and a tie to a model, and Wach data. For $(K, L)$ in Case A the Wach data give the embeddings $\tau_i : K_0 \to L$, $i \in \mathbb{Z}/n$. A *local situation* consists of $(K, L)$, a rank $r$, the embeddings $\tau_i$, top data of rank $r$ and Galois data $(\lambda, T_0, \chi_1, \varepsilon)$ that satisfy the standing hypotheses, as in Section 1.

**Theorem 7.10.1 (The top core from the three statements).** Assume that the period data are tied to a model, and fix Wach data. Assume the statement at one prime, the statement on twisted characters and the statement on the first character. Let $(K, L)$ be in Case A and let $\lambda \in \mathcal{O}$ be irreducible. Then the top core holds for the embeddings $\tau_i$ of the Wach data at $(K, L)$, the element $\lambda$ and the inertia group $I_K$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.TopDict.topCore_of_typings`.

*Proof.* Let $\ell \ge 5$, and fix data $t$, $h$, $w$, $\eta$, $\mathrm{typ}$, $U_i$, $\rho$ on $V$ with lattice $T_0$, and characters $\chi_c$ that satisfy the top hypotheses, and a finite $\mathcal{O}$-module $X$. Let $T$ be the lattice $T_0$ in the representation $\rho$.

*Step 1: a local situation.* Since $V$ is crystalline, the tie gives that the jumps of $V$ at each embedding, counted with multiplicity, are $\dim_L V$ in number. With this count, the top hypotheses give a rank $r$, top data of rank $r$ with the same $t$, and the cyclotomic character $\varepsilon$, such that the Galois data $(\lambda, T, \chi_1, \varepsilon)$ are of top type, the standing hypotheses hold, and the characters $\chi_1 \varepsilon^{-(c-1)}$ of the Galois data agree with the given $\chi_c$ for $1 \le c \le t$. They also give a count of the jumps $\tilde U_i$ that the next step uses. Let $S$ be the local situation formed by these data.

*Step 2: admissibility of extensions.* Since $\mathcal{O}$ is a complete discrete valuation ring, $\lambda$ is not a unit, and $X$ is finite, there is $a$ with $\lambda^a X = 0$. The admissible representations of $S$ are closed under submodules, quotients and products. With the crystalline calculus that the tie gives, the basic facts of Section 4 hold for $T_0$ and for $\mathcal{O}(\chi_c)$ for $1 \le c \le t$. The characteristic polynomial $\det(X - \rho(g)) \prod_{c=1}^{t} (X - \chi_c(g))$ of the top core is the one that the Galois data of $S$ give, since the characters agree. So, by the count of Step 1, a cocycle $z$ is admissible in the quotient sense if and only if there is $a_0$ such that for every $a \ge a_0$ with $\lambda^a X = 0$ the extension $E_a(z)$ of Section 1 is an admissible representation of $S$. The quotient module of the top core is $E_a(z)$, written as a quotient of $(T \otimes X) \times \mathcal{O}$.

*Step 3: change of coefficients.* Let $e : M \to M'$ be an isomorphism of $\mathcal{O}$-modules that commutes with two actions $W$ and $W'$ of $G_K$, and let $z' = (e \otimes 1) \circ z$. Then $e \otimes 1$ maps cocycles to cocycles and coboundaries to coboundaries, and it gives an isomorphism of $E_a(z)$ with $E_a(z')$ that commutes with the actions. So $E_a(z)$ is admissible if and only if $E_a(z')$ is admissible.

*Part (a).* Let $\rho'(g) = \chi_1(g)^{-1} \rho(g)$, with a lattice $T_0'$ on the module of $T_0$, and let $z$ be a cocycle in $T_0 \otimes X$ for the twisted action. It is a cocycle for the twisted coefficients of Section 1. By Step 2 and the basic facts for $T_0$, $z$ is admissible in the quotient sense if and only if its class is in $\mathrm{adm}(T_0, X)$. The statement at one prime gives $\mathrm{adm}(T_0, X) = H^1_f(X)$. Its class is in $H^1_f(X)$ if and only if $z(g) = \sum_j c_j(g) \otimes x_j + (gm - m)$ with lattice finite cocycles $c_j$ of $T_0'$ and some $m$: this is the definition of $H^1_f(X)$, by the image of $H_f \otimes X$ in the space $A(X)$, written for cocycles.

*Part (b).* Let $2 \le c \le t$ and let $T_c$ be a lattice of $L(\chi_c)$. Since $\chi_c$ takes values in $\mathcal{O}^\times$, there is an isomorphism $e : \mathcal{O}(\chi_c) \to T_c$ that commutes with the actions. Let $z$ be a cocycle in $T_c \otimes X$ and $z_0 = (e \otimes 1)^{-1} \circ z$. By Steps 2 and 3, $z$ is admissible in the quotient sense if and only if the class of $z_0$ is in $\mathrm{adm}(\mathcal{O}(\chi_c), X)$. This set is $\{0\}$ by the statement on twisted characters. So $z$ is admissible if and only if $z_0$ is a coboundary, that is, $z_0(g) = gm - m$ for some $m$. Since $e$ commutes with the actions, this holds if and only if $z$ is a coboundary.

*Part (c).* Let $T_1$ be a lattice of $L(\chi_1)$ and $z$ a cocycle in $T_1 \otimes X$. As in part (b), $z$ is admissible in the quotient sense if and only if the class of $z_0$ is in $\mathrm{adm}(\mathcal{O}(\chi_1), X)$, which is $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)$ by the statement on the first character. A class is in $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(X)$ if and only if some cocycle $z_0$ in it satisfies $z_0(g) = g b - b$ for some $b$ and all $g \in I_K$: if $z_0' = z_0 + (g \mapsto g b' - b')$ vanishes on $I_K$, then $z_0(g) = g(-b') - (-b')$ on $I_K$, and conversely. Transport by $e$ gives that $z$ is admissible if and only if it is unramified. $\square$

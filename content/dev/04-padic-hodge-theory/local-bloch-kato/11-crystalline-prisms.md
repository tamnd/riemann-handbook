## Crystalline prisms for the Breuil-Kisin prism

Sections 11 to 16 prove Theorem T: a surjection of potentially crystalline lattices of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$ is surjective on finite cocycles up to an explicit power of $\ell$. The proof goes through Breuil-Kisin modules over a tame field. This section builds the rings on which the Galois action on a Breuil-Kisin module is computed. They are two prisms of divided power series: one for the self-product of the Breuil-Kisin prism, and one for its diagonal. Each Galois element gives a map from the first into $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, and the theorem gives this map as a divided power series. The result is used in the next section, to build an integral stratification and the Taylor formula of a Breuil-Kisin module.

Fix a prime $\ell$ and integers $a, e \ge 1$ with $e + 1 = \ell^a$. Let $W = W(\mathbb{F}_{\ell^a})$, with Frobenius $\sigma$. A *system of roots* $\varpi$ is a sequence $(\varpi_n)_{n \ge 0}$ in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ with $\varpi_0^e = -\ell$ and $\varpi_{n+1}^\ell = \varpi_n$. The *tame field* $K_\varpi$ is the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ of degree $a$ with $\varpi_0$ adjoined. The sequence defines an element $\varpi^\flat$ of the tilt, with Teichmüller lift $[\varpi^\flat] \in A_{\inf}$.

**Definition 7.11.1 ($\delta$-ring).** A *$\delta$-structure* on a commutative ring $A$ is a map $\delta : A \to A$ with $\delta(1) = 0$,
$$\delta(ab) = a^\ell \delta(b) + b^\ell \delta(a) + \ell\, \delta(a)\delta(b), \qquad \delta(a + b) = \delta(a) + \delta(b) - \sum_{0 < k < \ell} \frac{1}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k} a^k b^{\ell - k},$$
together with a ring map $\varphi : A \to A$ with $\varphi(a) = a^\ell + \ell\,\delta(a)$.

If $A$ has no $\ell$-torsion, a ring map $\varphi$ with $\varphi(a) \equiv a^\ell \pmod{\ell A}$ for all $a$ gives a unique $\delta$-structure, with $\delta(a) = (\varphi(a) - a^\ell)/\ell$. All $\delta$-structures of this section arise in this way.

**Definition 7.11.2 (Prism).** Let $R$ be a ring. A *prism over $R$* is a ring $A$ with a $\delta$-structure and an ideal $I$ such that: $I$ is an invertible $A$-module; $A$ is complete for the ideal $(\ell) + I$; $\ell \in I + \varphi(I)A$; the $\ell$-power torsion of $A/I$ is killed by a fixed power of $\ell$; and a ring map $R \to A/I$ is given. A *map of prisms* $f : (A, I) \to (A', I')$ is a ring map that commutes with $\delta$, maps $I$ into $I'$, and respects the maps from $R$: if $a \bmod I$ is the image of $r \in R$, then $f(a) \bmod I'$ is the image of $r$.

This is the notion of Bhatt and Scholze, with $R$ in the role of the base. Two prisms over $\mathcal{O}_{K_\varpi}$ appear here. The *Breuil-Kisin prism* is $\mathfrak{S} = W[[X]]$ with $\varphi = \sigma$ on coefficients and $\varphi(X) = X^\ell$, the ideal $(E)$ with $E = X^e + \ell$, and the map $\mathcal{O}_{K_\varpi} \to W[[X]]/(E)$ that sends the class of $X$ to $\varpi_0$. The *$A_{\inf}$-prism* of $\mathcal{O}_{K_\varpi}$ is $A_{\inf}$ with its Frobenius and the kernel of $\theta$. There is a map of prisms $\mathfrak{S} \to A_{\inf}$ with $X \mapsto [\varpi^\flat]$, and each $g \in G_{K_\varpi}$ acts on $A_{\inf}$ by a map of prisms.

Now fix a ring $\mathcal{O}$, free of finite rank over $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, a normed field $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ with an ultrametric norm whose unit ball is $\mathcal{O}$, and an algebra map $t : W \to \mathcal{O}$. For $\tau \in \mathbb{Z}/a$ put $t_\tau = t \circ \sigma^{-\tau}$. Let $\mathrm{frob}$ be the map $X \mapsto X^\ell$ on $K[[X]]$.

**Definition 7.11.3 (Divided power series rings).** Let $\mathcal{S}_d$ be the subring of $\prod_{\tau \in \mathbb{Z}/a} K[[X]]$ generated by the tuples $F = (F_\tau)$ for which there are $w_n \in W$, with $w_n \to 0$ $\ell$-adically, such that
$$\lfloor n/e \rfloor !\; [X^n] F_\tau = t_\tau(w_n) \quad \text{for all } \tau, n.$$
Let $\mathcal{P}_d$ be the subring of $\prod_\tau K[[X]][[Y]]$ generated by the tuples with $i!\, \lfloor n/e \rfloor !\, [Y^i X^n] F_\tau = t_\tau(w_{i,n})$ for some $w_{i,n} \to 0$. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the ring of series $\sum c_k X^k \in K[[X]]$ with $|c_k| \cdot |\lfloor k/e \rfloor !| \le 1$ and $c_k \lfloor k/e \rfloor ! \to 0$.

The factor $\lfloor n/e \rfloor !$ makes these rings contain the divided powers $E^k/k!$ of $E$, since $X^{ek}/k!$ has the right size. So $\mathcal{S}_d$ is a divided power envelope of $(E)$ in $\mathfrak{S} \otimes K$. The ring $\mathcal{P}_d$ does the same in two variables: the second variable $Y$ is the difference of the two coordinates of the self-product, and $i!$ divides it.

**Theorem 7.11.4 (Crystalline prisms).** Let $\ell \ne 2$, and let $a$, $e$, $\varpi$, $\mathcal{O}$, $K$ and $t$ be as above, with tame descent data for $(K, \ell, e, a)$. Let $\mathrm{sh} : K[[X]] \to K[[X]][[Y]]$ be a ring map with $i!\, [Y^i]\, \mathrm{sh}(f) = f^{(i)}$ for all $f$ and $i$, and let $\varphi_2$ be a ring endomorphism of $K[[X]][[Y]]$ with $\varphi_2(f) = \mathrm{frob}(f)$ and $\varphi_2(\mathrm{sh} f) = \mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{frob} f)$ for $f \in K[[X]]$. Then there are prisms $P$ and $S_0$ over $\mathcal{O}_{K_\varpi}$, maps of prisms $p_0, p_1 : \mathfrak{S} \to P$ and $\delta : P \to S_0$, and ring maps $j_\tau : P \to K[[X]][[Y]]$ and $j_{0,\tau} : S_0 \to K[[X]]$ for $\tau \in \mathbb{Z}/a$, such that:

1. the ideal of $P$ is $(\ell)$, and $\delta p_0 = \delta p_1$;
2. $j_{0,\tau}(\delta y)$ is the constant term in $Y$ of $j_\tau(y)$;
3. $j_\tau(p_0 x) = \mathrm{ac}_\tau(\varphi x)$ and $j_\tau(p_1 x) = \mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{ac}_\tau(\varphi x))$ for $x \in \mathfrak{S}$, where $\mathrm{ac} : \mathfrak{S} \to \prod_\tau K[[X]]$ is the coefficient map;
4. $j_\tau(\varphi y) = \varphi_2(j_{\tau - 1}(y))$;
5. $i!\, [Y^i]\, j_\tau(y)$ and $X^i \cdot i!\, [Y^i]\, j_\tau(y)$ lie in $\mathcal{S}$ for all $\tau$, $y$ and $i$;
6. let $C$ be a prism over $\mathcal{O}_{K_\varpi}$ with ideal $(\ell)$ and a ring isomorphism $e_C : C \to A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ that carries the Frobenius of $C$ to that of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, and let $h : A_{\inf} \to C$ be a map of prisms with $e_C \circ h = \iota \circ \varphi$ for the inclusion $\iota : A_{\inf} \to A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. Then for each $g \in G_{K_\varpi}$ there is a map of prisms $\mathrm{ev} : P \to C$ with $\mathrm{ev} \circ p_0 = h \circ i_\varpi$ and $\mathrm{ev} \circ p_1 = h \circ g \circ i_\varpi$, for the map $i_\varpi : \mathfrak{S} \to A_{\inf}$, and such that for each $y \in P$ and $r \ge 0$ there is $n_0$ with
$$\ell^r \ \Big|\ 1 \otimes e_C(\mathrm{ev}\, y) - \sum_{i < n} \gamma_i(\mu(g)) \cdot c\big(X^i\, i!\, [Y^i]\, j(y)\big) \quad \text{in } \mathcal{O} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell} A_{\mathrm{cris}}$$
for all $n \ge n_0$. Here $\mu(g) = [\varepsilon_g] - 1 \in A_{\inf}$, where $\varepsilon_g$ is the element of the tilt given by the Kummer cocycle of $g$ on the system $\varpi$. Since $g$ fixes $\varpi_0$, $\theta(\mu(g)) = 0$, so $\mu(g)$ has divided powers $\gamma_i(\mu(g))$ in $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, with $i!\,\gamma_i(\mu(g)) = \mu(g)^i$. Finally, $c : \prod_\tau \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{O} \otimes A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ is the comparison map.

So $P$ plays the role of the self-product $\mathfrak{S} \times \mathfrak{S}$ in the prismatic site, with its two projections $p_0$ and $p_1$, and $\delta$ is the diagonal. Part 6 says that a pair of points of $\mathfrak{S}$ in $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ that differ by $g$ factors through $P$, and that the factorization is given by a Taylor series in $\mu(g)$.

*Proof.* Take $P = \mathcal{P}_d$ and $S_0 = \mathcal{S}_d$.

*$\delta$-structures.* Both rings have no $\ell$-torsion, since $K$ has characteristic 0. Every element of $\mathcal{S}_d$ lies in the generating set, so $\mathcal{S}_d$ is that set. The map $F \mapsto (\mathrm{frob}\, F_{\tau - 1})_\tau$ maps this set into itself, and $\mathrm{frob}(F) = F^\ell + \ell B$ with $B$ in the set. The same holds on $\mathcal{P}_d$ for the map $F \mapsto (\varphi_2 F_{\tau - 1})_\tau$, by the two conditions on $\varphi_2$. So both maps are lifts of Frobenius on rings with no $\ell$-torsion, and they give $\delta$-structures.

*Prisms.* Both rings are $\ell$-adically complete. Each is a prism with ideal $(\ell)$: the element $\ell$ is not a zero divisor, $\delta(\ell)$ is a unit, and $A/(\ell)$ has bounded $\ell$-power torsion. For the map from $\mathcal{O}_{K_\varpi}$, we use that $\varphi(E)$ lies in $\ell \mathcal{S}_d$: its image under the coefficient map is $\ell B$ with $B$ in the generating set. Then $x \mapsto \mathrm{ac}(\varphi x) \bmod \ell$ kills $E$, and it gives a map $\mathfrak{S}/(E) \to \mathcal{S}_d/(\ell)$. Composed with $\mathcal{O}_{K_\varpi} \to \mathfrak{S}/(E)$, it is the structure map of $S_0$. The same construction, through constants in $Y$, gives the structure map of $P$.

*The maps.* Let $p_0(x)$ be $\mathrm{ac}(\varphi x)$, seen as constants in $Y$, and let $p_1(x) = \mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{ac}(\varphi x))$. Both lie in $\mathcal{P}_d$, and $p_1(x) - p_0(x) = \ell B$ with $B$ in the generating set; for $p_1$ this uses $i!\, [Y^i]\, \mathrm{sh}(f) = f^{(i)}$. Both maps commute with $\varphi$, by the conditions on $\varphi_2$ and since $\mathrm{frob} \circ \mathrm{ac} = \mathrm{ac} \circ \varphi$ up to the shift of $\tau$, so they commute with $\delta$. They map $(E)$ into $(\ell)$, and $p_1$ respects the structure maps since $p_1(x) \equiv p_0(x) \pmod \ell$. The map $\delta$ takes the constant term in $Y$; it commutes with $\varphi$ because the constant term of $\varphi_2(F)$ is $\mathrm{frob}$ of the constant term of $F$. Then $\delta p_0 = \delta p_1$ since $\mathrm{sh}(f)$ has constant term $f$. Parts 1 to 4 follow, with $j_\tau$ and $j_{0,\tau}$ the projections to the $\tau$ entry. Part 5 holds for every element of the generating set of $\mathcal{P}_d$, and $X$ lies in $\mathcal{S}$.

*Part 6.* We use the following evaluation lemma: for each $g \in G_{K_\varpi}$ there is a ring map $E_g : \mathcal{P}_d \to A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ with $E_g(p_0 x) = \iota(\varphi(i_\varpi x))$ and $E_g(p_1 x) = \iota(\varphi(g\, i_\varpi x))$, which commutes with the Frobenius maps, and whose values satisfy the congruence of part 6 with $1 \otimes E_g(y)$ in place of $1 \otimes e_C(\mathrm{ev}\, y)$. Put $\mathrm{ev} = e_C^{-1} \circ E_g$. It commutes with $\delta$, since it commutes with $\varphi$ and $C$ has no $\ell$-torsion (as $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ has none). It maps $(\ell)$ into $(\ell)$, the ideal of $C$. By the hypothesis $e_C \circ h = \iota \circ \varphi$, we get $\mathrm{ev} \circ p_0 = h \circ i_\varpi$ and $\mathrm{ev} \circ p_1 = h \circ g \circ i_\varpi$. Then $\mathrm{ev}$ respects the structure maps, since $h \circ i_\varpi$ does and $\mathrm{ev}$ maps $(\ell)$ into the ideal of $C$. The congruence is the one of the lemma, since $e_C \circ \mathrm{ev} = E_g$. $\square$

**Remark 7.11.5 (Formalization).** The hypothesis $\ell \ne 2$ is introduced as `_` and is not used ([ExistsCrystallinePrisms.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/InertiaEigenvalues/ExistsCrystallinePrisms.lean#L29)). The proof sets `maxHeartbeats` to 4000000 (line 26). The tame descent data enter only as an instance argument. Each step of the proof is a lemma imported from a "Statement" file, and we did not open their proofs: the membership of $\varphi(E)$ in $\ell \mathcal{S}_d$, the completeness of $\mathcal{S}_d$ and $\mathcal{P}_d$, and the evaluation map of part 6. We do not unfold the coefficient map $\mathrm{ac}$, the comparison map $c$ or the Kummer element $\varepsilon_g$. The map $\mathrm{ev}$ is $e_C^{-1}$ composed with a map into $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ that does not depend on $h$; the map $h$ enters only through the hypothesis $e_C \circ h = \iota \circ \varphi$. The second name is a one-line wrapper with `maxHeartbeats` 1000000.

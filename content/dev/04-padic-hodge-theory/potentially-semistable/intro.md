This chapter is about integral $p$-adic Hodge theory at the primes above $\ell$, about the global bounds that it feeds, and about the $p$-adic monodromy theorem. Sections 01 and 02 prove a bound for the congruence length of a point of a Hecke algebra: the length is at most the length of a Bloch-Kato [[Selmer group|36YNSTESMQ]], plus [[Tate-Shafarevich group|BK9WBMF56G]] terms, the $\ell$-part of the class number, and local terms at the places above $\ell$. The local terms come from Wach lattices. Sections 03 to 06 build the parameters of the Wach ring over a tame subextension of the cyclotomic tower, the Herr complex of a Wach module, the descent of Herr cocycles to crystalline classes, the torsion of the defect of a short exact sequence of Wach lattices, and an explicit power of $\ell$ that carries a crystalline extension class into $H^1_f$. Section 07 assembles the twisted torsion class theorem in the good case, from local Bloch-Kato duality, Tate duality and the Euler characteristic formula.

Sections 08 to 12 supply the inputs of that theorem: the image of the dual exponential map, the passage from a coboundary over $A_{\inf}$ to a crystalline cocycle, the torsion of cocycles with coefficients in $A_{\inf}$, the local Bloch-Kato package with its lifting constants, and the comparison of Selmer lengths at good points. Sections 13 and 14 prove the integrality theorem of Bhatt and Scholze: crystalline lattices come from prismatic F-crystals.

Sections 15 to 27 prove that de Rham representations are potentially semistable. Section 15 reduces the theorem to a statement about matrices. Sections 16 to 20 construct eigenvectors of Frobenius in $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^+$ with prescribed residues and prove the lattice lemma, and section 21 lowers the weights of a framed representation by weight modifications. Sections 22 to 25 give the remaining inputs: Gauss norms on $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, the Euler characteristic of rational representations, Hyodo's theorem, and the passage through extensions and split representations. Sections 26 and 27 treat the case of weight zero: a bound on the ramification of towers of subgroups gives Sen's theorem that a representation trivial over $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ has finite image of inertia. Section 28, which stands apart, computes the Harish-Chandra restriction of a product of cuspidal characters of finite general linear groups, and ends with the notes and references of the chapter.

The chapter needs from Volume 1 [local fields and their cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/#local-fields), [Selmer groups](/learn/galois-cohomology/#selmer-and-tate-shafarevich-groups), [global class field theory](/learn/class-field-theory/#global-class-field-theory), [Wach lattices](/learn/padic-hodge/#lattices-wach-breuil-kisin-and-prismatic-f-crystals), [crystalline representations](/learn/padic-hodge/#crystalline-and-semistable-representations), [potentially semistable representations](/learn/padic-hodge/#potentially-semistable-representations) and [local Bloch-Kato theory](/learn/padic-hodge/#local-bloch-kato-theory). Its results are used in the step [EveryCharacter from theta integrals and local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/) of [Theta integrals and local matching](/proof/theta-matching/): the Selmer bound at a point, the clauses on Wach lattices and the twisted torsion class theorem are inputs of that step. The monodromy theorem and the restriction formula of section 28 are used by the trunk step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/).

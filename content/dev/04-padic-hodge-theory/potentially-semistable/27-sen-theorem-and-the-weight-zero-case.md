## Sen's theorem and the weight zero case

This section proves step 3 of the matrix form of the monodromy theorem, the weight zero case of section 21: an $r$-rational representation $\rho : G_K \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_\ell^{\mathrm{ur}})$ that is framed with weights $0$ is semistable admissible. The key input is Sen's theorem: if a continuous representation $G_K \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ becomes trivial over $\mathbb{C}_\ell$, then its inertia group has finite image (S. Sen, *Continuous cohomology and $p$-adic Galois representations*, Invent. Math. 62 (1980), 89 to 116). We prove Sen's theorem from [the result on detected towers](/entry/no-detected-subgroup-tower-rat-proof/) of section 26, applied to the congruence filtration of the image of $\rho$. The weight zero case then follows: the frame of weight $0$, reduced by the map $\theta : \mathrm{Fil}^0 \to \mathbb{C}_\ell$, makes the restriction of scalars of $\rho$ trivial over $\mathbb{C}_\ell$, and a representation with finite image of inertia is semistable admissible after a finite extension.

We keep the notation of sections 15 and 21: $\Omega$ is the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ its completion, $K$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ in $\Omega$ and $G_K \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$ its Galois group, $\sigma$ the Frobenius of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}$, $\rho_m(g)$ the matrix $\sigma^m(\rho(g))$ with entries in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, and $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathrm{Fil}^0)$ the matrices $U$ such that $U$ and $U^{-1}$ have entries in $\mathrm{Fil}^0$. An element $g \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$ acts on $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ by continuity, and on matrices entrywise. We use the [local fields](/learn/galois-cohomology/#local-fields) background and the [potentially semistable representations](/learn/padic-hodge/#potentially-semistable-representations) page.

### Inertia

**Definition 6.27.1 (Inertia subgroup).** The *inertia subgroup* $I_K \subseteq G_K$ is the subgroup generated by the $g \in G_K$ with $|g(x) - x| < 1$ for all $x \in \Omega$ with $|x| \leq 1$.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.Loc.IK`.

These are the elements that act trivially on the residue field of $\Omega$.

**Lemma 6.27.2 (Inertia elements).** Every $g \in I_K$ satisfies $|g(x) - x| < 1$ for all $x \in \Omega$ with $|x| \leq 1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PMon.Ram.norm_sub_lt_one_of_mem_inertia`.

*Proof.* We show that the set of $g$ with this property is a subgroup; it contains the generators. It contains $1$. Let $g, h$ have the property and $|x| \leq 1$. Galois elements preserve the norm, so $|h(x)| \leq 1$, and
$$gh(x) - x = \big(g(h(x)) - h(x)\big) + \big(h(x) - x\big)$$
has norm $< 1$ by the ultrametric inequality. For the inverse, $|g^{-1}(x)| \leq 1$, and $g^{-1}(x) - x = -\big(g(g^{-1}(x)) - g^{-1}(x)\big)$ has norm $< 1$. $\square$

### No detected towers over $K$

**Definition 6.27.3 (No towers over $K$).** The statement *no towers over $K$* says the following. Let $\ell$ be a prime, $K$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, and $N_0 \supseteq N_1 \supseteq \cdots$ open subgroups of $G_K$ with:

1. $g^\ell \in N_{k+1}$ for every $k$ and $g \in N_k$;
2. for every $j$ and $g \in N_0$, $g^\ell \in N_{j+1}$ implies $g \in N_j$;
3. for every $r \geq 0$ there is $k_0$ such that for every $k \geq k_0$ there are finitely many $x_i \in \mathbb{C}_\ell$ and maps $a_i : G_K \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ with $|g(x_i) - x_i - a_i(g)| \leq \ell^{-r}$ for all $i$ and all $g \in N_k$, and such that every $g \in N_k$ with $\ell^r \mid a_i(g)$ for all $i$ lies in $N_{k+r}$.

Then there is $k$ with $I_K \cap N_k \subseteq N_j$ for every $j$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PMon.Ram.NoTower`.

This is the statement of section 26 with $G_K$ in place of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$, with $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ in place of $\Omega$, and with $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-valued maps $a_i$. To pass from one to the other we use the following fact, proved elsewhere in this development.

1. (Approximation.) Let $r \geq 0$, $S \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$, $x \in \mathbb{C}_\ell$ and $a : G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ with $|g(x) - x - a(g)| \leq \ell^{-r}$ for $g \in S$. Then there are $y \in \Omega$ and $b : G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} \to \mathbb{Z}$ with $|g(y) - y - b(g)| \leq \ell^{-r}$ for $g \in S$, and such that $\ell^r \mid b(g)$ implies $\ell^r \mid a(g)$ for every $g$.

The element $y$ is an algebraic approximation of $x$, and $b(g)$ is an integer close to $a(g)$; since $\Omega$ is dense in $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ and $\mathbb{Z}$ is dense in $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, the bound survives.

**Theorem 6.27.4 (No detected towers over $K$).** The statement *no towers over $K$* holds.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PMon.Ram.noTower`.

*Proof.* Let $N_k$ be as in the definition. Since $K / \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is finite, $G_K$ is open in $G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$, so the $N_k$ are open subgroups of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$. We check the hypotheses of [the theorem on towers over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$](/entry/no-detected-subgroup-tower-rat-proof/) for $(N_k)$ viewed in $G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$. The sequence decreases. Hypothesis 1 is the same. For hypothesis 2, let $g \in N_0$ with $g^\ell \in N_{j+1}$; then $g \in G_K$ and hypothesis 2 over $K$ applies.

For hypothesis 3, let $r \geq 0$, take $k_0$ from 3 over $K$, and let $k \geq k_0$ with data $x_i$, $a_i$. Extend each $a_i$ by $0$ outside $G_K$. Approximation with $S = N_k$ gives $y_i \in \Omega$ and $b_i : G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} \to \mathbb{Z}$ with $|g(y_i) - y_i - b_i(g)| \leq \ell^{-r}$ on $N_k$. If $g \in N_k$ and $\ell^r \mid b_i(g)$ for all $i$, then $\ell^r \mid a_i(g)$ for all $i$, so $g \in N_{k+r}$.

The theorem over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ gives $k$ such that every $g \in N_k$ with $|g(x) - x| < 1$ for $|x| \leq 1$ lies in every $N_j$. If $g \in I_K \cap N_k$, then $g$ has this property by the Lemma, so $g \in N_j$ for every $j$. $\square$

### Sen's theorem

We use the following facts, proved elsewhere in this development. Let $\rho : G_K \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ be a homomorphism with continuous entries.

2. (Congruence filtration.) Let $N_k$ be the set of $g \in G_K$ with $|(\rho(g) - 1)_{ij}| \leq \ell^{-(k+2)}$ for all $i, j$. Then the $N_k$ are decreasing open subgroups of $G_K$; $g \in N_k$ implies $g^\ell \in N_{k+1}$; $g \in N_0$ and $g^\ell \in N_{k+1}$ imply $g \in N_k$; and $\rho(g) = 1$ if $g$ lies in every $N_k$.
3. (Detecting characters.) Let $M \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C}_\ell)$ with $\rho(g)\, g(M) = M$ for all $g \in G_K$. Then the filtration of fact 2 satisfies hypothesis 3 of the statement *no towers over $K$*.
4. (Finite image.) Let $f : G_K \to Q$ be a homomorphism to a group, $J \subseteq G_K$ a subgroup and $H \subseteq G_K$ an open subgroup with $f(g) = 1$ for every $g \in J \cap H$. Then $f(J)$ is finite.

Fact 2 is the filtration by the congruence subgroups $1 + \ell^{k+2} M_n(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$, pulled back by $\rho$. In fact 3, the relation $\rho(g)\, g(M) = M$ says that $\rho$ becomes trivial over $\mathbb{C}_\ell$, and this produces the elements $x_i \in \mathbb{C}_\ell$ of hypothesis 3. Fact 4 holds because $H$ has finite index in the compact group $G_K$.

**Theorem 6.27.5 (Sen's finiteness theorem).** Let $\ell$ be a prime, $K$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, and $\rho : G_K \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ a homomorphism with continuous entries. Assume that there is $M \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C}_\ell)$ with
$$\rho(g)\, g(M) = M \quad \text{for all } g \in G_K.$$
Then $\rho(I_K)$ is finite.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.sen_admissible_representation_finite_inertia` (also `GlobalTheory.PMon.senFinite`, a second name, and `GlobalTheory.PMon.Ram.senFinite_of_noTower`, the reduction to no towers).

*Proof.* Let $N_k$ be the congruence filtration of fact 2. By facts 2 and 3, the $N_k$ satisfy all hypotheses of the statement *no towers over $K$*, which holds by the previous theorem. So there is $k$ with $I_K \cap N_k \subseteq N_j$ for all $j$. Let $g \in I_K \cap N_k$. Then $g$ lies in every $N_j$, so $\rho(g) = 1$ by fact 2. Fact 4 with $f = \rho$, $J = I_K$ and $H = N_k$, which is open, shows that $\rho(I_K)$ is finite. $\square$

The hypothesis says that $\rho$ is $\mathbb{C}_\ell$-admissible, the condition of Sen's paper; the conclusion is that $\rho$ is potentially unramified.

### The weight zero case

We use the following facts, proved elsewhere in this development. Let $\rho : G_K \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_\ell^{\mathrm{ur}})$ be $r$-rational.

5. (Restriction of scalars.) There are a homomorphism $\rho_0 : G_K \to \mathrm{GL}_{n \times r}(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ with continuous entries and $p \in \mathrm{GL}_{n \times r}(\mathbb{Q}_\ell^{\mathrm{ur}})$ with entries fixed by $\sigma^r$, such that
$$p\, \rho_0(g) = \operatorname{diag}\big(\sigma^0(\rho(g)), \dots, \sigma^{r-1}(\rho(g))\big)\, p \quad \text{for all } g \in G_K.$$
Here $\operatorname{diag}$ is the block diagonal matrix, with rows and columns indexed by $n \times \{0, \dots, r-1\}$.
6. (The map $\theta$.) There is a ring homomorphism $\theta : \mathrm{Fil}^0 \to \mathbb{C}_\ell$ that is the identity on $\Omega$ and satisfies $\theta(g(x)) = g(\theta(x))$ for $g \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$ and $x \in \mathrm{Fil}^0$. It is also surjective with kernel $\mathrm{Fil}^1$.
7. (Unramified step.) If $\rho(I_K)$ is finite, then $\rho$ is semistable admissible.

The representation $\rho_0$ is $\rho$ viewed over the $\sigma^r$-fixed field, which is a degree $r$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ inside $K$, then restricted to $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. Its scalar extension is the sum of the $r$ conjugates of $\rho$ under $\sigma$. Fact 7 is the case of finite image on inertia: after a finite extension that kills $\rho(I_K)$, the representation is unramified.

**Theorem 6.27.6 (Weight zero case).** Let $\ell$ be a prime, with tied local period data, $K$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, $r \geq 0$ and $\rho : G_K \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_\ell^{\mathrm{ur}})$ an $r$-rational homomorphism that is framed with weights $0$. Then $\rho$ is semistable admissible.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.weightZero_potentiallySemistable_of_sen_theorem` (also `GlobalTheory.PMon.senStep`, the proof).

*Proof.* Step 1: the frames. Since the weights are $0$, the diagonal matrices in the frame equation are $1$. So for every $m < r$ there is $U_m \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathrm{Fil}^0)$ with
$$\rho_m(g)\, g(U_m) = U_m \quad \text{for all } g \in G_K.$$

Step 2: restriction of scalars. Take $\rho_0$ and $p$ from fact 5. The entries of $p$ are fixed by $\sigma^r$, so they lie in $K$ by $r$-rationality, and $g(p) = p$ for $g \in G_K$. Then also $g(p^{-1}) = p^{-1}$. Let
$$U = \operatorname{diag}(U_0, \dots, U_{r-1}), \qquad D(g) = \operatorname{diag}\big(\rho_0(g), \dots, \rho_{r-1}(g)\big),$$
with entries in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$. By Step 1, $D(g)\, g(U) = U$. Fact 5 gives $\rho_0(g) = p^{-1} D(g)\, p$.

Step 3: a trivialization over $\mathrm{Fil}^0$. Put $X = p^{-1} U$ and $Y = U^{-1} p$. The entries of $p$ and $p^{-1}$ lie in $\Omega$, and those of $U$ and $U^{-1}$ lie in $\mathrm{Fil}^0$, so $X$ and $Y$ have entries in $\mathrm{Fil}^0$, and $XY = 1$. For $g \in G_K$,
$$\begin{aligned} \rho_0(g)\, g(X) &= p^{-1} D(g)\, p \cdot g(p^{-1})\, g(U) \\ &= p^{-1} D(g)\, g(U) = p^{-1} U = X. \end{aligned}$$

Step 4: reduction by $\theta$. Let $M = \theta(X)$, applied entrywise. Since $\theta$ is a ring homomorphism, $\theta(X)\theta(Y) = 1$, so $M \in \mathrm{GL}_{n \times r}(\mathbb{C}_\ell)$. The entries of $\rho_0(g)$ lie in $\mathbb{Q}_\ell \subseteq \Omega$, where $\theta$ is the identity, and $\theta$ commutes with $g$. Applying $\theta$ to Step 3 gives
$$\rho_0(g)\, g(M) = M \quad \text{for all } g \in G_K.$$

Step 5: Sen's theorem. The entries of $\rho_0$ are continuous, so Sen's finiteness theorem gives that $\rho_0(I_K)$ is finite.

Step 6: back to $\rho$. Since $r$-rationality gives $r > 0$, the index $0$ is in $\{0, \dots, r-1\}$. For a matrix $A$ over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ of size $n \times r$, let $F(A)$ be the block at $(\cdot, 0), (\cdot, 0)$ of $p A p^{-1}$. By fact 5, $p\, \rho_0(g)\, p^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma^m(\rho(g)))_m$, whose block at $0$ is $\sigma^0(\rho(g)) = \rho(g)$. So $F(\rho_0(g)) = \rho(g)$, and $\rho(I_K) = F(\rho_0(I_K))$ is finite. Fact 7 shows that $\rho$ is semistable admissible. $\square$

With this theorem, all five steps of section 21 are proved, and so is [the matrix form](/entry/p-adic-monodromy-theorem-matrix-form/) of the monodromy theorem.

**Remark 6.27.7 (Formalization).** The surjectivity of $\theta$ and the description of its kernel are discarded at [WeightZeroPotentiallySemistableOfSenTheorem.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/InertiaEigenvalues/WeightZeroPotentiallySemistableOfSenTheorem.lean#L22); the proof only needs that $\theta$ is a Galois equivariant ring homomorphism on $\mathrm{Fil}^0$ that is the identity on $\Omega$. The group $I_K$ is defined as a subgroup closure ([DataNLoc.lean](lean:Formalism/SharpRestriction/LevelLetterCocycles/DataNLoc.lean#L469)) of a set that is already a subgroup, and the Lemma undoes the closure. Facts 1 to 7 are `GlobalTheory.T.approx_complexPadic_coboundary_algebraic_integer`, `GlobalTheory.T.exists_congruenceFiltration_galoisRep`, `GlobalTheory.T.exists_detecting_characters_congr_filtration`, `GlobalTheory.T.finite_image_of_trivial_on_inf_isOpen`, `GlobalTheory.T.weilRestriction_unramified_frobeniusConjugates`, `GlobalTheory.T.exists_theta_deRhamFilZero_surjective` and `GlobalTheory.T.finiteInertiaImpliesPotSemistable_proof`; we read their statements, not their proofs, and the explanations after them are only a guide. The matrix $M$ is built from $X$ and $Y$ as a unit with an explicit inverse, and Step 6 uses `fr_zero_apply`, which says that $\sigma^0$ is the identity. The names under `GlobalTheory.T` are `first | exact ...` wrappers.

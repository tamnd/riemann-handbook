## Herr cocycles and crystalline classes

Herr's complex computes Galois cohomology from a $(\varphi, \Gamma)$-module. For a crystalline lattice, the classes in $H^1_f$ should come from cocycles with entries in the Wach module, with denominators bounded by the Hodge-Tate weights. This section proves this up to a power of $p$: every class of $H^1_f$ over $K_s$, times a power of $p$, is the class of a Herr cocycle $(y, z)$ with $Q^M y$ and $z / X_s$ in the Wach module, where $M$ bounds the negative Hodge-Tate weights. The proof passes to the field $K_s$, where the statement is known, and descends the cocycle back to $K$. The result is an input of [Theorem 6.5.3](/entry/defect-torsion/) on the defect of a short exact sequence. We use the Wach data of the previous section and the [crystalline representations](/learn/padic-hodge/#crystalline-and-semistable-representations) of the Background.

Fix a good triple $(K, s, L)$ and a Wach datum, as in the previous section. Let $T$ be a lattice in a representation of $G_K$ over $L$.

**Definition 6.4.1 (Herr cocycles).** A *Herr cocycle* of $T$ is a pair $(y, z) \in D(T) \times D(T)$ with
$$(\gamma - 1) y = (\varphi - 1) z,$$
where $\gamma$ is the fixed element of $\Gamma$ with $v(\chi(\gamma) - 1) = s$. The Herr cocycles form an $\mathcal{O}$-module $Z^1(T)$. The Wach datum gives an $\mathcal{O}$-linear *class map* $\mathrm{cl} : Z^1(T) \to H^1(G_{K_s}, T)$, where $G_{K_s} = \{ g \in G_K : v(\chi(g) - 1) \geq s \}$.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.Loc.LocWach.divisibleCocycles` (also `Formalism.BasicNotions.Loc.herrCocycles`, the kernel of $(y, z) \mapsto (\gamma - 1)y - (\varphi - 1)z$).

The pair $(y, z)$ is a 1-cocycle of the complex
$$\begin{aligned} &0 \to D(T) \to D(T)^2 \to D(T) \to 0, \\ &x \mapsto \big((\varphi - 1)x, (\gamma - 1)x\big), \qquad (y, z) \mapsto (\gamma - 1)y - (\varphi - 1)z. \end{aligned}$$ Herr proved that this complex computes $H^\bullet(G_{K_s}, T)$ when $D(T)$ is the étale $(\varphi, \Gamma)$-module of $T$ and $\Gamma$ is pro-cyclic generated by $\gamma$ (L. Herr, *Sur la cohomologie galoisienne des corps $p$-adiques*, Bull. Soc. Math. France 126 (1998)). Here the class map is part of the datum.

For $a \in A$, write $a N(T)$ for the image of $N(T)$ under multiplication by $a$. The *integral $H^1_f$* of $T$ over $K_s$ is the submodule $H^1_f(G_{K_s}, T)$ of classes whose image in $H^1(G_{K_s}, T \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p)$ is crystalline, as in [the Background on local Bloch-Kato theory](/learn/padic-hodge/#local-bloch-kato-theory).

**Definition 6.4.2 (Bounded jumps).** Let $M, h : \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{N}$. The lattice $T$ has *jumps in $[-M, h]$* if for each letter $i$ and each embedding $\tau : K \to L$ over $\tau_i$, the Hodge-Tate jumps of $T$ at $\tau$ lie in $[-M_i, h_i]$. Put $\bar M = \max_i M_i$.

**Theorem 6.4.3 (Crystalline classes from Wach pairs).** Let $(K, s, L)$ be good with a Wach datum and a comparison datum. Let $T$ be a crystalline lattice with jumps in $[-M, h]$. For each $\kappa \in H^1_f(G_{K_s}, T)$ there are $c \in \mathbb{N}$ and a Herr cocycle $(y, z)$ with
$$Q^{\bar M} y \in N(T), \qquad z \in X_s N(T), \qquad \mathrm{cl}(y, z) = p^c \kappa.$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.NL.CrystallineDescent.wach_pow_smul_of_good_ext` (also `GlobalTheory.NL.CrystallineDescent.pow_smul_from_complex` and `GlobalTheory.T.H1f_class_wach_pair_filtration_jumps`, second names).

The proof needs a descent statement from $K_s$ to $K$. Over $K_s$ the Wach module is attached to $G_{K_s}$ itself, and the class map is the usual one; over $K$ one must find a cocycle for the datum of $K$.

The datum gives an embedding $j_D$ of $D(T)$ into $T \otimes \widetilde{A}$, where $\widetilde{A}$ is a ring of Witt vectors with Frobenius $\varphi$; it is compatible with $\varphi$ and with the action of Galois. Let $T|_{K'}$ be the restriction of $T$ to $G_{K'}$.

**Proposition 6.4.4 (Descent of Herr cocycles).** Let $(K, s, L)$ and $(K', s, L)$ be good with Wach data, with $K \subseteq K'$, the same maximal unramified subfield $K_0$, and $G_{K'} = G_{K_s}$ inside $G_K$ (so $K'_s = K'$). Let $T$ be a crystalline lattice for $G_K$ and $m \in \mathbb{N}$. Let $(y', z')$ be a Herr cocycle of $T|_{K'}$ with $Q^m y' \in N(T|_{K'})$ and $z' \in X_s N(T|_{K'})$. Then there is a Herr cocycle $(y, z)$ of $T$ with $Q^m y \in N(T)$ and $z \in X_s N(T)$, together with cocycles $c$ for $\mathrm{cl}(y, z)$ and $c'$ for $\mathrm{cl}(y', z')$ that agree on $G_{K_s} = G_{K'}$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.NL.CrystallineDescent.descend` (also `GlobalTheory.NL.CrystallineDescent.descend_of_eq_K0_of_isCrys`, the same statement).

*Proof.* Both $K$ and $K'$ have the same $K_0$, so the rings of letters agree. First, an element $y'$ with $Q^m y' \in N(T|_{K'})$ comes from an element $y$ of $D(T)$ with $Q^m y \in N(T)$ and $j_D(y) = j_D(y')$; in the same way $z'$ comes from an element of $X_s N(T)$. Second, $\varphi - 1$ is surjective on $T \otimes \widetilde{A}$, so $j_D(y') = (\varphi - 1) b$ for some $b$. For a cocycle $c$ of a subgroup of $G_K$ put $Z_c(g) = c(g) + (g - 1) b$. Since $(y', z')$ is a Herr cocycle, there is a cocycle $c'$ of $G_{K'}$ for $\mathrm{cl}(y', z')$ with $Z_{c'}(g) = 0$ when $g$ maps to $1$ in $\Gamma'$, and $Z_{c'}(g) = j_D(z')$ when $g$ maps to $\gamma'$. Through the identification $G_{K_s} = G_{K'}$ it gives a cocycle $c$ of $G_{K_s}$ with the same values; so $Z_c$ vanishes on the kernel of $G_{K_s} \to \Gamma$, and $Z_c(g_1) = j_D(z_1)$ with $z_1 \in X_s N(T)$ for an element $g_1$ that maps to $\gamma$. Third, there is a continuous homomorphism $\lambda : G_{K_s} \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ whose kernel is the kernel of $G_{K_s} \to \Gamma$, with $\lambda(g) = 1$ exactly when $g$ maps to $\gamma$. Then $Z_c$ factors through $\lambda$, and since $T$ is crystalline, every value $Z_c(g)$ is $j_D$ of an element of $X_s N(T)$; this is the step where crystallinity is used. Apply this at an element $g_0$ that maps to $\gamma$, to get $z \in X_s N(T)$ with $Z_c(g_0) = j_D(z)$. Finally, the relations $(\varphi - 1) b = j_D(y)$ and $Z_c(g_0) = j_D(z)$ imply that $(\gamma - 1) y = (\varphi - 1) z$. Indeed $\varphi$ fixes $c(g_0) \in T$ and commutes with $g_0$, so applying $\varphi - 1$ to $Z_c(g_0)$ gives $(\varphi - 1) j_D(z) = (g_0 - 1) j_D(y) = j_D((\gamma - 1) y)$, and $j_D$ is injective. So $(y, z)$ is a Herr cocycle, and $c$ represents $\mathrm{cl}(y, z)$. $\square$

*Proof of the Theorem.* Take the extension $K' \supseteq K$ with the same $K_0$ and $G_{K'} = G_{K_s}$; the triple $(K', s, L)$ is good. The restriction $T|_{K'}$ is crystalline, and its Hodge-Tate jumps at an embedding of $K'$ are those of $T$ at the restriction of the embedding to $K$, so $T|_{K'}$ has jumps in $[-\bar M, \bar h]$ with constant bounds. The class $\kappa$ pulls back to a class $\kappa' \in H^1_f(G_{K'}, T|_{K'})$, with a cocycle that agrees with one for $\kappa$. Over $K'$ the group $G_{K'_s}$ is all of $G_{K'}$, and the statement for constant bounds is the integral Herr description of $H^1_f$: there are $c$ and a Herr cocycle $(y', z')$ with $Q^{\bar M} y' \in N(T|_{K'})$, $z' \in X_s N(T|_{K'})$ and $\mathrm{cl}(y', z') = p^c \kappa'$. [Proposition 6.4.4](/entry/descend/) gives $(y, z)$ over $K$ with the same bounds and with a cocycle for $\mathrm{cl}(y, z)$ that agrees with one for $p^c \kappa'$ on $G_{K_s}$. Hence $\mathrm{cl}(y, z) = p^c \kappa$. $\square$

## Two constructions of the period rings

The proof uses [[Fontaine's period rings|K6HW8YWCQJ]] $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$, $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ in two places, and the two places build these rings in two different ways. The first construction starts from one definition of the ring of integers of $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ and inverts all the periods $\log[\eta]$ of primitive elements $\eta$ of the Tate module. The second starts from another definition of the same ring and inverts one period $t$. This section defines both and proves that they give isomorphic rings, with all the structure that the proof uses: the Galois action, the Frobenius, $\theta$, the filtration, the map $B_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, the logarithm and $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$. The comparison is used in [the next section](/entry/loc-tied6/) to move abstract period data from model 1 to model 2, and through it in the proof of [the basic properties of $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$](/entry/period-ring-base-base/) in the last section. The rings themselves are recalled in [the Background](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings).

Throughout, $\ell$ is a prime, $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ is an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, $C = \mathbb{C}_\ell$ is its completion and $\mathcal{O}_C$ is the ring of elements of norm at most $1$. The group $G = \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ acts on $C$ by isometries.

**Definition 1.1.1 (Tilt and $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$).** The *tilt* $\mathcal{O}_{C^\flat}$ is the set of sequences $x = (x^{(0)}, x^{(1)}, \dots)$ in $\mathcal{O}_C$ with $(x^{(n+1)})^\ell = x^{(n)}$, with the ring structure of the Background. Put $A_{\mathrm{inf}} = W(\mathcal{O}_{C^\flat})$, the ring of [[Witt vectors|6N3X2QX4W9]]. It has the Frobenius $\varphi$ induced by $x \mapsto x^\ell$, the Teichmüller lifts $[x]$, and the surjective ring map $\theta : A_{\mathrm{inf}} \to \mathcal{O}_C$ with $\theta([x]) = x^{(0)}$. The map $\theta$ extends to $A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell] \to C$.

**Definition 1.1.2 ($B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ and the filtration).** Let $J \subset A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]$ be the kernel of $\theta$. Put
$$B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ = \varprojlim_n A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]/J^n.$$
The map $\theta$ extends to $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ \to C$. The ring $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is a discrete valuation ring and $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is its fraction field. For $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ the subspace $\mathrm{Fil}^a B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is the $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$-submodule $t^a B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, where $t$ is the period defined below. Each model defines $\mathrm{Fil}^a$ by its own recipe. The comparison below uses only that in both models it is the submodule generated by $t^a$.

**Definition 1.1.3 (Divided powers and $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$).** Let $A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0$ be the $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$-subalgebra of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]$ generated by the elements $\gamma_n(x) = x^n/n!$ for $n \ge 0$ and $x \in A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ with $\theta(x) = 0$. Let $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ be the $\ell$-adic completion of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0$. The Frobenius and the action of $G$ extend to $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, and there is a natural map $A_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$.

**Definition 1.1.4 (The period $\log[\eta]$).** Let $u \in A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ with $\theta(u) = 1$. The partial sums
$$\log_N(u) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (-1)^k\, k!\; \gamma_{k+1}(u - 1)$$
lie in $A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0$, and $\log_M(u) - \log_N(u) \in \ell^n A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0$ when $\ell^n \le M \le N$, because $\ell^n$ divides $k!$ for $k \ge \ell^n$. Their limit $\log(u) \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ is the series $\sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^{k+1}(u-1)^k/k$. The *Tate module* of $C^\flat$ is the set of $\eta \in \mathcal{O}_{C^\flat}$ with $\eta^{(0)} = 1$. For such $\eta$ we have $\theta([\eta]) = 1$, and we put $\log[\eta] = \log([\eta])$. The element $\eta$ is *primitive* if $\eta^{(1)} \neq 1$.

Fix a compatible system $(\zeta_{\ell^n})_n$ of primitive $\ell^n$-th roots of unity in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, let $\epsilon = (1, \zeta_\ell, \zeta_{\ell^2}, \dots)$ and put $t = \log[\epsilon] \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. Then $\varphi(t) = \ell t$ and $g(t) = \chi(g)\, t$ for $g \in G$, where $\chi$ is the [[cyclotomic character|XSGB5K2KEG]].

The two constructions differ in the ring $\mathcal{O}_C$ they start from and in the way they make $t$ invertible.

**Definition 1.1.5 (Model 1).** Take for $\mathcal{O}_C$ the closed unit ball of $C$, viewed as a subring of $C$. Put $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^+ = A_{\mathrm{cris}}[1/\ell]$, and let $S$ be the multiplicative subset of $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^+$ generated by the elements $\log[\eta]$ for all primitive $\eta$ in the Tate module. Then $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{(1)} = S^{-1} B_{\mathrm{cris}}^+$. A ring map $f : B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{(1)} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{(1)}$ is *the inclusion* if (a) $f$ agrees on $A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0$ with the map $A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0 \subset A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell] \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, and (b) for each $n$ there is $N$ such that for each $x \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ the element $f(x)$ is some $y \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ with $\ell^N y \equiv a \pmod{J^n}$ for some $a \in A_{\mathrm{inf}}$. An element $y \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is *the logarithm of $\epsilon$* if $\theta([\epsilon]) = 1$ and $y = \log[\epsilon]$, the series taken in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$.

**Definition 1.1.6 (Model 2).** Take for $\mathcal{O}_C$ the ring of elements of norm at most $1$, viewed as a subset of $C$. Put $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{(2)} = A_{\mathrm{cris}}[1/\ell][1/t]$. A ring map $f : B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{(2)} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{(2)}$ is *the inclusion* if $f(x) = \iota(x)$ for each $x \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, where $\iota : A_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is the natural map. An element $y \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is *the logarithm of $\epsilon$* if for each $n \ge 0$
$$y - \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{i}\,([\epsilon] - 1)^i \in J^n.$$

In both models the Galois group acts through its action on $\mathcal{O}_C$, and $\varphi$ is induced by the Frobenius of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. In model 2, $\varphi$ and $g \in G$ extend to $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{(2)}$ because $\varphi(t) = \ell t$ and $g(t) = \chi(g) t$ with $\chi(g) \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$.

**Lemma 1.1.7 (Inverting one period).** Each primitive $\eta$ in the Tate module equals $\epsilon^a$ for some $a \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$, and $\log[\eta] = a\, t$. Hence the identity of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ extends to an isomorphism $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{(1)} \cong A_{\mathrm{cris}}[1/\ell][1/t]$.

Lean: `Cmp6.cBc` (also `Cmp6.isUnit_cB_cA_logTeich`, `Cmp6.cA_symm_tA`).

*Proof.* The Tate module is a free $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module of rank $1$ with generator $\epsilon$, and the primitive elements are the generators. So $\eta = \epsilon^a$ with $a$ a unit, and $\log[\epsilon^a] = a \log[\epsilon]$. Each element of $S$ is then a unit times a power of $t$. So $S^{-1}B_{\mathrm{cris}}^+$ and $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^+[1/t]$ have the same universal property. $\square$

**Lemma 1.1.8 (Two definitions of the logarithm).** Let $\epsilon \in \mathcal{O}_{C^\flat}$ and $y \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. Then $y$ is the logarithm of $\epsilon$ in the sense of model 1 if and only if it is the logarithm of $\epsilon$ in the sense of model 2.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.CmpTie.LogF.isLog_iff`.

*Proof.* Suppose $\theta([\epsilon]) = 1$ and $y = \log[\epsilon]$. Then $[\epsilon] - 1 \in J$, so the $i$-th term of the series lies in $J^i$, and the tail from $i = n$ on lies in $J^n$. Conversely, suppose the condition of model 2 holds. For $n = 1$ it says $y \in J$. For $n = 2$ it says $y - ([\epsilon] - 1) \in J^2$. Together, $[\epsilon] - 1 \in J$, so $\theta([\epsilon]) = 1$. Then $\log[\epsilon]$ is defined, and by the first part $y - \log[\epsilon] \in J^n$ for each $n$. Since $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is $J$-adically separated, $y = \log[\epsilon]$. $\square$

**Lemma 1.1.9 (The inclusion is unique).** In each model there is exactly one inclusion $B_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}$: the map $B_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ induced by $A_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.CmpTie.Incl.isCrisDRF_iff` (also `Formalism.BasicNotions.CmpTie.Incl.isCrisDRA_toBdRPlus_iff`).

*Proof.* In model 2, $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ is a localization of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, so a ring map from it is fixed by its values on $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. In model 1 the natural map satisfies (a) by construction. It satisfies (b) with $N = n$: for $x \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, the image of $\ell^n x$ in $A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]/J^n$ lies in the image of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$. Now let $f$ satisfy (a) and (b), and let $x \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. For each $k$ write $x = x_k + \ell^k z_k$ with $x_k \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0$. By (a) the difference of $f(x)$ and the natural image of $x$ equals $\ell^k$ times the same difference for $z_k$. Let $T$ be the image of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0$ in $A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]/J^n$. By (b), applied to $z_k$ with $k$ large, the image of the difference lies in $\ell^{k} T$ for each $k$. The ring $T$ is $\ell$-adically separated, so the difference vanishes modulo $J^n$. This holds for each $n$, so the difference vanishes. So $f$ is the natural map on $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, and therefore on its localization. $\square$

**Theorem 1.1.10 (Comparison of the two models).** There are ring isomorphisms
$$e_B : B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{(1)} \to B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{(2)}, \qquad e_D : B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{(1)} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{(2)}, \qquad e_P : B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+(1)} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+(2)}$$
with the following properties.

1. $e_B$ and $e_D$ are the identity on the images of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$.
2. $e_D$ restricts to $e_P$ on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, and $\theta \circ e_P = \theta$.
3. $e_B$ and $e_D$ commute with the action of each $g \in G$, and $e_B \circ \varphi = \varphi \circ e_B$.
4. A ring map $f : B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{(1)} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{(1)}$ is the inclusion of model 1 if and only if $e_D \circ f \circ e_B^{-1}$ is the inclusion of model 2.
5. For $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $x \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{(1)}$, $x \in \mathrm{Fil}^a$ if and only if $e_D(x) \in \mathrm{Fil}^a$.
6. Let $\epsilon$ be the element of $\mathcal{O}_{C^\flat}$ of an $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ datum for some period data (Definition 10.2.2 of the next section). For $y \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+(1)}$, $y$ is the logarithm of $\epsilon$ in model 1 if and only if $e_P(y)$ is the logarithm of $\epsilon$ in model 2.
7. $e_B$ is compatible with the two maps $A_{\mathrm{inf}} \to B_{\mathrm{cris}}$.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.CmpTie.compat_F` (also `Formalism.BasicNotions.CmpTie.cmp_F`, the existence statement).

*Proof.* The two definitions of $\mathcal{O}_C$ are isomorphic by a ring isomorphism $e$ that is the identity on the underlying elements of $C$. By functoriality, $e$ induces isomorphisms of the tilts, of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ and of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]$ that commute with $\theta$, $\varphi$ and $G$. Since they respect $J$, they induce $e_P$ on the $J$-adic completions, and $e_D$ is the induced map of fraction fields. In model 2, $A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0$ is generated by the divided powers of $\ker\theta$; the divided power $\gamma_n(x)$ of model 2 equals $x^n/n!$, so the two subalgebras $A_{\mathrm{cris}}^0$ correspond, and so do their $\ell$-adic completions. The resulting isomorphism of the $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ sends $\log[\epsilon]$ to $t$. By the Lemma on inverting one period it extends to $e_B$. Property 1 holds since all maps are ring maps that fix $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and 2, 3 and 7 hold since all maps are induced by $e$. For 5, the kernel of $\theta$ on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is generated by $t$ in both models, and $e_P$ sends $t$ to $t$, so $e_D(t^a B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+) = t^a B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. For 6, the proof uses nothing about $\epsilon$. The Lemma on the two definitions of the logarithm reduces the claim to model 2 on both sides, and the condition of model 2 is a statement about $[\epsilon]$ and the powers of $J$, which $e_P$ preserves. For 4, by the Lemma on uniqueness, $f$ is the inclusion of model 1 if and only if it is the natural map, and $e_D \circ f \circ e_B^{-1}$ is the inclusion of model 2 if and only if it is the natural map. The natural maps correspond under $e_B$ and $e_D$ because the square formed by $A_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ in the two models and the isomorphisms of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ and of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ commutes. $\square$

**Remark 1.1.11.** The isomorphisms are the identity on all the underlying elements. The work is in checking that each of the structures in the list corresponds. The list has seven items. Items 2 and 3 each combine two or three separate compatibilities, so that eleven identities are proved in all.

**Remark 1.1.12 (Formalization).** The formal theorem is a conjunction of eleven parts. Each part is a projection of one bundle of compatibilities over the second model ([Assembly.lean](lean:Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/PeriodRingComparison/CrystallinePeriodRings/Assembly.lean#L43)). The isomorphisms first pass through a cast from the first model to the general construction over the unit ball. These casts are identities ([CFDefs.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/CrystallineComparison/CFDefs.lean#L11)). Property 6 is stated only for the element $\epsilon$ of an $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ datum, as in the theorem above. We did not unfold the proof that the natural maps correspond, nor the construction of the action of $G$ on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ in model 2. The three lemmas are separate formal results. The word "theta" in the name of the formal development refers only to the map $\theta$. It contains no theta series and no Weil representation.

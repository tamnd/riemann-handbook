## The basic properties of the crystalline period ring

The local steps of the Galois row use about forty facts about $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. This section states them in one theorem and explains where each comes from. Most are direct consequences of the construction. Three carry real content: the fundamental exact sequence, the computation of the Galois invariants of $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$, and the fact that the Kummer cocycle has no period in $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$. The last one is proved through abstract period data, and this is where [the transport of ties](/entry/loc-tied6/) enters.

We work in model 2 of the first section, the model of [the comparison theorem](/entry/compat-f/) that is the target of the isomorphisms. So $B = A_{\mathrm{cris}}[1/\ell][1/t]$, and $\iota : B \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is the map induced by $A_{\mathrm{cris}} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. We write $q : \mathbb{Q}_\ell \to B$ for the structure map, $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ also for its image in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, $\theta : B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ \to C$ as before, and $\chi$ for the cyclotomic character. For a finite extension $L$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, $G_L \subset G$ is its Galois group.

**Definition 1.3.1 (Integral elements).** Let $A' \subset B$ be the subring generated by the image of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ and by $1/\ell$. It is the image of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]$. For $h \ge 0$ and $m \in \mathbb{Z}$, an element $x \in B$ is an *eigenvector of slope $(h, m)$* if $\varphi^h(x) = \ell^m x$.

**Definition 1.3.2 (Kummer cocycle and $B_{\mathrm{st}}$).** Let $\ell^\flat \in \mathcal{O}_{C^\flat}$ be given by a compatible system of $\ell$-power roots of $\ell$, so $(\ell^\flat)^{(0)} = \ell$. For $g \in G$ the element $g(\ell^\flat)/\ell^\flat$ lies in the Tate module, so it is $\epsilon^{\kappa(g)}$ for a unique $\kappa(g) \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell$. This is the *Kummer cocycle*. Since $\theta([\ell^\flat]/\ell) = 1$, the series $u = \log([\ell^\flat]/\ell)$ converges in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. Let $B_{\mathrm{st}} \subset B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ be the subring generated by the image of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, by $1/\ell$, $1/t$ and $u$.

The cocycle $\kappa$ records how $G$ moves the $\ell$-power roots of $\ell$. Part 3 of the theorem below says that $u$ is a period for it in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$: $g(u) - u = \kappa(g) t$. The next result says that $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ has no such period, even over a finite extension. This is why $B_{\mathrm{st}}$ needs the new element $u$.

**Proposition 1.3.3 (No Kummer period).** Let $L$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. There is no $x \in B$ with $g(x) - x = \kappa(g)\, t$ for all $g \in G_L$.

*Proof.* We do not work in $B$ directly. We use a result of the Galois row on the abstract period data of the previous section: for period data $P$ with an $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$ datum and a tie to model 1, there are no $b \neq 0$ and $b'$ in the ring $B_P$ of $P$ with
$$b\, g(b') - g(b)\, b' = b\, g(b)\, \kappa_P(g)\, t_P \quad \text{for all } g \in G_L,$$
where $\kappa_P$ is the Kummer cocycle defined from the roots of unity of $P$ and a fixed system of $\ell$-power roots of $\ell$. Its proof first replaces $G_L$ by $G$ and $(b, b')$ by a new pair $(y_0, y_1)$ with $y_0 \neq 0$ that satisfies the same relation for all $g \in G$. Then it uses $g(u) = u + \kappa(g) t$ and the fact that the $G$-invariants of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ are $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ to find $d \in \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ with $\iota(y_1 - d y_0) = \iota(y_0)\, u$, transported to $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ of model 1. Finally it shows that $u$ is not of this form. The Galois row also supplies period data $P$ with a tie to model 1. By [the transport of ties](/entry/loc-tied6/), $P$ has a tie $(e_B, e_D)$ to model 2. Under this tie, $e_D(\iota(t_P)) = a\, t$ for some $a \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and the two Kummer cocycles satisfy $\kappa(g) + \chi(g) c = c + a\, \kappa_P(g)$ for some $c \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell$. Hence
$$e_B(\kappa_P(g)\, t_P) = \kappa(g)\, t + \bigl(g(ct) - ct\bigr) \quad \text{for all } g \in G.$$
Now suppose $x \in B$ satisfies $g(x) - x = \kappa(g) t$ for $g \in G_L$. Put $b' = e_B^{-1}(x + ct)$. Then $g(b') - b' = e_B^{-1}\bigl(\kappa(g) t + g(ct) - ct\bigr) = \kappa_P(g)\, t_P$ for $g \in G_L$. This is the displayed relation with $b = 1$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Theorem 1.3.4 (Basic properties of $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$).** Let $\ell$ be a prime. The following hold.

1. *Rings and actions.* $B$ is a domain and $\iota$ is injective. The group $G$ acts on $B$, on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ and on $C$. The maps $\varphi$ and $\iota$ commute with this action. Each $g$ and $\varphi$ fix $q(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$, and $\iota \circ q$ is the structure map of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$.
2. *The period $t$.* $t$ is a unit of $B$, $\iota(t) = t \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, $\varphi(t) = \ell t$, and $g(t) = \chi(g)\, t$ for $g \in G$.
3. *The Kummer cocycle.* $g(u) = u + \kappa(g)\, t$ and $\kappa(gh) = \kappa(g) + \chi(g)\kappa(h)$ for $g, h \in G$. The maps $\kappa$ and $\chi$ are continuous. The ring $B_{\mathrm{st}}$ contains $\iota(B)$ and $u$.
4. *$\theta$ and the valuation.* $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is stable under $G$. On $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, $\theta$ is a ring map with $\theta(g x) = g\,\theta(x)$ and $\theta(c) = c$ for $c \in \mathbb{Q}_\ell$, and its kernel is $t B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. Each $x \neq 0$ in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is $t^i v$ with $i \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $v$ a unit of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. For $x \in C$ the map $g \mapsto g(x)$ is continuous.
5. *The fundamental exact sequence.* If $\varphi(x) = x$ and $\iota(x) \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, then $x \in q(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$. For each $z \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ there is $x \in B$ with $\varphi(x) = x$ and $\iota(x) - z \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$.
6. *Integral elements.* $A'$ is stable under $\varphi$ and $G$, and $\iota(A') \subset B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. If $x \in A'$ is an eigenvector of slope $(h, m)$ with $m \neq 0$, then $x = 0$.
7. *Invariants.* For each finite extension $L$ there are $b_1, \dots, b_f \in A'$, fixed by $G_L$, such that each $x \in B$ fixed by $G_L$ is $\sum_i q(c_i) b_i$ with $c_i \in \mathbb{Q}_\ell$.
8. *No Kummer period.* The Proposition above holds.

*Proof.* The theorem consists of forty separate assertions. We take the groups in turn.

(1) The map $\iota$ is the inclusion of model 2, so by the uniqueness lemma of the first section it is the natural map; its injectivity is proved in the Galois row. Since $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is a field, $B$ is a domain. The actions and their compatibilities come from functoriality of the constructions in $\mathcal{O}_C$.

(2) In model 2, $t$ is inverted by construction, and $\iota(t)$ is the image of $t \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. The relations $\varphi(t) = \ell t$ and $g(t) = \chi(g) t$ hold in $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, as recalled in the first section.

(3) Write $g(\ell^\flat) = \epsilon^{\kappa(g)} \ell^\flat$. Then $g([\ell^\flat]/\ell) = [\epsilon]^{\kappa(g)} [\ell^\flat]/\ell$, and the logarithm turns the product into the sum $u + \kappa(g) \log[\epsilon]$. Applying $gh$ to $\ell^\flat$ gives $\epsilon^{\kappa(g)} \epsilon^{\chi(g)\kappa(h)}$, since $g(\epsilon) = \epsilon^{\chi(g)}$; this is the cocycle relation. Each element of $B$ is $y/(\ell^m t^n)$ with $y \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, so $\iota(B) \subset B_{\mathrm{st}}$.

(4) The ring $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is a discrete valuation ring whose maximal ideal is $\ker\theta = t B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. This gives the statement on the kernel and the decomposition $x = t^i v$. For continuity, approximate $x \in C$ by $y \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$. The stabilizer of $y$ is open, and $G$ acts by isometries, so $g \mapsto g(x)$ is a uniform limit of locally constant maps.

(5) Put $B^+ = A_{\mathrm{cris}}[1/\ell]$. Two results on $B^+$ from $p$-adic Hodge theory do the work. Lemma A: if $y \in B^+$, $\varphi(y) = \ell^n y$ and $\iota(y) \in t^n B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, then $y = c\, t^n$ with $c \in \mathbb{Q}_\ell$. Lemma B: for $n \le m$ and $w \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ there is $y \in B^+$ with $\varphi(y) = \ell^m y$ and $\iota(y) - w \in t^n B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. For the first part, write $x = y/t^n$ with $y \in B^+$. Then $\varphi(x) = x$ gives $\varphi(y) = \ell^n y$, and $\iota(x) \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ gives $\iota(y) \in t^n B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. By Lemma A, $x = c$. For the second part, choose $n$ with $w = t^n z \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, take $y$ from Lemma B with $m = n$, and put $x = y/t^n$. Then $\varphi(x) = x$ and $\iota(x) - z = (\iota(y) - w)/t^n \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. These are the two halves of Fontaine's fundamental exact sequence $0 \to \mathbb{Q}_\ell \to B^{\varphi = 1} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}/B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ \to 0$.

(6) The ring $A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]$ is stable under $\varphi$ and $G$, and it maps into $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ by construction. This gives the first two statements. For the third, the map $A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell] \to B$ is injective, so we may compute in $A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]$. The Frobenius of $A_{\mathrm{inf}} = W(\mathcal{O}_{C^\flat})$ is bijective, because $\mathcal{O}_{C^\flat}$ is perfect, and it fixes $\ell$. So $\ell$ divides $\varphi(y)$ if and only if $\ell$ divides $y$, and $\varphi$ preserves the $\ell$-adic valuation of a nonzero element. If $\varphi^h(x) = \ell^m x$ with $x \neq 0$, comparing valuations gives $m = 0$.

(7) Let $L_0 = L \cap \mathbb{Q}_\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and choose a basis $c_1, \dots, c_f$ of $L_0$ over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. The field $\mathbb{Q}_\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is generated by roots of unity of order prime to $\ell$, and these lift to Teichmüller elements of $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$. So the images $b_i$ of the $c_i$ in $B$ lie in $A'$, and they are fixed by $G_L$. A result of the Galois row says that a $G_L$-invariant element of $B$ is the image of an element of $L_0$. Its proof uses the invariants of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ and the injectivity of $\iota$. Expand that element in the basis.

(8) This is the Proposition. $\square$

**Remark 1.3.5 (Formalization).** The forty assertions are the forty fields of one formal structure ([DieudonneManinBasisModification.lean](lean:Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/PeriodRingBases/PeriodRingUnits/DieudonneManinBasisModification.lean#L84)). The names of that file and of the folder that holds the theorem do not describe the content, which is about period rings only. In the formalization, $\theta$ on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is a total function that is set to $0$ outside $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. The statements only use it on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. The eigenvector statement in (6) includes $h = 0$, where it is trivial. Each group of assertions is filled by separate formal results. We did not unfold the results behind (6), the injectivity of $\iota$, the proofs of Lemmas A and B in (5), the invariants of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ used in (7), or the step in the proof of the Proposition that shows $u$ is not a quotient of elements of $\iota(B)$. The sketch for (6) is ours.

## Notes and references

The theorem of this section is the main output of the chapter. Nine results of the Galois row use it directly. They are results on the $p$-adic comparison of étale, crystalline and Hodge-Tate data. All of them lie below [the local steps](/entry/steps-holds/), which collect the crystalline, Hodge-Tate and Frobenius steps for the cohomology of a tower. These steps are used in the step [geometric realization of weight one](/step/geo-one-holds/) of [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/). [The transport of ties](/entry/loc-tied6/) also feeds the crystalline step directly.

J.-M. Fontaine, "Le corps des périodes $p$-adiques", in *Périodes $p$-adiques*, Astérisque 223 (1994), Exposé II, for the rings $A_{\mathrm{inf}}$, $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{st}}$.

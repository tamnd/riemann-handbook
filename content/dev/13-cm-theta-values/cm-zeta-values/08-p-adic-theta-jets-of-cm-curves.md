## p-adic theta jets of CM curves

This section bounds the $p$-adic size of the Taylor coefficients of theta functions on a CM elliptic curve and on its powers. First we prove the cube relation for a $p$-adic sigma series in a formal parameter. Then we bound the jets of its logarithm and show that torsion translates are integral up to a unit. From these facts we get the denominator bound for jets of torsion translates in one variable, and then in many variables. The bounds are fields of [the inputs of the norm bound](/entry/inp-all/), and they serve the step [nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/). The background is in [the sigma function](/learn/theta-functions-cm/#genus-one-the-sigma-function), [the cubical structure](/learn/theta-functions-cm/#the-cubical-structure) and [integrality](/learn/theta-functions-cm/#integrality).

Fix the host datum $D$. It gives an imaginary quadratic field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ and an embedding $\varphi_0$ of $K$ into $\mathbb{C}$. For an ideal $\mathfrak{c}$ of $\mathcal{O}_K$ and $\delta \in \mathbb{C}$, let $\Lambda(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ be the group generated by $\delta\varphi_0(c)$ for $c \in \mathfrak{c}$. We say that $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ is a *curve pair* if $\mathfrak{c} \neq 0$, the norm of $\mathfrak{c}$ is prime to $2d$, and $\delta^{12} = \Delta(\Lambda(\mathfrak{c}, 1))$, where $\Delta = g_2^3 - 27g_3^2$.

**Definition 1.8.1 (genus one datum).** Let $p$ be a prime. A *genus one datum* $g$ for $p$ consists of an embedding $\lambda : \overline{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathbb{C}_p$, a lattice $\Lambda$, a change of variables $C$ over $\mathbb{C}_p$, and two power series $w$ and $\mathrm{ex}$. Let $W = C \cdot W_\Lambda$, where $W_\Lambda$ is the curve $y^2 = x^3 + \lambda(-g_2/4)\, x + \lambda(-g_3/4)$, and let $l$ be the formal logarithm of $W$ built from $w$. The datum is *good* if $W$ has coefficients in $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ with $|\Delta(W)| = 1$, $w$ is the series of $W$ in the formal parameter, $\mathrm{ex}(0) = 0$ and $l(\mathrm{ex}(X)) = X$. The datum *sits at* $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ if $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ is a curve pair, the norm of $\mathfrak{c}$ is prime to $p$, $\Lambda = \Lambda(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$, and $g$ is good.

The group law is $a \oplus b = \mathrm{ex}(l(a) + l(b))$, and $a \ominus b = \mathrm{ex}(l(a) - l(b))$. Let $\varepsilon = u(C)^{-1}$. Let $\sigma^*$ be a fixed odd entire function with $\sigma^{*\prime}(0) = 1$ and with the quasi-periodicity law of the reduced sigma function of $\Lambda$. Let $E(X)$ be the series $\sigma^*(\varepsilon\, l(X))/(\varepsilon X)$, with Taylor coefficients mapped by $\lambda$. Put $\varphi = -\log E$ and
$$b(a, c) = \varphi(a \oplus c) - \varphi(a) - \varphi(c).$$

**Definition 1.8.2 (near equality).** For power series $f, h$ over $\mathbb{C}_p$ in some variables, write $f \simeq h$ if $f - h$ has constant term $0$ and $\exp(f - h)$ has all coefficients of norm at most $1$.

If $f \simeq 0$ and $h \simeq 0$, then $f + h \simeq 0$ and $-f \simeq 0$. The reason is that $\exp(f + h) = \exp(f)\exp(h)$, and the inverse of an integral series with constant term $1$ is integral.

**Theorem 1.8.3 (cube relation).** Let $g$ sit at a curve pair. In three variables $x, y, z$,
$$\begin{aligned}
&\varphi(x \oplus y \oplus z) - \varphi(x \oplus y) - \varphi(y \oplus z) - \varphi(z \oplus x) \\
&\quad + \varphi(x) + \varphi(y) + \varphi(z) \simeq 0.
\end{aligned}$$
The series $x \oplus y$ has coefficients of norm at most $1$. The form $b$ is additive in each variable up to $\simeq$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.theta_cube_relation_integral_formalGroup_biadditive` (also `GlobalTheory.ThetaCube.cube_addInt_beBiadd`, a second name).

*Proof.* The proof has three inputs. The first is a $p$-adic form of the addition formula $\wp(x) - \wp(y) = -\sigma(x+y)\sigma(x-y)/(\sigma(x)^2\sigma(y)^2)$. The second is a $p$-adic form of the Frobenius-Stickelberger formula in three points. That formula writes the determinant of the rows $(1, \wp(z_i), \wp'(z_i))$ as a constant times $\sigma(x+y+z)\sigma(x-y)\sigma(y-z)\sigma(z-x)/(\sigma(x)\sigma(y)\sigma(z))^3$. The third is the integrality of the group law, $x \oplus y$, which holds because $W$ is integral with unit discriminant.

From the first input we get the relation
$$\varphi(x \oplus y) + \varphi(x \ominus y) \simeq 2\varphi(x) + 2\varphi(y).$$
From the second input we get the relation
$$\begin{aligned}
&\varphi(x \oplus y \oplus z) + \varphi(x \ominus y) + \varphi(y \ominus z) + \varphi(z \ominus x) \\
&\quad \simeq 3\big(\varphi(x) + \varphi(y) + \varphi(z)\big).
\end{aligned}$$
Use the first relation for each of the three pairs, and subtract. The terms with $\ominus$ go away, and the cube relation is the result.

For biadditivity, expand $b(x \oplus y, z) - b(x, z) - b(y, z)$. Since $\oplus$ comes from a logarithm, it is commutative and associative. The terms $\varphi(z)$ cancel except one, and the result is exactly the left side of the cube relation. The same holds for $b(x, y \oplus z) - b(x, y) - b(x, z)$. $\square$

**Theorem 1.8.4 (jets of $\varphi$).** Let $p \neq 2$, and let $g$ sit at a curve pair. For $i \ge 0$ put $j_i = i!\,\mathrm{coeff}_i(\varphi \circ \mathrm{ex})$. Then:

1. $|j_i| \le 1$ for $i \neq 2$;
2. $|(\alpha_1 - \alpha_2)\, j_2| \le 1$ for each $\alpha$ in the closure of $\{(\lambda(a), \lambda(\bar a)) : a \in \mathcal{O}_K\}$;
3. $|j_2| \le 1$ if $p \neq d$;
4. $|j_2| \le p^{1/2}$ if $p = d$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.cm_normalized_theta_jets_integral` (also `GlobalTheory.ThetaJets.jetsPh_all`, a second name).

The numbers $j_i$ are the derivatives of $\varphi$ at $0$ in the logarithmic coordinate $u$, where $X = \mathrm{ex}(u)$.

*Proof.* We use three lemmas on a good datum. The first says that the cube relation gives $|j_i| \le 1$ for $i \neq 1, 2$. The second says that the relation $\varphi([-1]x) \simeq \varphi(x)$ gives $|j_1| \le 1$ when $p \neq 2$. The third says that the relation $b([a]x, y) \simeq b(x, [c]y)$ gives $|(a - c)\, j_2| \le 1$. Here $[a]x = \mathrm{ex}(a\, l(x))$.

The cube relation is the last theorem. Two further facts on CM points are proved elsewhere: the norm law $\varphi([\alpha_1]x) \simeq \alpha_1\alpha_2\, \varphi(x)$ and the conjugation law $b([\alpha_1]x, y) \simeq b(x, [\alpha_2]y)$, for $\alpha$ as in part 2. The norm law with $\alpha = (-1, -1)$ gives the hypothesis of the second lemma. The conjugation law gives part 2.

For parts 3 and 4, a lemma gives, for $p \neq 2$, an $\alpha$ as in part 2 with $|\alpha_1 - \alpha_2| = 1$ if $p \neq d$ and $|\alpha_1 - \alpha_2| = p^{-1/2}$ if $p = d$. For example, take $a = \sqrt{-d}$ in $\mathcal{O}_K$, with $d$ prime. Then $\alpha_1 - \alpha_2 = \pm 2\lambda(\sqrt{-d})$, and its square is $-4d$. Part 2 then gives $|j_2| \le 1$ if $p \neq d$ and $|j_2| \le p^{1/2}$ if $p = d$. $\square$

**Definition 1.8.5 (translates and torsion).** For a lattice $\Lambda$ of area $A$, a point $\omega$ and a function $G$, put
$$U_\omega G(z) = G(z + \omega)\exp\Big(-\frac{\pi z\bar\omega}{A} - \frac{\pi\omega\bar\omega}{2A}\Big).$$
For a genus one datum $g$, let $G^\sharp$ be the series $\sum_n \lambda(G^{(n)}(0)/n!)\, T^n$ evaluated at $T = \varepsilon\, l(X)$. A point $t$ is *prime-to-$p$ torsion* if $t \notin \Lambda$ and $nt \in \Lambda$ for some $n > 0$ prime to $p$.

**Theorem 1.8.6 (torsion translates).** Let $g$ sit at a curve pair, and let $t$ be prime-to-$p$ torsion. Then $E^{-1}(U_t\sigma^*)^\sharp$ has coefficients of norm at most $1$, and the constant term of $(U_t\sigma^*)^\sharp$ has norm $1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.cm_theta_torsion_translate_integral_series` (also `GlobalTheory.ThetaTransl.transl_holds`, a second name).

*Proof.* The lattice $\Lambda$ is a lattice of a CM curve, and the Taylor coefficients of $U_t\sigma^*$ at $0$ are algebraic. For each prime-to-$p$ torsion point $s$, the abscissa of $s$ on $W$ has norm at most $1$. A further result then writes
$$E^{-1}(U_s\sigma^*)^\sharp = c(s)\, w_s,$$
where $c(s) = \lambda(U_s\sigma^*(0))$ and $w_s$ is integral with constant term $1$.

Let $M(s) = 0$ for $s \in \Lambda$ and $M(s) = \log|c(s)|$ otherwise. Then $M$ is periodic modulo $\Lambda$ on $n$-torsion points, and it satisfies the parallelogram law
$$M(P + Q) + M(P - Q) = 2M(P) + 2M(Q)$$
for $n$-torsion $P$ and $Q \notin \Lambda$. Such a function vanishes on the $n$-torsion. Indeed, take $P = kt$ and $Q = t$. Induction on $k$ gives $M(kt) = k^2 M(t)$, and $M(nt) = 0$ gives $n^2 M(t) = 0$.

So $|c(t)| = 1$ if $c(t) \neq 0$. Since $t \notin \Lambda$, $\sigma^*(t) \neq 0$, and the exponential factor is not $0$. So $c(t) \neq 0$ and $|c(t)| = 1$. The constant term of $(U_t\sigma^*)^\sharp$ is $c(t)$. Finally $E^{-1}(U_t\sigma^*)^\sharp = c(t)\, w_t$ is integral. $\square$

**Definition 1.8.7 (unkilled two-torsion point).** A point $t_0$ is an *unkilled two-torsion point* of $\Lambda$ if $2t_0 \in \Lambda$ and $\tfrac12(1 \pm \sqrt{-d})\, t_0 \notin \Lambda$ for both signs. The normalized theta function is $\theta_0(z) = U_{t_0}\sigma^*(z)/U_{t_0}\sigma^*(0)$. The prime $p$ is *split* if $p = 2$ or $\big(\tfrac{-d}{p}\big) = 1$.

**Theorem 1.8.8 (denominators of torsion translates).** Assume the inputs of the norm bound for $D$. Let $p$ be a prime, $\lambda : \overline{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathbb{C}_p$ an embedding, and $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ a curve pair with norm of $\mathfrak{c}$ prime to $p$. Let $\Lambda = \Lambda(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ and let $t_0$ be an unkilled two-torsion point. Let $y \in \mathbb{C}$ and $N \ge 1$ with $Ny \in \Lambda$, and assume that $p$ is split if $p \mid N$. Then for each $n \ge 0$, the number $\theta_y^{(n)}(0)$ with $\theta_y = U_y\theta_0$ is algebraic, and
$$\big|\lambda\big(\theta_y^{(n)}(0)\big)\big| \le |N|_p^{-n} \cdot \begin{cases} p^{n/4} & \text{if } p = d, \\ 1 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.torsionTranslate_theta_jets_denominator_bound` (also `GlobalTheory.TorsionJets.jet_translate_le`, a second name).

*Proof.* A cocycle identity writes $U_y\theta_0$ as a constant times $U_{y + t_0}\sigma^*$. The inputs give algebraic Taylor coefficients for these translates, so $\theta_y^{(n)}(0)$ is algebraic. Choose a genus one datum $g$ at $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ with this $\lambda$ and $|u(C)| = 1$. Then $n!\,\mathrm{coeff}_n(G^\sharp \circ \mathrm{ex}) = \varepsilon^n \lambda(G^{(n)}(0))$, and $|\varepsilon| = 1$. So it is enough to bound the jets of $G^\sharp \circ \mathrm{ex}$. If $h$ has integral coefficients, then $|i!\,\mathrm{coeff}_i(h \circ \mathrm{ex})| \le 1$ for all $i$.

*Case $p \mid N$.* Then $p$ is split. The series of $z \mapsto \theta_y(Nz)$ is $h$ times the series of $z \mapsto \theta_0(Nz)$, with $h$ integral. In the split case the series $\theta_0^\sharp$ is integral. So the jets of $\theta_y(N\,\cdot)$ have norm at most $1$. These jets are $N^n \theta_y^{(n)}(0)$ up to the unit $\varepsilon^n$, which gives the bound $|N|_p^{-n}$.

*Case $p \nmid N$.* Then $|N|_p = 1$. If $p = 2$, then $p$ is split, the series $\theta_y^\sharp$ is integral, and its jets have norm at most $1$. If $p \neq 2$, then $f = E^{-1}\theta_y^\sharp$ is integral, and
$$\theta_y^\sharp \circ \mathrm{ex} = \exp(-\varphi \circ \mathrm{ex}) \cdot (f \circ \mathrm{ex}).$$
Let $s = p^{1/4}$ if $p = d$ and $s = 1$ otherwise. By the theorem on jets of $\varphi$, $|j_i| \le 1$ for $i \neq 2$ and $|j_2| \le s^2$. A lemma on exponentials of jets then bounds the $n$-th jet of the product by $s^n$. $\square$

**Theorem 1.8.9 (product of translates).** Keep the hypotheses on $D$, $p$, $\lambda$, $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ and $t_0$. Let $V$ be the complex space of the matrices of size $d' \times a$ over $E$ under the CM type, and let $b_1, \dots, b_r$ be an orthonormal basis of matrices for the trace form, with a local basis condition at $p$ for the standard lattice $Y$. Put $\Theta(z) = \prod_j \theta_0(\ell_j(z))$, where $\ell_j$ is the coordinate along $b_j$. Let $s \in V$ and $N \ge 1$ with $Ns$ in the group generated by $\Lambda\, b_j$, and assume that $p$ is split if $p \mid N$. Then for $y_1, \dots, y_n \in Y$, the derivative $D^n(U_s\Theta)(0)(y_1, \dots, y_n)$ is algebraic and satisfies the same bound as in the last theorem.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.jets_torsion_translates_cm_theta_product` (also `GlobalTheory.ProductJets.jets_translate_product`, a second name).

*Proof.* Write $Ns = \sum_j w_j b_j$ with $w_j \in \Lambda$. Then $s = \sum_j (w_j/N)\, b_j$. Orthonormality gives $\ell_j(b_k) = \delta_{jk}$, and the factor of $U_s$ splits along the coordinates. So
$$U_s\Theta(z) = \prod_j U_{w_j/N}\theta_0\big(\ell_j(z)\big).$$
Each factor satisfies the last theorem with $y = w_j/N$, since $N y = w_j \in \Lambda$. Let $A = |N|_p^{-1} p^{1/4}$ if $p = d$ and $A = |N|_p^{-1}$ otherwise. The $k$-th derivative of each factor is algebraic of norm at most $A^k$. The local basis condition gives $|\lambda(\ell_j(y_i))| \le 1$ with $\ell_j(y_i)$ algebraic. The Leibniz rule and the ultrametric inequality give the bound $A^n$ for the product. $\square$

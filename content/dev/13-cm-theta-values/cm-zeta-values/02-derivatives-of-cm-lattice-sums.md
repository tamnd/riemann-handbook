## Derivatives of CM lattice sums

To a lattice $L$ in a CM field $E$ and a CM type we attach sums $\sum_x \prod_i \varphi_i(x)^{-k-\kappa_i}\,\overline{\varphi_i(x)}^{\kappa_i}$. We deform the lattice in a polydisc and show that the derivatives at the origin of the deformed Eisenstein sum are these lattice sums, up to an explicit factor. We also prove a homothety rule and class invariance, and compute the Riemann form of the CM torus. In weight $2$ the Eisenstein sum does not converge, and we use the [Theorem on Hecke's series of weight two](/entry/hecke-weight-two-of-fe/). The results are used in the next section, where the lattice sums are written as jets of Eisenstein series, and in the proof of the [toric denominator bounds](/entry/join-out-of-five/).

### CM types, orders and lattices

Let $E$ be a CM field, $F$ its maximal totally real subfield, and $x \mapsto \bar x$ complex conjugation on $E$ ([complex multiplication](/learn/elliptic-curves/#complex-multiplication)). Let $g = [F : \mathbb{Q}]$, and let $\mathcal{O}_F$, $\mathcal{O}_E$ be the rings of integers.

**Definition 1.2.1 (CM type).** A *CM type* of $E$ is a family $\varphi = (\varphi_i)_{i \in I}$ of embeddings $E \to \mathbb{C}$ such that every embedding $\sigma : E \to \mathbb{C}$ is equal to $\varphi_i$ or to $\bar\varphi_i$ for exactly one index $i$.

So $|I| = g$, and for $x \in E$ we have $|N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(x)| = \prod_i |\varphi_i(x)|^2$.

Let $\mathbb{A}_f$ be the ring of finite adeles of $E$. For a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{c}$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$ let $\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{c}}$ be the subring of $E$ generated by $\mathcal{O}_F$ and $\mathfrak{c}\mathcal{O}_E$, and let $\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{c}}$ be the closure of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{c}}$ in $\mathbb{A}_f$.

**Definition 1.2.2 (CM lattice).** For $y \in \mathbb{A}_f^\times$ let

$$L(\mathfrak{c}, y) = \{x \in E : x\, y^{-1} \in \widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{c}}\}.$$

The class group of the order is $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{c}}) = \mathbb{A}_f^\times / E^\times \widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{c}}^\times$.

The lattice $L(\mathfrak{c}, y)$ is an $\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{c}}$-module. If $u \in \widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{c}}^\times$ then $L(\mathfrak{c}, yu) = L(\mathfrak{c}, y)$. If $\alpha \in E^\times$ then $L(\mathfrak{c}, \alpha y) = \alpha L(\mathfrak{c}, y)$. So the lattice up to homothety depends only on the class of $y$ in $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{c}})$.

### Lattice sums

Fix $k \geq 1$ and $\kappa = (\kappa_i) \in \mathbb{N}^I$. For $x \in E^\times$ and $\sigma \in \mathbb{C}$ put

$$t_{k,\kappa}(x, \sigma) = \prod_{i \in I} \varphi_i(x)^{-(k + \kappa_i)}\, \overline{\varphi_i(x)}^{\,\kappa_i}\, |\varphi_i(x)|^{-2\sigma}.$$

For a unit $u \in \mathcal{O}_F^\times$ we get $t_{k,\kappa}(ux, \sigma) = N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(u)^{-k}\,|N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(u)|^{-2\sigma}\, t_{k,\kappa}(x, \sigma)$, which is $t_{k,\kappa}(x, \sigma)$ when $k$ is even. In this section $k = 2s$ is even, and sums run over $(L \setminus 0)/\mathcal{O}_F^\times$.

**Definition 1.2.3 (lattice sum).** Let $L \subset E$. An entire function $S$ is a *lattice sum of type $(k, \kappa)$ for $L$* if for every $\sigma$ with $k/2 + \operatorname{Re}\sigma > 1$ the series $\sum_{x \in (L \setminus 0)/\mathcal{O}_F^\times} t_{k,\kappa}(x, \sigma)$ converges to $S(\sigma)$.

By the identity theorem a lattice sum is unique if it exists. For $k \geq 4$ the series converges at $\sigma = 0$, and $S(0)$ is its sum.

### The defining sum on the polydisc

Let $D = \{z \in \mathbb{C}^I : |z_i| < 1 \text{ for all } i\}$ be the unit polydisc. For $z \in D$ and $x \in E$ put

$$\lambda_i(x; z) = \varphi_i(x) + z_i\, \overline{\varphi_i(x)}.$$

For $x \neq 0$ this is not zero, since $|z_i| < 1$. At $z = 0$ the form $x \mapsto \lambda_i(x; 0)$ is the $i$-th coordinate of the CM torus $\mathbb{C}^I/\varphi(L)$. For other $z$ it is the coordinate of a deformed complex structure on the same real torus.

Let $c_k = ((k-1)!)^g\, d_F^{(k-1)/2}$ with $d_F = |\operatorname{disc} F|$, as in the previous section.

**Definition 1.2.4 (defining sum).** Let $L \subset E$ and $k \geq 1$. A function $g : \mathbb{C}^I \to \mathbb{C}$ is a *defining sum of weight $k$ for $L$* if for each $z \in D$ there is $G \in \mathbb{C}$ with $g(z) = c_k G$, where:

1. if $k \neq 2$, the series $\sum_{x \in (L \setminus 0)/\mathcal{O}_F^\times} \prod_i \lambda_i(x; z)^{-k}$ converges to $G$;
2. if $k = 2$, there is a function $H$, holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}\sigma > -\tfrac12$, such that $\sum_{x} \prod_i \lambda_i(x; z)^{-2}|\lambda_i(x; z)|^{-2\sigma}$ converges to $H(\sigma)$ for $\operatorname{Re}\sigma > 0$, and $G = H(0)$.

For $i \in I$ let $\partial_i$ be the complex derivative in the direction of the $i$-th basis vector, and for a word $l = (i_1, \dots, i_m)$ in $I$ let $\partial_l = \partial_{i_1} \cdots \partial_{i_m}$. For $\kappa \in \mathbb{N}^I$ put

$$r_k(\kappa) = \prod_{i \in I} (-1)^{\kappa_i}\, k (k+1) \cdots (k + \kappa_i - 1).$$

For example, $\partial_i^{m}\, \lambda_i(x; z)^{-k}$ at $z = 0$ equals $(-1)^m k(k+1)\cdots(k+m-1)\, \overline{\varphi_i(x)}^{\,m}\, \varphi_i(x)^{-k-m}$.

**Definition 1.2.5 (derivative formula).** We say that the *derivative formula of weight $k$ holds for $L$* if a defining sum of weight $k$ for $L$ exists, and every defining sum $g$ of weight $k$ for $L$ satisfies:

1. $g$ is holomorphic on $D$;
2. for every $\kappa \in \mathbb{N}^I$ and every word $l$ in which each $i$ occurs $\kappa_i$ times, and every lattice sum $S$ of type $(k, \kappa)$ for $L$, we have $\partial_l g(0) = c_k\, r_k(\kappa)\, S(0)$;
3. if $k \geq 4$, then for the same $\kappa$ and $l$ the series $S_0 = \sum_x \prod_i \varphi_i(x)^{-(k+\kappa_i)}\overline{\varphi_i(x)}^{\kappa_i}$ converges and $\partial_l g(0) = c_k\, r_k(\kappa)\, S_0$.

### Homothety and class invariance

For $\alpha \in E^\times$ put

$$\lambda_\infty(\alpha) = \prod_{i \in I} \varphi_i(\alpha)^{k + \kappa_i}\, \overline{\varphi_i(\alpha)}^{\,-\kappa_i}.$$

**Definition 1.2.6 (homothety rule).** The *homothety rule of weight $k$ holds for $L$* if for all $\kappa$, all $\alpha \in E^\times$ and every lattice sum $S$ of type $(k, \kappa)$ for $L$:

1. $\sigma \mapsto \lambda_\infty(\alpha)^{-1}\, |N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}\alpha|^{-\sigma}\, S(\sigma)$ is a lattice sum of type $(k, \kappa)$ for $\alpha L$;
2. every lattice sum $S'$ of type $(k, \kappa)$ for $\alpha L$ has $S'(0) = \lambda_\infty(\alpha)^{-1} S(0)$.

We need [Hecke characters](/learn/class-field-theory/#hecke-characters). A *Hecke character* of $E$ is a continuous homomorphism $\chi : \mathbb{A}_E^\times \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ that is trivial on $E^\times$. Write $\chi_f$ for its restriction to $\mathbb{A}_f^\times$ and $\chi_\infty$ for its restriction to $E^\times$ embedded at the infinite places. Then $\chi_f(\alpha)\chi_\infty(\alpha) = 1$ for $\alpha \in E^\times$. For $y \in \mathbb{A}_f^\times$ let $\|y\| = \prod_v N v^{-\operatorname{ord}_v(y_v)}$, which is $|N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}\alpha|^{-1}$ for $y = \alpha \in E^\times$, and put

$$\lambda_f(\chi, s, y) = \chi_f(y)\, \|y\|^s.$$

Let $\mathfrak{f}$ be an ideal of $\mathcal{O}_F$ and $Q$ a finite set of primes of $F$. We say that $\mathfrak{f}$ is an *admissible conductor* if $\mathfrak{f} \neq 0, \mathcal{O}_F$ and every prime $v \mid \mathfrak{f}$ splits in $E$, divides $\mathfrak{f}$ at most twice, and lies over a prime $l \neq 2$ with $l \nmid \operatorname{disc} E$ such that every prime of $E$ over $l$ has norm $l$. We say that $Q$ is *admissible for $\mathfrak{f}$* if each $q \in Q$ splits in $E$ and does not divide $\mathfrak{f}$. Put $\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{f}, Q) = \mathfrak{f}\prod_{q \in Q} q$.

Let $s \geq 1$. A Hecke character $\chi$ is *of type $(s, \kappa)$ for $(\mathfrak{f}, Q)$* if it is unitary, trivial on the ideles of $F$, satisfies

$$\chi_\infty(\alpha) = \prod_{i \in I} \Bigl(\frac{\varphi_i(\alpha)}{|\varphi_i(\alpha)|}\Bigr)^{2(\kappa_i + s)} \qquad (\alpha \in E^\times),$$

has level at most $1$ at the primes over $Q$, and has exact level $\operatorname{ord}_v \mathfrak{f}$ at the primes over $v \notin Q$.

**Definition 1.2.7 (class invariance).** *Class invariance holds in weight $2s$* if for all admissible $\mathfrak{f}$ and $Q$, all $\kappa$, every Hecke character $\chi$ of type $(s, \kappa)$ for $(\mathfrak{f}, Q)$, all $y, y' \in \mathbb{A}_f^\times$ with the same class in $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{f},Q)})$, and all lattice sums $S$ for $L(\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{f},Q), y)$ and $S'$ for $L(\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{f},Q), y')$ of type $(2s, \kappa)$,

$$\lambda_f(\chi, s, y)^{-1} S(0) = \lambda_f(\chi, s, y')^{-1} S'(0).$$

So the normalized value $\lambda_f(\chi, s, y)^{-1}S(0)$ is a function on the class group.

### The Riemann form

Let $V^* = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E, \mathbb{C})$. This is a complex vector space of dimension $2g$ with basis $e_i = \varphi_i$ and $f_i = \bar\varphi_i$; it is the first cohomology of the CM torus with complex coefficients. Let $P_i = \mathbb{C}e_i + \mathbb{C}f_i$. Let $\xi \in E^\times$ with $\bar\xi = -\xi$. The form $R_\xi(x, y) = \operatorname{tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\xi x \bar y)$ is a nondegenerate $\mathbb{Q}$-bilinear form on $E$. For $x \in E$ let $R_\xi(x, \cdot) \in V^*$ be the map $y \mapsto R_\xi(x, y)$.

**Definition 1.2.8 (dual form).** A $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear form $B$ on $V^*$ is *dual to $R_\xi$* if $B(R_\xi(x, \cdot), R_\xi(y, \cdot)) = R_\xi(x, y)$ for all $x, y \in E$.

For $\varepsilon = \pm 1$ let $\langle a, b \rangle_\varepsilon = \varepsilon (2\pi i)^{-1} B(a, b)$.

### The theorem

**Theorem 1.2.9 (derivatives of CM lattice sums).** Assume that for every totally real field $F'$ of degree at least $2$ and all fractional ideals $\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}$ of $F'$ there is a continuation $G$ of Hecke's series for $(\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b})$ such that $G(0, \cdot)$ is holomorphic and has the $q$-expansion of weight $2$. Let $E$ be a CM field with $[F : \mathbb{Q}] \geq 2$ and let $\varphi$ be a CM type of $E$. Then:

1. for every $s \geq 1$, every nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{c}$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$ and every $y \in \mathbb{A}_f^\times$, the derivative formula and the homothety rule of weight $2s$ hold for $L(\mathfrak{c}, y)$;
2. for every $s \geq 1$, class invariance holds in weight $2s$;
3. for every $\xi \in E^\times$ with $\bar\xi = -\xi$ there is exactly one form $B$ dual to $R_\xi$; for this $B$ and $\varepsilon = \pm 1$ we have $\langle P_i, P_j \rangle_\varepsilon = 0$ for $i \neq j$ and $\langle e_i, f_i \rangle_\varepsilon = \varepsilon\, \varphi_i(\xi)^{-1}/(2\pi i)$;
4. let $d \geq 1$ and $\delta \in E$ with $\delta^2 = -d$ and $\varphi_i(\delta) = i\sqrt d$ for all $i$, and let $\eta = \pm 1$. Then there is exactly one form $B$ dual to $R_{\eta\delta^{-1}}$; for this $B$ and $\varepsilon = \pm 1$ the planes $P_i$ are orthogonal and $\langle e_i, f_i \rangle_\varepsilon = \varepsilon\eta\sqrt d/(2\pi)$.

*Proof.* *Part 3.* The forms $R_\xi(x, \cdot)$ span $V^*$, since $R_\xi$ is nondegenerate and $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} V^* = [E : \mathbb{Q}]$. So a dual form is unique. In the basis $(e_i, f_i)$ we have $R_\xi(x, \cdot) = \sum_i \bigl(\varphi_i(\xi x) f_i + \overline{\varphi_i(\xi x)}\, e_i\bigr)$, since the trace is the sum over all embeddings. Define $B$ by $B(e_i, f_i) = \varphi_i(\xi)^{-1}$, $B(f_i, e_i) = -\varphi_i(\xi)^{-1}$, and $0$ on all other pairs of basis vectors. Using $\overline{\varphi_i(\xi)} = -\varphi_i(\xi)$ one checks that $B(R_\xi(x, \cdot), R_\xi(y, \cdot)) = \sum_i \bigl(\varphi_i(\xi x \bar y) + \overline{\varphi_i(\xi x \bar y)}\bigr) = R_\xi(x, y)$. This gives existence and the values.

*Part 4.* Apply part 3 to $\xi = \eta\delta^{-1}$. Then $\varphi_i(\xi)^{-1} = \eta\, i\sqrt d$, and $\varepsilon\,\eta\, i\sqrt d/(2\pi i) = \varepsilon\eta\sqrt d/(2\pi)$.

*Homothety.* Let $\alpha \in E^\times$. The map $x \mapsto \alpha x$ is a bijection from $(L \setminus 0)/\mathcal{O}_F^\times$ to $(\alpha L \setminus 0)/\mathcal{O}_F^\times$, and $t_{k,\kappa}(\alpha x, \sigma) = \lambda_\infty(\alpha)^{-1} \prod_i |\varphi_i(\alpha)|^{-2\sigma}\, t_{k,\kappa}(x, \sigma)$. Since $\prod_i |\varphi_i(\alpha)|^2 = |N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}\alpha|$, the first claim follows. The second follows from the uniqueness of lattice sums.

*Class invariance.* If $y$ and $y'$ have the same class, then $y' = \alpha y u$ with $\alpha \in E^\times$ and $u \in \widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{c}}^\times$, so $L(\mathfrak{c}, y') = \alpha L(\mathfrak{c}, y)$, and the homothety rule gives $S'(0) = \lambda_\infty(\alpha)^{-1} S(0)$. The conditions on the levels of $\chi$ give $\chi_f(u) = 1$, and $\|u\| = 1$. Since $\chi$ is trivial on $E^\times$, $\chi_f(\alpha) = \chi_\infty(\alpha)^{-1}$, and $\|\alpha\| = |N\alpha|^{-1}$. The infinity type of $\chi$ gives $\lambda_\infty(\alpha) = \chi_\infty(\alpha)\, |N\alpha|^{s}$: indeed both sides equal $\prod_i \varphi_i(\alpha)^{\kappa_i + 2s}\, \overline{\varphi_i(\alpha)}^{-\kappa_i}$. So $\lambda_f(\chi, s, y') = \chi_\infty(\alpha)^{-1}|N\alpha|^{-s}\lambda_f(\chi, s, y) = \lambda_\infty(\alpha)^{-1}\lambda_f(\chi, s, y)$, and the two normalized values agree.

*Derivative formula for $k = 2s \geq 4$.* Let $g$ be a defining sum. The series $\sum_x \prod_i \lambda_i(x; z)^{-k}$ converges absolutely and locally uniformly on $D$, since $|\lambda_i(x; z)| \geq (1 - |z_i|)|\varphi_i(x)|$ and $\sum_x |N x|^{-k/2}$ converges for $k > 2$. So $g$ is holomorphic on $D$, and we may differentiate term by term. By the formula for $\partial_i^m \lambda_i^{-k}$ above, $\partial_l g(0) = c_k\, r_k(\kappa)\, S_0$. For a lattice sum $S$ of type $(k, \kappa)$, the point $\sigma = 0$ lies in the region $k/2 + \operatorname{Re}\sigma > 1$, so $S(0) = S_0$.

*Derivative formula for $k = 2$.* Choose $\theta \in E \setminus F$ with $\operatorname{Im}\varphi_i(\theta) > 0$ for all $i$. By the Steinitz theorem for the $\mathcal{O}_F$-module $L$, there are fractional ideals $\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}$ of $F$ and $a_0 \in F$ with $L = \{a + b(\theta + a_0) : a \in \mathfrak{a}^\vee, b \in \mathfrak{b}\}$; replace $\theta$ by $\theta + a_0$. Identify $I$ with the real embeddings of $F$ by restriction. For $z \in D$ let

$$w_i(z) = \frac{\varphi_i(\theta) + z_i\overline{\varphi_i(\theta)}}{1 + z_i}.$$

This Cayley-type map sends $D$ into $\mathbb{H}^g$. For $x = a + b\theta$ we get $\lambda_i(x; z) = (1 + z_i)(a^{(i)} + b^{(i)} w_i(z))$. Let $G$ be a continuation of Hecke's series for $(\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b})$ as in the assumption, with $G(0, \cdot)$ holomorphic, and put $h(z) = \prod_i (1 + z_i)/(2\pi i)$ and

$$K(\sigma, z) = h(z)^{-2}\, |h(z)|^{-2\sigma}\, G(\sigma, w(z)).$$

Comparing terms, for $\operatorname{Re}\sigma > 0$ the series $\sum_x \prod_i \lambda_i(x; z)^{-2}|\lambda_i(x; z)|^{-2\sigma}$ converges to $K(\sigma, z)$, by property 1 of a continuation. Since $G$ is good, $K(\cdot, z)$ is holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}\sigma > -\tfrac12$. So $g_0 = c_2 K(0, \cdot)$ is a defining sum. Every defining sum equals $g_0$ on $D$, since $H$ is determined by its values on $\operatorname{Re}\sigma > 0$. The function $g_0$ is holomorphic on $D$, because $G(0, \cdot)$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb{H}^g$ and $w$ and $h$ are holomorphic.

For the derivatives, fix $\operatorname{Re}\sigma > 0$. Then the series for $K(\sigma, \cdot)$ may be differentiated term by term. The derivative $\partial_i^m$ of $\lambda_i^{-2}|\lambda_i|^{-2\sigma}$ in the holomorphic direction is $(-1)^m (2+\sigma)(3+\sigma)\cdots(1+\sigma+m)\, \overline{\varphi_i(x)}^{\,m}\, \lambda_i^{-2-m}|\lambda_i|^{-2\sigma}$ at $z = 0$, where $\partial_{z_i}$ is the Wirtinger derivative. Indeed $\partial_{z_i}\lambda_i = \overline{\varphi_i(x)}$, $\partial_{z_i}\bar\lambda_i = 0$, and $\lambda_i^{-2-m}|\lambda_i|^{-2\sigma} = \lambda_i^{-2-m-\sigma}\bar\lambda_i^{-\sigma}$ locally. So at $z = 0$ the $\kappa$-th derivative of $K(\sigma, \cdot)$ equals $r_{2+\sigma}(\kappa)\, \sum_x t_{2,\kappa}(x, \sigma) = r_{2+\sigma}(\kappa)\, S(\sigma)$, where $r_{2+\sigma}$ is the rising factor with $k$ replaced by $2 + \sigma$. Both sides are holomorphic in $\sigma$ on $\operatorname{Re}\sigma > -\tfrac12$: the left side because $K$ is built from a good family, the right side because $S$ is entire. By the identity theorem they agree at $\sigma = 0$. As $K(0, \cdot)$ is holomorphic, its Wirtinger derivatives are its complex derivatives, which gives $\partial_l g_0(0) = c_2\, r_2(\kappa)\, S(0)$. The clause for $k \geq 4$ is empty when $k = 2$. $\square$

**Remark 1.2.10 (Formalization).** The hypothesis of the theorem is the conclusion of the [Theorem 1.1.10](/entry/hecke-weight-two-of-fe/) of the previous section, quantified over all totally real fields of degree at least $2$; it is used only in weight $2$. Both names have this hypothesis; the first is proved from the second by `first | exact @…`. In weight $2$ the clause for $k \geq 4$ is vacuous ([CmLatticeDerivClassInvarianceOfHecke.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/CmLatticeDerivClassInvarianceOfHecke.lean#L176)). The type of a Hecke character, its level conditions and its infinity type are as stated here; the constant $c_k$ uses the degree and discriminant of $F$, not of $E$.

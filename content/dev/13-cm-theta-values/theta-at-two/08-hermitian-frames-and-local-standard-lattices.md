## Hermitian frames and local standard lattices

This section proves two facts on hermitian lattices with complex multiplication by $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$. The first gives, at a prime $l \geq 5$ inert in this field, a frame for the fourth power of a lattice that is unitary for a Frobenius lift over the maximal unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_l$. The [theorem on integral inert kernels](/entry/integral-inert-of-approx-basis/) uses it. The second says that a self-dual lattice can be moved by a global isometry to a lattice that agrees with the standard lattice at a given prime $l$. The [row theorem of the unitary hypotheses](/entry/all-rows-of-open0-alt/) uses it.

We keep the CM data of the [theorem on integral jets at two](/entry/jets-two/): integers $a_0 > 0$, $b_0$, $c_0$ and a prime $d$ with $$4 a_0 c_0 = b_0^2 + d, \qquad a_0 \text{ odd}, \qquad d \equiv 7 \pmod 8,$$ a number $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$, $\lambda \neq 0$, $\vartheta = (b_0 + i\sqrt d)/2$ and $L = \lambda(\mathbb{Z} a_0 + \mathbb{Z} \vartheta)$. We write $\sqrt{-d}$ for $i \sqrt d$.

**Definition 8.8.1 (Standard hermitian space).** Let $A = |\operatorname{Im}(\bar\omega_1 \omega_2)|$ for the basis $\omega_1 = \lambda a_0$, $\omega_2 = \lambda \vartheta$ of $L$; it is the covolume of $L$. For a finite set $m$, the *standard form* on $\mathbb{C}^m$ is $H_L(z, w) = A^{-1} \sum_i z_i \bar w_i$, and the *standard lattice* is $L^m$. For a form $H$ on a complex vector space and a subgroup $\Lambda$, the *dual* of $\Lambda$ is the set of $x$ with $\operatorname{Im} H(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}$ for all $y \in \Lambda$; $\Lambda$ is *self-dual* if it equals its dual. A linear map $g$ is an *isometry* from $H$ to $H'$ if $H'(gx, gy) = H(x, y)$ for all $x, y$. For subgroups $\Lambda_1, \Lambda_2$ we write $[\Lambda_1 \mid \Lambda_2] = [\Lambda_2 : \Lambda_1 \cap \Lambda_2]$ if this index is finite, and $[\Lambda_1 \mid \Lambda_2] = 0$ otherwise.

The lattice $L^m$ is self-dual for $H_L$: on one coordinate $\operatorname{Im} H_L(\omega_1, \omega_2) = \pm 1$. The numbers $[L^m \mid \Lambda]$ and $[\Lambda \mid L^m]$ are both nonzero exactly when $\Lambda$ and $L^m$ are commensurable.

**Definition 8.8.2 (Unitary matrices over the unramified field).** Let $l$ be a prime. Let $K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$ be the closure in $\mathbb{C}_l$ of the field generated by the roots of unity of order prime to $l$, with the absolute value of $\mathbb{C}_l$. A *Frobenius lift* on $K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$ is a ring map $\sigma$ with $l > 2$, $|l| = l^{-1}$, $|a| \in l^{\mathbb{Z}}$ for $a \neq 0$, and $|\sigma(a) - a^l| \leq l^{-1}$ for $|a| \leq 1$, as in the [kernel bound](/entry/formalism-l2-th1-d-kernel-of-f/). For a finite set $X$, a matrix $\gamma \in M_X(K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l)$ is *$\sigma$-unitary* if $|\gamma_{ab}| \leq 1$ and $\sigma^2(\gamma_{ab}) = \gamma_{ab}$ for all $a, b$, and $$\sum_{k \in X} \sigma(\gamma_{ka})\, \gamma_{kb} = \begin{cases} 1, & a = b, \\ 0, & a \neq b. \end{cases}$$

For a matrix $P \in M_I(R)$ we write $P^{\oplus 4} \in M_{4 \times I}(R)$ for the block diagonal matrix with four copies of $P$, and for $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ we let $S_k \in M_{4 \times I}$ be the matrix with entry $1$ at each position $((1, i), (k, i))$ and $0$ elsewhere. It moves block $k$ to block $1$.

**Lemma 8.8.3 (Frobenius on the square root).** Let $l \geq 5$ be a prime with $l \nmid d$ such that $-d$ is not a square modulo $l$. Let $\iota : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}_l$ be a field isomorphism and $\sigma$ a Frobenius lift on $K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$. Then $|\sigma(x)| = |x|$ for all $x$, and there is $\delta \in K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$ with $\delta = \iota(\sqrt{-d})$ and $\sigma(\delta) = -\delta$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.Th1.Top.FrP.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.HermFrame.frobStep`.

*Proof.* If $|a| = 1$, then $|\sigma(a) - a^l| < 1 = |a^l|$ gives $|\sigma(a)| = 1$. A nonzero $a$ has $|a| = l^k$, so $|l^k a| = 1$ and $|\sigma(a)| = |l|^{-k} |\sigma(l^k a)| = l^k$. The element $\delta = \iota(\sqrt{-d})$ is a root of $X^2 + d$, which is separable modulo $l$ as $l \nmid 2d$. By Hensel's lemma it lies in the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_l$, which is generated by roots of unity of order $l^2 - 1$; so $\delta \in K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$. The element $\sigma(\delta)$ is also a root of $X^2 + d$, so $\sigma(\delta) = \pm \delta$. Modulo the maximal ideal, $\sigma(\delta) \equiv \delta^l = (-d)^{(l-1)/2} \delta \equiv -\delta$ by Euler's criterion, since $-d$ is not a square modulo $l$. As $|2\delta| = 1$, $\delta \not\equiv -\delta$, so $\sigma(\delta) = -\delta$. $\square$

**Theorem 8.8.4 (Hermitian frame at an inert prime).** Let $a_0, b_0, c_0, d, \lambda, \vartheta, L$ be as above. Let $l \geq 5$ be a prime with $l \nmid d$ such that $-d$ is not a square modulo $l$. Let $\iota : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}_l$ be a field isomorphism and $\sigma$ a Frobenius lift on $K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$. Let $I$ be a finite set, $h \in M_I(\mathbb{C})$, $\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb{C}^I$ a subgroup, and $g_0, g_1$ linear automorphisms of $\mathbb{C}^{4 \times I}$. Assume:

1. $g_0$ is an isometry from the form of $h^{\oplus 4}$ to $H_L$, $\Lambda$ is self-dual for the form of $h$, $\vartheta \Lambda \subseteq \Lambda$, and $[L^{4 \times I} \mid g_0 \Lambda^4] \neq 0$, $[g_0 \Lambda^4 \mid L^{4 \times I}] \neq 0$;
2. $g_1$ is an isometry from the form of $h^{\oplus 4}$ to $H_L$, and $l \nmid [L^{4 \times I} \mid g_1 \Lambda^4]$.

Then there are a $\sigma$-unitary $\gamma \in M_{4 \times I}(K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l)$ and $B' \in M_I(\mathbb{C}_l)$ with $$\sum_{j \in I} B'_{ij}\, \gamma_{(a, j), w} = \iota\big((g_1^{-1} e_w)_{(a, i)}\big)$$ for all $a \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, $i \in I$ and $w \in 4 \times I$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.Th1.Top.frameExistsH` (also `Formalism.L2.Th1.Top.FrP.hermFrame_holds`, a second name).

In matrix form the conclusion reads $\iota(G) = (B')^{\oplus 4} \gamma$, where $G$ is the matrix of $g_1^{-1}$. So up to a block diagonal factor, the frame $g_1^{-1}$ is unitary and integral over $K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$.

*Proof.* The proof has a rational step, the lemma above, a local step and an assembly. We state the two steps that we take as given.

Rational step. Under the hypotheses of the theorem there are $P_0 \in M_I(\mathbb{C})$, rational matrices $M_1, M_2 \in M_{4 \times I}(\mathbb{Q})$ and $R_1, R_2 \in M_I(\mathbb{Q})$, and $c \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $|c|_l = 1$, such that, with $\Gamma_\pm = M_1 \pm \sqrt{-d}\, M_2$:

- $G = \lambda^{-1} P_0^{\oplus 4}\, \Gamma_+$;
- $\Gamma_+^{\mathsf T} (R_1 + \sqrt{-d} R_2)^{\oplus 4}\, \Gamma_- = c \cdot 1$;
- for each $k$ there are $U_1, U_2 \in M_{4 \times I}(\mathbb{Q})$ with $l$-integral entries and $S_k \Gamma_+ = \Gamma_+ (U_1 + \sqrt{-d} U_2)$.

Local step. Let $l \geq 5$ with $l \nmid d$ and $-d$ not a square modulo $l$. Let $\sigma$ be a ring map of $K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$ with $|\sigma(x)| = |x|$ for all $x$, and $\delta \in K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$ with $\delta^2 = -d$ and $\sigma(\delta) = -\delta$. Let $M_1, M_2, R_1, R_2, c$ be rational with $|c|_l = 1$, put $\Gamma^\delta_\pm = M_1 \pm \delta M_2$, and assume the second and third conditions above with $\delta$ in place of $\sqrt{-d}$. Then there are $T, T' \in M_I(K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l)$ with $T T' = 1$ such that $T'^{\oplus 4}\, \Gamma^\delta_+$ is $\sigma$-unitary.

Assembly. Take the data of the rational step. By the lemma there is $\delta \in K^{\mathrm{ur}}_l$ with $\delta = \iota(\sqrt{-d})$, $\sigma(\delta) = -\delta$, and $\sigma$ is isometric. Since $\iota$ is a field map, $\delta^2 = \iota(-d) = -d$. Applying $\iota$ to the relations of the rational step turns $\sqrt{-d}$ into $\delta$ and leaves the rational matrices fixed, so $\iota(\Gamma_\pm) = \Gamma^\delta_\pm$ and the two relations hold with $\delta$. The local step gives $T$ and $T'$. Put $$\gamma = T'^{\oplus 4}\, \Gamma^\delta_+, \qquad B' = \iota(\lambda)^{-1}\, \iota(P_0)\, T.$$ Then $\gamma$ is $\sigma$-unitary, and $$\begin{aligned} (B')^{\oplus 4} \gamma &= \iota(\lambda)^{-1}\, \iota(P_0)^{\oplus 4}\, (T T')^{\oplus 4}\, \Gamma^\delta_+ \\ &= \iota\big(\lambda^{-1} P_0^{\oplus 4}\, \Gamma_+\big) = \iota(G). \end{aligned}$$ The entry at $((a, i), w)$ of the left side is $\sum_j B'_{ij} \gamma_{(a,j), w}$, and the entry of $\iota(G)$ is $\iota((g_1^{-1} e_w)_{(a,i)})$. $\square$

**Theorem 8.8.5 (Local standard lattice).** Let $a_0, b_0, c_0, d, \lambda, \vartheta, L$ be as above, $m$ a finite set and $\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb{C}^m$ a subgroup. Assume that $\Lambda$ is self-dual for $H_L$, that $\vartheta \Lambda \subseteq \Lambda$, and that $[L^m \mid \Lambda] \neq 0$ and $[\Lambda \mid L^m] \neq 0$. Then for every prime $l$ there is a linear automorphism $\delta$ of $\mathbb{C}^m$ such that:

1. $\delta$ is an isometry of $H_L$;
2. $\delta x = x$ implies $x = 0$;
3. $[L^m \mid \delta \Lambda] \neq 0$ and $l \nmid [L^m \mid \delta\Lambda]$;
4. $[\delta \Lambda \mid L^m] \neq 0$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.Th1.H.localStandard`.

*Proof.* This is [Theorem 4.5.5 of Volume 10](/entry/unimodular-local-standard/). $\square$

The meaning is local. Let $\Lambda' = \delta\Lambda$. By 3 and 4, $\Lambda'$ and $L^m$ are commensurable, and $\Lambda' / (\Lambda' \cap L^m)$ has order prime to $l$. So $\Lambda' \otimes \mathbb{Z}_l \subseteq L^m \otimes \mathbb{Z}_l$. Both lattices are self-dual, $\Lambda'$ because $\delta$ is an isometry, and duality reverses inclusions. Hence $\Lambda' \otimes \mathbb{Z}_l = L^m \otimes \mathbb{Z}_l$: the lattice $\delta \Lambda$ is standard at $l$. Condition 2 says that $1$ is not an eigenvalue of $\delta$.

**Remark 8.8.6 (Formalization).** The frame theorem has two statements, `FrameExistsH` in [StatementK.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/UniformGaussModels/StatementK.lean#L12) and `HermFrame` in [IsUnitaryK.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/UniformGaussModels/OrthogonalGaussModels/HermitianBlockFrames/IsUnitaryK.lean#L144), and the proof of the first is the second. They are written over two copies of the CM data: the file IsUnitaryK.lean redefines the CM conditions, the lattice $L$ and the Frobenius lift, which also exist in QuadraticSemicharactersFactAltForm.lean and IsLawOfLog.lean. The automorphism $g_0$ does not occur in the conclusion; it is only passed to the rational step. The proof of `hermFrame_holds` is `hermFrame_of_locCore` applied to `local_core'`; the rational step `ratStep`, the lemma `frobStep` and the local step `local_core'` were read as statements only, and the proof of the lemma above is ours. The local step needs only that $\sigma$ is isometric, not that it lifts Frobenius. Condition 2 of the local standard lattice is used with the kernel ratio of [QuadraticSemicharactersFactAltForm.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/QuadraticSemicharacters/QuadraticSemicharactersFactAltForm.lean#L34), which asks that two frames differ on every nonzero vector.

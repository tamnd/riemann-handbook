## Multiplicative charts at split primes

Let $C$ be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by the ring of integers of $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, and let $q$ be a prime that splits in $K$. This section proves that the formal parameter of a good model of $C$ over $\mathbb{C}_q$ becomes integral after the substitution $z = \omega \log(1 + X)$ for a suitable period $\omega \in \mathbb{C}_q$. So at a split prime the formal group of $C$ is the multiplicative group in an explicit chart. The chart is used in the [existence of periods](/entry/exists-per/), and through it in the [moments of theta translates](/entry/moments-of-translates/) at split primes. The proof combines the [height one theorem](/entry/exists-iso-multiplicative/) with the exponential law of the chart.

### Models of the CM curve over $\mathbb{C}_q$

Let $d$ be a prime with $d \equiv 7 \pmod{16}$. Put $w = (1 + i\sqrt d)/2$. Then $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z} + \mathbb{Z} w$, and $2$ splits in $K$ because $d \equiv 7 \pmod 8$.

**Definition 4.2.1.** A prime $q$ is *split for $d$* if $q = 2$ and $d \equiv 7 \pmod 8$, or if $q \neq 2$ and the Jacobi symbol $\left(\frac{-d}{q}\right)$ equals $1$.

Let $L \subset \mathbb{C}$ be a lattice, with Weierstrass function $\wp_L$ and invariants $g_2(L)$ and $g_3(L)$ ([lattices and the Weierstrass function](/learn/elliptic-curves/#lattices-and-the-weierstrass-function)).

**Definition 4.2.2.** A *model at $q$* is a tuple $(\iota, L, c, W)$, where $\iota : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}_q$ is a field isomorphism, $L$ is a lattice in $\mathbb{C}$, $c \in \mathbb{C}$, and $W$ is a Weierstrass curve over $\mathbb{C}_q$ with coefficients $a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4, a_6$ and the usual quantities $b_2$, $c_4$, $c_6$ and $\Delta$. The model is *good* if $c \neq 0$, all $a_i$ have absolute value at most $1$, $\|\Delta\| = 1$, and
$$\frac{c_4}{12} = \iota\Big(\frac{g_2(L)}{c^4}\Big), \qquad \frac{c_6}{216} = \iota\Big(\frac{g_3(L)}{c^6}\Big).$$

So $W$ has good reduction, and it is isomorphic to the curve $\mathbb{C}/c^{-1}L$ transported by $\iota$. For $z \in \mathbb{C}$ put
$$x(z) = \frac{\wp_L(z)}{c^2} - \iota^{-1}\Big(\frac{b_2}{12}\Big), \qquad y(z) = \frac12\Big(\frac{\wp_L'(z)}{c^3} - \iota^{-1}(a_1)\, x(z) - \iota^{-1}(a_3)\Big),$$
and put $u(z) = -x(z)/y(z)$ for $z \notin L$ and $u(z) = 0$ for $z \in L$. Near $0$ we have $u(z) = c z + O(z^2)$. The function $u$ is the usual formal parameter $-x/y$ of $W$ at the origin, written in the complex variable $z$.

For a function $G$ that is holomorphic at $0$, let $\mathrm{tay}(G) \in \mathbb{C}[[z]]$ be its Taylor series at $0$. For a series $\ell \in \mathbb{C}_q[[X]]$ with $\ell(0) = 0$ put
$$\widehat{G}_\ell = \iota(\mathrm{tay}(G)) \circ \ell \in \mathbb{C}_q[[X]],$$
where $\iota$ acts on the coefficients.

**Definition 4.2.3.** Let $(\iota, L, c, W)$ be a model at $q$. A series $\ell \in \mathbb{C}_q[[X]]$ is a *chart* of the model if the model is good, $\ell(0) = 0$, all coefficients of $\widehat{u}_\ell$ have absolute value at most $1$, and the coefficient of $X$ in $\widehat{u}_\ell$ has absolute value $1$. An element $\omega \in \mathbb{C}_q$ gives a *multiplicative chart* if $\ell = \omega \log(1 + X)$ is a chart, where $\log(1 + X) = \sum_{n \geq 1} (-1)^{n+1} X^n / n$.

The coefficients of $\omega \log(1+X)$ are not integral. The condition asks that the composite $\widehat{u}_\ell$ is integral with a unit linear coefficient. So $\widehat{u}_\ell$ is an isomorphism between the open unit discs in the variables $X$ and $u$.

### The theorem

**Theorem 4.2.4 (multiplicative chart at a split prime).** Let $d$ be a prime with $d \equiv 7 \pmod{16}$, and let $q$ be a prime that is split for $d$. Let $(\iota, L, c, W)$ be a good model at $q$, with $L = \mathcal{O}_K$, and assume $a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4, a_6$ are algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then there is $\omega \in \mathbb{C}_q$ that gives a multiplicative chart of the model.

Here $L = \mathcal{O}_K$ means that a complex number lies in $L$ if and only if it is of the form $m + n w$ with $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

*Proof.* Let $U = \iota(\mathrm{tay}(u)) \in \mathbb{C}_q[[X]]$. Then $U(0) = 0$.

Step 1 (the formal group). There is a formal group law $F$ over $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_q}$ with
$$F(U(X), U(Y)) = U(X + Y),$$
whose coefficients lie in the finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_q$ generated by $a_1, \dots, a_6$. This is the formal group of $W$ in the parameter $u$, and the identity says that $u$ turns addition in $z$ into the group law. The coefficients are integral because $W$ has integral coefficients. The algebraicity of the $a_i$ makes the extension finite.

Step 2 (height one). There is a series $\ell \in \mathbb{C}_q[[X]]$ with $\ell(0) = 0$ and $U(\ell(X)) = X$. So $\ell$ is the inverse series of $U$, and it is a logarithm of $F$. For every $n$ the identity of Step 1 gives $[n]_F(X) = U(n \ell(X))$. The prime $q$ splits in $K$, so there are elements $\beta \in \mathcal{O}_K$ with $\|\iota(\beta)\|$ as small as we want, and multiplication by $\beta$ maps $L$ into $L$. The series of multiplication by $\beta$ has a unit coefficient in a bounded degree. Comparing this with $[q]_F$ shows that the coefficient of $X^q$ in $[q]_F(X) = U(q\ell(X))$ has absolute value $1$, and the coefficients in degree below $q$ have absolute value less than $1$. So $F$ has height one. This is the only step that uses the hypotheses on $d$, on $q$ and on $L$.

Step 3 (the isomorphism). By the [corollary on height one](/entry/ht-one-is-gm/), there is an isomorphism $f$ from $F$ to $\hat{\mathbb{G}}_m$ over $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_q}$.

Step 4 (the scale). Put $g = f \circ U$. By Step 1 and the homomorphism law of $f$,
$$g(X + Y) = g(X) + g(Y) + g(X) g(Y).$$
Differentiate in $Y$ at $Y = 0$. This gives $g'(X) = \gamma (1 + g(X))$ with $\gamma = g'(0) \neq 0$. The series $\log(1 + X)$ satisfies $(1 + X) \frac{d}{dX}\log(1+X) = 1$. With $\omega = \gamma^{-1}$, the two differential equations give $g(\omega \log(1 + X)) = X$. So
$$U(\omega \log(1 + X)) = f^{-1}(X),$$
the inverse series of $f$. The series $f$ has integral coefficients and a unit linear coefficient, so $f^{-1}$ has the same properties.

Step 5 (the chart). The model is good by hypothesis, and $\omega \log(1 + X)$ has constant term $0$. By Step 4, $\widehat{u}_\ell = f^{-1}$ for $\ell = \omega \log(1 + X)$. Its coefficients lie in $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_q}$, so they have absolute value at most $1$. Its linear coefficient $e_1$ is a unit, so $e_1 v = 1$ for some $v \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_q}$. Then $\|e_1\|\,\|v\| = 1$ with both factors at most $1$, so $\|e_1\| = 1$. $\square$

The chart identifies the formal group of $W$ with $\hat{\mathbb{G}}_m$ by $X \mapsto \widehat{u}_\ell(X)$. A point $\zeta - 1$ with $\|\zeta - 1\| < 1$ of the multiplicative disc corresponds to the point of the curve with parameter $\widehat{u}_\ell(\zeta - 1)$.

**Remark 4.2.5 (Formalization).** The theorem is a second name for its proof, with a larger heartbeat limit ([ExistsMultiplicativeChartAtSplitPrimeStatement.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/JetHarmonics/ExistsMultiplicativeChartAtSplitPrimeStatement.lean#L6)). The hypotheses that $d$ is a prime, that $d \equiv 7 \pmod{16}$, that $q$ is split and that $L = \mathcal{O}_K$ enter only in Step 2, through one lemma.

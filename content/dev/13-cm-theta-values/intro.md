This volume proves the results on [complex multiplication](/learn/elliptic-curves/#complex-multiplication) that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. They are about the values of lattice sums, elliptic functions and theta functions at CM points, and their integrality. The volume computes derivatives of CM lattice sums from Hilbert Eisenstein series of weight two, proves that theta jets and their torsion translates are integral at split primes, at inert primes and at the prime $2$, and proves that the torsion values of the Weierstrass function are algebraic integers. It proves that theta series have rational coefficients with bounded denominators, and it bounds the valuations of CM periods. Many results start from [global Artin reciprocity](/learn/class-field-theory/#global-class-field-theory). The local work uses [formal group laws](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws) and the [Fock model](/learn/theta/#the-fock-model). Volume 1 states the classical facts on [the algebraicity and integrality of CM values](/learn/theta-functions-cm/#algebraicity-and-integrality-of-cm-values). This volume proves the forms that the proof needs.

The proof uses the volume in two places. The first is the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/) with its branch [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/). That branch needs the key rules of Chapter 1, the harmonic values of Chapter 2 and the fans of Chapter 3. Chapter 2 also gives tangent linearity to EveryCharacter and a weight lemma to the step [theta local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/). Chapter 1 also feeds [the record of published inputs](/step/published-results-eigen/). The second place is [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/). Chapters 1, 3 and 8 feed its step [the nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/). Chapter 5 proves the service row of the record in the step [the unitary hypotheses](/step/hyps-alt-def/), through Volume 15. Chapter 4 gives depth bounds and rows to Branch 2 of the same chapter of the proof. Its rows hold on classes of families in the sense of [closed](/step/closed-def/).

Chapter 1 continues Hecke's Eisenstein series of weight two to a holomorphic function with an explicit $q$-expansion, [Theorem 1.1.10](/entry/hecke-weight-two-of-fe/). From this it gets the derivatives and the class invariance of CM lattice sums, [Theorem 1.2.9](/entry/cm-lattice-deriv-class-invariance-of-hecke/), the integrality of their divided power jets, [Theorem 1.3.7](/entry/jet-int/), and their Galois conjugation, [Theorem 1.4.5](/entry/lat-conj/). These give the toric package from the published inputs, [Theorem 1.5.8](/entry/join-out-of-five/). The chapter constructs Gross's curves, with complex multiplication by $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})$, good reduction outside $p$ and minimal discriminant $-p^3$, [Theorem 1.6.5](/entry/cm-curve-good-outside-prime/). It proves the archimedean property of key rules, [Theorem 1.7.6](/entry/arch-of-rule4/), and the $p$-adic integrality of theta jets, [Theorem 1.8.4](/entry/jets-ph-all/), and of their torsion translates, [Theorem 1.8.6](/entry/transl-holds/). Then it computes the sign of Weil operators on theta vacua at inert primes, [Theorem 1.9.4](/entry/vacuum-inert/). The last section proves a class number formula for column groups, [Theorem 1.10.3](/entry/class-number-all/), builds harmonic modules in the Fock model, [Theorem 1.10.5](/entry/harmonic-module-of/), and bounds the slopes of test lattices, [Theorem 1.10.7](/entry/slope-holds/).

Chapter 2 studies harmonic values. These are harmonic polynomials evaluated on the jets of sections of a line bundle on the universal abelian scheme. The frame reading identifies the harmonic polynomials of a fixed row degree with a tensor product of dual Weyl modules, [Theorem 2.1.8](/entry/main-field/). At a complex point the chapter builds an analytic chart, [Theorem 2.2.6](/entry/exists-analytic-chart/), and shows that a harmonic value is an ordinary derivative of a product of theta functions, [Theorem 2.3.5](/entry/harm-nab-eq-poly-deriv/). It gives lower bounds for sums of squares of theta rules, [Theorem 2.4.5](/entry/theta-rule-sum-lower/). At a finite place it builds translation units, [Theorem 2.5.5](/entry/exists-tr-unit/), sums harmonic values over the kernel of a similitude, [Theorem 2.6.3](/entry/sum-hv-formal-kernel/), and proves the chart bound at the host field, [Theorem 2.7.6](/entry/ch-bound-host/). The last sections prove the Gauss jet bound for theta series of Dwork type, [Theorem 2.8.4](/entry/thm-agauss/), and give short bases for hermitian Gram forms, [Theorem 2.9.6](/entry/gram-form-phi-holds/).

Chapter 3 proves that the theta series of the development have rational coefficients and, after a fixed scalar, integral coefficients. Rationality and integrality come from Artin reciprocity, [Theorem 3.1.4](/entry/series-rat-s-and-ser-int-of-global-artin/). Central flips and half level translates give denominators that are bounded uniformly in the level, [Theorem 3.3.2](/entry/series-bound/). The chapter bounds the coefficients, [Theorem 3.4.4](/entry/xi-bound-main/), and computes the conjugate of a flat series, [Theorem 3.4.7](/entry/conj-flat-series/). The series of a rational row have full rank, [Theorem 3.5.5](/entry/rank-of-ties/), and a dictionary of definite unitary forms keeps the Satake parameters at inert places, [Theorem 3.5.6](/entry/satake-agree/). From the theorem of Bhatt and Scholze it builds Wach lattices in all layers, [Theorem 3.6.3](/entry/wach-lattice-main/). Sections 7 to 9 assemble local data: Hermitian columns in standard form, [Theorem 3.7.4](/entry/cols-tied-sym-std/), the oscillator representation, [Theorem 3.8.3](/entry/exists-is-osc/), and lifts of multiplicities at the last step, [Theorem 3.9.4](/entry/exists-lift-last/). Section 10 proves a Chern-Weil theorem for good metrics, [Theorem 3.10.3](/entry/good-chern-weil-of-good-top-chern-form/), and the existence of fine invariant fans, [Theorem 3.10.6](/entry/exists-fine-invariant-fan/).

Chapter 4 proves local bounds on depths and valuations. A formal group of height one over $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ is isomorphic to the multiplicative group, [Theorem 4.1.4](/entry/exists-iso-multiplicative/), so a CM curve has a multiplicative chart at each split prime, [Theorem 4.2.4](/entry/exists-m-chart/). The chapter computes the moments of translates of a CM theta function, [Theorem 4.3.8](/entry/moments-of-translates/), and gets lattices at the leaves, [Theorem 4.4.6](/entry/leaves-sup-span-lemma-s-of-form-tie-k/). It builds packages of row data at good primes, [Theorem 4.5.7](/entry/good-prime-packages/), and turns them into depth bounds at a leaf, [Theorem 4.6.7](/entry/leaf-depth-all-run/). Five kernel laws give sharp valuations of columns at level primes, [Theorem 4.7.7](/entry/row-level-valuations/). The later sections prove the existence of period data, [Theorem 4.8.3](/entry/exists-per/), the finiteness of moments, [Theorem 4.9.5](/entry/moments-fin/), the integral moments of jets, [Theorem 4.10.5](/entry/jet-moments/), the nondegeneracy of biextensions at integral charts, [Theorem 4.13.5](/entry/ndg-at-chart/), and the odd block supply, [Theorem 4.16.3](/entry/odd-block-supply/).

Chapter 5 proves the service row of the unitary hypotheses from global Artin reciprocity, [Theorem 5.6.5](/entry/serv-h-row-of-artin/). Artin reciprocity gives good models at every prime $l \ge 5$ for the curves with complex multiplication by the ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$. These models give six hypotheses, and these give the service row, [Theorem 5.6.4](/entry/serv-h-row/). On the way it proves that the joints hold over the true columns, [Theorem 5.1.5](/entry/joints-all-true/), that every joint has a site, [Theorem 5.2.8](/entry/joint-at-nonempty/), the three laws at a site, [Theorem 5.3.7](/entry/site-law-xi-in/), and local models at a constructed point, [Theorem 5.5.7](/entry/models-at-bpt/). The second part proves inert theta admissibility, [Theorem 5.13.4](/entry/inert-theta-admissibility-of-complex-multiplication/), good models from a class map, [Theorem 5.14.6](/entry/good-model/), and torsion algebraicity with the curve dictionary, [Theorem 5.15.5](/entry/torsion-taylor-algebraic-lattice-exists-of-cm/). It bounds the valuations of CM periods at type primes, [Theorem 5.16.4](/entry/bound-at-of-ties/). The last sections prove the Rallis inner product formula from the Siegel-Weil formula, [Theorem 5.21.2](/entry/rallis-conv-of-siegel-weil/), and multiplicity one for algebraic modular forms from all places, [Theorem 5.21.3](/entry/glob-det-main/).

Chapter 6 treats a prime that is inert in the CM field. At an inert prime $p \ge 5$ it proves the laws of the pairing series from the sigma function, [Theorem 6.1.10](/entry/be-winert-of-a/), the integrality of theta kernels, [Theorem 6.2.4](/entry/kernels-integral-inert/), and the first kernel law, [Theorem 6.3.4](/entry/first-kernel-small-of-frame-twist/). From these the entries of the formal group systems at inert places are admitted, [Theorem 6.4.5](/entry/admissible-entries-of-kernel-law/). At an inert prime of odd order it turns labellings of Frobenius orbits into row data, [Theorem 6.6.3](/entry/label-set-geo-of-even-of-rank-ok-of-not-dvd-discr/), and builds packages, [Theorem 6.7.2](/entry/exists-pkg-cs-of-degree-bound-at-inert-primes/). The last section proves the product law of the local measures, [Theorem 6.8.3](/entry/mu-prod-law-of/), and a jet bound at a split prime, [Theorem 6.8.5](/entry/jet-at-split-amb-of/).

Chapter 7 proves that the torsion values of elliptic functions with complex multiplication are integral. It parametrizes the kernel of reduction of a Weierstrass curve by its formal group, [Theorem 7.1.5](/entry/formal-group-points-param/), and shows that torsion points of order prime to $l$ are unramified, [Theorem 7.3.10](/entry/tor-kur/). So the values of the Weierstrass function at the torsion points of a CM lattice are algebraic integers, [Theorem 7.4.2](/entry/cm-torsion-of-point/). An integral, unramified $j$-invariant gives a good model, [Theorem 7.5.2](/entry/good-reduction-of-integral-unramified/). At an inert prime Honda theory describes the formal group, [Theorem 7.6.5](/entry/cm-integrality-three/). The chapter bounds theta jets at a split prime, [Theorem 7.7.6](/entry/jet-at-split/), and proves that pending tuples are admissible, [Theorem 7.8.4](/entry/pending-tuple-admissible/). The last sections prove the genus theorem for unimodular Hermitian lattices, [Theorem 7.9.3](/entry/unimod-genus-iff/), and the density of the lowest $K$-type of a discrete series, [Theorem 7.10.2](/entry/dens-low-model/).

Chapter 8 proves that theta functions of CM lattices have integral jets at the prime $2$. It starts from the Frobenius-Stickelberger formula for three points, [Theorem 8.1.8](/entry/frob-stick/), and pulls it back along the logarithm of the formal group. This gives the integrality of the theta function of a half period, [Theorem 8.4.8](/entry/th0-two-analytic/), and its form with complex multiplication, [Theorem 8.5.8](/entry/th0-two-cm/). Section 6 turns this into integral jets at $2$, [Theorem 8.6.9](/entry/jets-two/). The last sections prove a kernel bound for the formal group law of height two, [Theorem 8.7.7](/entry/formalism-l2-th1-d-kernel-of-f/), hermitian frames at an inert prime, [Theorem 8.8.4](/entry/herm-frame-holds/), and local standard lattices, [Theorem 8.8.5](/entry/local-standard/).

Chapters 1 to 3 do not use Chapters 4 to 8, and Chapters 1 and 2 do not use each other. Chapter 3 uses a law of Chapter 1 for its family of theta vectors, Theorem 3.8.8, and the harmonic polynomials of Chapter 2 for its harmonic record, Theorem 3.8.7. Chapters 7 and 8 use no other chapter of this volume. Chapter 6 uses the pending tuples and split jets of Chapter 7, the hermitian frames of Chapter 8 and the biextensions of Section 13 of Chapter 4. Chapter 4 uses the columns of Chapter 3, the packages of Chapter 6 and the class map of Chapter 7. Chapter 5 uses Chapters 6, 7 and 8. So a reader can take Chapters 1 to 3 in order, and then Chapters 7 and 8, then Section 13 of Chapter 4, then Chapter 6, then the rest of Chapter 4, and Chapter 5 last.

## Torsion points are unramified

This section proves that a torsion point of order prime to $l$ on an elliptic curve with good reduction over the completed maximal unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_l$ has its coordinates in that field. This is the analytic form of the criterion of Néron, Ogg and Shafarevich in one direction: the torsion prime to $l$ of a curve with good reduction is unramified. The proof does not use Galois groups. It approximates the point by points with coordinates in the field, and the contraction estimate of [the previous section](/entry/formal-group-nsmul-estimate/) makes the approximation converge. The result gives [the integrality of the torsion values](/entry/cm-torsion-of-point/) of the Weierstrass function in the next section.

### The field and the curves

Let $l$ be a prime and let $\mathbb{C}_l$ be the completion of an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_l$, with $|l| = l^{-1}$ ([$p$-adic numbers](/learn/number-fields/#p-adic-numbers-and-local-fields)). Let $T \subset \mathbb{C}_l$ be the set of $0$ and the roots of unity of order prime to $l$.

**Definition 7.3.1 (The field $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$).** $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ is the closure in $\mathbb{C}_l$ of the subfield generated by $T$.

The field generated by $T$ is the maximal unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_l$ in $\mathbb{C}_l$, so $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ is its completion. It is a closed subfield of a complete field, hence complete. We use three of its properties.

**Lemma 7.3.2 (Properties of $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$).**

1. For each $x \in \mathbb{C}_l$ with $|x| \le 1$ there is $\xi \in T$ with $|x - \xi| < 1$.
2. Let $f$ be a polynomial with coefficients in $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ of absolute value at most $1$. Let $x \in \mathbb{C}_l$ with $|x| \le 1$, $f(x) = 0$ and $|f'(x)| = 1$. Then $x \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$.
3. Let $l \ne 2$, $c \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ and $y \in \mathbb{C}_l$ with $|y| = 1$ and $y^2 = c$. Then $y \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.res_full`, `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.hensel_mem`, `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.sqrt_mem`

*Proof.* (1) The residue field of $\mathbb{C}_l$ is an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_l$. Each of its nonzero elements is a root of unity of order prime to $l$, and it lifts to an element of $T$ with the same residue. (2) The polynomial $f$ has coefficients in the complete field $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$. By (1) there is $\xi \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ with $|x - \xi| < 1$; then $|f(\xi)| < 1$ and $|f'(\xi)| = 1$. Hensel's lemma in $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ gives a root $x' \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ with $|x' - \xi| < 1$, and Hensel's lemma in $\mathbb{C}_l$ says that the root near $\xi$ is unique, so $x = x'$. (3) Apply (2) to $f = X^2 - c$: here $|c| = 1$ and $|f'(y)| = |2y| = 1$. $\square$

**Definition 7.3.3 (Good pair).** Let $l$ be a prime. A pair $(g_2, g_3)$ of elements of $\mathbb{C}_l$ is a *good pair* if $l \ge 5$, $g_2, g_3 \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$, $|g_2| \le 1$, $|g_3| \le 1$ and $|g_2^3 - 27 g_3^2| = 1$. Its curve is
$$E : \ y^2 = x^3 - \tfrac{g_2}{4} x - \tfrac{g_3}{4} .$$

This is the curve of the Weierstrass function, with the point $(\wp, \wp'/2)$ ([lattices](/learn/elliptic-curves/#lattices-and-the-weierstrass-function)). Its discriminant is $\Delta = g_2^3 - 27 g_3^2$. Since $l \ge 5$, $|4| = 1$, so $E$ is an integral model with $|\Delta| = 1$: it has good reduction.

### Reduction lemmas

Let $(g_2, g_3)$ be a good pair. A point $P = (x, y)$ of $E(\mathbb{C}_l)$ is *integral* if $|x| \le 1$ and $|y| \le 1$.

**Lemma 7.3.4 (Integral points reduce to nonsingular points).** Let $W$ be an integral model over a field with an ultrametric absolute value, with $|\Delta| = 1$. Let $(x, y)$ satisfy the Weierstrass equation with $|x|, |y| \le 1$. Then
$$|2y + a_1 x + a_3| = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad |3x^2 + 2a_2 x + a_4 - a_1 y| = 1 .$$

Lean: `Formalism.L2.Th1.E.nonsingular_norm`

These are the two partial derivatives of the equation, up to sign. The lemma says that the reduced point is a nonsingular point of the reduced curve, which is nonsingular because $|\Delta| = 1$ (Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*, Proposition III.1.4).

**Lemma 7.3.5 (Three unit lemmas).** Let $W$ be as in the previous lemma. Write $\psi(P) = 2y + a_1 x + a_3$ and $\kappa(P) = 3x^2 + 2a_2 x + a_4 - a_1 y$ for $P = (x, y)$.

1. If $P$ is integral, $2P \ne O$ and $2P$ has $|x(2P)| \le 1$, then $|\psi(P)| = 1$.
2. If $P \ne O$ is integral and $2P = O$, then $\psi(P) = 0$ and $|\kappa(P)| = 1$.
3. If $P = (x_1, y_1)$ and $Q = (x_2, y_2)$ are integral, $P + Q$ and $P - Q$ are not $O$, and $|x(P + Q)| \le 1$ and $|x(P - Q)| \le 1$, then $|x_1 - x_2| = 1$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.Th1.E.UnitPsi2_of`, `Formalism.L2.Th1.E.UnitKap_of`, `Formalism.L2.Th1.E.UnitDiff_of`

*Proof.* (1) Since $2P \ne O$, $\psi(P) \ne 0$, and the slope $\lambda$ of the tangent satisfies $\lambda \psi(P) = \kappa(P)$. If $|\psi(P)| < 1$, then $|\kappa(P)| = 1$ by the previous lemma, so $|\lambda| > 1$. Then $x(2P) = \lambda^2 + a_1 \lambda - a_2 - 2x$ has absolute value $|\lambda|^2 > 1$, a contradiction. (2) $2P = O$ means $y = -y - a_1 x - a_3$, that is $\psi(P) = 0$; the previous lemma gives $|\kappa(P)| = 1$. (3) As $P \ne \pm Q$, we have $x_1 \ne x_2$. Let $\lambda_+$ and $\lambda_-$ be the slopes of the chords through $P, Q$ and through $P, -Q$. The formula $x(P \pm Q) = \lambda_\pm^2 + a_1 \lambda_\pm - a_2 - x_1 - x_2$ is a monic quadratic equation for $\lambda_\pm$ with integral coefficients, so $|\lambda_\pm| \le 1$. Subtracting the equations of $P$ and $Q$ gives
$$\psi(P) = (x_1 - x_2)(\lambda_+ + \lambda_- + a_1), \qquad \kappa(P) = (x_1 - x_2)(\lambda_+ \lambda_- + 2x_1 + x_2 + a_2) .$$
By the previous lemma one of $\psi(P)$, $\kappa(P)$ is a unit. So $|x_1 - x_2| = 1$. $\square$

**Lemma 7.3.6 (Torsion points are integral).** Let $(g_2, g_3)$ be a good pair and $E$ its curve. Let $P \ne O$ in $E(\mathbb{C}_l)$ with $NP = O$ for some $N$ with $l \nmid N$. Then $P \notin E_1$, so $P$ is integral.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.torsion_not_mem_formalGroup` (also `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.integral_of_not_mem_formalGroup`, `Formalism.ELL.formal_group_points_no_torsion`)

*Proof.* Suppose $P \in E_1$. Since $|N| = 1$, [the estimate for multiplication by $N$](/entry/formal-group-nsmul-estimate/) gives $|N u(P)| = |u(P)| \le |u(P)|^2$, because $u(NP) = u(O) = 0$. As $|u(P)| < 1$, this forces $u(P) = 0$, which is false for $P \ne O$ by [the parametrization theorem](/entry/formal-group-points-param/). So $|x| \le 1$. Then the right side of $y^2 = x^3 - \frac{g_2}{4}x - \frac{g_3}{4}$ has absolute value at most $1$, so $|y| \le 1$. $\square$

### Close points

**Lemma 7.3.7 (Distance of $x$-coordinates).** Let $W$ be an integral model of the form $y^2 = x^3 + a_4 x + a_6$. Let $A, B \ne O$ with $|y(A)|, |y(B)| \le 1$, $A + B \ne O$, $|x(A + B)| \le 1$, and $O \ne A - B \in E_1$. Then
$$|x(A) - x(B)| \le |u(A - B)| .$$

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.norm_x_sub_le_of_formalGroup` (also `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.chord_identity`)

*Proof.* For a curve $y^2 = x^3 + a_4 x + a_6$ and points with $A, B, A \pm B \ne O$, the chord formulas give the identity
$$\bigl(x(A) - x(B)\bigr)^2 \bigl(x(A + B) - x(A - B)\bigr) = -4\, y(A)\, y(B) .$$
Let $D = A - B = (x_D, y_D)$. Then $|x_D| > 1 \ge |x(A + B)|$, so $|x(A + B) - x_D| = |x_D|$. The right side has absolute value at most $1$. Hence $|x(A) - x(B)|^2 \le |x_D|^{-1} = |u(D)|^2$, by the lemma on the size of the parameter in the previous section. $\square$

**Lemma 7.3.8 (Lifting).** Let $(g_2, g_3)$ be a good pair and $P = (x, y) \in E(\mathbb{C}_l)$ with $|x| \le 1$ and $|y| = 1$. Then there is a point $Q \in E$ with coordinates in $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ and $P - Q \in E_1$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.tor_lift`

*Proof.* By part 1 of the lemma on $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$, choose $a \in T$ with $|x - a| < 1$. Put $c = a^3 - \frac{g_2}{4} a - \frac{g_3}{4} \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$. Then $|c - y^2| < 1$ and $|y^2| = 1$, so $|c| = 1$. As $\mathbb{C}_l$ is algebraically closed, $c$ has a square root $b$, with $|b| = 1$; by part 3, $b \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$. The point $Q = (a, b)$ lies on $E$ and is nonsingular, since $2b \ne 0$. If $P - Q \in E_1$, take $Q$. If $P + Q \in E_1$, take $-Q = (a, -b)$. Otherwise $P - Q$ and $P + Q$ are integral and not $O$ (a point outside $E_1$ is not $O$), and the third unit lemma gives $|x - a| = 1$, a contradiction. $\square$

### The theorems

**Theorem 7.3.9 (The $x$-coordinate of a torsion point).** Let $(g_2, g_3)$ be a good pair and $E$ its curve. Let $P \in E(\mathbb{C}_l)$, and let $N > 0$ with $l \nmid N$, $NP = O$ and $2P \ne O$. Then $x(P) \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.tor_x_mem`

*Proof.* Since $2P \ne O$, $P \ne O$. By the lemma on torsion points, $P$ and $2P$ are integral, and by the first unit lemma $|2y(P)| = 1$, so $|y(P)| = 1$. The lifting lemma gives $Q$ with coordinates in $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ and $D = P - Q \in E_1$. Let $M = l^{\varphi(N)}$. Then $|M| < 1$, and $M^k \equiv 1 \pmod N$ by Euler's theorem, so $M^k P = P$. Hence
$$P - M^k Q = M^k D .$$
If $M^k D = O$, then $x(M^k Q) = x(P)$. Otherwise apply the lemma on distances to $A = P$ and $B = M^k Q$. The hypotheses hold: $B = P - M^k D$ and $A + B = 2P - M^k D$ are not in $E_1$, because $P$ and $2P$ are not in $E_1$ (they are torsion of order prime to $l$) and $E_1$ is a group. So $B$ and $A + B$ are integral. The lemma gives
$$|x(P) - x(M^k Q)| \le |u(M^k D)| ,$$
and this tends to $0$ by the contraction corollary of [the previous section](/entry/formal-group-nsmul-estimate/). The points $M^k Q$ have coordinates in $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$, since the chord and tangent formulas are rational over the coefficients of $E$. So $x(P)$ is a limit of elements of the closed field $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$, and lies in it. $\square$

**Theorem 7.3.10 (Torsion points of order prime to $l$ are unramified).** Let $(g_2, g_3)$ be a good pair and $E$ its curve. Let $P \ne O$ in $E(\mathbb{C}_l)$, and let $N > 0$ with $l \nmid N$ and $NP = O$. Then $x(P) \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ and $y(P) \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.tor_kur` (also `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.L1.tor_two`)

*Proof.* Suppose first that $2P = O$. Then $y(P) = 0 \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$. By the lemma on torsion points $|x(P)| \le 1$, and by the second unit lemma $|\kappa(P)| = |3x^2 - g_2/4| = 1$. So $x(P)$ is a root of $f = X^3 - \frac{g_2}{4} X - \frac{g_3}{4}$ with $|f'(x(P))| = 1$, and part 2 of the lemma on $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ gives $x(P) \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$. Suppose now that $2P \ne O$. The previous theorem gives $x(P) \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$, and its proof gives $|y(P)| = 1$. Since $y(P)^2 = x(P)^3 - \frac{g_2}{4} x(P) - \frac{g_3}{4} \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ and $l \ne 2$, part 3 of that lemma gives $y(P) \in \breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$. $\square$

**Remark 7.3.11 (Formalization).** The field $\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ is called `MaximalUnramifiedExtension` in Lean, though it is the completion of that field: the closure is part of its definition at [Statement.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/CMGoodModels/Statement.lean#L14). The same name is defined again, independently, in other files of the development, for example [IsUnitaryK.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/UniformGaussModels/OrthogonalGaussModels/HermitianBlockFrames/IsUnitaryK.lean#L119). The unit lemmas are stated for a curve with $|a_i| \le 1$ and $|\Delta| = 1$ under a separate name from the integral models of the first section.

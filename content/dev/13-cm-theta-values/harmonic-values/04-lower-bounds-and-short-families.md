## Short harmonic families and archimedean bounds

The step [every character of literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) compares harmonic values at many places. At the archimedean places it needs three estimates. First, the space of harmonic polynomials has a spanning set of integral polynomials with small coefficients. Second, the value of a harmonic polynomial on a translated theta vacuum has an explicit upper bound. Third, the sum of the squares of a family of theta rules over all level classes has an explicit lower bound. This section proves the three estimates. They rest on [highest weights](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching), the [Fock model](/learn/theta/#the-fock-model) and the analytic theory of [theta functions on complex tori](/learn/theta-functions-cm/#theta-functions-on-complex-tori).

### Short families of harmonic polynomials

We keep the notation of the [first section](/entry/exists-io-lfrec/). Let $E$ be a CM [[number field|MWBDFS2KJ5]] of degree $2f$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and root discriminant $\delta_E = |d_E|^{1/2f}$. For a row degree $a$, put $D(a) = \sum_\tau \sum_{i < m_\tau} a(\tau, i)$, the total degree.

**Definition 2.4.1 (unimodular lattice).** An $\mathcal{O}_E$-submodule $\Lambda \subset E^n$ is *unimodular* if a vector $v \in E^n$ lies in $\Lambda$ exactly when $\sum_j \bar v_j w_j \in \mathcal{O}_E$ for every $w \in \Lambda$. This is self-duality for the standard [[Hermitian form|48P4A1CX85]].

**Definition 2.4.2 (lattice harmonic polynomials).** For an embedding $\sigma$, a row index $i$ and a vector $b \in E^n$, the *letter form* is $\ell_{\sigma, i, b} = \sum_j \sigma(b_j) X_{\sigma, i, j}$. Let $\mathcal{O}[\Lambda]$ be the ring generated by the integers of $F$ and the letter forms with $b \in \Lambda$. The set $\mathcal{H}_a(\Lambda)$ is the intersection of $\mathcal{H}_a$ with $\mathcal{O}[\Lambda]$.

**Theorem 2.4.3 (short harmonic families).** Let $\Lambda \subset E^n$ be a finitely generated unimodular lattice, and let $p + q \leq n$. Let $a$ be a row degree and let $L \geq 2$ be a real number with
$$\log \delta_E \leq 2L, \quad \log f \leq 2L, \quad \log(1 + D(a)) \leq 2L, \quad \log n \leq L.$$
Then there is a finite set $S \subset \mathcal{H}_a(\Lambda)$ such that:

1. for every $Q \in S$ and every embedding $\sigma' : F \to \mathbb{C}$, $\log \sum_m |\sigma'(Q_m)| \leq 140\,(p+q)\, L\, D(a)$;
2. $\mathcal{H}_a$ lies in the $F$-span of $S$;
3. $\# S \leq \bigl((2(p+q)+1) D(a) + 1\bigr)^{2 f n (2p + q)}$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Harm.exists_short_harmL_family` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_short_harmonic_family_unimodular`, a second name).

*Proof.* A dictionary of letters renames $\mathcal{H}_a$ to the space of row highest polynomials of a type $(k, k')$ of total degree $D(a)$, or shows that $\mathcal{H}_a = 0$. In the second case take $S = \emptyset$.

If $D(a) = 0$, every element of $\mathcal{H}_a$ is a constant. Take $S = \{1\}$ if $1 \in \mathcal{H}_a$, and $S = \emptyset$ if not. In the second case a nonzero constant in $\mathcal{H}_a$ would give $1 \in \mathcal{H}_a$ after scaling, so $\mathcal{H}_a = 0$.

If $D(a) \geq 1$, unimodularity says that $\Lambda$ is integral and equal to its dual for the Hermitian form. A result on few columns then gives a finite set of pairs $s$ of vector families in $\Lambda$ such that the products of minors of letter forms built from $s$ lie in $\mathcal{O}[\Lambda]$, span the row highest polynomials over every field of characteristic $0$ with distinct embeddings, and have $\ell^1$ norm at most
$$\Bigl((p+q)\sqrt{n}\;(p+q+1)!\;\bigl((2fn)\,(2(p+q)+1) D(a)\;(2 f^{3/2} n^2 \delta_E)\bigr)^{2(p+q)+1}\Bigr)^{D(a)}.$$
The number of pairs is bounded as in claim 3. The embeddings of $E$ into $F$ given by the elements of $\Psi$ and by their conjugates are all distinct, so the span claim applies with the field $F$. Rename back. Under the hypotheses on $L$, the logarithm of the norm bound is at most $140 (p+q) L D(a)$. $\square$

### The jet of a translated vacuum

The upper bound concerns theta functions in the Fock model. Let $X$ be a CM datum of rank $r$ over $E$ with signature $p_0 + q_0 = r$, a symplectic basis of its period lattice of rank $2g$, and a bijection between $\{1, \dots, g\}$ and the pairs (embedding in $\Psi$, row). Let $n$ be even and $w \in \mathcal{O}_E$ with $w + \bar w = 1$. Let $L \subset E^r$ be a self-dual lattice, $\Lambda \subset E^n$ a lattice of the admissible family, and $v$ a vacuum vector for the tensor lattice $\mathcal{O}_E^n \otimes L$. For a point $x$ of the domain, $\Theta_v(x, \cdot)$ is the theta function of $v$ at $x$. The projection of $v$ to the vacua of $\Lambda \otimes L$ and the Heisenberg translate by $y \in M_{n \times r}(E)$ are defined in the chapter on theta vacua. We do not unfold them here.

**Theorem 2.4.4 (jets of translated vacua).** Assume that $x$ is orthogonal with all scalings equal to $t > 0$. Let $\theta$ be a normalized theta function of type $1$ at $x$ with mean $1$, and let $\kappa \in \mathbb{C}$ satisfy $\prod_{b=1}^n \theta(z_b) = \kappa\, \Theta_v(x, z)$ for all $z$. Let $\lambda > 0$ satisfy $\lambda^2 \leq H_x(u, u)$ for every nonzero $u \in L$. Let $y \in M_{n \times r}(E)$, and let $P$ be a homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$ in the harmonic variables. Let $v'$ be the translate by $y$ of the projection of $v$ to the vacua of $\Lambda \otimes L$. Then
$$\begin{aligned}
\bigl|\kappa\, \partial_P \Theta_{v'}(x, \cdot)\bigr| \cdot [\mathcal{O}_E^n : \Lambda]^{r/2} &\leq \Bigl(\sum_m |P_m|\Bigr) (\pi e t d)^{d/2} \sqrt{2} \\
&\quad \cdot \Bigl(1 + \frac{4\sqrt{2gn}\; 2 f^{3/2} n^2 \delta_E}{\sqrt{\pi}\, \lambda}\Bigr)^{gn}.
\end{aligned}$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Dict.VacJet.jet_projA_le` (also `GlobalTheory.T.jet_translate_projected_vacuum_le`, a second name).

*Proof.* If $\kappa = 0$ the left side is $0$. Assume $\kappa \neq 0$. Self-duality of $L$ gives a complex torus $T$ of dimension $g$ with the lattice of $L$, and an identification of the theta functions at $x$ with the theta functions on $T$. Under it, $\theta$ becomes a section $s \neq 0$ of $L^2$ norm $1$. The projection of $v$ is a mean over a finite set $R$ of representatives:
$$\mathrm{pr}_\Lambda(v) = \frac{1}{\# R} \sum_{y' \in R} \varepsilon(y')\, \mathrm{tr}_{y'}(v), \qquad \# R = [\mathcal{O}_E^n : \Lambda]^r,$$
where $\varepsilon$ is a sign character. So $\kappa \Theta_{v'}$ is $(\# R)^{-1}$ times a signed sum of translates of $\prod_b s(z_b)$. A bound for normalized theta values on a torus, applied to this sum, gives the factor $(\pi e t d)^{d/2}\sqrt{2}$ for a derivative of order $d$, times a count of lattice points in a ball. The count uses the minimum $\lambda$ and gives the last factor. The factor $\sqrt{\# R}$ comes from the mean. $\square$

### The lower bound for theta rules

The lower bound is a statement on theta rules: harmonic values of the canonical connection on level theta sections. A *theta rule* for a harmonic polynomial $P_1$ at a tuple of level representatives is the rule $y \mapsto \nabla_y(s)(P_1)$ of the [third section](/entry/harm-nab-eq-poly-deriv/), where $s$ is the tuple of theta sections attached to the representatives. We do not unfold the attachment.

**Theorem 2.4.5 (sum of theta rules).** Assume chart independence. Let $\nabla$ be a canonical connection, $\sigma : F \to \mathbb{C}$ a good embedding and $U$ an unfolding. Let $y = U(k, x)$, where the frame $x$ is orthogonal and $|\tau(\xi_0)| = x_R$ for every embedding $\tau$. Let $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_E$ with $\alpha \bar\alpha = M$ and $\bar{\mathfrak{M}} = (\bar\alpha)$, where $M$ and $\bar{\mathfrak{M}}$ are the integer and the ideal of the level. Let $P_1 \in \mathcal{H}_a^{\mathcal{O}}$ be a product of blocks, of total degree $D$. Assume that admissible parameters exist for rank $r$, degree $D$, level $M$ and dimension $m$, and that $p \leq m$ or $q \leq m$. Then there are a torus $T$ of rank $r$ and parameters $\Pi$ with $\Pi.q = M$, $\Pi.m = m$ and a fixed phase, such that for every family $(f_t)$ of theta rules for $P_1$ indexed by $t \in (\Lambda / \bar{\mathfrak{M}}\Lambda)^n$,
$$\Bigl(\frac{2 x_R}{M}\Bigr)^{D} J_n(\Pi) \leq \sum_t |f_t(y)|^2.$$
Here $J_n(\Pi) = 4^{-n} \bigl(6 (2d^\ast)^{d^\ast/2}\bigr)^{-n} 2^{-(r+2) D} e^{-2\pi n C_\chi \mathrm{ph} / M}$ with $d^\ast = 2fm$.

*Proof.* Choose a unit frame at $y$. Its Hermitian matrix is $2 x_R$ times the identity. Build a torus $T$ with the same rows and check that its phase is the phase of $\Pi$. Rename the variables of $P_1$ to the blocks of the torus. The theta function of the unit frame gives a section $s \neq 0$ of $L^2$ norm $1$ on $T$. Choose a finite set $K$ of level translates and the parameters $\Pi$. The torus bound of the Cauchy estimate gives
$$J_n(\Pi) \leq \sum_{b \in K} \bigl|\partial_{P_1} (T_b s)(0)\bigr|^2.$$
Each point of $K$ comes from some $t$, so the sum over $K$ is at most the sum over all $t$, if each term for $t$ is equal to the term for its image. For this, write $f_t(y)$ as $M^{-D}$ times a polynomial derivative of a product of translated theta functions, by the [Theorem on harmonic values](/entry/harm-nab-eq-poly-deriv/). Move it to $T$ with a factor $\kappa$ with $|\kappa|^2 = M^{D}$. The norm of the result gives the factor $(2 x_R / M)^D$. $\square$

**Remark 2.4.6.** The three estimates have the same shape as the classical bounds for theta series of positive Hermitian lattices: an upper bound by a lattice point count in a ball, and a lower bound by orthogonality of translates in $L^2$ of a torus. See Mumford, *Tata Lectures on Theta I*, Chapter II.

## Level charts and multiplicity lifts

This section gives two more inputs of the column data of Section 7. The first input gives level charts at the middle steps $1 \le i < m$ of a chain: for each chart label, an open compact subgroup with a character of finite order, together with points and stabilizers that read the classes of the chain. The second input is the multiplicity lift at the last step of a chain. There, both unitary groups are definite at every archimedean place, and the theta integral against an automorphic form factors through the archimedean intertwiner. This gives a linear map between the two multiplicity spaces. The background is [Haar measure](/learn/analysis/#haar-measure), [compact groups](/learn/representations/#compact-groups), [highest weights](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching) and [the Fock model](/learn/theta/#the-fock-model).

### Level charts at the middle steps

We use the notation of Section 7: a base datum $B$ with fields $F \subset E$ and rank $m$, a datum $X$ over $B$, an admissible true column choice $I$, the pinning of $(B, X)$, laws $\ell$ and later data $Z$, and the assembled datum $D = D(I, \ell, Z)$. Fix a word $u$, an index $a$ of the character tuples of $I$, and a level $i$ with $1 \le i < m$. Let $Q$ be the set of finite places $v$ of $F$ under the places of the tuple $a$.

At level $i$, the chain of $u$ and $a$ has a finite set of classes $c$. Each class has a representative point, a lattice in $E^{N_i}$ and a stabilizer. The *chart label* of a class is the pair of its first component and its coset; let $\beta$ run over the labels. A *level chart* for $\beta$ is a lattice $L_\beta$, an open compact subgroup $U_\beta$ of $\mathrm{GL}_{N_i}(\mathbb{A}_{E,f})$ and a character $\chi_\beta : U_\beta \to \mathbb{C}^\times$, together with a point $y_c$ and a stabilizer map for each class $c$ with label $\beta$. with the conditions of a level chart at the level $T = T(u)$ of the word.

A family $k = (k_\beta)$ of adelic elements of $\mathrm{GL}_{N_i}$ is *admissible* if three conditions hold for each $\beta$. First, $k_\beta$ is a point of a decorated lattice whose lattice is the standard lattice $O_E^{N_i}$. Second, the local component of $k_\beta$ at $v$ is $1$ at each place $v$ that does not divide $T(u)$, is not under $Q$, and satisfies the condition on the generators $g_j$ (when $a$ is not the base index). Third, at each place $P$ above $T(u)$, the last row of $k_\beta^{-1}$ reduces modulo $P$ to the row that is $0$ off the diagonal and has the conjugate $\overline{\pi_T(u)}$ on the diagonal.

**Lemma 3.9.1 (One chart).** Let $I$, $(B, X)$, $\ell$, $Z$, $u$, $a$, $i$ and $Q$ be as above, and let $k$ satisfy the three conditions. Let $g_c$ be an adelic point of the decorated lattice of each class $c$, fix a class $c_0$, and let $p(c)$ hold if and only if $c$ has the first component of $c_0$, and the same reduction and the same coset as $c_0$. Then there are a level chart $K$ on the classes $c$ with $p(c)$ and a local datum $q$ at the places of $Q$, such that:

1. the lattice of $K$ is the base lattice at the places above $T(u)$;
2. the group of $K$ is $k\,U_q\,k^{-1}$, where $U_q$ is the level subgroup of the local datum $q$ and of the group of the characters of $c_0$;
3. on $k\,z\,k^{-1}$, the character of $K$ is the level type of the characters of $c_0$ times the inverse of the character of $q$, evaluated at $z$;
4. the character of $K$ has finite order;
5. the point of $K$ at $c$ is $g_c\,k^{-1}$, and the stabilizer map of $K$ at $c$ is the stabilizer map of the chain.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Mem.Col2.ChExM.chart_of`

*Proof.* The column spaces are standard in frame coordinates, and the base lattice has index $T^{2f}$ in its dual, where $f$ is the number of the base. The places of $Q$ are disjoint from the bad places of the primes of $B$, and they are not fixed by complex conjugation. So the cut of the level family of $c_0$ by its coset is open, and this gives the local datum $q$.

The third condition on $k$ is rewritten with the diagonal units $\mathrm{dia}_T(u, P)$, the classes of $\overline{\pi_T(u)}$ modulo $P$, which are units because $\overline{\pi_T(u)}$ is not in $P$. Then the development gives the facts on $k$: it is unitary, it is integral at the places above $T(u)$, and it fixes the base lattice there. The set of bad places of the datum is the union of the bad places of the primes and the places under $Q$. So $k$ is $1$ at each good place prime to $T(u)$. The classes with $p(c)$ are in bijection with the cosets of the level subgroup, and the stabilizer maps are injective with the correct range. A general chart construction over a number field then gives the level, the points and the stabilizers with the properties 1 to 5. $\square$

**Theorem 3.9.2 (Level charts at the middle steps).** Let $I$ be admissible, $(B, X)$ pinned, $\ell$ laws, $Z$ later data, $u$ a word, $a$ an index, $1 \le i < m$, and $Q$ the set of places under $a$. Let $k = (k_\beta)$ be admissible. Then there are level charts $K_\beta$ for all labels $\beta$, such that the column choice $I$ *reads* the charts at $(u, a, i)$, and each character $\chi_\beta$ has finite order.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Mem.Col2.ChExM.exists_charts`

Here *reads* means: two classes have the same label exactly when they have the same first component and the same coset; every label is the label of a class; an element is in the lattice of $K_\beta$ exactly when it is in the base lattice; $y_c\,k_\beta$ is a point of the class $c$; $x \in U_\beta$ exactly when the moved point $y_c\,x\,k_\beta$ lies over a representative of the same class; $\chi_\beta$ is given on these elements by the characters of the class; and the stabilizer maps are those of the chain.

*Proof.* The first condition of the reading holds by the definition of labels. The map from classes to labels is surjective; choose a class $s(\beta)$ with label $\beta$. The coset is determined by the reduction and the coset of the point, so the predicate "the label of $c$ is $\beta$" is the predicate $p$ of the lemma for $c_0 = s(\beta)$. Choose an adelic point $g_c$ of the decorated lattice of each class. Apply [the lemma on one chart](/entry/ch-ex-m-chart-of/) at each label $\beta$, with $k_\beta$, the points $g$ and $c_0 = s(\beta)$. This gives $K_\beta$ and $q_\beta$.

We check the reading. The lattice condition is part 1. By part 5, $y_c\,k_\beta = g_c$, a point of $c$. For $x$ in the adelic group, $y_c\,x\,k_\beta = g_c\,(k_\beta^{-1} x\,k_\beta)$. By part 2, $x \in U_\beta$ exactly when $k_\beta^{-1} x\,k_\beta \in U_{q_\beta}$. Since $c$ has the same first component, reduction and coset as $s(\beta)$, the level fact of the development identifies membership in $U_{q_\beta}$ with the condition that the moved point lies over the same class. For the characters, part 3 gives $\chi_\beta(x)$ in terms of $z = k_\beta^{-1} x\,k_\beta$, and the character fact of the development identifies this value with the character of the class. The stabilizers are part 5, and the finite order of $\chi_\beta$ is part 4. $\square$

### The multiplicity lift at the last step

Let $V$ and $W$ be Hermitian spaces over $E$ of the datum, of ranks $n = \operatorname{rk} V$ and $n' = \operatorname{rk} W$, with $n \le n'$, and assume that both are positive definite at every archimedean place. Let $\omega$ be a Weil representation of $\mathrm{U}(V)(\mathbb{A}) \times \mathrm{U}(W)(\mathbb{A})$ on the functions on $V \otimes W$, with an additive character $\psi$ and splitting characters $\chi_S$ and $\chi_T$. At each archimedean place $\tau$, the *local models* $M_\tau$ and $M'_\tau$ are finite dimensional irreducible representations of the compact groups $\mathrm{U}(n)$ and $\mathrm{U}(n')$. Let $\mathrm{Pol}$ be the space of polynomials in the entries of $n' \times n$ matrices at all $\tau$. A function $f$ is a theta function with a Fock vacuum at each $\tau$, and $\mathrm{pt}(P)$ is the pure tensor of $f$ with the polynomial $P$ at the archimedean places.

An *intertwiner* is a nonzero bilinear map $\mathrm{It} : \mathrm{Pol} \times \bigotimes_\tau M'_\tau \to \bigotimes_\tau M_\tau$ that is bounded, equivariant for $\mathrm{U}(V)$ at each $\tau$ (on the group and on the Lie algebra), and invariant for $\mathrm{U}(W)$ at each $\tau$. Let $J$ be a set of finite adelic points of $\mathrm{U}(V)$ with a function $\chi$, such that $\omega(\kappa, 1) f = \chi(\kappa) f$ for $\kappa \in J$, and such that $J$ contains the points of an open subgroup on which $\chi = 1$. The *multiplicity space* $\mathrm{Mult}(M, J, \chi)$ is the space of linear maps $F$ from $\bigotimes_\tau M_\tau$ to the functions on $\mathrm{U}(V)(\mathbb{A})$ that are left invariant under the rational points, satisfy $F(x)(g \kappa) = \chi(\kappa) F(x)(g)$ for $\kappa \in J$, are continuous, and are equivariant for the archimedean groups. Let $\nu$ be a Haar measure on $\mathrm{U}(W)(\mathbb{A})$, let $\mathcal{F}$ be a fundamental domain for the rational points, and let $C$ be a subspace of a multiplicity space for $W$. The *theta integral* of $F \in C$ is
$$\Theta(\varphi, F, v)(g) = \int_{\mathcal{F}} \theta_\omega(g, h)(\mathrm{pt}(\varphi))\,F(v)(h)\,d\nu(h).$$

**Theorem 3.9.3 (Multiplicity lift, definite case).** Under these assumptions there is a linear map $\iota : C \to \mathrm{Mult}(M, J, \chi)$ such that, for all $F \in C$, $\varphi \in \mathrm{Pol}$ and $v$, the integrand of $\Theta(\varphi, F, v)(g)$ is integrable on $\mathcal{F}$, and
$$\Theta(\varphi, F, v)(g) = \iota(F)\bigl(\mathrm{It}(\varphi, v)\bigr)(g).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.DefLift.ioExists_definite`

*Proof.* The integrand is integrable and the integral is continuous in $g$, by the definite theory. Extend the integral over $\mathcal{F}$ to a linear form on all functions. Then $B(\varphi, v, F) = \Theta(\varphi, F, v)$ is a trilinear map.

The real points act on $\mathrm{pt}(P)$ by substitution in $P$, up to nonzero scalars: $g \in \mathrm{U}(n)$ by columns and $u \in \mathrm{U}(n')$ by rows. With this, the equivariance of $\mathrm{It}$ becomes a column law $\mathrm{It}(c_\tau(g)\,g \cdot_{\mathrm{col}} P, x) = \pi_\tau(g)\,\mathrm{It}(P, x)$, and its invariance becomes a row law. The theta integral is invariant under the real translate of $h$, since $F$ is equivariant. So $B$ satisfies the same row law as $\mathrm{It}$.

The key step is a factorization: if $n \le n'$ and the representation of $\prod_\tau \mathrm{U}(n')$ is irreducible, then every bilinear map $B$ that satisfies the row law factors as $B = L \circ \mathrm{It}$ for a linear map $L$. To prove it, apply a linear form $l$ to $B$. Then $l \circ B$ is an invariant bilinear form on $\mathrm{Pol} \times X$. We show that $l \circ B$ lies in the space $U$ of the forms $l' \circ \mathrm{It}$. Through the Fischer pairing on the matrix entry polynomials, an invariant bilinear form is the same as an intertwiner from $X$ to $\mathrm{Pol}$. If an intertwiner is not in $U$, a column stable complement of $U$ in the intertwiners is nonzero. Both this complement and $U$ contain an intertwiner of column highest weight. For $n \le n'$, two intertwiners of column highest weight are proportional, so the complement meets $U$, which is a contradiction. This is a form of Howe duality for the compact pair $(\mathrm{U}(n), \mathrm{U}(n'))$.

Put $\iota(F) = L(\cdot)(F)$, as a function of the vector of $\bigotimes_\tau M_\tau$. Then the formula holds. It remains to show that $\iota(F)$ is in $\mathrm{Mult}(M, J, \chi)$. The images $\mathrm{It}(P, x)$ span $\bigotimes_\tau M_\tau$, because the span is stable under all $\pi_\tau(g)$, and the representation is irreducible. So each property can be checked on vectors $\mathrm{It}(P, x)$. Right translation by $\kappa \in J$ moves $\mathrm{pt}(P)$ to $\omega(\kappa, 1)\,\mathrm{pt}(P) = \chi(\kappa)\,\mathrm{pt}(P)$, since $\kappa$ is finite and acts on $f$ by $\chi(\kappa)$. Left invariance under the rational points is the invariance of the theta kernel, continuity is that of the integral, and equivariance at $\tau$ comes from the column law. $\square$

**Theorem 3.9.4 (Multiplicity lift at the last step).** Let $R$ be a real datum for a family of level data whose level characters are those of the base tuple, with models, Weil representations, vectors, pure tensors and intertwiners at each step of the chain. Then there is a choice of lifts $\iota_t$, one for each tuple $t$, such that the datum $R$ with these lifts satisfies the lift condition at the last step of the chain.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.DefLift.exists_lift_last` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_multiplicityLift_lastChainStep`, a second name).

*Proof.* Fix $t$. The definite end of the chain gives a multiplicity space $C$ for the last space $X$, inside a multiplicity space with a set $J'$ and a function $\chi'$. Apply [the definite lift](/entry/io-exists-definite/) with $V = Y_{m-1}$, the space at step $m - 1$, and $W = X$. The rank condition $\operatorname{rk} Y_{m-1} \le \operatorname{rk} X$ follows from the rank formulas of the datum. Both models are definite: the parameters at a step $j \ge m - 1$ have only plus signs. The Fock vacuum at each $\tau$ comes from the step vector, which is an orthogonal sum of idempotent parts with a theta part at infinity. The character condition on $J$ is the source law of the pair at the last step, with the characters of the flag level. The open subgroup, the measure and the fundamental domain come from the models. This gives $\iota_t$. $\square$

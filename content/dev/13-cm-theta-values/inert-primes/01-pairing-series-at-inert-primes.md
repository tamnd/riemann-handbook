## The pairing series at an inert prime

This section studies an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic field $K$, over $\mathbb{C}_p$, at a prime $p \ge 5$ that is inert in $K$. From the sigma function of the curve we form a power series $\varphi$ in the parameter of the formal group, and from $\varphi$ a series $\beta$ in two variables, the pairing series. The main theorem says that $\beta$ has coefficients in the unramified part of $\mathbb{C}_p$ up to a power of $p$, that it is biadditive, and that the action of $\mathcal{O}_K$ in one variable moves to the conjugate action in the other variable. All three laws hold up to a series whose exponential is integral over the unramified ring. The theorem is the input for the pairing property in [the first kernel law](/entry/first-kernel-small-of-frame/) of Section 3, and the curves are those of [the chapter on elliptic torsion](/entry/good-model/).

### The unramified ring and congruence of series

Let $p$ be a prime and $\mathbb{C}_p$ the completion of an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (see [$p$-adic numbers and local fields](/learn/number-fields/#p-adic-numbers-and-local-fields)). Let $W_p \subset \mathbb{C}_p$ be the closure of the subring generated by the roots of unity of order prime to $p$. It is the ring of integers of the completion of the maximal unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

**Definition 6.1.1 (Unramified up to $p$).** An element $x \in \mathbb{C}_p$ is *unramified up to $p$* if $p^k x \in W_p$ for some $k \ge 0$. A power series over $\mathbb{C}_p$ in any set of variables is *$W$-integral* if all its coefficients lie in $W_p$, and it is *unramified up to $p$* if all its coefficients are.

A series with integral coefficients is a series whose coefficients all have norm at most $1$. For two series $f, g$ in the same variables we compare $f - g$ through its exponential. This is the right test: a series $\varphi$ with $\exp(\varphi)$ integral is the logarithm of an integral series, and in theta functions the integrality statements are statements on exponentials.

**Definition 6.1.2 (Congruence of series).** Let $f$ and $g$ be power series over $\mathbb{C}_p$. We write $f \simeq g$ if $f - g$ has constant term $0$ and $\exp(f - g)$ has integral coefficients. We write $f \simeq_W g$ if $f - g$ has constant term $0$ and $\exp(f - g)$ is $W$-integral.

### The curve and its pairing series

Let $K$ be the imaginary quadratic field of a fixed datum, with its integer $d$, its ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, its conjugation $a \mapsto \bar a$, and a fixed embedding $\varphi_0$ of $K$ into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \subset \mathbb{C}$. For a lattice $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}$ let $g_2(\Lambda) = 60\,G_4(\Lambda)$ and $g_3(\Lambda) = 140\,G_6(\Lambda)$, and $\Delta(\Lambda) = g_2^3 - 27 g_3^2$, as in [lattices and the Weierstrass function](/learn/elliptic-curves/#lattices-and-the-weierstrass-function).

**Definition 6.1.3 (Curve ideal).** A *curve ideal* is a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{c}$ of $\mathcal{O}_K$ whose norm is prime to $2d$. For $\delta \in \mathbb{C}$ let $\Lambda(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ be the closure of $\delta\,\varphi_0(\mathfrak{c})$ in $\mathbb{C}$. We say that $\delta$ is a *twelfth root for* $\mathfrak{c}$ if $\delta^{12} = \Delta(\Lambda(\mathfrak{c}, 1))$.

**Definition 6.1.4 (Curve data at $p$).** *Curve data at $p$* consist of a ring map $\lambda : \overline{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathbb{C}_p$, a subgroup $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}$, a change of variables $C$ of Weierstrass curves over $\mathbb{C}_p$, and two power series $w$ and $e$ over $\mathbb{C}_p$ in one variable. The curve of the data is $E = C \cdot E_0$, where $E_0$ is $y^2 = x^3 - \tfrac14 \lambda g_2(\Lambda)\,x - \tfrac14 \lambda g_3(\Lambda)$. Its formal logarithm $\ell$ is formed from $E$ and $w$. The data are *good* if $E$ has integral coefficients and a discriminant of norm $1$, if $w$ is a parameter series for $E$, and if $e(0) = 0$ and $\ell(e(X)) = X$.

For good data, $e$ is the formal exponential, and the formal group law is $X +_E Y = e(\ell(X) + \ell(Y))$ (see [formal group laws](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws) and [the invariant derivation and the logarithm](/learn/formal-groups/#the-invariant-derivation-and-the-logarithm)). For $c \in \mathbb{C}_p$ put $[c](X) = e(c\,\ell(X))$. Let $\sigma^*$ be an odd function with derivative $1$ at $0$ that is periodic up to sign for the lattice, and let $\varepsilon = u^{-1}$ for the scale $u$ of $C$. Put
$$\varphi(X) = -\log \frac{\sigma^*(\varepsilon\,\ell(X))}{\varepsilon X}, \qquad \beta(X, Y) = \varphi(X +_E Y) - \varphi(X) - \varphi(Y).$$
We call $\varphi$ the *sigma series* and $\beta$ the *pairing series*. In the variables $z = \ell(X)$ and $w = \ell(Y)$, the series $\beta$ compares $\sigma^*(\varepsilon(z + w))$ with $\sigma^*(\varepsilon z)\,\sigma^*(\varepsilon w)$, so it measures how far the sigma function is from a character.

**Definition 6.1.5 (Inert setting).** Data $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta, g)$ are *at* $(K, p)$ if $\mathfrak{c}$ is a curve ideal with norm prime to $p$, $\delta$ is a twelfth root for $\mathfrak{c}$, the lattice of $g$ is $\Lambda(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$, and $g$ is good. The prime $p$ is *inert* if the Jacobi symbol $\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)$ equals $-1$. The data are *over $W$* if the five Weierstrass coefficients of $E$ lie in $W_p$.

Put $\tau(a) = \lambda\varphi_0(a)$ for $a \in K$, and let $\mathcal{O}_K^{(p)} \subset \mathbb{C}_p^2$ be the closure of the set of pairs $(\tau(a), \tau(\bar a))$ with $a \in \mathcal{O}_K$.

### The theorem

**Theorem 6.1.6 (Laws of the pairing series).** Let $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta, g)$ be at $(K, p)$ with $p \ge 5$ inert, and over $W$. Then:
1. every coefficient of $\beta(X_0, X_1)$ is unramified up to $p$;
2. $\beta(X_0 +_E X_1, X_2) \simeq_W \beta(X_0, X_2) + \beta(X_1, X_2)$ and $\beta(X_0, X_1 +_E X_2) \simeq_W \beta(X_0, X_1) + \beta(X_0, X_2)$;
3. for every $(\alpha, \alpha') \in \mathcal{O}_K^{(p)}$, $\beta([\alpha] X_0, X_1) \simeq_W \beta(X_0, [\alpha'] X_1)$.

We prove the theorem from four facts. Each fact is a statement for all data at $(K, p)$ with $p \ge 5$ inert and over $W$.

- (S) *Sigma series over $W$*: the sigma series is unramified up to $p$.
- (E) *Integral endomorphisms*: for every $(\alpha, \alpha') \in \mathcal{O}_K^{(p)}$, the series $[\alpha](X)$ has integral coefficients.
- (B) *Biadditivity*: the two laws of part 2 hold with $\simeq$ in place of $\simeq_W$.
- (N) *Norm law*: for $a \in \mathcal{O}_K$ with norm prime to $p$, $\varphi([\tau(a)] X) \simeq \tau(a)\tau(\bar a)\,\varphi(X)$.

**Proposition 6.1.7 (Pairing laws from four facts).** Facts (S), (E), (B) and (N) imply the conclusion of the theorem for all data at $(K, p)$ with $p \ge 5$ inert and over $W$.

*Proof.* Fix the data. The formal group law $X +_E Y$ has integral coefficients, and, since the curve is over $W$, $W$-integral coefficients. First we extend the norm law from the elements $(\tau(a), \tau(\bar a))$ with norm prime to $p$ to all of $\mathcal{O}_K^{(p)}$. The set $\mathcal{O}_K^{(p)}$ is closed under addition, multiplication and the swap of the two entries. A pair in $\mathcal{O}_K^{(p)}$ whose two entries are units is a limit of pairs $(\tau(a), \tau(\bar a))$ with the norm of $a$ prime to $p$, and the set of pairs that satisfy the norm law is closed; so the norm law holds for pairs with unit entries. Since $p \ne 2$, every pair in $\mathcal{O}_K^{(p)}$ is a sum of two pairs in $\mathcal{O}_K^{(p)}$ with unit entries. With (E) and the biadditivity (B), the norm law passes to such sums. This gives $\varphi([\alpha] X) \simeq \alpha\alpha'\,\varphi(X)$ for all $(\alpha, \alpha') \in \mathcal{O}_K^{(p)}$. Next, the norm law and biadditivity give the conjugation law $\beta([\alpha] X_0, X_1) \simeq \beta(X_0, [\alpha'] X_1)$: expand $\beta$ at $X_0 +_E X_1$ under multiplication by $\alpha$ and compare the two sides with the norm law. Since $p$ is inert, the entries of a pair in $\mathcal{O}_K^{(p)}$ lie in $W_p$; with (E) this makes $[\alpha](X)$ a $W$-integral series. By (S), $\varphi$ is unramified up to $p$, and so $\beta(a, b)$ is unramified up to $p$ for any two series $a, b$ with $W$-integral coefficients and constant term $0$. This gives part 1. Last, if $A$ and $B$ are unramified up to $p$ and $A \simeq B$, then $\exp(A - B)$ is integral and its coefficients are unramified up to $p$, so they lie in $W_p$, and $A \simeq_W B$. Apply this to the two laws of (B) and to the conjugation law. This gives parts 2 and 3. $\square$

### Facts (S) and (B) from the sigma function

The two facts (S) and (B) follow from one statement on the sigma function.

**Definition 6.1.8 (Sigma curve condition).** The *sigma curve condition* says: for all data at $(K, p)$ with $p \ge 5$ inert and over $W$, there is $s_2$ in the maximal unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ in $\mathbb{C}_p$ such that the rescaled series $\sigma^*(u^{-1}X)$ equals $u^{-1}\,\sigma_E(s_2; X)$, where $\sigma_E(s_2; X)$ is the sigma series of the Weierstrass curve $E$ with second parameter $s_2$.

**Proposition 6.1.9 (Pairing laws from the sigma function).** The sigma curve condition, (E) and (N) imply the conclusion of the theorem.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.KerInert.beWInert_of_sigma`

*Proof.* Under the sigma curve condition, $\varphi$ equals the sigma series of $E$ in its own parameters. The coefficients of this series are polynomials in the Weierstrass coefficients of $E$ and in $s_2$, with rational coefficients. These all lie in the maximal unramified extension, and every element of this field is unramified up to $p$. This gives (S). The coboundary of the sigma series of $E$ is biadditive up to $\simeq$, and this gives (B). Now apply [the proposition on four facts](/entry/be-winert-of/). $\square$

The sigma curve condition in turn follows from two facts on algebraicity: the invariants $g_2$ and $g_3$ of the lattices in question are algebraic numbers, and the second Eisenstein value $s_2(\Lambda)$ is algebraic. These are classical for lattices with complex multiplication (see [algebraicity and integrality of CM values](/learn/theta-functions-cm/#algebraicity-and-integrality-of-cm-values)).

**Theorem 6.1.10 (Pairing laws from algebraicity).** Assume that $g_2(\Lambda)$ and $g_3(\Lambda)$ are algebraic, that $s_2(\Lambda)$ is algebraic, and that (E) and (N) hold. Then the conclusion of the theorem on the laws of the pairing series holds for all data at $(K, p)$ with $p \ge 5$ inert and over $W$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.KerInert.beWInert_ofA`

*Proof.* The two algebraicity facts give the sigma curve condition. Then this is [the proposition on the sigma function](/entry/be-winert-of-sigma/) applied to it, (E) and (N). $\square$

The four facts of the last theorem are proved elsewhere in the development, and the bundle of the four is the input to Section 3. There, the three laws become the pairing property of a formal group system: biadditivity in each slot and the conjugation law for the elements fixed by the square of Frobenius.

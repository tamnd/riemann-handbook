This theorem is a one-line instance with no content of its own. It applies `admissible_entries_of_kernel_law` to the first kernel law `first_kernel_law_global_small`, under a hypothesis on good models of CM elliptic curves. The work is in those two lemmas, which have no entries. Its only user is [the inert admissibility from complex multiplication](/entry/inert-theta-admissibility-of-complex-multiplication/), which turns the hypothesis into an instance and calls this theorem.

## Statement

Let $\mathrm{CM2}$ be the following statement. Let $d$ be a squarefree natural number with $d \equiv 3 \pmod 4$. Let $L$ be a lattice in $\mathbb{C}$ with $\omega L \subseteq L$, where $\omega = (1 + i\sqrt{d})/2$. For every prime $\ell \geq 5$ with $\ell \nmid d$ and every field isomorphism $\iota \colon \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}_\ell$, there are $g_2, g_3$ in the closure of the field generated by the roots of unity of order prime to $\ell$ in $\mathbb{C}_\ell$, with $|g_2|, |g_3| \leq 1$ and $|g_2^3 - 27 g_3^2| = 1$, and with $g_2 = \lambda^4 \iota(g_2(L))$, $g_3 = \lambda^6 \iota(g_3(L))$ for some $\lambda \neq 0$.

**Theorem.** If $\mathrm{CM2}$ holds, then `AdmAtEnt2` holds.

`AdmAtEnt2` says: for every datum $D$, word $u$, tuple $\mu$, place $w$, chain of levels at $w$ with its system $X$, and further local data, suppose four conditions (`CurveIn`, the theta series hypotheses below the maximal level, `XiIn` and `LawInLt`) and the ties at all levels $j < m$. Then the pending datum of $X$ is `admitted`: it satisfies the 13 conditions of that definition.

## Proof

`admissible_entries_of_kernel_law` turns the first kernel law `TranslatesOfInertKernelAtPlace` into `AdmAtEnt2`. `first_kernel_law_global_small` proves that law. The law gives three of the 13 conditions (`adm2`, `adm2b`, `adm3`) at primes $p \geq 5$.

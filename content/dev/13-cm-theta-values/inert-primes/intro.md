This chapter treats a prime that is inert in the CM field. Sections 1 to 4 work with a curve with complex multiplication at an inert prime $p \ge 5$. They prove the laws of the pairing series that comes from the sigma function, the integrality of the theta kernels of self-dual lattices, and the first kernel law. From these results it follows that the entries of the formal group systems at inert places are admitted, which is the input of [the inert theta admissibility theorem](/entry/inert-theta-admissibility-of-complex-multiplication/). Sections 5 to 7 work at an inert prime $l$ of odd order. They prove facts on self-conjugate charts, turn a labelling of the Frobenius orbits into row data with a bound for the valuations of the blocks, and build the packages that [the theorem on packages at good primes](/entry/good-prime-packages/) needs. Section 8 proves the product law of the local measures and the equivariance of a theta step, which [the chain of theta distributions with a character](/entry/ch-th-ok-chi/) of Volume 14 uses, and a bound for the jets at a split prime.

From Volume 1 the chapter needs the following. Section 1 uses the [sigma function](/learn/theta-functions-cm/#genus-one-the-sigma-function) and [formal group laws](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws) to form the pairing series. Section 2 uses [Hermitian lattices](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#lattices-in-quadratic-and-hermitian-spaces) in the reduction from a general pair of lattices to the standard lattice. Sections 6 and 7 use [the splitting of primes and Frobenius elements](/learn/number-fields/#decomposition-groups-and-frobenius-elements) to label the orbits of embeddings at an inert prime. Section 8 uses [Hecke characters](/learn/class-field-theory/#hecke-characters) and [their infinity types](/learn/class-field-theory/#infinity-type-and-algebraic-characters) in the product formula, and [Hilbert's Theorem 90](/learn/etale-cohomology/#sheaves-and-their-cohomology) to write the determinant of a unitary map as $x/\bar{x}$ in the proof of the product law.

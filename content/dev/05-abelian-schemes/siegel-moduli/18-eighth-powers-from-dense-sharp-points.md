## Eighth powers from dense sharp points

This section proves the second half of the comparison of theta families with Siegel families. Let $t$ be a theta level object over a reduced base whose points come, on a dense set, from the sharp class of [the previous section](/entry/good-class-sharp-class/). We show that $t$ is itself an eighth power $B^8$ of a principal Siegel object, with the polarization twisted by the $E_8$ form, the endomorphisms acting by matrices and the level points given by the sharp basis. Applied to the universal object of a theta family for the sharp class, this gives a principally polarized abelian scheme with a symplectic level structure over the theta family. This is the step from theta families to Siegel families. The background is [Siegel moduli schemes and level structures](/learn/abelian-varieties/#siegel-moduli-schemes-and-level-structures) and [the theorem of the cube](/learn/abelian-varieties/#the-theorem-of-the-cube).

We use the E8 matrices, the sharp bases, the Siegel objects, the sharp objects and the sharp class of [the previous section](/entry/good-class-sharp-class/), and the sets $S_\mathcal{C}$ of points that come from a class $\mathcal{C}$ of [the section on theta families](/entry/nonempty-theta-fam/). We write $E_{uv}$ for the $8 \times 8$ matrix unit, $\Delta$ for the diagonal and $E(x, y) = \sum_{u, v} (W + W^T)_{uv}\, \omega_h(x_u, y_v)$ for the form on $(\mathbb{Z}^{2h})^8$.

**Theorem 5.18.1 (Eighth powers from dense sharp points).** Assume the laws, the second laws and the theorem of the cube. Let $h, g \ge 0$, let $W$ be an E8 matrix, $J$ a set, $a_j$ ($j \in J$) integer $8 \times 8$ matrices and $\mathrm{bas}$ a sharp basis for $W$. Assume that for all $u, v$ there is $j \in J$ with $a_j = E_{uv}$. Let $k$ be odd, $o$ a ring, $b$ a base of level $k$ on $o$, $em : o \to \mathbb{C}$ a ring map, $S$ a locally Noetherian [[reduced|ZXSN8D4MCB]] scheme with $s : S \to \operatorname{Spec} o$, and $t$ a theta level object of genus $g$ and level $k$ over $s$. If the set of points of $S$ that come from the sharp class for $(W, a, \mathrm{bas}, em)$ is [[Dense|0BSJSX7ME2]] in $S$, then there is a principal Siegel object $c$ of genus $h$ and level $k$ over $s$ such that $t$ is sharp over $c$ for $(W, a, \mathrm{bas})$.

*Proof.* *A matrix action.* A lemma on dense sharp points adjoins all integer $8 \times 8$ matrices to $J$. It uses that every matrix is an integer combination of the $E_{uv}$, and the $E_{uv}$ are among the $a_j$. The result is a map $\rho$ from $8 \times 8$ integer matrices to endomorphisms of $A$ with $\rho(m m') = \rho(m) \circ \rho(m')$, $\rho(m + m') = \rho(m) + \rho(m')$ and $\rho(1) = 1$, with $\rho(a_j) = \alpha_j$. These identities hold at the points from the sharp class, and they spread from a dense set since $S$ is reduced.

*The factor.* A lemma on abelian schemes with a matrix action gives an abelian scheme $B$ over $S$ and homomorphisms $\mathrm{pr}_u : A \to B$ that identify $A$ with $B^8$, with $\mathrm{pr}_u \circ \rho(m) = \prod_v \mathrm{pr}_v^{\,m_{uv}}$. Roughly, $B$ is the image of the idempotent $\rho(E_{11})$, and the $\rho(E_{1u})$ identify the other factors with it. This gives conditions 1 and 3 of a sharp object.

*The bundle.* A lemma on symmetric biadditive bundles gives a line bundle $P$ on $B \times_S B$, trivial along $\{1\} \times B$ and $B \times \{1\}$, with $P \cong \mathrm{sw}^* P$ and $L \cong \bigotimes_{u, v} (\mathrm{pr}_u, \mathrm{pr}_v)^* P^{\otimes W_{uv}}$. It uses the theorem of the cube, the global functions law, and the fact that $L$ is adjoint primitive for the matrix action, which holds on the dense set and so everywhere. This is condition 2.

*The level map.* Put $\mathrm{lev}(x) = \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ \prod_i P_i^{\,c_i(x)}$, where $c(x) = \mathrm{bas}^{-1}(x, 0, \dots, 0)$ is the vector of $\mathbb{Z}^{2g}$ whose image has $x$ in the first factor and $0$ in the others. Then $\mathrm{lev}$ is additive and $\mathrm{lev}(x)^k = 1$, since $P_i^k = 1$. A lemma on level points of a factor shows condition 4, $\mathrm{pr}_u \circ P_i = \mathrm{lev}(\mathrm{bas}(e_i)_u)$, and the first two geometric conditions of a Siegel object: at each geometric point the kernel of $\mathrm{lev}$ is $k\,\mathbb{Z}^{2h}$, and its image is all of $B[k]$.

*The Weil pairing.* At a geometric point, the commutator pairing of $L$ on the level points of $t$ is $z^{E(X, Y)}$, where $X$ and $Y$ are the coordinates of the points in $(\mathbb{Z}^{2h})^8$. A lemma on Weil pairings of eighth powers compares this with the pairing of $\Delta^* P$ on $B$, through the form $W + W^T$. It gives the third geometric condition, that the Weil pairing of $\Delta^* P$ on $\mathrm{lev}(a)$ and $\mathrm{lev}(b)$ is $z^{2 \omega_h(a, b)}$. So $c = (B, P, \mathrm{lev})$ is a Siegel object.

*Principal.* A lemma on E8 forms shows that $\Delta^* P$ is relatively ample, since $L$ is and $W + W^T$ is positive definite. A lemma on biadditive slices shows that a section $y$ of $B$ with $P|_{\{y\} \times B}$ trivial is $1$, since $W + W^T$ is unimodular and $L$ is principal. So $c$ is principal, and $t$ is sharp over $c$ by construction. $\square$

**Remark 5.18.2.** The hypothesis on matrix units is used only in the first step. It makes the endomorphisms of $t$ large enough to split $A$ into eight copies of one factor. The density hypothesis is used to carry identities and the shape of $L$ from the points of the sharp class to all of $S$; this needs $S$ to be reduced. The hypothesis that $k$ is odd is used only through $k > 0$. The construction of $c$ is explicit: $B$ is the common factor of $A$, $P$ comes from $L$ and $W$, and $\mathrm{lev}$ is read off from the first coordinate of the points $P_i$.

The chapter builds Siegel moduli schemes and PEL moduli schemes from theta level objects. For the background see [the theorem of the cube](/learn/abelian-varieties/#the-theorem-of-the-cube), [the complex uniformization](/learn/abelian-varieties/#the-complex-uniformization), [Siegel moduli schemes and level structures](/learn/abelian-varieties/#siegel-moduli-schemes-and-level-structures), [semi-abelian degenerations and Mumford's construction](/learn/abelian-varieties/#semi-abelian-degenerations-and-mumfords-construction) and [theta functions with characteristics](/learn/theta-functions-cm/#theta-functions-with-characteristics). The classical construction of Siegel moduli schemes is by geometric invariant theory; here they come from theta values, as in Mumford's theory of theta structures, and the compactification and degeneration data follow Faltings and Chai. Riemann-Roch on abelian varieties, the degree of an isogeny and Serre's rigidity lemma are classical. The eighth power trick with the $E_8$ form is the step that turns theta families into Siegel families.

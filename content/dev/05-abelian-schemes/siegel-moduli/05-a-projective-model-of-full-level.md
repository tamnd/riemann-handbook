## A projective model of full level

We now pass from theta families to the PEL moduli spaces of the chapter. For a CM field $E$ with a host and a PEL datum $D$, the moduli spaces are schemes over number fields, one for each level. This section builds one projective model over $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1+\rho_0}{2}, \tfrac1n, \zeta_N]$ that receives all moduli spaces of level $A = N$, for a suitable auxiliary prime $n$ and a power $N$ of $n$. The model comes from the framed model of [the previous section](/entry/nonempty-ample-framed-model-peltheta-family/), and its ample frames are what the local model systems of the next section need. The background is [Siegel moduli schemes and level structures](/learn/abelian-varieties/#siegel-moduli-schemes-and-level-structures).

**Definition 5.5.1 (Level).** A *level* for the host consists of an odd integer $M \ge 3$, an ideal $\mathfrak{M}$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$ generated by elements of $\mathbb{Z}[w_0]$ with $\mathfrak{M} \bar{\mathfrak{M}} = (M)$ and $\mathfrak{M} + \bar{\mathfrak{M}} = \mathcal{O}_E$, and an integer $A \ge 1$. It is *good at* a prime $p$ if the prime to $p$ part of $M$ is at least $3$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.Geo.Tor.Level`, `Level.GoodAt`.

Let $K_A = \mathbb{Q}(\rho_0, \zeta_A) \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ for a level with $A$; it has a base of level $A$, with $\rho = \rho_0$ and $\zeta = \zeta_A$.

**Definition 5.5.2 (PEL object of a level).** Let $B$ be a ring in which every positive integer is a unit, with $\rho \in B$, $\rho^2 = -d$, and a primitive $A$-th root of unity $\zeta$. A *PEL object* of level $l$ over $s : S \to \operatorname{Spec} B$ is an abelian scheme $G \to S$ with $\mathcal{O}_E$-action, proper, with a relatively ample line bundle $L$ with trivial $K(L)$, together with two additive maps $\eta, \eta_A$ from the lattice $\Lambda$ of $D$ to the sections of $G$, with $\eta$ killing $\mathfrak{M} \Lambda$ and $\eta_A$ killing $A \Lambda$, such that at every geometric point $x$ over $\beta : B \to k$ the determinant condition holds with $\rho = \beta(\rho)$ and there is a Tate isomorphism carrying $\zeta_A$ to $\beta(\zeta)$, $\eta_A$ to its evaluation at $A$, and $\eta$ on $\bar{\mathfrak{M}} \Lambda$ to its evaluation at $M$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.Geo.Tor.Obj`, `Base`, `ObjMap`.

**Definition 5.5.3 (PEL moduli space).** A *moduli space* of level $l$ is a quasi-projective scheme $X \to \operatorname{Spec} K_A$, smooth of relative dimension $\dim D$, with a universal PEL object, such that every PEL object over a reduced scheme over $K_A$ is the pullback of the universal one along exactly one map, and such that it is infinitesimally rigid: a point $x$ over $k[\varepsilon]$ whose object maps to the universal one over $x$ and over the constant point $\bar x$ equals $\bar x$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.Geo.Tor.Moduli`.

A *coefficient field* for $l$ is a finite extension $F \supseteq K_A$ in $\mathbb{C}$ that contains all conjugates of $E$ and $\zeta_{M'}$ with $M' = \operatorname{lcm}(M, A)$; we write $X_F$ for the base change of $X$ to $F$.

**Definition 5.5.4 (Auxiliary inert prime).** A prime $n$ is an *auxiliary inert prime* for the host if $n$ is odd, $-d$ is not a square modulo $n$, and $n$ does not divide the [[discriminant|E0VZTNPJXA]] of $E$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Models.AuxiliaryInertOddPrime`.

Let $R_{n,N} = \mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1+\rho_0}{2}, \tfrac1n, \zeta_N] \subseteq \mathbb{C}$, with $\rho = \rho_0$ and $\zeta = \zeta_N$ in it (Lean: `GlobalTheory.Models.RZ`, `RZ.rt`, `RZ.ze`). Let $p$ be a prime, $\mathbb{Z}_{(p), M_1} = \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{M_1}, \tfrac1m : p \nmid m]$, and $h \ge 1$.

**Definition 5.5.5 (Siegel family).** A *Siegel family* of genus $h$ at $p$ and level $M_1$ is a scheme $X$ over $\mathbb{Z}_{(p), M_1}$, smooth of relative dimension $h(h+1)/2$ and quasi-compact, with a universal principally polarized abelian scheme of dimension $h$ with a symplectic level $M_1$ structure, such that: every such object over the unit ball $O$ of an algebraically closed complete field $C$ with $|p| < 1$, which is principally polarized over $C$ and whose reduction carries a relatively ample line bundle, comes from a point of $X(O)$; and $X$ is infinitesimally rigid.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.Geo.Tor.SiegelFamily`, `SiegPre`, `SiegPre.Principal`.

Let $s : \mathcal{M} \to \operatorname{Spec} R_{n,N}$ be a scheme with an abelian scheme $G$ with $\mathcal{O}_E$-action and a map $\mathrm{lev}$ from $\Lambda$ to the sections of $G$.

**Definition 5.5.6 (Family of full level).** $(\mathcal{M}, G, \mathrm{lev})$ is a *full level object* if $G$ is proper with relatively ample $L$ and trivial $K(L)$, $\mathrm{lev}$ is additive and kills $N\Lambda$, and at every geometric point of characteristic $0$ the determinant condition holds and there is a Tate isomorphism carrying $\zeta_N$ to the image of $\zeta_N$ and $\mathrm{lev}$ to its evaluation at $N$. It is a *family of full level* if in addition $\mathcal{M}$ is reduced, flat and quasi-projective over $R_{n,N}$ with smooth generic fibre, every full level object over a reduced, locally noetherian, flat scheme over $R_{n,N}$ is a pullback along exactly one map, and $\mathcal{M}$ is infinitesimally rigid.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Models.FullObj`, `GlobalTheory.Models.FamCore`.

**Definition 5.5.7 (Siegel comparison).** The family satisfies the *Siegel comparison* if for every prime $p \ne n$ there are a Siegel family $\mathcal{A}$ of genus $h = \tfrac12 [E:\mathbb{Q}] (p_D + q_D)$ at $p$ and level $N$ and a symplectic basis of $\Lambda$ such that, for every ring $O \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{Z}_{(p),N}$ and $R_{n,N}$ and every pullback $T \to \mathcal{M}$ of $\mathcal{M}$ to $O$, the object over $T$, forgotten to a Siegel object through the basis, gives a finite and formally unramified map $T \to \mathcal{A}_O$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Models.SiegClause`, `GlobalTheory.Models.SiegData`.

Here $p_D, q_D$ are the signature of $D$. Fix moduli spaces $X_l$ for all levels $l$.

**Definition 5.5.8 (Model of full level).** A *model of full level* $N$ for $n$ consists of:

1. a normal scheme $Z$, projective and flat over $R_{n,N}$, with a good semi-abelian scheme $G$ with $\mathcal{O}_E$-action, quasi-projective over $R_{n,N}$, whose abelian locus $U$ contains the generic points;
2. for every level $l$ with $A = N$ a map $a_l : X_l \to Z$ with a map of the universal object to $G$, such that $X_l \to Z_{K_A} \cap U$ is finite and surjective;
3. compatibility of the $a_l$ with maps $X_{l'} \to X_l$ that respect $\eta_A$ and an inclusion $K_A \subseteq K_{A'}$, and extension to $Z$ of the automorphisms of $X_l$ that come from $\mathcal{O}_E$-linear maps of $\Lambda$ and automorphisms of $K_A$ fixing $\rho_0$;
4. a family $(\mathcal{M}, G_\mathcal{M}, \mathrm{lev})$ over $R_{n,N}$, reduced and flat with $G_\mathcal{M}$ proper, and a finite surjective map $u : U \to \mathcal{M}$ over $R_{n,N}$ with semi-abelian maps, which is an isomorphism over an open set containing the generic fibre, and through which $\eta_A$ on $X_l$ matches $\mathrm{lev}$;
5. the Siegel comparison for $(\mathcal{M}, G_\mathcal{M}, \mathrm{lev})$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Models.Top`.

**Definition 5.5.9 (Full level models exist).** The statement *full level models exist* is: for every CM field $E$ with a host, every PEL datum $D$ with $p_D + q_D \ge 1$, all moduli spaces $X_l$ and every auxiliary inert prime $n$, there are $J \ge 1$, $k$ and a model of full level $n^J$ for $n$ with ample frames of level $(n^k)^2$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Models.TopAmpExists`.

**Theorem 5.5.10 (Full level model from a family).** Assume the homomorphism extension theorem (every homomorphism between the generic fibres of two semi-abelian schemes over a discrete valuation ring extends uniquely), the laws and the second laws. Let $n$ be an auxiliary inert prime, $N = n^j$ with $j \ge 1$, $m \ge 1$ and $p_D + q_D \ge 1$. Let $(\mathcal{M}, G_\mathcal{M}, \mathrm{lev})$ be a family of full level over $R_{n,N}$ with the Siegel comparison, and let $Z$ be a framed model of level $m$ of $(\mathcal{M}, G_\mathcal{M})$. Then there is a model of full level $N$ with ample frames of level $m^2$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.TopOfFamily.exists_top` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_fullLevel_model_of_family_birationalModel`, a second name).

*Proof.* We take for the model the framed model $Z$, with its semi-abelian scheme $G$ and its frames; conditions 1 and the frames hold by [the definition of a framed model](/entry/models-nonempty-z-on-of-components/), and the generic points of $Z$ lie in the abelian locus $U$ since $j(W)$ is dense and contained in it. A lemma on the abelian locus gives a finite surjective map $u : U \to \mathcal{M}$ with semi-abelian maps, which is an isomorphism over an open set $W_0$ containing the generic fibre, and a section $i : W_0 \to U$.

Let $l$ be a level with $A = N$. Since $n$ is prime to $M$, there is a flat injective map $\beta : R_{n,N} \to K_A$ over $\mathbb{C}$, and a lemma on moduli gives a map $X_l \to \mathcal{M}$ over $\beta$, finite and surjective onto $\mathcal{M}_{K_A}$, under which $\eta_A$ matches $\mathrm{lev}$. Its image lies in the generic fibre, so in $W_0$, and we put $a_l = i \circ (X_l \to W_0)$ followed by $U \subseteq Z$. The finiteness in condition 2 then follows from that of $X_l \to \mathcal{M}_{K_A}$. Two maps $X_{l'} \to \mathcal{M}$ that are compatible with $\eta_A$ and $\mathrm{lev}$ agree, by the moduli property of $\mathcal{M}$ and the laws, since $N \ge 3$ is odd; this gives condition 3 for maps of moduli spaces. For an automorphism of $X_l$ from a linear map $g$ of $\Lambda$ and an automorphism $\sigma$ of $K_A$, a lemma on changes of full level gives an automorphism of $\mathcal{M}$ and its abelian scheme over an automorphism of $R_{n,N}$; by the homomorphism extension theorem there is an automorphism $\tau$ of $Z$ that agrees with it on a dense part of $j(W)$, and since $Z$ is reduced and separated, $a_l \circ \tau = r \circ a_l$ follows from agreement on a dense set. Condition 4 holds with $u$, and the Siegel comparison is assumed. $\square$

**Theorem 5.5.11 (Full level models exist from the inputs).** Assume the homomorphism extension theorem, the laws, the second laws, the semistable reduction theorem, the uniformization and torsion of degenerations, the geometric statement on theta systems, the reduction criterion of [the section on theta families](/entry/nonempty-theta-fam/), the theorem of the cube and the uniformization theorem. Then full level models exist.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Models.topAmpExists` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_fullLevel_model_ample_frames`, a second name).

*Proof.* Let $E$, $D$ with $p_D + q_D \ge 1$, the moduli spaces $X_l$ and an auxiliary inert prime $n$ be given. Choose a symplectic basis of $\Lambda$ and a finite set $J$ with $a : J \to \mathcal{O}_E$ whose image generates $\mathcal{O}_E$ as a ring and contains $w_0$ and $\bar w_0$. The genus $h = \tfrac12 [E:\mathbb{Q}](p_D + q_D)$ is at least $1$.

Three bounds on $j$ come from three results: $j_1$ from [the PEL theta families with smooth generic fibre](/entry/exists-theta-fam-is-pel-smooth/), $(k, j_2)$ from [the framed models of PEL theta families](/entry/nonempty-ample-framed-model-peltheta-family/), and $j_3$ from the existence of quasi-projective Siegel families of large level, which uses the laws, the second laws, the theorem of the cube and the uniformization theorem. Take $j \ge \max(j_1, j_2, j_3, 1)$ and $N' = n^j$, so that $N = N'^2 = n^{2j}$.

The ring $R_{n,N}$ has a base of level $N$ with $\zeta = \zeta_N$, and $R_{n,N} \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ is an arithmetic base. The first result gives a theta family $\mathcal{M}$ over $R_{n,N}$ for the PEL class with $\rho = \rho_0$, with a PEL structure $G_\mathcal{M}$ and smooth generic fibre of dimension $\dim D$. The second gives a framed model of level $n^k$ of $(\mathcal{M}, G_\mathcal{M})$. A lemma on universal families gives $\mathrm{lev}$ such that $(\mathcal{M}, G_\mathcal{M}, \mathrm{lev})$ is a family of full level, and a lemma on CM families gives the Siegel comparison, from the Siegel families of level $N$ given by $j_3$. Now [the full level model from a family](/entry/exists-full-level-model-of-family-birational-model/) gives a model of full level $N = n^{2j}$ with ample frames of level $(n^k)^2$, and we output $J = 2j \ge 1$ and $k$. $\square$

**Corollary 5.5.12 (Full level models from semistable reduction).** The semistable reduction theorem implies that full level models exist.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.topAmpExists_of_semistableReduction` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_fullLevelAmpleModel_of_semistableReduction`, a second name).

*Proof.* Apply the previous theorem. The homomorphism extension theorem, the laws, the second laws, the uniformization and torsion of degenerations, the theorem of the cube and the uniformization theorem are proved in other chapters. The geometric statement on theta systems follows from the semistable reduction theorem by [the theorem in part two](/entry/zarhin-geometric-of-semistable-reduction/). The reduction criterion follows from the laws, the second laws, the uniformization and torsion of degenerations and the split form of [semistable reduction](/entry/semistable-reduction-st/) over $p$-adic fields, which is again derived from the semistable reduction theorem. $\square$

So the only open input of the full level models is the semistable reduction theorem, which is proved in its own chapter.

**Remark 5.5.13 (Formalization).** The model of full level is the structure `Top`; the condition on automorphisms is its field `sym`, which only asks for some automorphism $\rho$ of $Z$ with $a_l \circ \rho = r \circ a_l$ and says nothing about its action on $G$ or on the base ([AuxiliaryInertOddPrime.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/ModuliSpaces/AuxiliaryInertOddPrime.lean#L115)). The proof of the first theorem uses the lemmas `T.abelian_locus_open_family_over_level`, `T.moduli_finite_surjective_over_generic_family` and `T.fullLevelChange_acts_on_unitaryFamily`, and the second those named `T.exists_universal_quasiProjective_siegelFamily_large_level`, `T.exists_universal_fullLevel_of_pelThetaFamily` and `T.cmFamily_finite_unramified_over_siegelFamily`; we followed their statements. The reduction criterion is filled by `T.goodReduction_of_thetaPoint_units`, applied to `T.semistableReductionSplit_of_padic`. In [ExistsFullLevelAmpleModelOfSemistableReduction.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/ModuliProofs/ExistsFullLevelAmpleModelOfSemistableReduction.lean#L24) the term `uniformisationExistence_and_torsion_statements` is written out twice, once for the theorem and once inside the reduction criterion, and the laws likewise. The geometric statement on theta systems is filled by `T.zarhinGeometric_of_semistableReduction`, whose name does not describe its content. The second names are wrappers proved by `first | exact @...`.

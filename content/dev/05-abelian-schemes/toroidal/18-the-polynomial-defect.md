## The polynomial defect

This section shows that each basic heat operator has a defect that is a polynomial of degree at most $2$ in the level $k$, when the degeneration data are monomial. [The theorem on heat operators with polynomial defect](/entry/exists-basic-heat-poly-defect/) of Section 17 uses this result. The proof compares $h$ with a second heat operator $h'$, obtained from $h$ by a translation and a gauge change. For $h'$ a lemma on theta shifts computes the defect directly, and $h'$ differs from $h$ by a scalar that is polynomial in $k$. We use the notation of Section 17. In particular, $R$, $Q$, $O$, $H$, $v_1, \dots, v_n$ and $\partial_1, \dots, \partial_n$ are fixed, $q : H \to A$ is the projection, $\Gamma_{k,\alpha}$ is the module of sections of Section 17 whose Fourier terms have weight $\alpha$ and level $k$, and $\sigma_1 = u \cdot T_{c(\mu)}^* \sigma + c\, \sigma_2$ is the shift relation with constant $c$.

**Lemma 7.18.1 (Shift relations add).** Let $k \ge 1$, $\mu, \alpha \in X$, $\sigma, \sigma', \sigma'' \in \Gamma_{k,\alpha}$, $\sigma_1, \sigma'_1 \in \Gamma_{k,\alpha+k\mu}$ and $c_1, c_2 \in R$. Suppose that $\sigma_1$ satisfies the shift relation with $\sigma$ and constant $0$, that $\sigma'_1$ satisfies the shift relation with $\sigma''$, constant $c_1$ and $\sigma_2 = \sigma_1$, and that $\sigma'' = \sigma' + c_2 \sigma$. Then $\sigma'_1$ satisfies the shift relation with $\sigma'$, constant $c_1 + c_2$ and $\sigma_2 = \sigma_1$.

*Proof.* Let $u$ be a normalized generator as in the shift relation. Then $\sigma_1 = u \cdot T_{c(\mu)}^* \sigma$ and $\sigma'_1 = u \cdot T_{c(\mu)}^* \sigma'' + c_1 \sigma_1$. The pullback of $\sigma'' = \sigma' + c_2 \sigma$ along $T_{c(\mu)}$ gives
$$\sigma'_1 = u \cdot T_{c(\mu)}^* \sigma' + c_2\, u \cdot T_{c(\mu)}^* \sigma + c_1 \sigma_1 = u \cdot T_{c(\mu)}^* \sigma' + (c_1 + c_2)\, \sigma_1. \qquad \square$$

Let $x$ be a point of $H$ over $R$, and let $e$ be a generator of the line bundle $q^* L \otimes (q \circ T_x)^* L^{-1}$ on $H$, where $T_x$ is the translation by $x$. For a section $\tau$ of $q^* L^k$, the *gauge translate* of $\tau$ is the section $\rho = T_x^* \tau \cdot e^k$ of $q^* L^k$. Its local values are $\rho(r) = \tau(x \cdot r)\, e(r)^k$.

**Theorem 7.18.2 (Conjugate heat operator for a period shift).** Let $R$, $Q$, $O$, $H$, $v$ and $\partial$ be as in Section 17. Suppose that $Q$ is monomial in nonzero $t_1, \dots, t_m$ with exponents $\gamma$, let $D$ be a derivation of $R$ with $D(t_i) \in (t_i)$ for each $i$, let $h$ be a basic heat operator for $D$, and let $\mu \in X$. Then there are a basic heat operator $h'$ for $D$ with the same symbol $\Theta$ and the same vector $u$ as $h$, an additive map $w : X \to R$ and $\pi, \pi' \in R$, with the following property. Let $k \ge 1$, $\alpha \in X$, $\sigma \in \Gamma_{k,\alpha}$ and $\sigma_1, \sigma'_1 \in \Gamma_{k,\alpha+k\mu}$. Suppose that $\sigma_1$ satisfies the shift relation with $\sigma$ and constant $0$, and that $h$ acts from $\sigma_1$ to $\sigma'_1$. Then there is $\sigma'' \in \Gamma_{k,\alpha}$ such that $h'$ acts from $\sigma$ to $\sigma''$, and $\sigma'_1$ satisfies the shift relation with $\sigma''$, constant $2k\, w(\alpha) + 2k^2 \pi + 2k\, \pi'$ and $\sigma_2 = \sigma_1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.HeatDefect.exists_conj_shift` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_conjugate_heatOperator_periodShift`, a second name).

*Proof.* There are four steps.

1. A lemma on theta shifts and Fourier terms applies to the monomial data, $D$ and $\mu$. It gives a point $x$ of $H$ over $R$, a generator $e$ as above whose local functions have a weight $\beta \in X$, an additive map $w : X \to R$ and $\pi, \pi' \in R$. For each $k \ge 1$ and $\alpha$ it also gives $a, b \in R$ with $a \ne 0$ and two properties. First, if $\sigma_1$ satisfies the shift relation with $\sigma$ and constant $0$, $\tau$ and $\tau_1$ are Fourier terms of $\sigma$ and $\sigma_1$, and $\rho$ is the gauge translate of $\tau_1$, then $a \rho = b \tau$. Second, let $\tau''$ and $\tau'_1$ be Fourier terms of $\sigma''$ and $\sigma'_1$, and $\rho'$ the gauge translate of $\tau'_1$. If $a \rho' + 2k D(a)\, \rho = b \tau'' + 2k D(b)\, \tau$, then $\sigma'_1$ satisfies the shift relation with $\sigma''$, constant $2k\, w(\alpha) + 2k^2 \pi + 2k\, \pi'$ and $\sigma_2 = \sigma_1$.
2. A lemma on conjugation by a translation and a gauge applies to $h$, $x$, $e$ and $\beta$. It gives a basic heat operator $h'$ with the same $\Theta$ and $u$ as $h$, such that $h'$ takes the gauge translate of $\tau$ to the gauge translate of $\tau'$ whenever $h$ takes $\tau$ to $\tau'$.
3. Let $k$, $\alpha$, $\sigma$, $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma'_1$ be as in the theorem, and let $h$ take a Fourier term $\tau_1$ of $\sigma_1$ to a Fourier term $\tau'_1$ of $\sigma'_1$. A lemma on basic heat operators gives $\sigma''$ such that $h'$ acts from $\sigma$ to $\sigma''$: $h'$ takes a Fourier term $\tau$ of $\sigma$ to a Fourier term $\tau''$ of $\sigma''$. Let $\rho$ and $\rho'$ be the gauge translates of $\tau_1$ and $\tau'_1$. By step 2, $h'$ takes $\rho$ to $\rho'$.
4. By the first property of step 1, $a \rho = b \tau$. The operator $\mathcal{H}_{i,k}$ contains the term $2k\, d_i$, and $d_i$ lifts $D$, so a lemma on the Leibniz rule for constants gives $a \rho' + 2k D(a)\, \rho = b \tau'' + 2k D(b)\, \tau$. The second property of step 1 now gives the shift relation for $\sigma'_1$ and $\sigma''$. $\square$

Only step 1 uses the monomial data and the condition $D(t_i) \in (t_i)$.

**Theorem 7.18.3 (Polynomial defect).** Let the data be as in the previous theorem, without $\mu$. Then there are a map $w : X \times X \to R$ that is additive in the second variable and maps $\pi, \pi' : X \to R$ such that $h$ has defect
$$c(k, \mu, \alpha) = 2k\, w(\mu, \alpha) + 2k^2 \pi(\mu) + 2k\, \pi'(\mu).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.HeatDefect.defect_polynomial` (also `GlobalTheory.T.heatOperator_defect_polynomial_monomial_periods`, `GlobalTheory.Heat.exists_polynomial_defect_of_isMonomial` and `GlobalTheory.T.exists_polynomial_defect_heatOperator_monomial`, three more names for the same statement).

*Proof.* For each $\mu \in X$, the previous theorem gives a basic heat operator $h'_\mu$ with the same $\Theta$ and $u$ as $h$, an additive map $w_1(\mu, \cdot)$ and $\pi_1(\mu), \pi'_1(\mu) \in R$. A lemma on basic heat operators with the same symbol and the same vector $u$ applies to $h$ and $h'_\mu$. It gives an additive map $\ell_\mu : X \to R$ and $\pi_2(\mu), \pi'_2(\mu) \in R$ such that the following holds for $k$, $\alpha$ and $\sigma, \sigma', \sigma'' \in \Gamma_{k,\alpha}$: if $h$ acts from $\sigma$ to $\sigma'$ and $h'_\mu$ acts from $\sigma$ to $\sigma''$, then
$$\sigma'' = \sigma' + \bigl(2k\, \ell_\mu(\alpha) + 2k^2 \pi_2(\mu) + 2k\, \pi'_2(\mu)\bigr)\, \sigma.$$
Put $w(\mu, \alpha) = w_1(\mu, \alpha) + \ell_\mu(\alpha)$, $\pi = \pi_1 + \pi_2$ and $\pi' = \pi'_1 + \pi'_2$. Then $w$ is additive in $\alpha$.

Let $k \ge 1$, $\mu, \alpha \in X$, $\sigma, \sigma' \in \Gamma_{k,\alpha}$ and $\sigma_1, \sigma'_1 \in \Gamma_{k,\alpha+k\mu}$. Suppose that $\sigma_1$ satisfies the shift relation with $\sigma$ and constant $0$, and that $h$ acts from $\sigma$ to $\sigma'$ and from $\sigma_1$ to $\sigma'_1$. The previous theorem gives $\sigma''$ such that $h'_\mu$ acts from $\sigma$ to $\sigma''$, and $\sigma'_1$ satisfies the shift relation with $\sigma''$, constant $c_1 = 2k\, w_1(\mu, \alpha) + 2k^2 \pi_1(\mu) + 2k\, \pi'_1(\mu)$ and $\sigma_2 = \sigma_1$. The lemma on the same symbol gives $\sigma'' = \sigma' + c_2 \sigma$ with $c_2 = 2k\, \ell_\mu(\alpha) + 2k^2 \pi_2(\mu) + 2k\, \pi'_2(\mu)$. The lemma on adding shift relations gives the shift relation for $\sigma'_1$ and $\sigma'$ with constant $c_1 + c_2$, and
$$c_1 + c_2 = 2k\, w(\mu, \alpha) + 2k^2 \pi(\mu) + 2k\, \pi'(\mu). \qquad \square$$

**Remark 7.18.4 (Formalization).** The proofs are in [ExistsConjugateHeatOperatorPeriodShift.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsConjugateHeatOperatorPeriodShift.lean#L41) and [HeatOperatorDefectPolynomialMonomialPeriods.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/HeatOperatorDefectPolynomialMonomialPeriods.lean#L41). In `defect_polynomial` the monomial data and the condition on $D$ go only to `exists_conj_shift`, and in `exists_conj_shift` they go only to `T.thetaShift_fourierTerm_translation_gauge` (line 43).

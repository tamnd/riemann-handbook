## Integral minimal compactifications

This section proves that $X_F$ has a minimal compactification $C$ in the sense of Section 2. This is the first step of [the theorem on the separating normalized blowup](/entry/u-poin-st-x-sep/). The construction maps $X_F$ to projective space by a theta map of Section 8 over a localization $o$ of the ring of integers of $F$. Then it takes the normalization of projective space in $X_F$ along this map. The theta map has finite geometric fibres when the level is large, so the normalization is finite over projective space, and so it is projective over $o$. The pullback of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ is ample, and Section 8 gives its relation with the Hodge bundle. The extension property and the integrality of theta constants come from the same properties of the theta map. We use the notation of Sections 1, 2 and 8, with $g = \tfrac{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}{2}(p + q)$.

**Lemma 7.9.1 (Base ring).** Let $F \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be a subfield of finite degree over $\mathbb{Q}$, with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_F$, and let $N \ne 0$ be an integer. Let $o = \mathcal{O}_F[1/N]$ and let $b : o \to F$ be the inclusion. Then $b$ is injective, $o$ is a Dedekind domain of finite type over $\mathbb{Z}$, $N$ is a unit in $o$, and each $x \in F$ is $b(a)/b(d)$ for some $a, d \in o$ with $b(d) \ne 0$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.UMinTop.exists_baseRing_away`.

*Proof.* The ring $o$ is the localization of $\mathcal{O}_F$ at the powers of $N$. These powers are not zero divisors, so $o$ is a domain and $b$ is injective. A localization of a Dedekind domain is a Dedekind domain. The ring $o$ is generated over $\mathbb{Z}$ by a finite basis of $\mathcal{O}_F$ and $1/N$. Each $x \in F$ is a quotient of elements of $\mathcal{O}_F$. $\square$

**Lemma 7.9.2 (Normalization in projective space).** Let $o$ be a Dedekind domain of finite type over $\mathbb{Z}$, $F$ a field of characteristic $0$ and $b : o \to F$ an injective map with $F$ the field of fractions of $b(o)$. Let $X$ be a normal scheme over $F$ that is separated, locally of finite type and quasi-compact, and let $f : X \to \mathbb{P}^n_o$ be a map over $\operatorname{Spec} b$. Suppose that for each algebraically closed field $K$ and each point $x \in X(K)$, the set of $y \in X(K)$ over the same point of $\operatorname{Spec} F$ with $f(y) = f(x)$ is finite. Let $Z$ be the normalization of $\mathbb{P}^n_o$ in $X$ along $f$, with $p : Z \to \mathbb{P}^n_o$. Then:

1. $p$ is finite and $Z$ is normal;
2. there is an open immersion $i_F : X \to Z \times_o F$ with dense image, with $\mathrm{pr}_2 \circ i_F$ the structure map of $X$ and $p \circ \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ i_F = f$;
3. the image of $\mathrm{pr}_1 \circ i_F$ is dense in $Z$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.UMinTop.integral_normal_model_dense`.

*Proof.* Projective space over $o$ is proper. As $f$ followed by the structure map of $\mathbb{P}^n_o$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, so is $f$. So the normalization of $\mathbb{P}^n_o$ in $X$ along $f$ exists, and $f$ factors through it. A lemma on normalizations in projective space shows that $p$ is finite and $Z$ is normal. This lemma uses the finite fibres of $f$. A second lemma shows that the induced map $X \to Z \times_o F$ is an open immersion with dense image, and that its image in $Z$ is dense. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9.3 (Integral minimal compactifications).** Let $E$, $H$ and $D$ be as in Section 1 with $p + q \ge 1$. Then for each level $l$, moduli scheme $X$ and coefficient field $F$, there is a minimal compactification $C$ of $X_F$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.umin_theta_compactification_exists` (also `GlobalTheory.T.unitary_integral_compactification_exists_via_theta`, `GlobalTheory.UPoinc.uMinSt_holds` and `GlobalTheory.T.unitary_integral_compactifications_exist`, second names; `GlobalTheory.UPoinc.UMinSt`, the statement).

*Proof.* We build the five items of Section 2.

1. *The ring.* Put $N_0 = 3 M' d \operatorname{disc}(E)$. This is not zero, since $M' \ge 1$, $d$ is a prime and the discriminant of a number field is not zero. The lemma on the base ring gives $o = \mathcal{O}_F[1/N_0]$ with $b$. In $o$ the integers $3$, $M'$, $d$ and $\operatorname{disc}(E)$ are units. This gives item 1.
2. *The level.* Put $j = 81^g \cdot g!$. Then $81^g \cdot g! < 3^j \cdot 81$. Put $m = 3^j \cdot 81 = 3^{j+4}$. Then $m$ is odd, $m \ge 3$, and $m$ is a unit in $o$ because $3$ is a unit. Let $n$ be such that $n + 1 = m^{4g}$.
3. *The theta map.* The scheme $X_F$ is normal and locally noetherian of characteristic $0$, and it is separated, locally of finite type and quasi-compact over $F$. The fibres of the universal datum over $X$ are principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension $g$. A lemma on theta maps of abelian schemes over normal bases of characteristic $0$ then gives a theta map $f : X_F \to \mathbb{P}^n_o$ of level $m$ over $\operatorname{Spec} b$.
4. *Finite fibres.* A lemma on theta maps of large level uses $81^g \cdot g! < 3^j \cdot 81$. It shows that $f$ has finite geometric fibres in the sense of the lemma on normalizations.
5. *The scheme.* The lemma on normalizations gives $Z_C = Z$ with $p$ finite, $Z_C$ normal, and $i_F$ an open immersion with dense image whose composite $i$ into $Z_C$ has dense image. This gives item 2, except projectivity.
6. *The bundle.* A finite scheme over $\mathbb{P}^n_o$ is projective over $o$, and the pullback of an ample bundle by a finite map is ample. Put $\mathcal{O}_Z(1) = p^* \mathcal{O}(1)^{\otimes 8 (n+1)!}$. Then $Z_C$ is projective over $o$ and $\mathcal{O}_Z(1)$ is ample. Put $N = 4 (n+1)! (n+1)!$. Let $Y$ be a model of $X_F$ over $\mathrm{id}_F$ with $\omega_Y$ a line bundle and $h : Y \to Z_C \times_o F$ with $h \circ j = i_F$. Then $g_Y = p \circ \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ h$ satisfies $g_Y \circ j = f$. [The theorem on the pullback of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ by a theta map](/entry/theta-map-pull-o1-power/) gives $h^* \mathrm{pr}_1^* \mathcal{O}_Z(1) \cong g_Y^* \mathcal{O}(1)^{\otimes 8(n+1)!} \cong \omega_Y^{\otimes N}$. This gives item 3.
7. *Extension.* Let $R$, $t$, $y$ and $\sigma : \operatorname{Spec} R \to \operatorname{Spec} o$ be as in item 4 of Section 2. A lemma on uniformized points extends $f \circ y$ to a map $\xi_0 : \operatorname{Spec} R \to \mathbb{P}^n_o$ over $\sigma$. The ring $R$ is an integrally closed domain, because it is a Mumford base. Since $p$ is finite, the valuative lifting property of finite maps to normal domains lifts $\xi_0$ to $\xi : \operatorname{Spec} R \to Z_C$ that extends $i \circ y$. Then $\xi$ lies over $\sigma$. This gives item 4.
8. *Integrality.* A lemma on theta constants shows that each theta system over a normal model $M$ with a map to $\mathbb{P}^n_o$ that is compatible with $f$ has divisors in the sense of Section 7. Apply it with the map $Z_M \to Z_C \to \mathbb{P}^n_o$. This gives item 5. $\square$

The minimal compactification of a Siegel moduli scheme over the integers is built by Faltings and Chai from modular forms (G. Faltings and C.-L. Chai, *Degeneration of Abelian Varieties*, Springer, 1990, Chapter V). See also [toroidal and minimal compactifications](/learn/abelian-varieties/#toroidal-and-minimal-compactifications). The construction here uses theta constants of one large level in place of modular forms of all weights. The ample bundle is a power of the Hodge bundle on each model, as item 3 asks.

## Separation on the normalized blowup

This section proves that the normalized blowup of a minimal compactification along a boundary ideal is separating in the sense of Section 2. This is the last step of [the theorem on the separating normalized blowup](/entry/u-poin-st-x-sep/). At a point of the blowup over a monomial family, we put the family and the boundary traits near it into one raw Fourier system of Section 5. The class sums of Section 5 make it a *Fourier system*, in which products of terms over orbits are approximated by germs. The approximating elements of the blowup then control the values of the parameters of the family. We use the notation of Sections 2 to 5. $C$ is a minimal compactification of $X_F$ with ring $o$ and scheme $Z_C$, $Z = Z_C \times_o F$, and $i_F : X_F \to Z$.

**Definition 7.6.1 (Fourier system).** A *Fourier system of level $N_1$* is a raw Fourier system $\Phi$ with two more properties. First, there is $C$ such that for each $y$ with $m_y = 1$ and each $x \ne 0$, some $h \in \mathfrak{m}_A$ has $\mathrm{ev}_y(h) \mid t_{y,1}^{C \delta_{y,1}(x, x)}$. Second, for each $y_0$ and $N_0$ there are $N$ and $k_y$ as in item 1 of class sums in Section 5 such that for each tuple $a_1, \dots, a_l$ in $X_{y_0}$, labels $c_1, \dots, c_l$ and $e \in \mathbb{N}$, there is $f \in A$ with this property: for each $y$ and $j$ there is a finite set $S$ in the orbit of $a$ of Section 3, with $\sum_i \sum_k \delta_{y,k}(\mu_i, \mu_i) \ge j$ for $\mu$ in the orbit outside $S$, such that $\mathrm{ev}_y(f) - \sum_{\mu \in S} \prod_i \mathrm{term}_y(\mu_i, c_i) \in \mathfrak{m}_{V_y}^j + \mathrm{ev}_y(\mathfrak{m}_A^e) V_y$.

**Definition 7.6.2 (Cover, traits and members).** Let $M$ be a scheme with a map $g : M \to Z_C$, $z_1 \in M$, $A = \mathcal{O}_{M, z_1}$, $\zeta : \operatorname{Spec} A \to Z$, and $\Phi$ a raw Fourier system over $A$.

1. $M$ is a *normal cover at $z_1$* if $M$ is normal, $g$ is a finite map to an open subset of $Z_C$ that contains the image of $Z$, $\zeta$ followed by the projection to $Z_C$ is $\operatorname{Spec} A \to M \to Z_C$, and $\zeta$ maps the generic point into $i_F(X_F)$.
2. $\Phi$ *has the traits through* an open $U \subseteq M$ if the following holds. Let $T$ be a boundary trait of Section 3 and $\mathrm{ev} : A \to V_T$ a local map such that $\zeta \circ \operatorname{Spec} \mathrm{ev}$ is $i_F \circ y_T$ on $\operatorname{Spec} \operatorname{Frac} V_T$, and this point of $M$ lies in $U$. Then there are $y$ with $m_y = 1$ and $V_y$ a principal ideal ring, an injective local map $f : V_T \to V_y$ with $f \circ \mathrm{ev} = \mathrm{ev}_y$, and an $\mathcal{O}_E$-isomorphism $e : X_y \to X_T$, with $t_{y,1}^2 = f(\varpi)$ and $\delta_{y,1}(a, b) = \beta_T(ea, eb)$.
3. Let $(t, P, y, s, \delta)$ be a monomial family over $V$ with $\xi : \operatorname{Spec} V \to Z$. It is the *member* $y$ of $\Phi$ at level $N_1$ if there are a finite injective $\rho : V \to V_y$ with $\zeta \circ \operatorname{Spec} \mathrm{ev}_y = \xi \circ \operatorname{Spec} \rho$, a bijection $h$ from $\{1, \dots, m_y\}$ to the indices of $s$, and an isomorphism $e : X_y \to X_P$, with $t_{y,k}^2 = \rho(s_{h(k)})$ and $\delta_{y,k}(a, b) = M' \delta_{h(k)}(ea, eb)$, such that the terms at $y$ are theta terms of level $N_1$ for the pullback of $P$ along $\rho$. In a *weak member* the theta terms need not be normalized.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.USep.IsCov` (also `GlobalTheory.GSep.HasTraitsOn`, `GlobalTheory.USep.IsMember`, `GlobalTheory.GSep.IsMemberP`).

We do not unfold "theta terms". The proofs below use one fact about them: if $v$ is a unit with $v \cdot \mathrm{term}(0, c_0) = 1$, then $v$ times weak theta terms are theta terms.

**Theorem 7.6.3 (Raw Fourier system on a chart).** Let $p + q \ge 1$, $\varphi$ an invariant function, $V$ a local domain that is a Mumford base with a monomial family, and $\xi : \operatorname{Spec} V \to Z$ that extends $i_F \circ y$. Let $n$ be an odd prime and $j_0 \in \mathbb{N}$. Then there are $j \ge j_0$, a general theta setup $X_1$ with $Z_M \to Z_C$, a point $z_1$ with $\mathcal{O}_{Z_M, z_1}$ a domain, $\zeta$, a nonempty open $U$, a raw Fourier system $\Phi$ over $\mathcal{O}_{Z_M, z_1}$ with an index $y$ and a label $c_0$, such that $Z_M$ is a normal cover at $z_1$, $\Phi$ has the traits through $U$, the family is the weak member $y$ at level $(n^j)^2$, each $u_{y'}(0, c_0)$ is a unit, $m_{y'} \ne 1$ only for $y' = y$, and $\Phi$ is exact and has class sums at level $(n^j)^2$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.GSep.exists_vPre_traitsOn_chart` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_unitary_fourier_system_traits_chart`, a second name).

*Proof.* A lemma gives $j \ge j_0$, a general theta setup $X_1$ of level $(n^j)^2$ linked to $X_F$, a normal cover at $z_1$, a complete local domain $V_1$ with a finite injective $V \to V_1$ compatible with $\xi$, and square roots $r_k$ of the $s_k$. A second lemma gives a nonempty open $U$ on which the theta ring is regular. The family becomes a member over $V_1$ with elements $r_k$ and forms $M' \delta_k$. Each trait with image in $U$ becomes a member after we adjoin a square root of $\varpi$. The index set is the family together with these traits. A lemma gives the coefficients $u$, with $u(0, c_0)$ a unit for the label $c_0$ of the theta divisor, and weak theta terms at the family. Exactness comes from a lemma on geometric uniformizations, which uses [the twisted theta sums](/entry/twist-sum-translate/) of Section 5. Class sums come from [the theorem on class sums at deeper levels](/entry/gsep-deep-sums-class-sums-deeper/) of Section 5, applied to the deep towers that a lemma gives for $X_1$. $\square$

**Theorem 7.6.4 (Fourier system with a member).** Let the data be as in the previous theorem. Then there are $j \ge j_0$, an integral scheme $M$ with $g : M \to Z_C$, a point $z_1$, $\zeta$, a nonempty open $U$, and a Fourier system $\Phi$ of level $(n^j)^2$ over $\mathcal{O}_{M, z_1}$, such that $M$ is a normal cover at $z_1$, $\Phi$ has the traits through $U$, and the family is a member of $\Phi$ at level $(n^j)^2$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.USep.exists_system_with_traits_and_family` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_unitary_fourier_system_traits_family`, a second name).

*Proof.* Apply the previous theorem with $\max(j_0, 1)$, and let $N_1 = (n^j)^2 \ge 3$. Exactness gives the first property of a Fourier system. Class sums give the second, because the local ring is integrally closed. Let $v_{y'} = u_{y'}(0, c_0)^{-1}$. A lemma approximates the $u_{y'}(0, c_0)$ by images of one element of $A$, so the second property still holds after we multiply all coefficients at $y'$ by $v_{y'}$. Traits do not use the coefficients. At the family the term at $0$ times $v_y$ is $1$, so the weak theta terms become theta terms. $\square$

**Theorem 7.6.5 (Separation on the normalized blowup).** Let $p + q \ge 1$, and let $\varphi$ be a polarized fan with no self-intersections in rank at most $\min(p, q)$. Let $d \ge 1$ and $J$ an ideal sheaf on $Z_C$ whose support does not meet $i(X_F)$, with order $d\varphi$ along boundary traits through $i$. Let $\pi : S \to Z$ be a proper normalized blowup of $J \mathcal{O}_Z$, and $j : X_F \to S$ an open immersion with $\pi \circ j = i_F$ and $\pi^{-1}(i_F(X_F)) = j(X_F)$. Then $j$ is separating for $\varphi$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.USep.sepB_of_normBl` (also `GlobalTheory.T.separation_normalizedBlowup_unitaryCompactification`, a second name).

*Proof.* Take $V$, the monomial family, $\xi$, $\mathrm{ev} : V \to K$ and $k_0$ as in the definition of a separating map.

1. A lemma on ratios of theta terms gives a bound $j_0$ for the genus of the family and $n = 3$.
2. The previous theorem with $n = 3$ gives $j \ge j_0$, a Fourier system $\Phi$ of level $9^j$ and the member $y$, with $t_k^2 = \rho(s_{h(k)})$.
3. A lemma extends $\mathrm{ev}$ to $\mathrm{ev}_1 : V_y \to K$ with $\mathrm{ev}_1 \circ \rho = \mathrm{ev}$, and gives approximating elements of degree $2d$ from $J$, its order and the blowup.
4. A support degree $d_0$ comes from supporting tuples of smooth cones. Then a lemma gives $\mathrm{ev}_1(t_k) = 0$, ratios $u(\mu, c)/u(\mu, c')$ in $k_0$, and, for each linear form $\lambda$ that kills every $\delta_k$, two unit coefficients whose exponents differ by $M\lambda$ with a ratio in $k_0$. This step uses that $\varphi$ has no self-intersections.
5. Condition 1 of separation: $\mathrm{ev}(s_{h(k)}) = \mathrm{ev}_1(t_k)^2 = 0$.
6. Condition 2: step 1 and the ratios of step 4 give the descent of the abelian part over $V_y$, and a lemma moves it back to $V$ along $\rho$.
7. Condition 3: fix $i$. A lemma moves the base ring to $V_y$, and a second lemma gives $\mathrm{ev}_1(u)^2 = r \prod_k \mathrm{ev}(s_{m+k})^{M' \varepsilon_{m+k}(\mu, \mu)}$ with $r \in k_0$. A Smith normal form gives $n \ne 0$ and a linear form $\lambda$ that is $n$ times the coordinate of $\varepsilon_{m+i}$ on balanced forms and kills the $\delta_k$. Step 4 for $\lambda$ gives $\mathrm{ev}(s_{m+i})^{M' M n} \in k_0$. As $k_0$ is algebraically closed, $\mathrm{ev}(s_{m+i}) \in k_0$. $\square$

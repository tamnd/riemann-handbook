## Lower bases and tautological charts

This section proves two results that Sections 10 and 11 state and use. The first result makes a lower base from linear points of an object over a local domain. It gives [the theorem on lower bases of linear points](/entry/lower-datum-object-comes-from-base-of-linear-points/) of Section 10, which the construction of canonical families in Section 11 uses. The second result shows that a canonical family over a chart ring, with boundary parameters equal to the coordinates $u_i$, is a tautological chart with rigid coordinates. Section 11 uses it in the proof of [the theorem on tautological charts through a family](/entry/exists-tautological-chart-versality/). We use the notation of Sections 10 and 11. In particular, a lower base $L$ for $D'$ has a regular local domain $W$, a map $b : F \to W$, an object $o$, degeneration data $Q$ over $W$ with character lattice $X_Q$, points $c(y)$ for $y \in X_Q$ and an isomorphism $\varphi_B$ from the group of $o$ to the abelian part of $Q$.

**Theorem 7.13.1 (Lower base of linear points).** Let $D'$ be a Hermitian datum and $F$ a coefficient field for $l$. Let $V$ be a local domain with a map $b_V : F \to V$, let $o'$ be an object of the moduli problem of $D'$ and $l'$ over $\operatorname{Spec} V$, and let $X$ be an $\mathcal{O}_E$-module that is free of finite rank over $\mathbb{Z}$. Let $x : X \to G_{o'}(V)$ be a map with $x(y + z) = x(y)\, x(z)$ and $x(a y) = a \cdot x(y)$ for $y, z \in X$ and $a \in \mathcal{O}_E$. Then there are a lower base $L$ for $D'$, a local map $\theta_0 : W \to V$ with $\theta_0 \circ b = b_V$, an $\mathcal{O}_E$-linear isomorphism $e : X \to X_Q$ and a map of objects $m : o' \to o$ over $\operatorname{Spec} \theta_0$, such that $\varphi_B \circ m \circ x(y) = c(e y) \circ \operatorname{Spec} \theta_0$ for each $y \in X$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.UCanon.lowBase_of_linearPoints`.

*Proof.* There are five steps.

1. A lemma on lower objects of linear points gives a regular local domain $W$ with a map $b : F \to W$ of essentially finite type, an object $o$ of the moduli problem of $D'$ and $l'$ over $\operatorname{Spec} W$, a map $x_0 : X \to G_o(W)$ that is additive and $\mathcal{O}_E$-linear, a local map $\theta_0 : W \to V$ with $\theta_0 \circ b = b_V$, and a map of objects $m : o' \to o$ over $\operatorname{Spec} \theta_0$ with $m \circ x(y) = x_0(y) \circ \operatorname{Spec} \theta_0$ for each $y$. The lemma also gives the two fineness conditions of a lower base, with the points $x_0(y)$ in place of the points $c(y)$ and $\varphi_B$, and the formula $\dim W + \operatorname{trdeg}_F \kappa(W) = n_{D'} + \operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}_E}(X)\, \tfrac{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}{2}(p' + q')$.
2. The group $G_o$ is proper and semi-abelian over $W$, so it is an abelian scheme. Let $g$ be its relative dimension.
3. A lemma on degeneration data of a principal triple applies to $o$, $g$ and $x_0$. It gives degeneration data $Q$ over $W$, an $\mathcal{O}_E$-module structure on $X_Q$ with an action of $\mathcal{O}_E$ on $Q$, an $\mathcal{O}_E$-linear isomorphism $e : X \to X_Q$ and a map $\varphi_B$ from $G_o$ to the abelian part of $Q$. The data $Q$ satisfy the conditions on degeneration data in the definition of a lower base, $\varphi_B$ satisfies item 3 of Section 10, and $\varphi_B \circ x_0(y) = c(e y)$ for each $y \in X$. Item 3 says that $\varphi_B$ is a cartesian map of group schemes over the identity of $\operatorname{Spec} W$, so $\varphi_B$ is an isomorphism.
4. We show that $W$, $b$, $o$, $Q$ and $\varphi_B$ form a lower base. Let $f_1, f_2 : W \to A$ be maps to a reduced ring that agree on $F$, let $o''$ be an object over $A$ with maps $m_1, m_2$ to $o$ over $\operatorname{Spec} f_1$ and $\operatorname{Spec} f_2$, and let $z(y')$ for $y' \in X_Q$ be points of $G_{o''}$ with $\varphi_B \circ m_k \circ z(y') = c(y') \circ \operatorname{Spec} f_k$ for $k = 1, 2$. Put $z'(y) = z(e y)$ for $y \in X$. Then $\varphi_B \circ m_k \circ z'(y) = c(e y) \circ \operatorname{Spec} f_k = \varphi_B \circ x_0(y) \circ \operatorname{Spec} f_k$. Since $\varphi_B$ is a monomorphism, $m_k \circ z'(y) = x_0(y) \circ \operatorname{Spec} f_k$. The fineness condition of step 1 now gives $f_1 = f_2$. The same argument gives the fineness condition over dual numbers. The isomorphism $e$ gives $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}_E} X = \operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}_E} X_Q$, so the formula of step 1 is the dimension formula of a lower base.
5. For each $y \in X$, step 1 and step 3 give $\varphi_B \circ m \circ x(y) = \varphi_B \circ x_0(y) \circ \operatorname{Spec} \theta_0 = c(e y) \circ \operatorname{Spec} \theta_0$.

This gives $L$, $\theta_0$, $e$ and $m$. $\square$

The relative dimension $g$ of step 2 is only an input to the lemma of step 3. It does not appear in the lower base.

**Theorem 7.13.2 (Canonical families are tautological charts).** Let $E$, $H$ and $D$ be as in Section 1 with $p + q \ge 1$, and let $\varphi$ be a polarized fan with no self-intersections in rank at most $\min(p, q)$. Let $k$ be a field, $m, e \ge 0$ and $R = k[[u_1, \dots, u_m, v_1, \dots, v_e]]$ the chart ring. Let $C_1 = (t_1, P_1, y_1, s^1, \delta^1)$ be a monomial family over $R$ with a canonical structure. Suppose that $C_1$ has $m$ parameters and $s^1_i = u_i$ for each $i$. Then $C_1$ is a tautological chart with rigid coordinates.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.UCanon.isTautB_of_canon` (also `GlobalTheory.T.canonicalFamily_isPointRigid_tautologicalChart`, a second name).

*Proof.* We check the items of the definition of a tautological chart in Section 11 one by one.

1. A lemma on chart rings shows that $R$ is a Mumford base.
2. A lemma on canonical families gives rigid coordinates for $C_1$: an extension of $s^1$ by units $s_1, \dots, s_N$ and of $\delta^1$ to a basis $\varepsilon$ of balanced forms, such that the periods of $P_1$ are monomial in the extended parameters with exponents $M' \varepsilon$, and such that the extended family is point rigid. If we forget point rigidity, this extension gives item 6.
3. Item 1 is the hypothesis on $m$ and $s^1$.
4. A lemma on monomial families over a Mumford base gives item 2, the open classification. This lemma uses only that $R$ is a Mumford base. It does not use the canonical structure.
5. A lemma on local injections of canonical families gives item 3, the injectivity. This is the only step that uses $p + q \ge 1$ and the two hypotheses on $\varphi$.
6. A lemma on rigidity along a stratum gives the rigidity of item 4, and a lemma on point rigid families deduces the point rigidity of item 4 from the rigid coordinates of step 2.
7. A lemma on residue fields of canonical charts gives item 5: the residue field of $R$ is essentially of finite type over $F$, and $m + e + \operatorname{trdeg}_F \kappa(R) = n_D$.

So $C_1$ is a tautological chart. With the rigid coordinates of step 2 it is a tautological chart with rigid coordinates. $\square$

## The symbol is the Kodaira-Spencer matrix

This section shows that the symbol $\Theta$ of a basic heat operator with defect $0$ is a Kodaira-Spencer matrix of its derivation $D$, in the sense of Section 16, on the abelian locus of a uniformized semi-abelian datum. The heat operator lives on the Raynaud extension $H$, and the Kodaira-Spencer matrix lives on the semi-abelian group $G$. The proof compares the two at one formal point. A formal neighbourhood of the origin of $H$ gives a point $x$ of $H$ over a power series ring $\Omega$. The uniformization carries $x$ to a point $x'$ of $G$, and the heat operator acts on the values at $x'$ of the sections of $L^k$. A second operator with the same property comes from a Kodaira-Spencer matrix of $D$, and a uniqueness lemma for the symbol identifies the two matrices. We use the notation of Sections 16 and 17. In particular, $R$ is a complete Noetherian local domain that contains $\mathbb{Q}$, $\operatorname{Frac}$ denotes the fraction field, and for a point $y$ of a scheme over $\operatorname{Spec} \Omega$ and a function $f$ near $y$ we write $y^* f \in \Omega$ for the value of $f$ at $y$.

We first construct the formal point. For a ring map $\varphi : R \to \Omega$ and an ideal $I$ of $R$, we write $\varphi(I)\Omega$ for the ideal of $\Omega$ generated by $\varphi(I)$.

**Theorem 7.20.1 (Formal point at the origin).** Let $G$ be a smooth commutative group scheme over $\operatorname{Spec} R$ with $G$ integral. Let $v_1, \dots, v_n$ be a basis of the tangent vectors of $G$ at the origin and $\partial_1, \dots, \partial_n$ invariant vector fields for them. Let $D : R \to R$ be a map, $W$ an open of $G$ that contains the origin, and $d$ a lift of $D$ on $W$. Then there are a local domain $\Omega$, a ring map $\varphi : R \to \Omega$, a point $x$ of $G$ over $\operatorname{Spec} \Omega$ and derivations $\delta_1, \dots, \delta_n$ and $d_\Omega$ of $\Omega$ such that:

1. $\Omega$ is complete for the $\varphi(\mathfrak{m}_R)\Omega$-adic topology;
2. $x$ lies over $\operatorname{Spec} \varphi$ and lands in $W$;
3. $d_\Omega(\varphi(r)) = \varphi(D r)$ and $\delta_a(\varphi(r)) = 0$ for each $r \in R$ and each $a$;
4. $x^*(\partial_a f) = \delta_a(x^* f)$ for each function $f$ on an open in which $x$ lands, and $x^*(d f) = d_\Omega(x^* f)$ if this open also lies in $W$;
5. if $f$ is a function on an open in which $x$ lands and $x^* f = 0$, then $f = 0$.

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the stalk of $G$ at the origin $e$, with the map $r \mapsto r$ from $R$ to $\mathcal{O}$ and the evaluation $\operatorname{ev} : \mathcal{O} \to R$ at $e$. There are six steps.

1. A lemma on lifts near the unit shows that $D$ is a derivation of $R$, since it has a lift $d$ on an open that contains $e$. The fields $\partial_a$ and $d$ give derivations $\delta^0_a$ and $d^0$ of $\mathcal{O}$ that commute with the germ maps.
2. Invariant vector fields on a commutative group scheme commute, so the $\delta^0_a$ commute. An invariant vector field kills the constants, so $\delta^0_a(r) = 0$, and $d^0(r) = D(r)$ for $r \in R$.
3. A lemma on smooth commutative group schemes gives local coordinates at $e$: elements $z_1, \dots, z_n$ of $\mathcal{O}$ with $\operatorname{ev}(z_i) = 0$ and $\operatorname{ev}(\delta^0_a z_i) = 1$ if $a = i$ and $0$ otherwise, such that the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}$ is generated by $\mathfrak{m}_R$ and the $z_i$.
4. Put $\Omega = R[[z_1, \dots, z_n]]$ and let $\varphi$ be the inclusion of constants. A lemma on Taylor maps gives a ring map $T : \mathcal{O} \to \Omega$ with
$$T(s) = \sum_{m \in \mathbb{N}^n} \frac{1}{m!}\, \operatorname{ev}\bigl((\delta^0)^m s\bigr)\, z^m.$$
Since the $\delta^0_a$ kill the constants, $T(r) = r$ for $r \in R$. The constant coefficient of $T(s)$ is $\operatorname{ev}(s)$, so $T$ is a local map. Let $x$ be the composite of $\operatorname{Spec} T$ with the canonical map from the spectrum of the stalk to $G$. Then $x$ lies over $\operatorname{Spec} \varphi$, $x$ lands in each open that contains $e$, and $x^* f = T(f_e)$ for the germ $f_e$ of $f$ at $e$. The ring $\Omega$ is a local domain, and it is complete for the $\mathfrak{m}_R\Omega$-adic topology because $R$ is Noetherian and complete. This gives items 1 and 2.
5. Let $\delta_a = \partial / \partial z_a$. Since the $\delta^0_a$ commute, a lemma on Taylor maps gives $T \circ \delta^0_a = \delta_a \circ T$. A lemma on derivations of power series rings, with the coordinates of step 3, gives a derivation $d_\Omega$ of $\Omega$ with $d_\Omega(r) = D(r)$ on constants and $d_\Omega \circ T = T \circ d^0$. This gives items 3 and 4.
6. If $x^* f = 0$, then $T(f_e) = 0$. A lemma on Taylor maps of local rings, with the coordinates of step 3, gives $f_e = 0$. Since $G$ is integral, the germ map is injective, so $f = 0$. This gives item 5. $\square$

The next lemma is a standard fact on localizations.

**Lemma 7.20.2 (Derivations of localizations).** Let $A$ be a ring, $M$ a multiplicative subset and $\delta$ a derivation of $A$. Then $\delta$ extends to a derivation of $M^{-1} A$.

*Proof.* Let $S = M^{-1}A$ and $S[\varepsilon]$ the dual numbers over $S$. The map $a \mapsto a + \delta(a)\varepsilon$ is a ring map $A \to S[\varepsilon]$, by the Leibniz rule. It takes each $m \in M$ to a unit, because the first component of $m + \delta(m)\varepsilon$ is a unit in $S$. So it extends to a ring map $L : S \to S[\varepsilon]$. The first component of $L$ is the identity of $S$, since this holds on $A$. So the $\varepsilon$-component of $L$ is a derivation of $S$ that extends $\delta$. $\square$

We now come to the main theorem. Let $E$ and $H_E$ be as in Section 1, where we write $H_E$ for the datum $H$ of Section 1 because $H$ now denotes a Raynaud extension, and let $t$ be a semi-abelian datum on $\operatorname{Spec} R$ with group $G$ and line bundle $L$, and let $P$ be a uniformization of $t$ along the maximal ideal of $R$ by degeneration data $Q$ with an action of $O$. Let $H$ be a Raynaud extension of $Q$. A tangent vector $v'$ of $G$ at the origin *corresponds* to a tangent vector $v$ of $H$ at the origin if the uniformization takes $v$ to $v'$; similarly for points of $H$ and of $G$ over a ring. We have not unfolded this relation.

**Theorem 7.20.3 (Symbol of a commuting heat operator).** Let $E$, $H_E$, $R$, $t$, $P$, $Q$, $O$ and $H$ be as above. Suppose that $Q$ is monomial in nonzero $t_1, \dots, t_m$ with exponents $\gamma$, and that the abelian locus of $G$ is the basic open $V = D(t_1 \cdots t_m)$ of $\operatorname{Spec} R$. Let $v_1, \dots, v_n$ be a basis of the tangent vectors of $H$ at the origin, $\partial_1, \dots, \partial_n$ invariant vector fields for them, and $v'_1, \dots, v'_n$ tangent vectors of $G$ at the origin such that $v'_a$ corresponds to $v_a$. Let $D$ be a derivation of $R$ and $h$ a basic heat operator for $D$ with symbol $\Theta$ and defect $0$. Then $\Theta$ is a Kodaira-Spencer matrix of $D$ along $V$, on the affine open $\operatorname{Spec} R$, for the tangent vectors $v'_a$.

*Proof.* There are eight steps.

1. Lemmas on the Raynaud extension show that $H$ is smooth over $R$ and integral. The opens $W_i$ of $h$ cover $H$, so the origin lies in some $W_{i_0}$.
2. [The theorem on the formal point](/entry/exists-formal-point/) applies to $H$, $v$, $\partial$, $D$, $W_{i_0}$ and $d_{i_0}$. It gives $\Omega$, $\varphi$, $x$, $\delta_a$ and $d_\Omega$.
3. The map $\varphi$ is injective. Let $\varphi(r) = 0$. The value at $x$ of the constant function $r$ on $H$ is $\varphi(r) = 0$, so by item 5 of that theorem the constant function $r$ is $0$. Its value at the origin is $r$, so $r = 0$.
4. A lemma on points of the uniformized group gives a point $x'$ of $G$ over $\operatorname{Spec} \Omega$ that lies over $\operatorname{Spec} \varphi$ and corresponds to $x$, and invariant vector fields $\partial'_a$ on $G$ for the $v'_a$, such that $x'^*(\partial'_a f) = \delta_a(x'^* f)$, and $x'^* f = 0$ implies $f = 0$. A lemma on tangent bases shows that the $v'_a$ form a basis.
5. Let $g$ be a generator of the pullback of $L$ along $x'$, which exists because $\Omega$ is local. For $k \ge 1$, call $s \in \operatorname{Frac} \Omega$ a *value of level $k$* if there are $r \in R$ with $r \ne 0$ and a section $\sigma$ of $L^k$ on $G$ such that $\varphi(r)\, s$ is the value of $\sigma$ at $x'$ in the trivialization $g^k$. By the lemma on localizations, each $\delta_a$ extends to a derivation of $\operatorname{Frac} \Omega$. For a map $d$, elements $u_a$, $p$, $p'$ of $\operatorname{Frac} \Omega$ and a matrix $\Lambda$ over $\operatorname{Frac} \Omega$, put
$$\mathcal{H}_k(s) = 2k\, d s - \sum_{a,b} \Lambda_{ab}\, \delta_a \delta_b s + 2 \sum_a u_a\, \delta_a s + (2k^2 p + 2k p') s.$$
6. A lemma on commuting heat operators uses that $h$ is basic with defect $0$. It gives a derivation $d_1$ of $\operatorname{Frac} \Omega$ with $d_1(\varphi(r)) = \varphi(D r)$ and elements $u, p, p'$ such that, with $\Lambda_1 = \varphi(\Theta)$, the operator $\mathcal{H}_k$ takes values of level $k$ to values of level $k$ for each $k \ge 1$.
7. The derivation $D$ extends to a derivation $D_V$ of $\Gamma(V) = R[1/(t_1 \cdots t_m)]$, by the lemma on localizations. A lemma on the abelian locus gives a symmetric matrix $\Theta_1$ over $\Gamma(V)$ that is a Kodaira-Spencer matrix of $D_V$ along $V$, on the affine open $V$. A lemma on Kodaira-Spencer matrices at formal points gives a ring map $\iota : \Gamma(V) \to \operatorname{Frac} \Omega$ that extends $\varphi$, a derivation $d_2$ with $d_2(\varphi(r)) = \varphi(D r)$ and elements $u_2, p_2, p'_2$ such that, with $\Lambda_2 = \iota(\Theta_1)$, the operator $\mathcal{H}_k$ also takes values of level $k$ to values of level $k$.
8. The matrices $\Lambda_1$ and $\Lambda_2$ are symmetric, and $d_1$ and $d_2$ agree on $\varphi(R)$. A lemma on the uniqueness of the symbol gives $\Lambda_1 = \Lambda_2$. The map $\iota$ is injective: an element of $\Gamma(V)$ has the form $a / (t_1 \cdots t_m)^e$, and $\iota(a) = \varphi(a)$ in $\operatorname{Frac} \Omega$, so $\iota$ of it is $0$ only if $\varphi(a) = 0$, that is $a = 0$ by step 3. So $\Theta_1$ is the image of $\Theta$ in $\Gamma(V)$. A last lemma takes a Kodaira-Spencer matrix of $D_V$ on the affine open $V$, with entries from $R$, to a Kodaira-Spencer matrix of $D$ on the affine open $\operatorname{Spec} R$. $\square$

Only step 6 uses that $h$ is basic and has defect $0$. The monomial data and the condition on the abelian locus go to the lemmas of steps 4, 6, 7 and 8.

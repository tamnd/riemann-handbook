This chapter builds integral models of Harris and Taylor type and studies their geometry. It proves that a moduli problem of abelian schemes with polarization, endomorphisms and level structure of the type that Harris and Taylor use has a smooth quasi-projective fine moduli scheme over every noetherian ring in which the integers prime to $p$ are units ([the existence of a model](/entry/model-nonempty/)). The first section reduces this to an auxiliary Siegel problem: abelian schemes of genus $h$ with a polarization whose kernel is killed by an integer $d$ and with a symplectic level $M$ structure. Sections 2 to 7 prove that this Siegel problem is representable when $M \geq 3$ is odd and prime to $d$. They start from a Siegel family given by geometric invariant theory and show that it is a fine moduli scheme for principally polarized objects, by gluing over Artinian fibre products, lifting along small extensions, completion and algebraization, flat descent and limits; then Zarhin's trick passes to polarizations with kernel killed by $d$. Sections 8 to 15 build the schemes of homomorphisms with fixed pullback and of actions of an order, prove that they are finite and unramified, and supply the tools for the cut-out: images of idempotents, the universal matrix, vanishing for ample line bundles and bases of sections stable under base change. Sections 16 to 18 cut out the Lie and level loci and remove the auxiliary level. Sections 19 to 22 prove smoothness of relative dimension $a(n-1)$ through first-order deformations and count the Kodaira-Spencer directions. Section 23 shows that only finitely many classes of points contribute to the count of marked fixed points, and Section 24 describes the complex points by lattices and period maps. Sections 25 and 26 build Dieudonné modules from quasi-logarithms and show that the characteristic polynomial of an endomorphism on de Rham homology is the image of its integer characteristic polynomial. Sections 27 to 29 construct the scheme of isogenies of type $T_j$, through quotients by finite étale subgroups and isogenies with a quasi-inverse. Section 30 identifies primitive de Rham classes with rigidified extensions of the $p$-divisible group. Section 31 proves that every object over the fraction field of a complete discrete valuation ring has potentially good reduction when the datum is compact, and deduces that the model is proper and projective.

The chapter needs from Volume 1 the [abelian schemes](/learn/abelian-varieties/#abelian-schemes), [polarizations and Zarhin's trick](/learn/abelian-varieties/#polarizations-and-zarhins-trick), [Siegel moduli schemes and level structures](/learn/abelian-varieties/#siegel-moduli-schemes-and-level-structures), [Néron models and good reduction](/learn/abelian-varieties/#neron-models-and-good-reduction), [local rings and dimension](/learn/commutative-algebra/#local-rings-and-dimension), [completion](/learn/commutative-algebra/#completion), [Grothendieck's existence theorem](/learn/commutative-algebra/#formal-functions-and-grothendiecks-existence-theorem), [Picard groups](/learn/commutative-algebra/#picard-groups) and [deformation functors](/learn/deformations-patching/#deformation-functors). The models, their tangent spaces, their isogenies and their de Rham homology feed [the Kodaira and Spencer package in weight one](/entry/kodaira-spencer-of-weight-one/), which the step [GeoOne holds](/step/geo-one-holds/) of [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/) uses.

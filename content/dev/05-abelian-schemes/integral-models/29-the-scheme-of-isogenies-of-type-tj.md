## The scheme of isogenies of type T_j

This section proves [the theorem on isogenies of type $T_j$](/entry/harris-taylor-type-tj-isogenies-representable/): the $c$-isogenies of type $T_j$ out of an object of level $N$ are classified by a [[finite|N9C4X7XKE4]] [[étale|H84VHC8PX2]] scheme over the base. The proof starts from the scheme $Z$ of [the previous section](/entry/isogeny-rep/), which classifies isogenies of abelian schemes with a quasi-inverse for $c$. A point of $Z$ is a $c$-isogeny of objects when three conditions hold: the kernel is stable under $O$, the polarization descends, and the isogeny has type $T_j$. Each condition cuts out an open and closed part of $Z$, so their intersection $Y$ is again finite and étale. Over $Y$ the extra structure descends along the universal isogeny, and rigidity for $N \geq 3$ gives the universal property.

We keep the notation of Section 8.27: $D$ is a datum for $p$, $b$ a base over $R$, objects of level $N$ are written $o = (A, \iota, P, \eta)$ (Definition 8.1.3), and $c$-isogenies and type $T_j$ are as defined there. A quasi-inverse pair for $c$ is as in [the previous section](/entry/isogeny-rep/).

### Three conditions over a map

Let $o = (A, \iota, P, \eta)$ be an object of level $N$ over $s : Y \to \operatorname{Spec} R$, let $B$ be a commutative group scheme over $Y$, let $\psi : A \to B$ and $\psi' : B \to A$ be homomorphisms, and let $t : T_b \to Y$ be a map of schemes. A scheme $T$ over $Y$ is *over $t$* if $T \to Y$ factors through $t$.

**Definition 8.29.1 (Conditions over a map).** We say:

1. $\psi$ has *$O$-stable kernel over $t$* if for every $T$ over $t$, every point $g$ of $A$ over $T$ with $\psi \circ g = 0$, and every $a \in O$, we have $\psi \circ \iota(a) \circ g = 0$;
2. $\psi'$ has *polarization kernel $c$ over $t$* if for every $T$ over $t$ and every point $x$ of $B$ over $T$ with $c^3 x = 0$, the pullback of $P$ along $(\psi' \circ \operatorname{pr}_1, \psi' \circ x \circ \operatorname{pr}_2) : B \times_Y T \to A \times_Y A$ is trivial if and only if $c x = 0$;
3. $\psi$ has *type $T_j$ for $(r, w)$ over $t$* if the two conditions of type $T_j$ (Section 8.27) hold at every geometric point $x : \operatorname{Spec} k \to Y$ of characteristic $0$ that factors through $t$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.UPel.HT.ES.KerStableAt` (also `GlobalTheory.UPel.HT.ES.PolKerAt`, `GlobalTheory.UPel.HT.ES.TjAt`).

For $t = \mathrm{id}_Y$, condition 3 is type $T_j$ itself.

**Lemma 8.29.2 (Descent along an isogeny).** Let $p$ be a unit in $R$, $c = p^e$, $Y$ locally noetherian, $o$ an object over $s : Y \to \operatorname{Spec} R$, $B$ an abelian scheme over $Y$, and $(\psi, \psi')$ a quasi-inverse pair for $c$ from $A$ to $B$. Assume that $\psi$ has $O$-stable kernel and $\psi'$ has polarization kernel $c$, both over $\mathrm{id}_Y$. Then there are an object $o'$ over $s$, a $c$-isogeny $\chi : o \to o'$, and an isomorphism $\theta : A' \to B$ of group schemes over $Y$ with $\psi = \theta \circ \chi$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.UPel.HT.ES.IsogRep.exists_obj`.

*Proof.* Take $A' = B$, $\theta = \mathrm{id}$ and $\chi = \psi$. Input 8 gives the action $\iota'$, the line bundle $P'$ and all conditions of Definition 8.1.3 on $(B, \iota', P')$. Put $\eta' = \psi \circ \eta$; it is additive, $O$-linear and kills $N \Lambda$ since $\eta$ is, and input 8 gives the Tate condition. So $o' = (B, \iota', P', \eta')$ is an object. Then $\psi$ is a $c$-isogeny: $c = p^e$, $\psi$ commutes with $O$, $\eta' = \psi \circ \eta$, $P^{\otimes c} \cong (\psi \times \psi)^* P'$, and $\psi'$ is a quasi-inverse. $\square$

### The theorem

**Theorem 8.29.3 (The scheme of isogenies of type $T_j$).** Let $R$ be a [[noetherian|0670T59HGE]] ring, $b$ a base for $D$ over $R$, and $N \geq 3$ an integer prime to $p$ such that $p$ is a unit in $R$. Let $r \in \mathbb{Z}$ and let $w$ be a maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_F$ with $u_r \subseteq w$. Let $j \leq n$ and $c = p^e$, where $e > 0$ if $j > 0$. Assume $m = 1$. Let $S$ be a locally noetherian scheme, $s : S \to \operatorname{Spec} R$, and $o$ an object of level $N$ over $s$. Then there are a finite étale map $\pi : Y \to S$, an object $o_0$ of level $N$ over $s \pi$ with a map of objects $o_0 \to o$ over $\pi$, an object $o'$ of level $N$ over $s \pi$, and a $c$-isogeny $\psi : A_0 \to A'$ from $o_0$ to $o'$ of type $T_j$ for $(r, w)$, such that the following holds. Let $T$ be a scheme, $x : T \to S$ a map, $o_1$ and $o_1'$ objects of level $N$ over $s x$ with a map of objects $o_1 \to o$ over $x$, and $\psi_1 : A_1 \to A_1'$ a $c$-isogeny from $o_1$ to $o_1'$ of type $T_j$ for $(r, w)$. Then there is a unique $y : T \to Y$ with $\pi y = x$ such that there exist maps of objects $\varphi_0 : o_1 \to o_0$ and $\varphi_1 : o_1' \to o'$ over $y$ with $\psi \circ \varphi_0 = \varphi_1 \circ \psi_1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.UPel.HT.ES.IsogRep.exists_rep`.

*Proof.* Here $c = p^e > 0$ is a unit on $S$.

Step 1 (the scheme $Z$). Input 1 for $A$ and $k = c$ gives a finite étale $Z \to S$. By input 2 there is an object $o_Z$ over $Z$ with a map $o_Z \to o$; its abelian scheme $A_Z$ is a group pullback of $A$, so input 1 gives an abelian scheme $B$ over $Z$ and a universal quasi-inverse pair $(\psi_Z, \psi_Z')$ for $c$ from $A_Z$ to $B$.

Step 2 (the scheme $Y$). Input 5 gives open and closed subsets $W_1$, $W_2$, $W_3$ of $Z$ for the three conditions on $(\psi_Z, \psi_Z')$. Input 7 for $U = W_1 \cap W_2 \cap W_3$ gives a finite étale $\pi : Y \to S$ and a monomorphism $i : Y \to Z$ over $S$, and a map to $Z$ over $S$ factors through $i$ if and only if its image lies in $U$. As $Y \to S$ is finite, $Y$ is locally noetherian.

Step 3 (the isogeny over $Y$). Input 2 gives an object $o_0$ over $Y$ with a map $o_0 \to o_Z$ over $i$, and so a map $o_0 \to o$ over $\pi$. Input 1 pulls $(\psi_Z, \psi_Z')$ back to a quasi-inverse pair $(\psi_0, \psi_0')$ from $A_0$ to an abelian scheme $B_0$ over $Y$. The image of $i$ lies in $U$, so by input 5 the three conditions hold for $(\psi_Z, \psi_Z')$ over $i$, and by input 6 they hold for $(\psi_0, \psi_0')$ over $\mathrm{id}_Y$. The lemma above gives an object $o'$ over $Y$ and a $c$-isogeny $\psi : o_0 \to o'$ with $\psi_0 = \theta \circ \psi$ for an isomorphism $\theta$. By input 6 again, $\psi$ has type $T_j$ for $(r, w)$ over $\mathrm{id}_Y$, which is type $T_j$.

Step 4 (existence of $y$). Let $T$, $x$, $o_1 \to o$, $o_1'$ and $\psi_1$ be as in the statement. By input 9, $\psi_1$ has a quasi-inverse $\psi_1'$. The given map $o_1 \to o$ makes $A_1$ a group pullback of $A$ along $x$, so the universal property of $Z$ gives $z : T \to Z$ over $S$ with maps $\theta_1 : A_1 \to A_Z$ and $\theta_1' : A_1' \to B$ that are group pullbacks along $z$, with $\theta_1$ followed by $A_Z \to A$ equal to the given map, and with $\psi_Z \circ \theta_1 = \theta_1' \circ \psi_1$. By input 4 there is a map of objects $o_1 \to o_Z$ over $z$ whose underlying map, followed by $A_Z \to A$, is the given map. Since $A_Z$ is the fibre product of $A$ and $Z$ over $S$, a map into $A_Z$ is fixed by its two projections, so this underlying map is $\theta_1$. Input 10 gives $\psi_Z' \circ \theta_1' = \theta_1 \circ \psi_1'$. Now $\psi_1$ is a $c$-isogeny of type $T_j$, so by input 9 the three conditions hold for $(\psi_1, \psi_1')$ over $\mathrm{id}_T$, and by input 6 they hold for $(\psi_Z, \psi_Z')$ over $z$. By input 5 the image of $z$ lies in $U$, and by input 7 we get $y : T \to Y$ with $i y = z$, so $\pi y = x$. Input 4 gives a map of objects $\varphi_0 : o_1 \to o_0$ over $y$. The isomorphism $\theta$ followed by $B_0 \to B$ makes $A'$ a group pullback of $B$ along $i$, so $\theta_1'$ and $y$ give a map $\varphi : A_1' \to A'$ that makes $A_1'$ a group pullback of $A'$ along $y$. The two maps $\psi \circ \varphi_0$ and $\varphi \circ \psi_1$ from $A_1$ to $A'$ agree after the two projections of this fibre product, so they are equal. So $\psi \circ \varphi_0 = \varphi \circ \psi_1$, and input 11 makes $\varphi$ the underlying map of a map of objects $\varphi_1 : o_1' \to o'$ over $y$.

Step 5 (uniqueness of $y$). Let $y'$ be a second map with $\pi y' = x$ and maps of objects $\varphi_0'$ and $\varphi_1'$ over $y'$ with $\psi \circ \varphi_0' = \varphi_1' \circ \psi_1$. Composing with the maps $o_0 \to o_Z$ and $o' \to B$, the map $i y'$ satisfies the universal property of $Z$ for $\psi_1$: the composed map $o_1 \to o_Z \to o$ has the same underlying map as the given map $o_1 \to o$, by rigidity (input 3). By the uniqueness for $Z$, $i y' = z = i y$, and since $i$ is a monomorphism, $y' = y$. $\square$

By rigidity, the universal property need not ask that $\varphi_0$ be compatible with the given map $o_1 \to o$.

**Remark 8.29.4 (Formalization).** The theorem is in [HarrisTaylorTypeTjIsogeniesRepresentable.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/HarrisTaylorTypeTjIsogeniesRepresentable.lean#L108).

## The Siegel problem with kernel killed by d

This section proves that the Siegel problem $(h, M, d)$ of the first section is representable when $h \geq 1$, $M \geq 3$ is odd and $\gcd(M, d) = 1$. This is the first of the four results that the first section uses. The previous sections show that a Siegel family is a fine moduli scheme for principal Siegel objects. We go from principal objects to objects with kernel killed by $d$ in two steps. First we add an action of a fixed ring $O$ that satisfies a Rosati condition. A finite scheme over the Siegel family represents these actions. Then Zarhin's trick replaces a Siegel object $c$ of genus $h$ with kernel killed by $d$ by a principal Siegel object of genus $8h$ with an action of $O$, and $c$ is recovered as the image of an idempotent $e \in O$. For Zarhin's trick see [polarizations and Zarhin's trick](/learn/abelian-varieties/#polarizations-and-zarhins-trick) and Moret-Bailly, *Pinceaux de variétés abéliennes*, Astérisque 129 (1985), Chapitre IX. For [abelian schemes](/learn/abelian-varieties/#abelian-schemes) and [Siegel moduli schemes](/learn/abelian-varieties/#siegel-moduli-schemes-and-level-structures) see Volume 1.

### Actions of an order

**Definition 8.7.1 (Order datum).** An *order datum* consists of a ring $O$, not necessarily commutative, an additive map $a \mapsto \bar{a}$ from $O$ to $O$, elements $e_1, \dots, e_r$ that generate $O$ as an abelian group, a matrix $W \in M_r(\mathbb{Z})$ such that $W + W^{T}$ is positive definite over $\mathbb{R}$, and an integer $d_0 > 0$ with
$$\sum_{i, j} W_{ij}\, e_j \bar{e}_i = d_0 \quad \text{in } O.$$

The last condition is a positivity condition. It makes the scheme of actions below finite.

**Definition 8.7.2 (Action with the Rosati condition).** Let $A$ be an abelian scheme over $S$ with a line bundle $P$ on $A \times_S A$. A *right action* of $O$ on $A$ is a map $a \mapsto [a]$ from $O$ to the endomorphisms of the group scheme $A$ with $[1] = 1$, $[ab] = [b][a]$ and $[a + b] = [a] + [b]$. It satisfies the *Rosati condition* for $P$ if for every $a \in O$ the line bundles $([a] \times 1)^* P$ and $(1 \times [\bar{a}])^* P$ on $A \times_S A$ are isomorphic. Let $(c, [\cdot])$ and $(c', [\cdot]')$ be Siegel objects with right actions over $s$ and $s'$, and $f : S' \to S$. A *map* $(c', [\cdot]') \to (c, [\cdot])$ over $f$ is a map $\varphi : c' \to c$ over $f$ of Siegel objects with $[a] \varphi = \varphi [a]'$ for every $a \in O$.

When $P$ is the line bundle of a polarization $\mu : A \to A^\vee$, the Rosati condition says that $\mu [a] = [\bar{a}]^\vee \mu$, so the Rosati involution of $\mu$ sends $[a]$ to $[\bar{a}]$.

We use the following results.

1. (Siegel families exist.) For $h \geq 1$ and odd $M \geq 3$ there is a Siegel family $F = (X, s, u)$ of genus $h$ and level $M$ (Mumford, Fogarty and Kirwan, *Geometric Invariant Theory*, 3rd ed., Chapter 7). By the theorem of the previous section, it is a fine moduli scheme for principal Siegel objects.
2. (Noetherian bases.) The ring $B_M$ is a Dedekind domain. A scheme that is quasi-projective over a noetherian ring is locally noetherian, and a finite map followed by a quasi-projective map to $\operatorname{Spec}$ of a noetherian ring is quasi-projective.
3. ([Actions are finite](/entry/adjoint-actions-order-finite-unramified-scheme/).) Let $S$ be locally noetherian, let $A$ be an abelian scheme over $S$ with a line bundle $P$ on $A \times_S A$ that is trivial on $0 \times A$ and $A \times 0$, symmetric, with $\Delta^* P$ relatively ample, and fix an order datum. Then there are a finite map $\pi : H \to S$, a group pullback $\varphi : A_H \to A$ along $\pi$ and a right action on $A_H$ with the Rosati condition for $(\varphi \times \varphi)^* P$, with this property. For every $t : T \to S$, every group pullback $\varphi' : A' \to A$ along $t$ and every right action on $A'$ with the Rosati condition for $(\varphi' \times \varphi')^* P$, there is a unique $g : T \to H$ with $\pi g = t$ for which there is a group pullback $\psi : A' \to A_H$ along $g$ with $\varphi \psi = \varphi'$ that commutes with the actions.
4. (Pullbacks.) If $c$ is a Siegel object over $S$ and $\varphi : A' \to A$ is a group pullback along $f : S' \to S$, then $A'$ with the pullbacks of $P$ and of the level structure is a Siegel object $c'$ with a map $c' \to c$ over $f$. If $c$ is principal, then so is $c'$.
5. (Rigidity.) Let $M \geq 3$ be odd and let $u$ be principal. Two maps $c \to u$ of Siegel objects over the same map $f$ have the same underlying map of abelian schemes. This is a form of Serre's lemma that an automorphism of a polarized abelian variety that fixes the points of order $M \geq 3$ is the identity (Mumford, *Abelian varieties*, §21).

The Rosati condition depends only on the class of $P$ in $\operatorname{Pic}(A \times_S A)$. So if $P \cong P'$, an action has the Rosati condition for $P$ if and only if it has it for $P'$.

**Theorem 8.7.3 (Principal Siegel objects with an action of an order).** Let $h \geq 1$, let $M \geq 3$ be odd, and fix an order datum. Then there are a locally noetherian scheme $H$, a quasi-projective map $s_H : H \to \operatorname{Spec} B_M$, a principal Siegel object $z_H$ of genus $h$ and level $M$ over $s_H$ and a right action on $z_H$ with the Rosati condition, with this property. For every scheme $S$, every $t : S \to \operatorname{Spec} B_M$ and every principal Siegel object $z$ over $t$ with a right action with the Rosati condition, there is a unique $g : S \to H$ with $s_H g = t$ such that there is a map from $z$ with its action to $z_H$ with its action over $g$.

*Proof.* Let $F = (X, s, u)$ be a Siegel family (result 1), with $u = (A, P, \lambda)$. By result 2, $X$ is locally noetherian. Result 3 for $A$ and $P$ gives $\pi : H \to X$, $\varphi : A_H \to A$ and an action on $A_H$. Let $s_H = s \pi$. By result 2, $s_H$ is quasi-projective and $H$ is locally noetherian. Result 4 gives the Siegel object $z_H$ with abelian scheme $A_H$ and a map $\varphi : z_H \to u$ over $\pi$, and $z_H$ is principal. Its line bundle is $(\varphi \times \varphi)^* P$, so the action on $A_H$ has the Rosati condition for $z_H$.

Existence. Let $z = (A_z, P_z, \lambda_z)$ be principal over $t$ with an action. By the theorem of the previous section there is $f : S \to X$ with $s f = t$ and a map $\varphi_z : z \to u$ over $f$. Then $P_z \cong (\varphi_z \times \varphi_z)^* P$, so the action on $A_z$ has the Rosati condition for $(\varphi_z \times \varphi_z)^* P$. Result 3 gives $g : S \to H$ with $\pi g = f$ and a group pullback $\psi : A_z \to A_H$ along $g$ with $\varphi \psi = \varphi_z$ that commutes with the actions. Then $s_H g = s f = t$. The line bundle $P_z$ is isomorphic to $(\varphi_z \times \varphi_z)^* P = (\psi \times \psi)^* (\varphi \times \varphi)^* P$, which is the pullback along $\psi \times \psi$ of the line bundle of $z_H$. For the level structures, $\psi \lambda_z(a)$ and $\lambda_{z_H}(a) g$ are two maps to the fibre product $A_H = A \times_X H$, and their components $\varphi \psi \lambda_z(a) = \varphi_z \lambda_z(a) = \lambda(a) f$ in $A$ and $g$ in $H$ agree. So $\psi$ is a map of Siegel objects with actions over $g$.

Uniqueness. Let $g' : S \to H$ with $s_H g' = t$ and a map $\psi'$ from $z$ with its action to $z_H$ over $g'$. Then $\varphi \psi'$ is a map $z \to u$ over $\pi g'$, so $\pi g' = f$ by the uniqueness in the theorem of the previous section. Now $\varphi \psi'$ and $\varphi_z$ are two maps $z \to u$ over $f$, so result 5 gives $\varphi \psi' = \varphi_z$. Thus $\psi'$ is a group pullback along $g'$ with $\varphi \psi' = \varphi_z$ that commutes with the actions, and the uniqueness in result 3 gives $g' = g$. $\square$

### Zarhin's trick

Fix $h \geq 1$, odd $M \geq 3$ and $d > 0$ with $\gcd(M, d) = 1$, and let $h' = 8h$.

**Definition 8.7.4 (Zarhin datum and Zarhin relation).** A *Zarhin datum* consists of an order datum, an idempotent $e \in O$, an additive map $j : \mathbb{Z}^{2h} \to \mathbb{Z}^{2h'}$, and elements $o_1, \dots, o_n \in O$ with additive maps $\kappa_1, \dots, \kappa_n : \mathbb{Z}^{2h'} \to \mathbb{Z}^{2h}$. Let $c = (A, P, \lambda)$ be a Siegel object of genus $h$ and $z = (A_z, P_z, \lambda_z)$ a Siegel object of genus $h'$ over the same $t : S \to \operatorname{Spec} B_M$, with a right action on $A_z$. Then $z$ is *Zarhin related* to $c$ if there are maps of group schemes $i : A \to A_z$ and $q : A_z \to A$ with the following properties:

1. $q i = 1$ and $i q = [e]$;
2. $P \cong (i \times i)^* P_z$;
3. $i \lambda(a) = \lambda_z(j(a))$ for every $a \in \mathbb{Z}^{2h}$;
4. $\lambda_z(a) = \sum_{k} [o_k]\, i\, \lambda(\kappa_k(a))$ for every $a \in \mathbb{Z}^{2h'}$.

So $A$ is the image of the idempotent $[e]$ on $A_z$, the polarization of $c$ is the restriction of the polarization of $z$, and the two level structures determine each other.

We use the following results.

6. ([Zarhin's trick](/entry/exists-zarhin-trick-family-siegel-kernel-killed/).) There is a Zarhin datum with these two properties. (a) For every Siegel object $c$ of genus $h$ with kernel killed by $d$ over a scheme $S$ on which $d$ is a unit, there is a principal Siegel object $z$ of genus $h'$ over $S$ with a right action with the Rosati condition that is Zarhin related to $c$. (b) If $z_1$ and $z_2$ are two such objects for the same $c$, there is a map from $z_1$ with its action to $z_2$ with its action over the identity of $S$.
7. ([The image of the idempotent](/entry/siegel-family-idempotent-image-open-locus/).) Fix a Zarhin datum. Let $S'$ be locally noetherian, $t : S' \to \operatorname{Spec} B_M$, and $z$ a principal Siegel object of genus $h'$ over $t$ with a right action with the Rosati condition. Then there are a quasi-compact immersion $\jmath : Y \to S'$ such that $d$ is a unit on $Y$ and a Siegel object $c_Y$ of genus $h$ over $t \jmath$ with kernel killed by $d$, with two properties. (a) For every $y : T \to Y$ and every map $c' \to c_Y$ over $y$, there is a principal Siegel object $w$ of genus $h'$ over $T$ with a right action with the Rosati condition, Zarhin related to $c'$, and a map from $w$ with its action to $z$ with its action over $\jmath y$. (b) Let $g : T \to S'$ and let $c'$ be a Siegel object of genus $h$ over $t g$ with kernel killed by $d$, where $d$ is a unit on $T$. If some $w$ over $T$ with an action is Zarhin related to $c'$ and has a map to $z$ with its action over $g$, then there is a unique $y : T \to Y$ with $\jmath y = g$ such that there is a map $c' \to c_Y$ over $y$.
8. (Composition.) Maps of Siegel objects with actions compose. A quasi-compact immersion followed by a quasi-projective map is quasi-projective.

**Theorem 8.7.5 (Representability of the Siegel problem).** Let $h \geq 1$, let $M \geq 3$ be odd, and let $d > 0$ with $\gcd(M, d) = 1$. Then the Siegel problem $(h, M, d)$ is representable.

*Proof.* Fix a Zarhin datum as in result 6. The theorem on actions of an order, for genus $h' = 8h \geq 1$ and the order datum of the Zarhin datum, gives $H$, $s_H$, $z_H$ and its action. The scheme $H$ is locally noetherian, so result 7 applies to $z_H$ and gives $\jmath : Y \to H$ and $c_Y$. Let $X = Y$, $s = s_H \jmath$ and $u = c_Y$. Then $u$ has kernel killed by $d$, $d$ is a unit on $X$, and $s$ is quasi-projective by result 8.

Existence. Let $c'$ be a Siegel object of genus $h$ over $t : S \to \operatorname{Spec} B_M$ with kernel killed by $d$, where $d$ is a unit on $S$. Result 6(a) gives $z'$ with an action, Zarhin related to $c'$. The theorem on actions gives $g : S \to H$ with $s_H g = t$ and a map from $z'$ with its action to $z_H$ over $g$. Result 7(b), with $w = z'$, gives $y : S \to Y$ with $\jmath y = g$ and a map $c' \to u$ over $y$. Then $s y = s_H g = t$.

Uniqueness. Let $f : S \to Y$ with $s f = t$ and a map $c' \to u$ over $f$. Result 7(a) gives $w$ with an action, Zarhin related to $c'$, and a map from $w$ to $z_H$ over $\jmath f$. Result 6(b) gives a map from $w$ to $z'$ over the identity, and with the map from $z'$ to $z_H$ over $g$ we get a map from $w$ to $z_H$ over $g$ (result 8). The object $w$ is principal with the Rosati condition, so the uniqueness in the theorem on actions gives $\jmath f = g$. Then $f$ and $y$ both satisfy the conclusion of result 7(b) for $g$ and $c'$, so $f = y$. $\square$

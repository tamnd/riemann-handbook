This volume proves the results on abelian schemes, their moduli and their compactifications that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. It constructs the dual of an abelian scheme over any base and studies its cohomology and deformations. It proves the criteria for good reduction of Néron, Ogg and Shafarevich and of Serre and Tate. It builds the key invariant of theta characteristics, Mumford's construction of semi-abelian degenerations, the Siegel and PEL moduli schemes with their projective models, the integral models of Harris and Taylor, and toroidal compactifications of unitary and Siegel moduli schemes. Volume 1 gives the definitions of [abelian schemes](/learn/abelian-varieties/#abelian-schemes), [duality and the Poincaré bundle](/learn/abelian-varieties/#duality-and-the-poincar-bundle), [polarizations and Zarhin's trick](/learn/abelian-varieties/#polarizations-and-zarhins-trick), [Néron models and good reduction](/learn/abelian-varieties/#neron-models-and-good-reduction), [Siegel moduli schemes and level structures](/learn/abelian-varieties/#siegel-moduli-schemes-and-level-structures), [semi-abelian degenerations and Mumford's construction](/learn/abelian-varieties/#semi-abelian-degenerations-and-mumfords-construction) and [toroidal and minimal compactifications](/learn/abelian-varieties/#toroidal-and-minimal-compactifications), and states many of these results. This volume proves them.

The proof uses the volume in three places. The first is [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/). Its step [GeoOne holds](/step/geo-one-holds/) uses [the Kodaira-Spencer map of weight one](/entry/kodaira-spencer-of-weight-one/), which takes the integral models of Chapter 8, their tangent spaces, their isogenies and their de Rham homology. The second place is the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/). The dual abelian scheme of Chapter 1 is the statement on duals in [the CM and abelian variety inputs](/step/cm-abelian-inputs-from-literature/), and the theorem of Serre and Tate of Chapter 3 is used there too. The identity component of Chapter 1 is used in [EveryCharacter from Row0b](/step/every-character-of-row/). The third place is the branch [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/). It needs the metaplectic representation, Zarhin's trick and the key rule of Chapter 2, the last three results of Chapter 3, Mumford's construction of Chapter 4, Part I and the full level models of Chapter 5, the recognition theorem and the group models of Chapter 6, and the toroidal compactifications of Chapters 7 and 9.

Chapter 1 proves that every abelian scheme over any scheme has a dual abelian scheme with a Poincaré bundle, and biduality, [Theorem 1.6.4](/entry/dual-abelian-scheme/). The proof reduces to an affine base, then to a ring of finite type over $\mathbb{Z}$, and then by noetherian induction to a reduced ring. There the base is pinched from normal domains, which have a relatively ample line bundle, [Theorem 1.19.1](/entry/exists-rel-ample-of-affine/). The first section proves that the identity component of a smooth group scheme is an open subgroup scheme, [Theorem 1.1.2](/entry/identity-component-open/). The later sections prove that abelian schemes are unobstructed, [Theorem 1.11.4](/entry/abelian-unobstructed/), the lifting of homomorphisms along nilpotent divided powers, [Theorem 1.13.7](/entry/abelian-scheme-hom-lifting-nilpotent-divided-powers/), the Hodge and de Rham structure, [Theorem 1.15.1](/entry/ab-coh-abelian-hodge-de-rham/), ample roots, [Theorem 1.19.3](/entry/exists-ample-root/), and the algebraization of morphisms, [Theorem 1.20.2](/entry/formal-algebraisation-morphisms/). The last two sections study periods over $\mathbb{C}$, [Theorem 1.21.3](/entry/span-periods-eq-top-and-finrank/), and their variation in families, [Theorem 1.22.1](/entry/gauss-manin-periods-of-span-taylor/).

Chapter 2 first proves three results that later volumes use as they stand: the real metaplectic representation on the Schwartz space, [Theorem 2.1.4](/entry/real-metaplectic-representation/), Zarhin's trick over a valuation ring, [Corollary 2.2.7](/entry/zarhin-statement-of-dual-cube/), and the packing bound for small sections of a hermitian line bundle, [Theorem 2.3.6](/entry/packing-statement/). The rest of the chapter builds the key invariant. For a principally polarized abelian variety with a theta section, the Koszul complex of $g$ sections is exact, [Theorem 2.4.5](/entry/koszul-sections-exact-field/), and its torsion gives a unit, the key value. This value is holomorphic in the period matrix, [Theorem 2.5.3](/entry/ka-val-locally-holomorphic/), is a unit over a discrete valuation ring, [Theorem 2.6.1](/entry/is-unit-of-finite-zeros/), and does not depend on the sections, [Theorem 2.8.6](/entry/ka-val-independent/). The chapter ends with the key invariant, [Theorem 2.9.6](/entry/ka-st/), and the key rule for half characteristics, [Theorem 2.10.5](/entry/exists-key-rule-char-half/).

Chapter 3 proves the criterion of Néron, Ogg and Shafarevich over a discrete valuation ring with perfect residue field, [Theorem 3.1.8](/entry/nos-neron-ogg-shafarevich/), and the theorem of Serre and Tate that an abelian variety with complex multiplication has potential good reduction, [Theorem 3.1.11](/entry/serre-tate-good-reduction/). The hard implication passes to the inertia fixed field, [Theorem 3.3.5](/entry/has-good-reduction-of-inertia-fixed/), and descends the model along the unramified extension, [Theorem 3.2.9](/entry/exists-scheme-model-descent-unramified/), which gives [Theorem 3.4.7](/entry/has-good-reduction-of-infinite-torsion-unramified/). The last three sections prove that a self-dual Hermitian lattice has a semistable majorant of degree zero, [Theorem 3.5.14](/entry/semi-point/), that a formal biextension with an action of an order has a canonical gauge, [Theorem 3.6.8](/entry/canon-gauge-exists/), and a comparison of charts and frames along a map of semi-abelian schemes, [Theorem 3.7.7](/entry/jrel-of-sab-map/).

Chapter 4 proves Mumford's construction: over a complete normal noetherian local domain of characteristic $0$, degeneration data come from a semi-abelian scheme with a uniformisation, [Theorem 4.1.5](/entry/has-of-base/). The proof reduces to data of unitary PEL type, [Theorem 4.2.7](/entry/exists-pel-ok-factor/), builds a labelled cover over a modification of the base, [Theorem 4.3.3](/entry/has-fin-of-lab-cover/) and descends the group to the base, [Theorem 4.7.4](/entry/descend-of-emb/). The chapter also proves an extension criterion from complete regular charts, [Theorem 4.17.9](/entry/semi-abelian-descent-from-complete-regular-charts/), an ampleness result in the style of Raynaud, [Theorem 4.18.3](/entry/is-ample-lb-of-generic-of-symm/), and the algebraicity of formal endomorphisms, [Theorem 4.21.2](/entry/formal-end-algebraic/). For the converse, a semi-abelian scheme with a uniformisation comes from degeneration data, [Theorem 4.22.10](/entry/exists-deg-data-unif/). The last section proves the archimedean arc condition, [Theorem 4.23.2](/entry/arc-clause-closed/).

Chapter 5 builds the moduli spaces of PEL type for a CM field and their models over the integers. It bounds the tangent space of a PEL family by the Kodaira-Spencer map, [Theorem 5.2.7](/entry/finrank-derivation-le/), constructs theta families with smooth generic fibre, [Theorem 5.3.9](/entry/exists-theta-fam-is-pel-smooth/), and builds one projective model of full level, [Theorem 5.5.11](/entry/top-amp-exists/). From semistable reduction it deduces Part I: Siegel families of large level exist at every prime, and the PEL moduli spaces have systems of local models, [Corollary 5.7.9](/entry/part-i-of-semistable-reduction/). The second part proves the inputs: moduli schemes at every level, [Corollary 5.11.5](/entry/moduli-scheme-exists/), the key formula, [Theorem 5.12.4](/entry/key-formula-holds/), the extension statement from semistable reduction, [Theorem 5.13.2](/entry/zh-geo-of-semistable-reduction/), the sharp $E_8$ class, [Theorem 5.17.6](/entry/good-class-sharp-class/), and the eighth powers, [Theorem 5.18.1](/entry/exists-is-sharp-of-dense/).

Chapter 6 has three parts. The first builds arcs in the projective models of PEL moduli spaces. It builds series of theta values, [Theorem 6.1.13](/entry/exists-series/), carries the relations of complex theta null values to them by an identity principle, [Theorem 6.2.8](/entry/theta-series-identity-principle-complex-points/), and turns them into a moduli point, [Theorem 6.4.10](/entry/exists-point/). This gives the arc family, [Theorem 6.6.6](/entry/exists-arc-family-point-with-frames-of-laws/), the fourth clause of the models, [Theorem 6.7.11](/entry/arc-family-fourth-condition-invertible-pushforward/), Part I, [Theorem 6.7.12](/entry/model-systems-joint-properties-of-laws/), and the recognition theorem, [Theorem 6.8.11](/entry/degeneration-recognition-of-abelian-parts-iso-labels/). The second part extends an abelian scheme with a theta structure over a dense open set of a normal projective model to a semi-abelian scheme, [Theorem 6.12.13](/entry/zh-geo/). The third part proves Weil's theorem on birational group laws in the form of a neutral group model, [Theorem 6.17.18](/entry/exists-neutral-group-model/), potential semi-abelian models over $p$-adic fields, [Theorem 6.19.13](/entry/pot-model-semi-abelian/), the semistable reduction statement, [Theorem 6.19.17](/entry/semistable-reduction-st/), and the EGA inputs, [Theorem 6.20.19](/entry/ega-of-laws/).

Chapter 7 proves the existence of toroidal compactifications of unitary moduli schemes, in the form of Faltings, Chai and Lan, [Theorem 7.1.12](/entry/toroidal-for-fan-ks/). For a smooth polarized fan with no self-intersections, the model is smooth with a strict normal crossings boundary, a twist of a power of the Hodge bundle by the boundary is ample, and the logarithmic Kodaira-Spencer map is an isomorphism. The proof reduces to rigid charts, [Theorem 7.1.11](/entry/rigid-for-fan-holds/). It builds the compactification as a normalized blowup of a minimal compactification, [Theorem 7.9.3](/entry/unitary-integral-compactifications-exist/), along a boundary ideal, [Theorem 7.4.6](/entry/u-ideal-st-holds/), and proves that the blowup separates points, [Theorem 7.6.5](/entry/sep-b-of-norm-bl/). Then come smoothness and normal crossings, [Theorem 7.15.2](/entry/smooth-and-snc/), and the logarithmic Kodaira-Spencer isomorphism, [Theorem 7.16.3](/entry/has-log-ks/). The last section builds smooth polarized fans, [Theorem 7.26.5](/entry/exists-invariant-smooth-proj-fan-no-self-intersections/).

Chapter 8 proves that a moduli problem of Harris-Taylor type has a smooth quasi-projective fine moduli scheme over every noetherian ring in which the integers prime to $p$ are units, [Theorem 8.1.8](/entry/model-nonempty/). It first shows that Siegel families are fine moduli schemes, [Theorem 8.6.3](/entry/exists-unique-classify/), and that the auxiliary Siegel problem is representable, [Theorem 8.7.5](/entry/sd-rep-of-coprime/). The models are smooth of relative dimension $a(n-1)$, [Theorem 8.19.5](/entry/smooth-of-relative-dimension/). The chapter counts the Kodaira-Spencer directions, [Theorem 8.21.2](/entry/ks-dim-at-point/) and describes the complex points by marked periods, [Theorem 8.24.5](/entry/complex-points-classify-marked-periods/). It constructs the scheme of isogenies of type $T_j$, [Theorem 8.29.3](/entry/isog-rep-exists-rep/), and identifies primitive de Rham classes with rigidified extensions, [Theorem 8.30.3](/entry/de-rham-vector-extension/). The last section proves that the model is projective when the datum is compact, [Theorem 8.31.6](/entry/is-proj-over-of-compact-b/).

Chapter 9 proves the existence of an integral toroidal compactification of the Siegel moduli schemes over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_n, 1/n]$ for odd $n \ge 3$, in the form of Faltings and Chai, [Theorem 9.1.9](/entry/siegel-toroidal-integral-holds/). The compactification is a normalized blowup, [Theorem 9.18.3](/entry/poin-st/), of a minimal compactification, [Theorem 9.19.1](/entry/min-st5/). Its charts come from traits: a trait gives a lower base, [Theorem 9.4.5](/entry/exists-low-base-of-trait/), and degeneration data, [Theorem 9.5.6](/entry/exists-deg-from-of-trait/), and the blowup has charts at every point, [Theorem 9.7.6](/entry/chart-st5-holds/). On the way the chapter proves formal algebraization, [Theorem 9.2.6](/entry/global-theory-formal-algebraisation/) and roots of the Mumford bundle over étale local bases, [Theorem 9.3.6](/entry/exists-rig-lb-s-fam-etale/).

Chapters 1, 3 and 7 use no other chapter of this volume. Chapter 2 takes the dual abelian scheme of Theorem 1.6.4 as an input of Zarhin's trick, and Chapter 8 uses only the Hodge and de Rham structure of Theorem 1.15.1. Chapter 5 uses the key invariant and the key rule of Chapter 2. Chapters 5 and 6 form one block: Chapter 6 supplies the arc family, Part I, the extension theorem and the semistable reduction statement that Sections 7 and 13 of Chapter 5 use. Chapter 9 uses Theorems 1.20.2, 4.17.9 and 7.26.5, and Section 21 of Chapter 4 feeds its theorem on adjoint pairs. So a reader can take Chapter 1 first, then Chapters 2, 3, 4, 7 and 8 in any order, then Chapters 6 and 5, and Chapter 9 last.

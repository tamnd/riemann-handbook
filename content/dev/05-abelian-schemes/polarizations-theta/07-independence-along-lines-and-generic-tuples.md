## Independence along lines and generic tuples

This section proves the first two cases of the independence of the key value from the choice of sections. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, two tuples of sections that are joined by a line of tuples with finite zeros have the same key value. As a consequence, two generic tuples, whose coordinates in a basis are algebraically independent over a field of definition, have the same key value. The argument for lines is arithmetic: the key value along the line is a rational function of the parameter that is a unit at every point, by [the unit property](/entry/is-unit-koszul-invariant-of-finite-closed-fibre-zeros/), so it is constant. The result for generic tuples is used in [the independence of the sections](/entry/key-invariant-independent-of-sections/). The background is in [discrete valuation rings and lattices](/learn/commutative-algebra/#discrete-valuation-rings-and-lattices) and in [fields and extensions](/learn/algebra/#fields-and-extensions).

Throughout, $x$ is a key input over a ring $R$ with abelian scheme $A \to \operatorname{Spec} R$ and line bundle $L$, and $\Gamma_n = \Gamma(A, L^n)$.

**Definition 2.7.1 (Lines of sections).** Let $a, b \in \Gamma_n$ and $\tau \in R$. The section $(1 - \tau) a + \tau b$ of $L^n$ is the *point at* $\tau$ of the line from $a$ to $b$. For tuples $s_0, s_1 \in \Gamma_n^g$ the *line from* $s_0$ *to* $s_1$ is the family of tuples $s_\tau$ with $s_{\tau, i} = (1 - \tau) s_{0, i} + \tau s_{1, i}$. For a basis $b_1, \dots, b_N$ of $\Gamma_n$ and $c \in R^N$, the section $s$ *has coordinates* $c$ in $b$ if $s = \sum_k c_k b_k$.

A line from $s_0$ to $s_1$ over a field $K$ comes from a single tuple over the polynomial ring $K[X]$: take the line from $s_0$ to $s_1$ at $\tau = X$, after base change of $x$ to $K[X]$. Its specialization at $X = a$ is $s_a$. This is the tuple to which we apply the unit property, at the discrete valuation rings $K[X]_{(X - a)}$.

**Lemma 2.7.2 (Constant rational functions).** Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and let $c \in K(X)$ be nonzero. Assume that for every $a \in K$, every discrete valuation ring $O$ with a homomorphism $K[X] \to O$ whose composite with a residue map $O \to K$ is evaluation at $a$, every injective $h : O \to K(X)$ compatible with $K[X] \to K(X)$, every $u \in O$ and every nonzero integer $e$ with $c^e = h(u)$, the element $u$ is a unit. Then $c$ is a constant in $K$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.KeyLine.exists_eq_C`.

*Proof.* Write $c = p/q$ with $p, q \in K[X]$ coprime and $q$ monic. If $q$ has a root $a$, take $O = K[X]_{(X - a)}$ and $e = -1$: then $c^{-1} = q/p$ lies in $O$ and is not a unit, a contradiction. So $q = 1$. If $p$ has a root $a$, take $e = 1$: then $c = p$ lies in $O$ and is not a unit. So $p$ has no root, and since $K$ is algebraically closed, $p$ is a constant. $\square$

**Theorem 2.7.3 (Independence along a line).** Assume the laws. Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, let $x$ be a key input over $K$ of dimension $g$, and let $(n, l, l', \alpha)$ be admissible for $g$. Let $s_0, s_1 \in \Gamma_n^g$ and assume that for every $t \in K$ the tuple $s_t$ on the line from $s_0$ to $s_1$ has finite zeros. If $\kappa_0$ is a key value of $s_0$ and $\kappa_1$ is a key value of $s_1$, then $\kappa_0 = \kappa_1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.KeyLine.kaVal_eq_of_line`.

*Proof.* For a ring $R$ with a homomorphism $\psi : K[X] \to R$ and a unit $\kappa$ of $R$, say that $V(R, \psi, \kappa)$ holds if there are a pullback $y$ of $x$ along $\psi|_K$ and the tuple $s_\psi$ on the line from the pullbacks of $s_0$ and $s_1$ at $\tau = \psi(X)$ such that $\kappa$ is a key value of $s_\psi$. We use four properties of $V$.

1. *Base change.* If $V(R, \psi, \kappa)$ holds for a local noetherian ring $R$ and $g : R \to R'$ is a homomorphism to a local ring, then $V(R', g \circ \psi, g(\kappa))$ holds. This is the base change property of key values, applied to the pullback $y \to x$ of the line.
2. *Uniqueness.* If $L$ is a field and $V(L, \psi, \kappa)$ and $V(L, \psi, \kappa')$ hold, then $\kappa = \kappa'$. The field $L$ contains $K$, so it has characteristic $0$, and this is the uniqueness of key values over a field of characteristic $0$.
3. *Lifting.* Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring with $\psi : K[X] \to O$ and a residue map $r : O \to K$, and assume that $V(K, r \circ \psi, \kappa)$ holds. Then there are a discrete valuation ring $O'$, an injective local homomorphism $j : O \to O'$, a residue map $r' : O' \to K$ with $r' \circ j = r$, and a unit $u$ of $O'$ with $V(O', j \circ \psi, u)$. This is the existence of a key value after an extension of the discrete valuation ring, which applies since the transversal zeros of the special tuple lift. It needs that $\prod_j (l - jn)(l' - jn)$ is a unit in $O$, which holds because $O$ contains $K$.
4. *Units.* Let $a \in K$ and let $O$, $\psi$ and $r$ be as in the lemma, with $r \circ \psi$ evaluation at $a$. Let $h : O \to L$ be injective into a field, let $V(L, h \circ \psi, \kappa)$ hold, and let $\kappa^e = h(u)$ with $u \in O$ and $e \neq 0$. Then $u$ is a unit. Indeed, the tuple on the line over $O$ has closed fibre the tuple $s_a$ over $K$, whose zeros are finite by assumption, so the common zeros on the closed fibre are finite, and the unit property applies. The ring $O$ is an algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ through $K$.

Now let $a \in K$ with a key value $\kappa$ of $s_a$, so that $V(K, \mathrm{ev}_a, \kappa)$ holds. Let $O = K[X]_{(X - a)}$. By lifting, there are $O' \supseteq O$ and a unit $u$ of $O'$ with $V(O', \psi', u)$. Choose a field $E$ with an embedding of $O'$ that extends $K[X] \to K(X)$; by base change, $\mu = u$ in $E$ satisfies $V(E, K[X] \to E, \mu)$. Also, if $\mu$ equals a constant $k \in K$, then $\kappa = k$: reduce $u$ by $r'$, and use base change and uniqueness over $K$.

Do this for $a = 0$ and $a = 1$, with values $\mu_0 \in E_0$ and $\mu_1 \in E_1$. First, $\mu_0$ lies in $K(X)$. Indeed, for any two embeddings $g_1, g_2$ of $E_0$ into a field $L$ that agree on $K(X)$, base change gives $V(L, \psi, g_1(\mu_0))$ and $V(L, \psi, g_2(\mu_0))$ for the same $\psi$, and uniqueness gives $g_1(\mu_0) = g_2(\mu_0)$. In characteristic $0$, an element fixed in this way by all pairs of embeddings over $K(X)$ lies in $K(X)$. So $\mu_0 = c \in K(X)^\times$.

Next, $c$ satisfies the hypothesis of the lemma, by property 4 applied to $h$ composed with the embedding into $E_0$. So $c = k$ is a constant, and $\kappa_0 = k$. Finally, choose a field $L$ with embeddings $j_0 : E_0 \to L$ and $j_1 : E_1 \to L$ that agree on $K(X)$. Then $j_0(\mu_0)$ and $j_1(\mu_1)$ satisfy $V$ for the same map $K[X] \to L$, so they are equal by uniqueness. Hence $\mu_1 = k$ in $E_1$, and $\kappa_1 = k = \kappa_0$. $\square$

The hypothesis on the line is the hard one to check in practice. For generic tuples it follows from the next fact, which is the reason to pass through generic sections: if $s$ has finite zeros over an algebraically closed field $k$ and $s'_{i_0}$ has coordinates that are algebraically independent over $k$, then for every $t$ the tuple obtained from $s$ by replacing $s_{i_0}$ with $(1 - t) s_{i_0} + t s'_{i_0}$ has finite zeros. Also, a tuple of sections whose coordinates are algebraically independent over a field of definition has $d = g!\, n^g$ transversal zeros, and then a key value exists, since $\prod_j (l - jn)(l' - jn) \neq 0$ in a field of characteristic $0$.

**Theorem 2.7.4 (Independence for generic tuples).** Assume the laws. Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$, let $K$ be an algebraically closed field containing $k$, let $x$ be a key input over $k$ of dimension $g$, and let $x'$ be a pullback of $x$ to $K$. Let $(n, l, l', \alpha)$ be admissible for $g$. Let $b_1, \dots, b_{n^g}$ be a basis of $\Gamma(A, L^n)$ over $k$ and let $b'_j$ be the pullback of $b_j$. Let $T, T'$ be families in $K$ indexed by $\{1, \dots, g\} \times \{1, \dots, n^g\}$ such that the joint family $(T, T')$ is algebraically independent over $k$. Let $s, s' \in \Gamma(A', L'^n)^g$ have coordinates $T(i, \cdot)$ and $T'(i, \cdot)$ in $b'$. If $\kappa$ is a key value of $s$ and $\kappa'$ is a key value of $s'$, then $\kappa = \kappa'$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.KeyGeneric.kaVal_eq_of_generic` (also `GlobalTheory.T.keyInvariant_eq_of_independent_generic_tuples`, a second name).

*Proof.* The field $K$ has characteristic $0$, and $n \geq 4$ by admissibility. For a set $S$ of indices let $s^S$ be the tuple with $s^S_i = s'_i$ for $i \in S$ and $s^S_i = s_i$ otherwise. Its coordinates are a subfamily of $(T, T')$ with one entry for each pair $(i, j)$, so they are algebraically independent over $k$. We prove by induction on $S$ that every key value $\rho$ of $s^S$ equals $\kappa$. For $S = \{1, \dots, g\}$ this gives $\kappa' = \kappa$.

For $S = \emptyset$ the tuple $s^S$ is $s$. Apply the theorem on lines with $s_0 = s_1 = s$. Every tuple on this line is $s$, which has finite zeros since it is generic. So $\rho = \kappa$.

For the step, let $i_0 \notin S$ and let $\rho$ be a key value of $s^{S \cup \{i_0\}}$. The tuple $s^S$ is generic, so it has $d$ transversal zeros and a key value $\nu$, and $\nu = \kappa$ by induction. The tuples $s^S$ and $s^{S \cup \{i_0\}}$ differ only at $i_0$, so the line from $s^S$ to $s^{S \cup \{i_0\}}$ changes only the entry $i_0$, to $(1 - t) s_{i_0} + t s'_{i_0}$. To see that it has finite zeros, let $E$ be the algebraic closure in $K$ of the field generated over $k$ by the coordinates of $s^S$. The coordinates $T'(i_0, \cdot)$ are algebraically independent over $E$, and the tuple $s^S$ is defined over $E$, with finite zeros there, because it is generic over $k$. By the fact above, applied over $E$, every tuple on the line has finite zeros. The theorem on lines gives $\nu = \rho$. Hence $\rho = \kappa$. $\square$

**Remark 2.7.5.** The induction replaces one section at a time because the fact on finite zeros needs all but one entry to be fixed and defined over a smaller field. A direct line from $s$ to $s'$ has the entries $(1 - t) s_i + t s'_i$ for all $i$ at once, and its finiteness of zeros at every $t$ is not clear. Generic tuples are not enough for the key invariant, since the sections that occur in practice are special. The next section removes this restriction.

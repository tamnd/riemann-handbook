## The unit property over a discrete valuation ring

This section proves that the key value is a unit at a place of good reduction when the sections have only finitely many common zeros on the closed fibre. The precise form is a statement about a key input over a discrete valuation ring $O$ of characteristic $0$ and its pullback to a field $F$ that contains $O$: if a power of the key value of the pulled back sections lies in $O$, then it is a unit of $O$. This is the integrality half of the key rule, and it is used in [the independence along lines](/entry/ka-val-eq-of-line/), where the key value is moved along a line through a family of sections. The background is in [abelian schemes](/learn/abelian-varieties/#abelian-schemes) and in [discrete valuation rings](/learn/commutative-algebra/#regular-local-rings-and-discrete-valuation-rings).

Throughout, $O$ is a discrete valuation ring with an algebra structure over $\mathbb{Q}$, with closed point $\mathfrak{m}$, and $f : O \to F$ is an injective ring homomorphism to a field. Let $x$ be a key input over $O$ with abelian scheme $\pi : A \to \operatorname{Spec} O$ of relative dimension $g$ and line bundle $L$, and let $x_F$ be a key input over $F$ with a pullback $x_F \to x$ along $f$, in the sense of [the section on the Koszul complex](/entry/koszul-sections-exact-field/). For a section $s$ of $L^n$ the *nonzero locus* of $s$ is the open set of points of $A$ at which $s$ does not vanish. The *common zeros of* $s_1, \dots, s_g$ *on the closed fibre* are the points $y$ of $A$ with $\pi(y) = \mathfrak{m}$ that lie in no nonzero locus of an $s_i$.

**Theorem 2.6.1 (Unit property of the key value).** Assume the laws. Let $O$, $F$, $f$, $x$ and $x_F$ be as above, with the pullback $x_F \to x$ along $f$, and let $(n, l, l', \alpha)$ be admissible for $g$. Let $s_1, \dots, s_g$ be sections of $L^n$ over $O$, and let $s_{F,1}, \dots, s_{F,g}$ be sections over $F$ such that $s_{F,i}$ is the pullback of $s_i$ for each $i$. Assume that the common zeros of $s_1, \dots, s_g$ on the closed fibre form a finite set. Let $\kappa \in F^\times$ be a key value of $s_F$, let $a \in O$ and let $e$ be a nonzero integer with $\kappa^e = f(a)$. Then $a$ is a unit of $O$.

The proof has three steps. First we make the ring complete and large enough that the finitely many points which enter the key value of $s_F$ come from points over the ring. Second, over a complete ring with these points, we build a key value that is the image of a unit. Third, we compare the two key values by base change and by uniqueness over a field of characteristic $0$. We use the following two facts about key values, which are proved together with the definition of the key value. The *base change property*: if $\kappa$ is a key value of $s$ over a local noetherian ring $R$ and $x' \to x$ is a pullback along $R \to R'$ to a local ring with sections $s'$ pulled back from $s$, then the image of $\kappa$ is a key value of $s'$. The *uniqueness*: over a field of characteristic $0$, two key values of the same sections are equal.

**Lemma 2.6.2 (Key value over a complete ring).** Assume the laws. Let $O$ be a complete discrete valuation ring in which every positive integer is a unit, let $f : O \to F$ be injective, let $x$, $x_F$ and the sections $s$, $s_F$ be as in the theorem, and assume that the common zeros of $s$ on the closed fibre are finite. Let $z'_1, \dots, z'_d$ be transversal zeros of $s_F$, with $d = g!\, n^g$, such that every $z'_a$ is the pullback of an $O$-point $z_a$ of $A$. Assume that for $k \in \{l, l'\}$ and $0 \leq j \leq g$ there are distinct $O$-points $\xi_{I, r}$ of $A$, indexed as in the theta comparison at level $k - jn$, with $\prod_r \xi_{I, r}^{\alpha(k - jn)_{rc}} = 1$ for all $I$ and $c$. Then there is a unit $u \in O^\times$ such that $f(u)$ is a key value of $s_F$.

*Proof.* Each $z_a$ is a zero of all $s_i$ with Jacobian whose image in $F$ is nonzero, and the $z_a$ are distinct, since their pullbacks are. A point of $A$ outside the closed fibre at which all $s_i$ vanish lies over the generic point, so it comes from a common zero of $s_F$, and hence it lies on some $z_a$. So the zero scheme of $s$ is the union of the finitely many closed fibre zeros and the sections $z_a$. Fix $k \in \{l, l'\}$ with $gn < k$. The modules $\Gamma(A, L^{k - jn})$ are free over $O$, with bases $b_j$ whose pullbacks are bases over $F$. Since $k - jn \geq 1$ is a unit of $O$ and the points $\xi_{I, r}$ are of order dividing $k - jn$ and are distinct, the theta comparison constant of $b_j$ is the image of a unit $\lambda_j \in O^\times$.

The theorem on complete intersections over a complete discrete valuation ring now applies to the Koszul complex of $s$ on $b$. It gives a matrix $P$ over $O$ and an evaluation matrix $E_0$ with the following properties. The matrix of values $(b_{0,k}(z_a))$ is $P E_0$; the Koszul matrices with $E_0$ in place of the evaluation form an acyclic complex over $O$ with square torsion $t_0 \in O$; and $\det(P)^2$ is associated to the product $\prod_a \operatorname{jac}(s, z_a)$. The uniqueness of the square torsion shows that $t_0$ is a unit $t$ of $O$. Let $u_a$ be the unit by which the chart value of $z'_a$ differs from that of $z_a$. Then the square torsion of the augmented Koszul complex of $s_F$ at level $k$ is
$$f(t) \Bigl(\prod_a u_a\Bigr)^{-2k} \delta^2, \qquad \text{where } \delta = f(\det P),$$
and the Jacobian product is $J = f(e_k)\,\delta^2$ with $e_k \in O^\times$. Do this for $k = l$ and $k = l'$, with constants $t, e$ and $t_2, e_2$. Since the two Jacobian products are equal, $\delta_2^2 = f(e_2^{-1} e)\, \delta^2$.

Put these values into the key formula. The formula is homogeneous: if the two torsions are multiplied by $c^{-2l} \delta^2$ and $c^{-2l'} \delta^2$ and the Jacobian by $c^{-gn}\delta^2$, the key value does not change. This is a direct check on the exponents $gn - 2l'$, $2l - gn$ and $8P^{3g+2}(l' - l)$ of the formula. So the key value of $s_F$ is the image of
$$\kappa_O(t, t_2 e_2^{-1} e, \lambda, \lambda_2, e) \in O^\times,$$
the value of the key formula on units of $O$. $\square$

*Proof of the theorem.* Let $\kappa$ be a key value of $s_F$, with transversal zeros $z'_a$ and with distinct points $\xi'_{I, r}$ in the theta comparisons at the levels $l - jn$ and $l' - jn$. These are finitely many $F$-points of $A_F$, and so finitely many $F$-points of $A$ over $\operatorname{Spec} F \to \operatorname{Spec} O$. Since $A$ is proper over $O$, there are a complete discrete valuation ring $O'$, a local homomorphism $j : O \to O'$, a field $\Omega$, a homomorphism $i : F \to \Omega$ and an injective homomorphism $h : O' \to \Omega$ with $h \circ j = i \circ f$, such that each of these $F$-points, pushed to $\Omega$, comes from an $O'$-point of $A$. To see this, let $L \subseteq F$ be the subfield generated over the fraction field of $O$ by finitely many elements over which the points are defined. Since $L$ is finitely generated, there is a discrete valuation ring $V$ of $L$ that dominates $O$. By the valuative criterion of properness, each point over $L$ extends to a $V$-point of $A$. Let $O'$ be the completion of $V$, and let $\Omega$ be a field that receives both $F$ and the fraction field of $O'$ compatibly on $L$; such a field is a quotient of $F \otimes_L \operatorname{Frac}(O')$ by a maximal ideal.

Let $x'$ be the base change of $x$ to $O'$, with sections $s'$ pulled back from $s$, and let $y$ be the base change of $x_F$ to $\Omega$, with sections $s_\Omega$ pulled back from $s_F$. The two pullbacks to $\Omega$ agree, so $y$ is also a pullback of $x'$ along $h$, and $s_\Omega$ is the pullback of $s'$. The points above give $O'$-points $z_a$ and $\xi_{I, r}$ of the abelian scheme of $x'$ whose pullbacks are the images of $z'_a$ and $\xi'_{I, r}$ in $y$, and the images of the transversal zeros are transversal zeros of $s_\Omega$. The common zeros of $s'$ on the closed fibre are finite: they lie over the finitely many common zeros of $s$ on the closed fibre, and the closed fibre of $x'$ is the base change of the closed fibre of $x$ to the residue field of $O'$, so each of these has finitely many points above it. Every positive integer is a unit in $O'$, since $O'$ is an algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$.

By the lemma there is $u \in O'^{\times}$ such that $h(u)$ is a key value of $s_\Omega$. By the base change property, $i(\kappa)$ is also a key value of $s_\Omega$. The field $\Omega$ has characteristic $0$, so the uniqueness gives $i(\kappa) = h(u)$. Then
$$h(j(a)) = i(f(a)) = i(\kappa)^e = h(u)^e = h(u^e),$$
and $j(a) = u^e$ since $h$ is injective. So $j(a)$ is a unit of $O'$. Since $j$ is a local homomorphism, $a$ is a unit of $O$. $\square$

**Remark 2.6.3.** The finiteness of the closed fibre zeros is what makes the zero scheme of $s$ finite and flat over $O$: the sections $s_1, \dots, s_g$ then form a regular sequence in the local rings of $A$ along the closed fibre, so the Koszul complex is exact over $O$ and not only over $F$. Without this hypothesis the Koszul complex over $O$ need not be exact, and the argument above gives nothing.

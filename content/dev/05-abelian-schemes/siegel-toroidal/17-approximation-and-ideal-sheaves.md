## Approximation and ideal sheaves

This section builds, on the rational model $Z_1$ of a theta setup, an ideal sheaf whose orders along traits are a fixed multiple of $\varphi(\beta)$. First, the Fourier condition of the previous section gives functions on $Z_1$ whose $p$-adic size along one classical trait is exactly $e^{-\mathrm{sc}\,d\varphi(\beta)}$ and along the others at most this size, up to an arbitrarily small error. Second, theta values give a normal model $Y$ proper over $Z_1$ with an effective Cartier divisor $D$ of the right orders. Third, the approximating functions show that a power of $D$ comes from an ideal sheaf on $Z_1$. The next section moves this ideal sheaf to $Z$.

We keep the notation of the previous sections. $X_1$ is a theta setup for $(F, W, i)$, with theta system $S$ of ratio $r$ and level $N_S$ over the arithmetic base $(o_1, \Omega_1)$, rational model $Z_1$ and finite map $\pi_1 : Z_1 \to Z$, as in the section on [uniformized members](/entry/exists-u-mem/). Classical traits $c$ at $z_1 \in Z_1$ for a prime $p$ have scale $\mathrm{sc}_c > 0$, value map $\mathrm{val}_c$ and form $\beta_c$, as in the section on [traits and classical points](/entry/trait-uniq/). The valuation condition for ideal sheaves along traits is the one of the section on [charts on the normalized blowup](/entry/chart-st5-holds/).

**Definition 9.17.1 (Approximation at classical points).** Let $\varphi$ be an invariant function and $d \ge 0$. The setup $X_1$ has *approximation of order $d$* for $\varphi$ if for each prime $p$, each $z_1 \in Z_1$, each classical trait $c_0$ at $z_1$ for $p$ and each $e \ge 0$ there is $f \in \mathcal{O}_{Z_1, z_1}$ with
$$|\mathrm{val}_{c_0}(f)| = \exp\bigl(-\mathrm{sc}_{c_0}\, d\,\varphi(\beta_{c_0})\bigr)$$
such that for each classical trait $c$ at $z_1$ for $p$, either $|\mathrm{val}_c(f)| \le \exp(-\mathrm{sc}_c\, d\,\varphi(\beta_c))$, or there is $h \in \mathfrak{m}_{z_1}$ with $|\mathrm{val}_c(f)| \le |\mathrm{val}_c(h)|^e$. For a $p$-adic Fourier pre-system $\Phi$ over a local ring $A$, *approximation of order $d$* is the same condition with $\mathrm{ev}_y$, $\mathrm{sc}_y$ and $\beta_y$ for $y \in \Phi_Y$ in place of $\mathrm{val}_c$, $\mathrm{sc}_c$ and $\beta_c$, and $f \in A$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Poinc.ApproxAt` (also `GlobalTheory.Poinc.FPre.Approx`).

For $d = 0$ the condition holds with $f = 1$. The function $f$ is a $p$-adic stand-in for a local equation of an ideal of order $d\varphi(\beta)$ along traits: its size along $c_0$ is right, and along any other trait it is no larger, except where it is as small as a large power of an element of the maximal ideal.

The proof uses supports of $\varphi$. Let $\varphi$ be an invariant function, $q \ge 0$ and $d \ge 1$. For a lattice $X$ and a cone $\gamma = (\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_s)$ of $\varphi$ on $X$, a *support* of $\gamma$ at level $d$ is a family $a_1, \dots, a_m$ that spans $X$ such that $d\,\varphi(\beta) \le \sum_i \beta(a_i, a_i)$ for each positive semidefinite integral form $\beta$, with equality for each $\gamma_j$, and such that equality holds only if some positive multiple of $\beta$ is a combination of the $\gamma_j$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{N}$. Then $\varphi$ *has supports* at level $d$ in rank $q$ if every cone on every lattice of rank at most $q$ has a support at level $d$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Poinc.HasSupports` (also `GlobalTheory.Poinc.Supports`).

So $\beta \mapsto \sum_i \beta(a_i, a_i)$ is a linear function that lies above $d\varphi$ and touches it exactly on the cone $\gamma$.

**Theorem 9.17.2 (Approximation at classical points).** Let $W$ be an $E_8$ matrix with theta integrality for $F$ and $i$, let $X_1$ be a theta setup for $(F, W, i)$, assume $g \ge 1$, and let $\varphi$ be a polarization function of rank $g$. Then there is $d \ge 1$ such that $X_1$ has approximation of order $d$ for $\varphi$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Poinc.exists_approxAt` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_approxPoincareSeries_at_thetaSetup_points`, a wrapper).

*Proof.* A theorem on supports of polarization functions gives $d_0 \ge 1$ such that $\varphi$ has supports at level $d_0$ in rank $g$; we use only its statement. Since $W + W^T$ is positive definite, its entry $2W_{00}$ is positive, so $W_{00} \ge 1$. The size condition on the theta system gives $rN_S \ge 3$. Put $d = W_{00}\, d_0 \ge 1$.

Fix $p$, $z_1$, $c_0$ and $e$. The point $z_1$ is the image of the closed point of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p}$ under $y_{c_0}$, and a lemma on such images shows that it is closed. A theorem on classical trait systems at closed points gives a $p$-adic Fourier pre-system $\Phi$ of rank at most $g$ over $A = \mathcal{O}_{Z_1, z_1}$ that is classical, matches the classical traits at $z_1$ for $p$, and satisfies the abelian bound; we use only its statement. By the theorem on the [Fourier condition from classical traits](/entry/poinc-four-of-classical/), $\Phi$ satisfies the Fourier condition at level $rN_S$. The ring $o_1$ is a Dedekind domain and $Z_1$ is locally of finite type over it, so $Z_1$ is locally noetherian. A theorem on approximation from Fourier systems now applies, with the supports at level $d_0$, the positive definite matrix $W$ and $rN_S \ge 3$; it gives approximation of order $d$ for $\Phi$. We use only its statement.

It remains to pass from $\Phi$ to the classical traits. Let $y \in \Phi_Y$ and let $c$ be a classical trait with $\mathrm{ev}_y = \mathrm{val}_c$ and an isomorphism $e' : X_y \cong Y_c$ with $\mathrm{sc}_y\,\beta_y(a, b) = \mathrm{sc}_c\,\beta_c(e' a, e' b)$. We claim
$$\mathrm{sc}_y\, \varphi(\beta_y) = \mathrm{sc}_c\, \varphi(\beta_c).$$
Indeed, the pullback of $\beta_c$ along $e'$ equals $(\mathrm{sc}_y/\mathrm{sc}_c)\,\beta_y$ as a real form, where $\mathrm{sc}_c > 0$ because $|\iota_c(\varpi)| < 1$. The function $\varphi$ is invariant under isomorphisms of lattices, and it is homogeneous of degree $1$ on positive semidefinite real forms on lattices of rank at most $g$. The form $\beta_y$ is positive definite and $X_y$ has rank at most $g$, so $\varphi(\beta_c) = (\mathrm{sc}_y/\mathrm{sc}_c)\,\varphi(\beta_y)$, which is the claim. Multiplying by $d$ gives $\mathrm{sc}_y\, d\varphi(\beta_y) = \mathrm{sc}_c\, d\varphi(\beta_c)$.

Since $\Phi$ matches the classical traits, $c_0$ comes from some $y_0 \in \Phi_Y$. Approximation for $\Phi$ at $y_0$ and $e$ gives $f \in A$ with $|\mathrm{ev}_{y_0}(f)| = \exp(-\mathrm{sc}_{y_0}\, d\varphi(\beta_{y_0}))$, which by the claim is $|\mathrm{val}_{c_0}(f)| = \exp(-\mathrm{sc}_{c_0}\, d\varphi(\beta_{c_0}))$. For any classical trait $c$, choose $y$ that matches $c$. The alternative for $y$ becomes the alternative for $c$, by the claim in the first case and by $\mathrm{ev}_y = \mathrm{val}_c$ in the second. $\square$

The order $d$ does not depend on $p$, $z_1$ or $c_0$. If $X_1$ has approximation of order $d$, it has approximation of order $md$ for each $m \ge 1$, by a theorem on multiples that we use only through its statement.

**Theorem 9.17.3 (A normal model with a Cartier divisor).** Let $g \ge 1$ and $n \ge 3$ odd. Let $(Z, z, i)$ be a theta compactification of $F$, let $\varphi$ be a polarization function of rank $g$, let $W$ be an $E_8$ matrix with theta integrality for $F$ and $i$, and let $X_1$ be a theta setup for $(F, W, i)$. Then there are $\iota \ge 1$, a rational model $Y$ of $\Omega_1$ over $o_1$, a map $\pi_Y : Y \to Z_1$ and an effective Cartier divisor $D$ on $Y$ such that:

1. $\pi_Y \circ \mathrm{pt}_Y = \mathrm{pt}_{Z_1}$, and $\pi_Y$ is proper;
2. $Y$ is normal;
3. for each $y$ in the support of $D$, $\pi_1(\pi_Y(y)) \notin i(X)$;
4. for each trait $\mathcal{T}$ for $F$, with ring $V$, uniformizer $\varpi$, generic point $y$ and form $\beta$, and each $\xi : \operatorname{Spec} V \to Y$ with $\pi_1 \circ \pi_Y \circ \xi \circ \iota_V = i \circ y$, there is an integer $a \ge 0$ with $a = \iota\,\varphi(\beta)$ such that the ideal sheaf of $D$ generates the ideal $(\varpi^a)$ of $V$ along $\xi$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Poinc.exists_model_divisor_of_orders` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_normal_model_cartierDivisor_thetaSetup`, a wrapper).

*Proof.* A theorem on local tuples of theta values gives $\iota \ge 1$ and finitely many finite families $t_k = (t_{k,j})_j$ of nonzero elements of $\Omega_1$ with the following property. On each rational model $M$ of $\Omega_1$ that is admitted by $S$, for each point $x$ of $M$ there is $k$ such that for each trait $\mathcal{T}$ through the admitted open set whose closed point specializes to $x$, with $a = \iota\varphi(\beta)$, every $t_{k,j}$ has order at least $a$ along $\mathcal{T}$ and some $t_{k,j}$ has order exactly $a$. A theorem on admissible projective theta models gives a model $Y$ admitted by $S$, normal, with a proper map $\pi_Y : Y \to Z_1$ that satisfies (1), on which every family $t_k$ has divisors. We use only the statements of these two theorems.

Let $V_0 = (\pi_1 \circ \pi_Y)^{-1}(i(X))$. It is open since $i$ is an open immersion, and it contains the image of $\mathrm{pt}_Y$, since $\pi_1 \circ \pi_Y \circ \mathrm{pt}_Y = \pi_1 \circ \mathrm{pt}_{Z_1} = i \circ u \circ \operatorname{Spec}(S_R \subseteq \Omega_1)$. So $V_0$ is nonempty, and the map $\pi_1 \circ \pi_Y$ on $V_0$ lifts to $v : V_0 \to X$ with $i \circ v = \pi_1 \circ \pi_Y$.

The divisor is glued from the tuples. A gluing lemma applies a theorem on effective Cartier divisors with order $\iota\varphi(\beta)$ along each trait whose generic point is $\mathrm{pt}_Y$ followed by a field map; the tuples $t_k$ supply the local equations. It checks the hypotheses with two facts on $Y$. First, since $Y$ is normal, an element $s \in \Omega_1$ lies in $\mathcal{O}_{Y, x}$ as soon as its image lies in $V$ for every trait $\mathcal{T}$ with $\xi$ centred at $x$ and generic point through $\mathrm{pt}_Y$. Second, every point of $Y$ is the centre of such a trait. The result is $D$ with support off $V_0$, which is (3), and with the orders of (4) for traits whose generic point passes through $\mathrm{pt}_Y$. We use only the statement of the theorem on effective Cartier divisors. A theorem on extension of trait orders from the generic point gives (4) for all traits with $\pi_1 \circ \pi_Y \circ \xi \circ \iota_V = i \circ y$; such a $\xi$ sends the generic point of $\operatorname{Spec} V$ into $V_0$. We use only the statement of that theorem. $\square$

The theta values have divisors only after the modification $\pi_Y$. The next theorem removes it at the cost of a power of $D$.

**Theorem 9.17.4 (Descent of a divisor power).** Let $g, n, (Z, z, i), \varphi, W, X_1$ be as in the previous theorem, and assume that $Z_1$ is projective over $o_1$. Let $\iota \ge 1$, $Y$, $\pi_Y$ and $D$ satisfy (1) to (4) of the previous theorem. Then there are $\iota' \ge 1$ and an ideal sheaf $J_1$ on $Z_1$ such that:

1. for each $y$ in the support of $\pi_Y^{-1} J_1 \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y$, $\pi_1(\pi_Y(y)) \notin i(X)$;
2. for each trait $\mathcal{T}$ for $F$ and each $\xi : \operatorname{Spec} V \to Y$ with $\pi_1 \circ \pi_Y \circ \xi \circ \iota_V = i \circ y$, there is an integer $a \ge 0$ with $a = \iota'\varphi(\beta)$ such that $\pi_Y^{-1} J_1 \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y$ generates $(\varpi^a)$ along $\xi$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Poinc.DescentPow.exists_idealSheaf_comap_orders` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_idealSheaf_descending_divisor_power_thetaSetup`, a wrapper).

*Proof.* By the [approximation theorem](/entry/exists-approx-at/) there is $d \ge 1$ such that $X_1$ has approximation of order $d$. Let $L = \operatorname{lcm}(\iota, d)$, with $L = k\iota = md$ and $k, m \ge 1$. By the theorem on multiples, $X_1$ has approximation of order $L$. The divisor $kD$ has ideal sheaf $I_D^k$, so along $\xi$ it generates $(\varpi^{ka})$ with $ka = k\iota\varphi(\beta) = L\varphi(\beta)$. Its support is contained in the support of $D$, since a local equation $f$ of $D$ is invertible exactly where $f^k$ is.

Let $U = (\pi_1 \circ \pi_Y)^{-1}(i(X))$. As in the previous proof, $U$ is a nonempty open set, and by (3) the support of $kD$ does not meet $U$. We apply a theorem on ideal sheaves from approximations; we use only its statement. For two normal rational models with a proper map $\pi_Y$ that satisfies (1), a nonempty open $U \subseteq Y$, and an effective Cartier divisor $kD$ with support off $U$, it gives an ideal sheaf $J_1$ on $Z_1$ with $\pi_Y^{-1} J_1 \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y = I_{kD}$. Its hypothesis is an approximation property at closed points: for each closed $z_0 \in Z_1$ and each closed $x \in Y$ over $z_0$, there must be a prime $p$ and a point $y_0 : \operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p} \to Y$, centred at $x$ with generic point in $U$, such that for each $e$ there is $f \in \mathcal{O}_{Z_1, z_0}$ with two properties. First, at $y_0$ the value of $f$ has the absolute value of a local equation of $kD$ at $x$. Second, at every $\mathcal{O}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_q}$-point $y$ of $Y$ centred over $z_0$ with generic point in $U$, the value of $f$ is at most that of a local equation of $kD$ at the centre of $y$, or at most the $e$-th power of the value of a nonunit of $\mathcal{O}_{Z_1, z_0}$.

We check this hypothesis. A theorem on classical points centred at closed points gives $p$ and $y_0$, centred at $x$ with generic point in $U$. Composing $y_0$ with $\pi_Y$ and using (4) for $kD$, a theorem on classical traits through classical points gives a classical trait $c_0$ at $z_0$ for $p$ whose value map is the value at $y_0$, and for which the absolute value at $y_0$ of a local equation of $kD$ is $\exp(-\mathrm{sc}_{c_0}\, L\varphi(\beta_{c_0}))$. Approximation of order $L$ for $c_0$ and $e$ gives $f$, and the first property holds. Let $y$ be an $\mathcal{O}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_q}$-point centred over $z_0$ with generic point in $U$. The two points $\pi_Y \circ y_0$ and $\pi_Y \circ y$ have the same closed point $z_0$, so $q = p$, by a theorem on primes of classical points. The same construction gives a classical trait $c$ for $y$. If the first alternative of approximation holds for $c$, its bound is the second property with a local equation. In the other case there is $h \in \mathfrak{m}_{z_0}$; it is a nonunit, and $|\mathrm{val}_c(f)| \le |\mathrm{val}_c(h)|^e$ is the second property. We use only the statements of the three theorems on classical points.

So $J_1$ exists with $\pi_Y^{-1} J_1 \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y = I_{kD}$. With $\iota' = L$, (1) follows from the support of $kD$ and (2) from its orders. $\square$

**Remark 9.17.5 (Formalization).** The approximation condition at $d = 0$ is proved to hold with $f = 1$, and a theta setup with $W = 0$ is proved to be empty, in two examples after the definition ([ThetaSetupStructureMapComp.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/ThetaSetupStructureMapComp.lean#L209)). The structure `Setup` already contains $g \ge 1$, $n \ge 3$, $n$ odd, the $E_8$ condition and theta integrality as fields (lines 42 to 60), so these hypotheses of the three theorems are redundant. In `exists_approxAt` the hypothesis $g \ge 1$ is introduced and not used ([ExistsApproxPoincareSeriesAtThetaSetupPoints.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsApproxPoincareSeriesAtThetaSetupPoints.lean#L89)); the claim $\mathrm{sc}_y\varphi(\beta_y) = \mathrm{sc}_c\varphi(\beta_c)$ is `sc_evalZ_eq` (line 59). In `exists_model_divisor_of_orders` the hypotheses that $(Z, z, i)$ is a theta compactification and theta integrality are not used ([ExistsNormalModelCartierDivisorThetaSetup.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsNormalModelCartierDivisorThetaSetup.lean#L209)); the gluing is `OrdGlue.glue` (line 117). In `exists_idealSheaf_comap_orders` the projectivity of $Z_1$ and the hypotheses on $n$ are not used, and the theta compactification is used only to know that $i$ is an open immersion ([ExistsIdealSheafDescendingDivisorPowerThetaSetup.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsIdealSheafDescendingDivisorPowerThetaSetup.lean#L71)). The statement file of the model theorem imports `Mathlib` twice ([ExistsNormalModelCartierDivisorThetaSetupStatement.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsNormalModelCartierDivisorThetaSetupStatement.lean#L1)).

## Traits and classical points

The rest of the chapter builds the ideal sheaf whose normalized blowup is the toroidal compactification. Its orders along [traits](/entry/exists-deg-from-of-trait/) are prescribed by a polarization function $\varphi$, so the first question is whether the order is well defined: two traits that meet at the same point of $X$ after an extension of discrete valuation rings must give the same value of $\varphi$, up to the ramification. This section proves that their period forms are proportional, with the ramification indices as the factor. It also fixes three notions used in the next sections: theta compactifications, rational models, and classical traits, which are traits seen through a $p$-adic point of a rational model.

We keep the notation of the previous sections. $F$ is a Siegel family of genus $g$ and level $n$ with base $s : X \to \operatorname{Spec}\Lambda_n$ and universal datum $(A, P, \ell)$. A trait $\mathcal{T}$ has ring $V$, uniformizer $\varpi$, generic point $y : \operatorname{Spec} K_V \to X$, degeneration data $D$ with lattice $Y$, and form $\beta$, as in the section on [degeneration data along a trait](/entry/exists-deg-from-of-trait/).

**Definition 9.15.1 (Rational model).** Let $o$ be a ring and $\Omega$ a field that is an $o$-algebra. A *rational model* $M$ of $\Omega$ over $o$ is an integral scheme $Z_M$ with a separated, quasi-compact map $s_M : Z_M \to \operatorname{Spec} o$ that is locally of finite type, and a point $\mathrm{pt} : \operatorname{Spec}\Omega \to Z_M$ over $o$ with image the generic point of $Z_M$, such that each $x \in \Omega$ is the value at $\mathrm{pt}$ of some section of $\mathcal{O}_{Z_M}$ over a nonempty open set. So $\mathrm{pt}$ identifies $\Omega$ with the function field of $Z_M$, and we view each local ring $\mathcal{O}_{Z_M, x}$ as a subring of $\Omega$. A family $(t_k)_{k \in J}$ of elements of $\Omega$ *has divisors* on $M$ if for each $x \in Z_M$ there is $k$ with $t_k \ne 0$ and $t_{k'}/t_k \in \mathcal{O}_{Z_M, x}$ for all $k'$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.ZH.RatModel` (also `GlobalTheory.ZH.RatModel.HasDiv`).

A family has divisors when the rational map $Z_M \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^{J}$ given by $(t_k)$ is defined everywhere: near each point one coordinate divides all others.

A *theta system* $S$ over $(o, \Omega)$ has a genus, a level $N \ge 2$ and a ratio $r \ge 1$, a finitely generated $o$-subalgebra $S_R$ of $\Omega$ with fraction field $\Omega$, and theta values $\mathrm{th}(x) \in S_R$ for $x \in (\mathbb{Z}/rN)^{2g}$, not all $0$; a *realization* of $S$ gives a polarized abelian scheme over $S_R$. These are the objects of the section on [uniformized members](/entry/exists-u-mem/); a pair $(o, \Omega)$ over which they live is an *arithmetic base*. A polarized abelian scheme $(P_X, L)$ over $T$ is *sharp over* $u : T \to X$ for $W$ if $P_X$ is the eighth fibre power of $u^* A$, with projections $\mathrm{pr}_1, \dots, \mathrm{pr}_8$, and $L$ is isomorphic to $\bigotimes_{a, b} (\mathrm{pr}_a, \mathrm{pr}_b)^* P^{\otimes W_{ab}}$.

**Definition 9.15.2 (Theta compactification).** Let $z : Z \to \operatorname{Spec}\Lambda_n$ and $i : X \to Z$. Then $(Z, z, i)$ is a *theta compactification* of $F$ if:

1. $z$ is projective, $Z$ is normal, $i$ is an open immersion with dense image, and $z \circ i = s$;
2. there is an $8 \times 8$ integer matrix $W$ with $W + W^{T}$ positive definite of determinant $1$ that satisfies *theta integrality*: for each arithmetic base $(o', \Omega)$, each theta system $S$ over it with a realization $P_X$, and each $u : \operatorname{Spec} S_R \to X$ such that $P_X$ is sharp over $u$ for $W$ and $u$ sends the generic points of $\operatorname{Spec} S_R$ to generic points of $X$, and for each rational model $M$ of $\Omega$ over $o'$ with $Z_M$ normal and each $\pi : Z_M \to Z$ with $\pi \circ \mathrm{pt} = i \circ u \circ \operatorname{Spec}(S_R \subseteq \Omega)$, the theta values $\mathrm{th}$ of $S$ have divisors on $M$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Poinc.IsThetaCompact` (also `GlobalTheory.Poinc.ThetaInt`, `GlobalTheory.Poinc.SharpOver`).

The matrix $W$ is an $E_8$ matrix: $W + W^T$ has even diagonal entries $2W_{aa}$, so it is the Gram matrix of an even unimodular positive definite lattice of rank $8$, which is the $E_8$ lattice (J.-P. Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Chapter V). Theta integrality says that the theta constants of the twisted eighth power of $A$ extend to a morphism to projective space on every normal model over $Z$. The minimal compactification constructed in the last section is a theta compactification.

Let $X_1$ be a theta setup for $(F, W, i)$, with rational model $Z_1$ and finite map $\pi_1 : Z_1 \to Z$, as in the section on uniformized members. Let $p$ be a prime, $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with its absolute value, and $\mathcal{O}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p}$ its ring of integers ([$p$-adic numbers](/learn/number-fields/#p-adic-numbers-and-local-fields)).

**Definition 9.15.3 (Classical trait).** Let $z_1 \in Z_1$. A *classical trait* $c$ of $X_1$ at $z_1$ for $p$ consists of a point $y_c : \operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p} \to Z_1$ that sends the closed point to $z_1$, a trait $\mathcal{T}_c$ for $F$ with ring $V_c$, and a field map $\iota_c : K_{V_c} \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ with $|\iota_c(x)| \le 1$ if and only if $x \in V_c$, such that the generic point of $y_c$, followed by $\pi_1$, equals $i \circ y \circ \operatorname{Spec}\iota_c$. Its *scale* is $\mathrm{sc}_c = -\log|\iota_c(\varpi)|$, and for $a \in \mathcal{O}_{Z_1, z_1}$ its *value* $\mathrm{val}_c(a) \in \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ is the value of $a$ at the generic point of $y_c$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Poinc.CTrait` (also `GlobalTheory.Poinc.CTrait.sc`, `GlobalTheory.Poinc.CTrait.val`).

Since $\varpi$ is not a unit of $V_c$, $|\iota_c(\varpi)| < 1$ and so $\mathrm{sc}_c > 0$. Since $y_c$ is centred at $z_1$, $|\mathrm{val}_c(a)| \le 1$ for each $a$, and $|\mathrm{val}_c(a)| < 1$ if and only if $a \in \mathfrak{m}_{z_1}$. A classical trait is a trait together with a way to measure it $p$-adically at $z_1$: if $\beta_c(\chi, \chi) = 2k$, the term $\psi(\chi) \sim \varpi^k$ of the trait has absolute value $e^{-\mathrm{sc}_c\,\beta_c(\chi, \chi)/2}$ under $\iota_c$.

The next theorem compares two traits with the same generic point after base change. We say that a domain $R$ is an admissible base of characteristic $0$ as in the section on [the image of an idempotent](/entry/image-unif/); in particular it is a CN domain.

**Theorem 9.15.4 (Proportional forms of traits).** Let $F$ be a Siegel family over $(o, \zeta)$, of any genus and level, and let $\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{T}'$ be traits for $F$ with rings $V, V'$, uniformizers $\varpi, \varpi'$, generic points $y, y'$, lattices $Y, Y'$ and forms $\beta, \beta'$. Let $R$ be a local domain that is a discrete valuation ring and an admissible base of characteristic $0$. Let $f : V \to R$ and $f' : V' \to R$ be injective local maps, and $f_K : K_V \to K_R$, $f'_K : K_{V'} \to K_R$ the maps that extend them. Assume that $y \circ \operatorname{Spec} f_K = y' \circ \operatorname{Spec} f'_K$. Let $w \in R$ be irreducible and $m, m' \ge 0$ with $f(\varpi)$ associate to $w^{m}$ and $f'(\varpi')$ associate to $w^{m'}$. Then there is an isomorphism $e : Y \cong Y'$ with
$$m\,\beta(a, b) = m'\,\beta'(e(a), e(b)) \quad \text{for all } a, b \in Y.$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Poinc.TraitUniq.traitUniq` (also `GlobalTheory.Poinc.TraitUniqSt`, the statement).

*Proof.* Base change along $f$ and $f'$ turns the semi-abelian groups $G, G'$ of the traits into semi-abelian groups $G_R, G'_R$ over $R$. The generic fibre of $G_R$ is the base change of $A_K$ along $f_K$, which is the group pullback of $A$ along $y \circ \operatorname{Spec} f_K$; the same holds for $G'_R$ with $y' \circ \operatorname{Spec} f'_K$. As the two points agree, the generic fibres are isomorphic as group pullbacks of $A$. An isomorphism of the generic fibres of two semi-abelian schemes over a normal base extends uniquely to an isomorphism $E : G_R \cong G'_R$ ([Néron models](/learn/abelian-varieties/#neron-models-and-good-reduction)). Base change of the degeneration data along $f$ and $f'$ also gives degeneration data over $R$ that uniformize $G_R$ and $G'_R$, with lattices identified with $Y$ and $Y'$ and with $\psi$-coordinates equal to the images of those of $\mathcal{T}$, $\mathcal{T}'$ up to units. The bundles match: $\Lambda$ of the bundle of one is isomorphic to $\Lambda$ of the other, as both are pullbacks of $P$. A theorem on squares of uniformization coordinates, applied to two uniformizations of the same polarized group, gives $e : Y \cong Y'$ such that, whenever $r$ is a $\psi$-coordinate of $\chi$ and $r'$ one of $e(\chi)$, $f(r)^2$ and $f'(r')^2$ are associate. We use only the statement of this theorem.

Now let $\chi \in Y$ with $\beta(\chi, \chi) = 2k$ and $\beta'(e\chi, e\chi) = 2k'$. Then $r$ is associate to $\varpi^k$ and $r'$ to $\varpi'^{k'}$, so $f(r)^2 \sim w^{2mk}$ and $f'(r')^2 \sim w^{2m'k'}$. As $w$ is irreducible in the discrete valuation ring $R$, comparing valuations gives $mk = m'k'$, that is, $m\,\beta(\chi, \chi) = m'\,\beta'(e\chi, e\chi)$. Both forms are symmetric, so the identity
$$2\beta(a, b) = \beta(a + b, a + b) - \beta(a, a) - \beta(b, b)$$
and its analogue for $\beta'$ give the claim for all $a, b$. $\square$

Let $\varphi$ be a polarization function of rank $q$. It is invariant under isomorphisms of lattices and homogeneous of degree $1$ on positive semidefinite forms, so if $\beta$ is positive semidefinite and $Y$ has rank at most $q$, the theorem gives $m\,\varphi(\beta) = m'\,\varphi(\beta')$. So the order $d\varphi(\beta)$ of an ideal along a trait, divided by the ramification of the trait, does not depend on the trait; this is what makes valuation $d\varphi$ along traits a consistent condition. The theorem is used in the comparison of the scales of classical traits and in the theorem on orders of ideals on proper models.

**Remark 9.15.5 (Formalization).** The theorem `traitUniq` is the proof of the statement `TraitUniqSt`; its helpers are the polarization step `form_of_assoc` ([TraitLatticesIsoFormsProportionalRamification.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/TraitLatticesIsoFormsProportionalRamification.lean#L23)), the extension of isomorphisms `extIso` (line 59), the generic fibre `genFib` (line 78) and the base change `pull` (line 107). It is used in `EvalZEqMul.lean`, `ExistsTraitFiniteExtensionClassicalPointFactors.lean` and `IdealOrdersOfProperModel.lean`. The predicate of an admissible base, the realization of a theta system and the theorem on squares of coordinates are not unfolded here. Theta integrality asks for divisors on every normal rational model over $Z$, not only on one.

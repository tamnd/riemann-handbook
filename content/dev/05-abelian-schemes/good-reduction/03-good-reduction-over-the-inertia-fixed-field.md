## Good reduction over the inertia fixed field

Let $R$ be a discrete valuation ring with perfect residue field, $K$ its fraction field, and $V$ a valuation ring above $R$ of a separable closure $\Omega$ of $K$. The fixed field $K'$ of the inertia group of $V$ is the field of the strict henselization of $R$ in classical terms. This section proves that an abelian variety over $K$ has good reduction over $R$ as soon as it has good reduction over the valuation ring of $K'$. The proof checks that $R$, the valuation ring of $K'$ and the restrictions of the decomposition group form an unramified descent setting, and then applies [the descent of the model](/entry/exists-scheme-model-descent-unramified/) of the previous section. In [the next section](/entry/has-good-reduction-of-infinite-torsion-unramified/) the result reduces the criterion of Néron, Ogg and Shafarevich to the case of a strictly henselian base.

### The inertia fixed field

Throughout this section, $R$ is a [[discrete valuation ring|MGM3CNJW9V]] whose residue field $k$ is perfect, $K$ is its fraction field, $\Omega$ is a [[separable closure|JJ91A1V8NQ]] of $K$, and $V$ is a valuation ring of $\Omega$ above $R$, with valuation $v$. Write $D_V$ and $I_V$ for the decomposition and inertia groups of $V$, as in [the first section](/entry/nos-neron-ogg-shafarevich/). Thus $\sigma \in I_V$ if and only if $\sigma(V) = V$ and $v(\sigma y - y) < 1$ for each $y \in V$.

**Definition 3.3.1 (Inertia fixed field).** The *inertia fixed field* is the subfield $K' = \Omega^{I_V}$ of elements fixed by $I_V$. Its valuation ring is $W = V \cap K'$.

For the classical description of $K'$ and $W$ see [henselian and strictly henselian rings](/learn/commutative-algebra/#henselian-and-strictly-henselian-rings) and [decomposition groups, inertia and Frobenius](/learn/galois-representations/#decomposition-groups-inertia-and-frobenius) in Volume 1.

**Lemma 3.3.2 (The decomposition group preserves $K'$).** Let $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/K)$ with $\sigma(V) = V$. Then $\sigma(K') \subseteq K'$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.Lit.Nos.C5.mapsTo_fixedField_inertia`.

*Proof.* Since $\sigma(V) = V$, also $\sigma^{-1}(V) = V$, and so $y \in V$ if and only if $\sigma y \in V$. Applied to $w^{-1}$, this shows that $v(\sigma w) < 1$ if and only if $v(w) < 1$, for $w \in \Omega$, and the same holds for $\sigma^{-1}$.

Let $\tau \in I_V$. We claim that $\rho = \sigma^{-1} \tau \sigma$ lies in $I_V$. First $\rho(V) = \sigma^{-1}\tau\sigma(V) = V$. Next, for $y \in V$, the element $z = \sigma y$ lies in $V$ and
$$
\rho(y) - y = \sigma^{-1}\bigl(\tau(z) - z\bigr),
$$
which has valuation $< 1$ since $v(\tau z - z) < 1$. This proves the claim.

Now let $x \in K'$. Then $\tau(\sigma x) = \sigma(\rho x) = \sigma x$, because $\rho \in I_V$ fixes $x$. So $\sigma x$ is fixed by $I_V$, that is, $\sigma x \in K'$. $\square$

Each $\sigma \in D_V$ therefore restricts to an automorphism $\sigma|_{K'}$ of $K'$ over $K$, with inverse $\sigma^{-1}|_{K'}$.

**Lemma 3.3.3 (The valuation ring of $K'$).** The ring $W$ is a discrete valuation ring. Let $\pi$ be a uniformizer of $R$. Then $v(\pi) < 1$, and each $y \in K'$ with $v(y) < 1$ can be written $y = z\pi$ with $z \in V$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.isDiscreteValuationRing_fixedField_inertia_alt` (also `GlobalTheory.T.uniformizer_of_valuationRing_fixedField_inertia`, the second statement).

*Proof.* This is classical. The extension $K'/K$ is unramified at $V$, so its value group is that of $K$, generated by $v(\pi)$. We do not unfold the proof. $\square$

**Lemma 3.3.4 (Residues fixed by the decomposition group).** Let $x \in V$ and assume that $v(\sigma x - x) < 1$ for each $\sigma \in D_V$. Then there is $r \in R$ with $v(x - r) < 1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.exists_dvr_congr_of_residue_fixed_by_decomposition`.

*Proof.* In terms of the residue field $\kappa(V)$, which is an algebraic extension of $k$, the hypothesis says that the residue $\bar x$ is fixed by the image of $D_V \to \operatorname{Aut}(\kappa(V)/k)$. This image is the whole group of automorphisms, and $\kappa(V)/k$ is normal (Bourbaki, *Commutative Algebra*, Chapter V, §2). The elements fixed by all automorphisms of a normal algebraic extension of $k$ form a purely inseparable extension of $k$, which is $k$ since $k$ is perfect. So $\bar x \in k$, and any $r \in R$ with residue $\bar x$ works. $\square$

### Descent from the inertia fixed field

**Theorem 3.3.5 (Good reduction over the inertia fixed field).** Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $K$. If $A \times_K K'$ has good reduction over $W$, then $A$ has good reduction over $R$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.Lit.Nos.C5.hasGoodReduction_of_inertiaFixed`.

*Proof.* We check the five conditions of an unramified descent setting from [the previous section](/entry/exists-compatible-projective-embeddings/), with $R$, $W$, $K$ and $K'$.

1. By the lemma on the valuation ring of $K'$, $W$ is a discrete valuation ring, with fraction field $K'$. Since $V$ lies above $R$, the image of $R$ in $\Omega$ lies in $V \cap K' = W$, which gives an injective map $R \to W$ compatible with $K \to K'$.

2. An element $x \in W$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}_W$ if and only if $v(x) < 1$: for $x \neq 0$ both conditions say $x^{-1} \notin W$, and $x^{-1} \in W$ if and only if $x^{-1} \in V$. Let $\pi$ be a uniformizer of $R$, so $\mathfrak{m}_R W = \pi W$. By the lemma on the valuation ring of $K'$, $v(\pi) < 1$, so $\pi W \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_W$. Conversely, if $x \in \mathfrak{m}_W$, then $v(x) < 1$ and $x = z \pi$ with $z \in V$. Since $z = x\pi^{-1}$ lies in $K'$, it lies in $W$, so $x \in \pi W$. Hence $\mathfrak{m}_R W = \mathfrak{m}_W$.

3. Let $D$ be the set of restrictions $\sigma|_{K'}$ for $\sigma \in D_V$, defined by the lemma on the decomposition group. For $x \in W$, $\sigma x \in V \cap K' = W$, and similarly $\sigma^{-1} x \in W$, since $\sigma^{\pm 1}(V) = V$.

4. Let $x \in W$ with $\sigma x - x \in \mathfrak{m}_W$ for each $\sigma|_{K'} \in D$. By 2, $v(\sigma x - x) < 1$ for each $\sigma \in D_V$. The lemma on residues gives $r \in R$ with $v(x - r) < 1$, that is, $x - r \in \mathfrak{m}_W$ by 2 again.

5. By hypothesis there are an abelian scheme $G$ over $W$ and an isomorphism of group schemes $e : G \times_W K' \cong A \times_K K'$.

The descent of the model gives a scheme $X$ over $R$ with $X \times_R K = A$ and $X \times_R W = G$, compatibly with $e$. It remains to see that $X$ is an abelian scheme over $R$ whose generic fibre is $A$ as a group scheme. The map $R \to W$ is a local homomorphism of discrete valuation rings, so it is faithfully flat. Since $X \times_R W \cong G$ is smooth and proper over $W$, the scheme $X$ is smooth and proper over $R$ by faithfully flat descent, and the group law of $A$ extends to $X$. This last step is a cited result, which we do not unfold. $\square$

The hypothesis on $k$ is used in the lemma on residues: automorphisms of $\kappa(V)$ over $k$ fix every element of $\kappa(V)$ which is purely inseparable over $k$, and for a perfect $k$ these elements lie in $k$.

**Remark 3.3.6 (Formalization).** The last step, from the model $X$ to good reduction, is `GlobalTheory.T.hasGoodReduction_of_unramified_abelianScheme_model`, and it is cited, not unfolded. The lemma on the valuation ring of $K'$ is a wrapper of `GlobalTheory.InertiaFixedDVR.isDiscreteValuationRing_fixedField_inertia`, proved by a `first | exact` tactic with a raised heartbeat limit ([IsDiscreteValuationRingFixedFieldInertiaStatement.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/IsDiscreteValuationRingFixedFieldInertiaStatement.lean#L11)), and both it and the lemma on residues assume that the residue field is perfect. The description of $\mathfrak{m}_W$ by the valuation uses the helper `UpperTheory.Lit.Nos.C5.val_lt_one_iff` ([HasGoodReductionOfInertiaFixed.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/GoodReduction/HasGoodReductionOfInertiaFixed.lean#L19)). The set $D$ is defined as the set of automorphisms of $K'$ which agree with some $\sigma \in D_V$ on $K'$, so it is a set and not a group, which is all the descent of the model asks.

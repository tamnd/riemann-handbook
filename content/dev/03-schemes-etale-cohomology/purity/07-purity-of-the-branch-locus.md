## Purity of the branch locus

The previous section made the closure of a torsor étale at every prime of height at most $1$. This section extends étaleness to every prime $\mathfrak{P}$ of $R$ with $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{P} \cap A) \leq 1$, which is step 4 of the proof of [étale enlargement](/entry/etale-enlarge/). The tool is the purity theorem of Zariski and Nagata: a finite cover of a regular scheme by a normal scheme that is étale in codimension one is étale. We prove it by induction on the dimension, with a discriminant argument at the end. The freeness that this argument needs in dimension at least $3$ is the subject of the next section.

A local ring $R$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ is a [[regular local ring|63XXTF6B5M]] if it is noetherian and $\mathfrak{m}$ is generated by $\dim R$ elements. A regular local ring is a normal domain.

**Theorem 2.7.1 (Purity theorem of Zariski and Nagata).** Let $R$ be a regular local ring, and let $A$ be a finite $R$-algebra that is a normal domain and contains $R$. If $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is étale over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ for every prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$ of height $1$, then $A$ is étale over $R$.

See SGA 1, Exposé X, Théorème 3.1, where the theorem is stated for regular schemes and covers that are étale outside a closed set of codimension at least $2$.

### Purity for the closure

**Theorem 2.7.2 (Purity for the closure).** Let $q$ be a natural number; the definition of a $\mathbb{Z}/q$-torsor makes sense for every $q$. Let $A$ be a noetherian, normal, local domain, essentially of finite type over $\mathbb{Z}$, let $E_A$ be an étale $A[x]$-algebra with a prime $Q_A$, and let $D_A$ be a $\mathbb{Z}/q$-torsor over the generic fibre of $R = (E_A)_{Q_A}$. Let $C$ be the closure of $R$ in $D_A$. If $C$ is étale at every prime of $R$ of height at most $1$, then $C$ is étale at every prime $\mathfrak{P}$ of $R$ with $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{P} \cap A) \leq 1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.integralClosureEtaleAt_of_height_le_one_purity` (also `GlobalTheory.KeyOriginEnlarge.closureEtaleAt_of_comap_height_le_one`).

*Proof.* The ring $R$ is a noetherian normal domain, and $A \to R$ is injective, since $R$ is a localization of an étale algebra over $A[x]$. A $\mathbb{Z}/q$-torsor is finite étale over its base, so $D_A$ is finite étale over $R_K$. Hence $C$ is finite over $R$, and $C$ is integrally closed in its localization $C \otimes_R \operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Let $\mathfrak{P}$ be a prime with $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{P} \cap A) \leq 1$. Then $A_{\mathfrak{P} \cap A}$ is a field or a discrete valuation ring, so it is regular, and $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is a localization of an étale algebra over the regular ring $A_{\mathfrak{P} \cap A}[x]$. So $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is regular. Now [the local purity theorem](/entry/etale-at-prime-of-regular-of-is-integrally-closed-in/) applies. $\square$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.cyclic_torsor_etale` (also `GlobalTheory.T.closure_finite_over_base`, `GlobalTheory.T.isIntegrallyClosedIn_etale_genFib_of_injective` and `GlobalTheory.T.regular_height_one_localization`).

In this proof we take four facts as given: torsors are finite étale, the closure is finite over $R$, the closure is integrally closed in its localization, and $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is regular.

**Remark 2.7.3.** None of the four facts in the proof uses that $A$ is local or essentially of finite type over $\mathbb{Z}$, so the same argument works for every noetherian normal domain $A$. The finiteness of the closure uses only that $R$ is a noetherian normal domain and that $D_A$ is finite étale over $R_K$.

### Purity at one prime

**Theorem 2.7.4 (Local purity).** Let $R$ be a domain, and let $C$ be a finite $R$-algebra that is integrally closed in $C \otimes_R \operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Let $\mathfrak{P}$ be a prime of $R$ such that $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is regular. Assume that $C_{\mathfrak{P}'}$ is étale over $R_{\mathfrak{P}'}$ for every prime $\mathfrak{P}' \subseteq \mathfrak{P}$ of height at most $1$. Then $C_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is étale over $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.etale_atPrime_of_regular_of_isIntegrallyClosedIn` (also `GlobalTheory.PurityLoc.etale_atPrime_of_isIntegrallyClosedIn`).

*Proof.* Let $E = C \otimes_R \operatorname{Frac}(R)$. The hypothesis at $\mathfrak{P}' = 0$ says that $E$ is étale over the field $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$, so $E$ is reduced and [[artinian|20QEAM8AX2]]. The ring $C$ is the integral closure of $R$ in $E$, and the integral closure commutes with localization, so $C_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is the integral closure of $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$ in $E_{\mathfrak{P}} = E$. Every nonzero element of $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is a unit in $E$, so $E$ is torsion free over $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$. The primes of $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$ of height at most $1$ are the localizations of the primes $\mathfrak{P}' \subseteq \mathfrak{P}$ of height at most $1$, so the closure is étale at each of them. Now [purity for an artinian algebra](/entry/etale-integral-closure-of-is-artinian-ring/) applies to $R_{\mathfrak{P}}$ and $E$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.7.5 (Purity for an artinian algebra).** Let $R$ be a regular local ring, and let $E$ be a reduced artinian $R$-algebra that is torsion free as an $R$-module. Assume that the integral closure $C$ of $R$ in $E$ is finite over $R$, and that $C_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is étale over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ for every prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of height at most $1$. Then $C$ is étale over $R$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PurityProd.etale_integralClosure_of_isArtinianRing`.

*Proof.* If the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $R$ has height at most $1$, then the hypothesis at $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{m}$ is the claim. Otherwise $\dim R \geq 2$, so $R$ is not a field. A reduced artinian ring is a finite product of fields $E = \prod_I E/I$, over its maximal ideals $I$. The [[integral closure|G0V86SJBQW]] of $R$ in a finite product is the product of the integral closures $C_I$ of $R$ in the factors $E/I$. Each $C_I$ is a direct factor of $C$, so it is finite over $R$, and it is étale at the primes of height at most $1$. It is a normal domain, as the integral closure of a ring in a field. It contains $R$, because $E/I$ is torsion free over $R$: the image of $R$ in each factor is injective, since a nonzero $r \in R$ acts injectively on $E$, hence invertibly on the artinian ring $E$, hence invertibly on each factor. So [the purity theorem for a normal domain](/entry/etale-of-etale-height-one-of-regular-local-ring/) applies to each $C_I$, and $C = \prod_I C_I$ is étale. $\square$

### Purity for a normal domain

**Theorem 2.7.6 (Purity for a normal domain).** Let $R$ be a noetherian local domain whose maximal ideal is generated by $\dim R$ elements, with $\dim R \geq 1$ and $R$ not a field, and let $A$ be a finite $R$-algebra that is a normal domain, with $R \to A$ injective. If $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is étale over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ for every prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$ of height $1$, then $A$ is étale over $R$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.etale_of_etale_height_one_of_regularLocalRing` (also `GlobalTheory.BranchPurity.etale_of_etale_height_one`, `GlobalTheory.BranchPurity.finiteRing_sq_sub_mul_sq_eq_of_card`).

This is the purity theorem of Zariski and Nagata stated above. The two conditions $\dim R \geq 1$ and "$R$ is not a field" say the same thing for a noetherian local domain; the second implies the first.

*Proof.* We argue by strong induction on the number $n$ of generators of $\mathfrak{m}$, which is $\dim R$. The proof has two parts: first $A$ is a [[free|0DZ9DKAKZR]] $R$-module, then the discriminant of $A$ is a unit.

Freeness when $\dim R \leq 2$. We take as given that $A$ is free. The argument is as follows. In dimension $1$, $R$ is a discrete valuation ring and $A$ is a finite torsion free module, hence free. In dimension $2$, the normal ring $A$ has depth $2$ as an $R$-module, and over a regular local ring of dimension $2$ a finite module of depth $2$ is free, by the formula of Auslander and Buchsbaum (Matsumura, *Commutative Ring Theory*, Theorem 19.1). In dimension $2$ one can also argue directly, with a Koszul relation for a pair of generators $(x, y)$ of $\mathfrak{m}$.

Freeness when $\dim R \geq 3$. We use [the freeness theorem](/entry/free-of-etale-on-punctured-spectrum/) of the next section. It needs $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ to be étale over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ for every prime $\mathfrak{p} \neq \mathfrak{m}$. At $\mathfrak{p} = 0$ this follows from the hypothesis at any height one prime: the fraction field of $A$ is a localization of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$, so it is étale over $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Let $0 \neq \mathfrak{p} \neq \mathfrak{m}$. Then $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a regular local ring of smaller dimension, and not a field. The ring $A_{\mathfrak{p}} = R_{\mathfrak{p}} \otimes_R A$ is a normal domain, finite over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ and containing it, and it is étale over $R_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ at the height one primes $\mathfrak{p}'$ of $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$, because these are localizations of height one primes of $R$. By the induction hypothesis $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is étale over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$.

The discriminant. Now $A$ is free over $R$, with a basis $e_1, \dots, e_r$. Let $d = \det(\operatorname{Tr}_{A/R}(e_k e_l))$ be the [[discriminant|3BCYVS9E17]]. For a height one prime $\mathfrak{p}$, the algebra $A_{\mathfrak{p}} \otimes \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ is étale over the residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, so its discriminant, which is the image of $d$, is nonzero. Hence $d \notin \mathfrak{p}$ for every height one prime $\mathfrak{p}$. Suppose that $d$ is not a unit, so $d \in \mathfrak{m}$. Choose $t \neq 0$ in $\mathfrak{m}$, with $t = d$ if $d \neq 0$; this is possible because $R$ is not a field. A minimal prime over $(t)$ is nonzero and has height at most $1$ by [[Krull's principal ideal theorem|SJZW2HN1SC]], so it has height $1$. If $d \neq 0$ it contains $d$, and if $d = 0$ it contains $d$ trivially. This contradicts the previous step, so $d$ is a unit. A finite free algebra with a unit discriminant is étale. $\square$

**Remark 2.7.7 (Formalization).** In the purity theorem for the closure, the proof does not use that $A$ is local or essentially of finite type over $\mathbb{Z}$: [IntegralClosureEtaleAtOfHeightLeOnePurity.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/IntegralClosureEtaleAtOfHeightLeOnePurity.lean#L42). We read the statements of its four facts, and of the freeness in dimension at most $2$ and the discriminant step, not their proofs; the case of dimension at most $2$ is one lemma, with a Koszul relation. The name of the induction speaks of squares in a finite ring, but its statement is the purity theorem with $n$ the number of generators of $\mathfrak{m}$: [EtaleOfEtaleHeightOneOfRegularLocalRing.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/EtaleOfEtaleHeightOneOfRegularLocalRing.lean#L42). The induction does not ask for $1 \leq \dim R$; the theorem above it asks for both conditions and discards the first: [EtaleOfEtaleHeightOneOfRegularLocalRing.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/EtaleOfEtaleHeightOneOfRegularLocalRing.lean#L72). The second name of the theorem imports Mathlib twice and raises the heartbeat limit: [EtaleOfEtaleHeightOneOfRegularLocalRingStatement.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/EtaleOfEtaleHeightOneOfRegularLocalRingStatement.lean#L5). Regularity in the purity theorem for a normal domain is a condition on numbers of generators, while the local purity theorem and purity for an artinian algebra use the Mathlib class of regular local rings; the proofs convert between the two.

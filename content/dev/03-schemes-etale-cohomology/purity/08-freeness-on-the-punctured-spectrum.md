## Freeness on the punctured spectrum

The discriminant argument of [the purity theorem for a normal domain](/entry/etale-of-etale-height-one-of-regular-local-ring/) needs $A$ to be a free module over the regular local ring $R$. The previous section proved this in dimension at most $2$. This section proves it in dimension at least $3$, when $A$ is already known to be étale over every prime other than the maximal ideal. The method is a hyperplane section: we cut $R$ by one element of a regular system of parameters, and we go down in dimension until we reach dimension $2$. Depth controls the induction.

Let $R$ be a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ and let $M$ be an $R$-module. A sequence $r_1, \dots, r_n$ in $\mathfrak{m}$ is $M$-*regular* if $r_k$ is a nonzerodivisor on $M/(r_1, \dots, r_{k-1})M$ for each $k$, and $M \neq (r_1, \dots, r_n)M$. We say that $M$ has *depth at least* $n$ if there is an $M$-regular sequence of length $n$ in $\mathfrak{m}$. The *punctured spectrum* of $R$ is the set of primes $\mathfrak{p} \neq \mathfrak{m}$. A finite $R$-algebra $A$ is *étale on the punctured spectrum* if $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is étale over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ for every $\mathfrak{p} \neq \mathfrak{m}$.

Two classical facts on depth are behind the proof. First, for a finite module $M \neq 0$ over a regular local ring $R$, the formula of Auslander and Buchsbaum gives $\operatorname{pd} M + \operatorname{depth} M = \dim R$, so $M$ is free if and only if $\operatorname{depth} M = \dim R$ (Matsumura, *Commutative Ring Theory*, Theorem 19.1). Second, a normal noetherian domain satisfies Serre's condition $(S_2)$ (Matsumura, Theorem 23.8). For a normal domain $A$ that is finite over a regular local ring $R$ of dimension at least $2$, this gives $\operatorname{depth}_R A \geq 2$.

### The freeness theorem

**Theorem 2.8.1 (Freeness from étaleness on the punctured spectrum).** Let $R$ be a noetherian local domain whose maximal ideal is generated by $\dim R$ elements, with $\dim R \geq 3$, and let $A$ be a finite $R$-algebra that is a normal domain, with $R \to A$ injective. If $A$ is étale on the punctured spectrum of $R$, then $A$ is a free $R$-module.

*Proof.* By Serre's condition, as above, $A$ has depth at least $2$ as an $R$-module. Such a ring is a regular local ring. Now apply [the theorem on algebras of depth two](/entry/purity-free-of-ring-krull-dim/) below. $\square$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.modDepthGE_two_of_isIntegrallyClosed_of_isRegularLocalRing'`.

**Remark 2.8.2.** The proof uses the bound $\dim R \geq 3$ only to get $\dim R \geq 2$ for the depth bound, and the theorem below holds in every dimension. So the argument proves the theorem for $\dim R \geq 2$. The previous section uses it only in dimension at least $3$, and treats dimension $2$ separately.

### Algebras of depth two

We say that a regular local ring $R$ has *the freeness property* if every finite $R$-algebra $A$ of depth at least $2$ that is étale on the punctured spectrum of $R$ is a free $R$-module. Here $A$ need not be a domain.

**Theorem 2.8.3 (Algebras of depth two are free).** Every regular local ring has the freeness property.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PurityStep.purityFree_of_ringKrullDim`.

*Proof.* Induction on $n = \dim R$.

Dimension at most $2$. Let $A$ be a finite $R$-algebra of depth at least $2$. Since $\mathfrak{m}$ is generated by $\dim R \leq 2$ elements, the depth of $A$ is at least the number of generators of $\mathfrak{m}$. We take as given that such a module is free. When $\dim R = 2$ this is the formula of Auslander and Buchsbaum; when $\dim R < 2$ no module has depth $2$, because the depth of a finite module $M \neq 0$ is at most $\dim R$, so the claim is empty.

Dimension $n + 1 \geq 3$. We take as given that the claim for $R$ follows from the freeness property of every complete regular local ring of dimension $n + 1$. The idea is that the completion $\hat{R}$ is [[faithfully flat|C31PYYPRGC]] over $R$, so depth, étaleness on the punctured spectrum and freeness pass between $A$ and $\hat{R} \otimes_R A$. Here a prime of $\hat{R}$ other than its maximal ideal contracts to a prime of $R$ other than $\mathfrak{m}$, because it does not contain $\mathfrak{m}\hat{R}$, which is the maximal ideal of $\hat{R}$.

So let $R$ be complete of dimension $n + 1$. The maximal ideal is not contained in $\mathfrak{m}^2$, because otherwise Nakayama's lemma gives $\mathfrak{m} = 0$ and $R$ is a field of dimension $0$. Choose $f \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$. Then $\bar{R} = R/(f)$ is a regular local ring of dimension $n \geq 2$, and by induction $\bar{R}$ has the freeness property. Let $A$ be a finite $R$-algebra of depth at least $2$, étale on the punctured spectrum.

Step 1. We take as given that $A$ has depth at least $3$, from the freeness property of $\bar{R}$ and the completeness of $R$. This is where the induction enters.

Step 2. The element $f$ is a nonzerodivisor on $A$. Indeed, $f \neq 0$, since $f \notin \mathfrak{m}^2$. The maximal ideal is not an associated prime of $A$, since $A$ has positive depth. For a prime $\mathfrak{p} \neq \mathfrak{m}$, the localization $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is étale, hence flat, over the domain $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$, so it has no torsion, and $\mathfrak{p}$ can be an associated prime of $A$ only if $\mathfrak{p} = 0$. So $f$ lies in no associated prime of $A$.

Step 3. Since $f$ is $A$-regular, the depth of $\bar{A} = A/fA$ over $\bar{R}$ is at least $3 - 1 = 2$. The algebra $\bar{A}$ is finite over $\bar{R}$. It is étale on the punctured spectrum of $\bar{R}$, because a prime of $\bar{R}$ other than the maximal ideal comes from a prime $\mathfrak{p} \neq \mathfrak{m}$ of $R$ that contains $f$, and $\bar{A}_{\mathfrak{p}} = A_{\mathfrak{p}}/fA_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a base change of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$.

Step 4. By the freeness property of $\bar{R}$, the module $\bar{A}$ is free over $\bar{R}$. Lift a basis of $\bar{A}$ to elements $a_1, \dots, a_r$ of $A$. By Nakayama's lemma they generate $A$, so there is an exact sequence $0 \to K \to R^r \to A \to 0$. Tensor it with $\bar{R}$. Since $f$ is a nonzerodivisor on $A$, we have $\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(\bar{R}, A) = 0$, so the sequence $0 \to K/fK \to \bar{R}^r \to \bar{A} \to 0$ is exact. The map $\bar{R}^r \to \bar{A}$ is an isomorphism, so $K/fK = 0$, and $K = 0$ by Nakayama's lemma, since $K$ is finite over the noetherian ring $R$. So $A \cong R^r$ is free. $\square$

**Remark 2.8.4 (Formalization).** The freeness theorem has a statement that asks for $\dim R \geq 3$, but its proof works for $\dim R \geq 2$: [FreeOfEtalePunctured.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/FreeOfEtalePunctured.lean#L77). Its second name imports Mathlib twice and raises the heartbeat limit: [FreeOfEtalePuncturedStatement.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/FreeOfEtalePuncturedStatement.lean#L5). For the depth bound, the reduction to complete rings, the depth three step and the lifting step, we read the statements, not the proofs; the arguments given for steps 2 and 4 are the standard ones, and we did not read the formal proofs of these steps. The reduction to complete rings asks for the freeness property of every complete regular local ring of the same dimension, and not only of the completion of $R$: [PurityFreeOfCompleteRegularLocalStatement.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/PurityFreeOfCompleteRegularLocalStatement.lean#L8). This is what the induction provides, since it runs over all regular local rings of a given dimension. The depth three step takes the freeness property of $R/(f)$ as a hypothesis: [DepthGeThreeFiniteEtalePuncturedRegularStatement.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/DepthGeThreeFiniteEtalePuncturedRegularStatement.lean#L8). Depth is defined by the existence of a regular sequence in the maximal ideal, as above, with a definition from another part of the development: [ModDepthGE.lean](lean:ReciprocityTheory/CommutativeAlgebra/CohenMacaulay/ModDepthGE.lean#L7). The base case covers dimensions $0$, $1$ and $2$ with one lemma. The theorem on algebras of depth two uses the Mathlib class of regular local rings, and the freeness theorem uses the condition on numbers of generators.

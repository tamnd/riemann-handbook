This theorem proves the lemma `EqvQA`, which [the affine proper base change](/entry/affine-proper-base-change/) uses at level $0$ of its Čech nerve. Its only user is [its second name](/entry/quasi-affine-equivalence-intersection-number-base-change/). The proof is the term `EqvQA_of a31 a0 a14 a32`, and `EqvQA_of` has a short argument with three cases.

## Statement

A ring is *QA* if it is a quotient of an absolutely integrally closed domain. A pair $(S, J)$ is *good* if $J$ lies in the Jacobson radical of $S$ and each idempotent of $S/J$ lifts to an idempotent of $S$.

**Theorem.** Assume the axioms A1, A9, B1 and B2. Let $S$ be a QA ring, let $(S, J)$ be good, and let $q \neq 0$ be a unit in $S$. Then for all $i$, restriction
$$H^n(\operatorname{Spec} S, \mathbb{Z}/q(i)) \to H^n(\operatorname{Spec} S/J, \mathbb{Z}/q(i))$$
is bijective in all degrees $n$.

## Proof

The inputs are four lemmas of the same file. `a0` (`QAClosed`): a QA ring is absolutely integrally closed, and quotients of QA rings are QA. `a14`: when $B$ has a primitive $q$-th root of unity $\zeta$, there is a natural isomorphism $e_{B,\zeta}$ from the locally constant functions $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \mathbb{Z}/q$ to $H^0(\operatorname{Spec} B, \mathbb{Z}/q(i))$. `a32` (`LCGood`): if $(S, J)$ is good, restriction of locally constant functions from $\operatorname{Spec} S$ to $\operatorname{Spec} S/J$ is bijective. `a31` (`AcycQA`): for a QA ring with $q$ a unit, $H^n(\,\cdot\,, \mathbb{Z}/q(i))$ is $0$ for $n > 0$.

1. **$n < 0$.** Both groups are $0$ by A9.
2. **$n = 0$.** $S$ is absolutely integrally closed, so the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_q$, which is monic of degree $\varphi(q) > 0$, has a root $\zeta$ in $S$ (`prim_of_aic`). By naturality of $e$, the restriction map is $e_{S/J,\bar\zeta} \circ (\text{restriction of functions}) \circ e_{S,\zeta}^{-1}$. All three maps are bijective, by `a14` and `a32`.
3. **$n > 0$.** $S/J$ is QA by `a0`, and $q$ is a unit in it. So both groups are $0$ by `a31`.

This chapter has two parts. The first part is analytic. It proves Hörmander's $L^2$ estimate for $\bar\partial$ on étale Stein spaces, builds sections of a high multiple of a positive line bundle with given first jets at finitely many points, and uses them to make an étale frame off a hypersurface that contains a finite zero set. On the complement of that hypersurface it proves a twisted estimate in the manner of Ohsawa and Takegoshi, and from this an $L^2$ extension theorem: a section of $K_Y + E$ given at the finite set of common zeros of $n$ sections extends to all of $Y$ with a bound on its norm. The second part is algebraic. It states the klt condition for local rings in terms of places and discrepancies, proves two forms of inversion of adjunction, moves the klt condition up an étale tower, and with this proves the local and the global forms of the inductive step of Angehrn and Siu, which cuts a non-klt centre down at several points at once. The chapter needs from Volume 1 the [complex manifolds](/learn/geometry/#complex-manifolds), [line bundles and divisors](/learn/geometry/#line-bundles-and-divisors), [metrics and curvature](/learn/positivity-resolution/#metrics-curvature-and-positivity), [$L^2$ estimates](/learn/positivity-resolution/#l2-estimates), [multiplier ideals and klt pairs](/learn/positivity-resolution/#multiplier-ideals-and-nadel-vanishing), [resolution and principalization](/learn/positivity-resolution/#resolution-of-singularities-and-principalization) and [the theorem of Angehrn and Siu](/learn/positivity-resolution/#the-angehrn-siu-theorem).

The $L^2$ extension theorem proves [the $L^2$ extension theorem for log canonical bundles](/entry/l2-extension-log-canonical-of-literature/), and Hörmander's estimate and the inductive step of Angehrn and Siu are used in the step [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) of [the literature part of the proof](/proof/literature/). The algebraic part assumes principalisation of ideals in characteristic zero, which the proof takes from the literature.

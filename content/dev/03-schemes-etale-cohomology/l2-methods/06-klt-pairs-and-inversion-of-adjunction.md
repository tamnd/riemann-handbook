## Klt pairs over local rings and inversion of adjunction

Sections 6 to 9 turn to the algebraic side of the theorem of Angehrn and Siu. The goal is to make a pair fail to be klt at given points by adding a small multiple of a divisor of a section, and the tool for this is that the klt condition can be moved up and down along a hypersurface. This section states the klt condition for a local ring of a point of a variety, in terms of valuations, and proves two forms of inversion of adjunction. The first says that a pair is klt if it is klt along a hypersurface $S$ in the sense of adjunction. The second says that a pair is klt if its restriction to a hypersurface $\{f = 0\}$ is klt. Section 7 applies the second form once for each coordinate of an étale tower.

We work over $\mathbb{C}$ with a field $K$ that contains $\mathbb{C}$, the function field of a variety. For the background see [klt pairs and multiplier ideals](/learn/positivity-resolution/#multiplier-ideals-and-nadel-vanishing) and [resolution and principalization](/learn/positivity-resolution/#resolution-of-singularities-and-principalization).

**Definition 10.6.1 (Place and order).** A *place* of $K$ is a valuation ring $\mathcal{O}_E \subset K$ that contains $\mathbb{C}$, is a discrete valuation ring and is essentially of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$. We write $\operatorname{ord}_E : K^\times \to \mathbb{Z}$ for its normalised valuation.

A place is the local ring of a prime divisor $E$ on some normal model of $K$: a divisorial valuation. For a ring $R \subseteq K$, a place $E$ *contains* $R$ if $R \subseteq \mathcal{O}_E$, that is, the centre of $E$ on $\operatorname{Spec} R$ exists. It *is a prime of* $R$ if it contains $R$ and $\mathcal{O}_E$ is a localisation of $R$: each element of $\mathcal{O}_E$ is $a/b$ with $a, b \in R$ and $\operatorname{ord}_E b = 0$. The primes of $R$ are the prime divisors of $\operatorname{Spec} R$.

**Definition 10.6.2 (Discrepancy).** Let $\eta$ be a nonzero rational top form on $K$ over $\mathbb{C}$, of degree $n$, and let $\varphi = \sum_g \varphi_g [g]$ be a finite formal sum with $g \in K$ and $\varphi_g \in \mathbb{Q}$. For a place $E$, let $\operatorname{ord}_E \eta$ be the order of $\eta$ against a generator of the free module of top forms of $\mathcal{O}_E$ over $\mathbb{C}$, and put
$$\operatorname{disc}_E(\eta, \varphi) = \operatorname{ord}_E \eta - \sum_g \varphi_g \operatorname{ord}_E g.$$

If $\eta = dx_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dx_n$ for local coordinates $x$ at a smooth point and $\Delta = \sum_g \varphi_g \operatorname{div}(g)$, then $\operatorname{disc}_E(\eta, \varphi)$ is the discrepancy $a(E; X, \Delta)$ of the pair. On a log resolution it is the coefficient of $E$ in $K_{X'} - \pi^*(K_X + \Delta)$.

**Definition 10.6.3 (Klt and effective).** Let $R \subseteq K$ be a subring. The pair $(\eta, \varphi)$ is *klt over* $R$ if $\eta \neq 0$, $\varphi_0 = 0$, and $\operatorname{disc}_E(\eta, \varphi) > -1$ for each place $E$ that contains $R$. It is *effective at* $R$ if $\eta \neq 0$, $\varphi_0 = 0$, and $\operatorname{disc}_E(\eta, \varphi) \le 0$ for each prime $E$ of $R$.

For the local ring $R$ of a point $x$ of a normal variety, klt over $R$ is the usual condition that the pair is klt at $x$: all places with centre through $x$, including the prime divisors through $x$, have discrepancy $> -1$. Effective at $R$ says that the boundary has nonnegative coefficients at $x$.

**Definition 10.6.4 (Local model).** A subring $R \subseteq K$ is a *local model* of $K$ if it contains $\mathbb{C}$, is a local ring, is essentially of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$, and has fraction field $K$. A *transcendence basis* is a family $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ of elements of $K$ that is a transcendence basis over $\mathbb{C}$. We write $dx = dx_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dx_n$.

A local model is the local ring of a point of a variety with function field $K$.

**Definition 10.6.5 (Klt along a prime in the sense of adjunction).** Let $T$ be a subring and $S$ a place of $K$. The pair $(\eta, \varphi)$ is *klt along* $S$ over $T$ if $\eta \neq 0$, $\varphi_0 = 0$ and the following holds. Let $Q \supseteq T$ be a local model of Krull dimension $2$ whose maximal ideal is generated by two elements $t, u$, let $S$ and $E$ be primes of $Q$ with $\operatorname{ord}_S t = 1$ and $\operatorname{ord}_E u = 1$, and suppose $\operatorname{disc}_P(\eta, \varphi) = 0$ for all primes $P$ of $Q$ other than $S$ and $E$. Then $\operatorname{disc}_E(\eta, \varphi) > -1$.

This is a test on regular surface germs over $T$ that are cut by $S$ and a second curve $E$. On such a germ the boundary is supported on $S \cup E$. When $S$ has coefficient $1$, the condition $\operatorname{disc}_E > -1$ says that the restriction of the boundary to $S$ has coefficient $< 1$ at the point $S \cap E$. In this sense the condition says that the pair induced on $S$ by adjunction is klt.

We assume *principalisation*: each ideal of a variety over $\mathbb{C}$ becomes locally principal after a sequence of blow-ups of smooth centres. This is the theorem of Hironaka.

**Theorem 10.6.6 (Inversion of adjunction over a local model).** Assume principalisation. Let $T \subseteq K$ be an integrally closed local model, $x$ a transcendence basis of $K$, $\varphi$ a finite formal sum, $f \in T$ nonzero and $S$ a prime of $T$ with $\operatorname{ord}_S f = 1$. Suppose that $\operatorname{disc}_S(dx, \varphi) = 0$, that $(dx, \varphi)$ is effective at $T$, and that $(dx, \varphi + [f])$ is klt along $S$ over $T$. Then $(dx, \varphi)$ is klt over $T$.

In geometric terms: $S$ is a prime divisor through the point, cut out by $f$ to first order, and $S$ has coefficient $0$ in $\Delta$. Then $\Delta + S$ has coefficient $1$ on $S$, and the theorem says that if $(X, \Delta + S)$ is plt near $S$ in the sense of adjunction, then $(X, \Delta)$ is klt at the point.

*Proof.* The ring $T$ is the local ring of a point $y$ of a projective integral variety $Y$ of dimension $n$ over $\mathbb{C}$, with a ring isomorphism of function fields over $\mathbb{C}$ that maps $T$ onto the local ring at $y$. This is the existence of a projective model of a local ring essentially of finite type. Places, forms, orders and discrepancies move along the isomorphism, so we may work on $Y$.

The sum $\varphi + [f]$ has rational coefficients, so there are $m > 0$ and $g \in K^\times$ with $\operatorname{ord}_E(\varphi + [f]) = \operatorname{ord}_E(g)/m$ for each place $E$. Then $\operatorname{disc}_E(dx, \varphi + [f]) = \operatorname{ord}_E(dx) - \operatorname{ord}_E(g)/m$, the discrepancy of $E$ for the pair defined by $dx$ and $g^{1/m}$. We check the hypotheses of inversion of adjunction on the projective variety $Y$.

- The stalk at $y$ is integrally closed, since $T$ is.
- The function $f$ is regular at $y$, since $f \in T$.
- $S$ is a prime of the local ring at $y$, with $\operatorname{ord}_S f = 1$.
- $\operatorname{disc}_S(dx, \varphi + [f]) = \operatorname{disc}_S(dx, \varphi) - \operatorname{ord}_S f = 0 - 1 = -1$.
- For each prime $E$ of the local ring at $y$, $\operatorname{disc}_E(dx, \varphi + [f]) = \operatorname{disc}_E(dx, \varphi) - \operatorname{ord}_E f \le 0$, since $(dx, \varphi)$ is effective at $T$ and $\operatorname{ord}_E f \ge 0$.
- $(dx, \varphi + [f])$ is klt along $S$ over the local ring at $y$, by transport of the hypothesis.

We now use inversion of adjunction on a projective variety, which we take as given in the following form. Assume principalisation. Let $Y$ be a projective integral variety of dimension $n$ over $\mathbb{C}$, $x$ a transcendence basis of its function field, $m > 0$, $g, f \in K^\times$ and $y \in Y$ with normal stalk and $f$ regular at $y$. Let $S$ be a prime of the local ring at $y$ with $\operatorname{ord}_S f = 1$ and discrepancy $-1$ for $(dx, g^{1/m})$, suppose all primes of the local ring at $y$ have discrepancy $\le 0$, and suppose that the pair is klt along $S$. Then for each place $E$ with centre through $y$, the discrepancy of $E$ plus $\operatorname{ord}_E f$ is $> -1$. Compare Kollár and Mori, Theorem 5.50.

So for each place $E$ that contains $T$, $-1 < \operatorname{disc}_E(dx, \varphi + [f]) + \operatorname{ord}_E f = \operatorname{disc}_E(dx, \varphi)$. Also $dx \neq 0$, because $x$ is a transcendence basis, and the coefficient of $0$ in $\varphi$ is $0$, because it is so in $\varphi + [f]$ and $f \neq 0$. So $(dx, \varphi)$ is klt over $T$. $\square$

The second form puts the hypothesis on a hypersurface $\{f = 0\}$ as a klt condition on its own local ring. Here $T$ lies in a field $L$ and the hypersurface ring $\psi(T)$ lies in a field $K$.

**Theorem 10.6.7 (Klt from the restriction to a hypersurface).** Assume principalisation. Let $T \subseteq L$ be an integrally closed local model and let $\psi : T \to K$ be a ring map over $\mathbb{C}$ whose kernel is the principal ideal $(f)$ with $f \neq 0$, such that $\psi(T)$ is a local model of $K$. Let $w = (w_1, \dots, w_n)$ in $T$ with $\psi(w)$ a transcendence basis of $K$, and let $\theta$ be a finite formal sum of elements of $T$ with $\psi(g) \neq 0$ for each $g$ in its support. Suppose that $(d(f, w), \theta)$ is effective at $T$, and that $(d\psi(w), \psi_*\theta)$ is klt over $\psi(T)$. Then $(d(f, w), \theta)$ is klt over $T$.

Here $d(f, w) = df \wedge dw_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dw_n$, and $\psi_*\theta = \sum_g \theta_g [\psi(g)]$. This is the classical statement that if $(H, \Delta|_H)$ is klt for a Cartier divisor $H$ not in the support of $\Delta$, then $(X, \Delta)$ is klt near $H$ (Kollár and Mori, Theorem 5.50 and its corollaries).

*Proof.* We use three facts, of which we read only the statements.

- (a) *The place of the hypersurface.* There is a prime $S$ of $T$ and a surjective ring map $\psi_S : \mathcal{O}_S \to K$ that extends $\psi$, with $f$ irreducible in $\mathcal{O}_S$. Since $T$ is normal, the prime ideal $(f)$ has a discrete valuation ring as its localisation, and $\psi_S$ is the residue map onto the fraction field of $\psi(T)$.
- (b) *Lifting a transcendence basis.* Then $(f, w)$ is a transcendence basis of $L$, $\operatorname{ord}_S(d(f,w)) = 0$, $\operatorname{ord}_S f = 1$, and $\operatorname{ord}_S g = 0$ for each $g$ with $\psi(g) \neq 0$.
- (c) *Adjunction from the residue field.* If $(d\psi(w), \psi_*\theta)$ is klt over a ring $R \subseteq K$ whose elements are images of elements of $T$, then $(d(f,w), \theta + [f])$ is klt along $S$ over $T$.

Apply (c) with $R = \psi(T)$. So $(d(f,w), \theta + [f])$ is klt along $S$ over $T$. By (b), $\operatorname{disc}_S(d(f,w), \theta) = 0 - \sum_g \theta_g \operatorname{ord}_S g = 0$, since each $g$ in the support of $\theta$ has $\psi(g) \neq 0$. Now the theorem on inversion of adjunction over a local model, with the transcendence basis $(f, w)$, the prime $S$ and the hypothesis that $(d(f,w), \theta)$ is effective at $T$, gives that $(d(f,w), \theta)$ is klt over $T$. $\square$

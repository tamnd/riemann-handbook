## The level lattice

The comparison of the Galois row compares étale cohomology with a crystalline cohomology of finite level. At level $k$ the coefficients are a subring $A_k$ of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+/t^k$ with divided powers on an ideal $I_k$. This section proves that each $A_k$ has the properties of a lattice base: its divided power filtration is finite, its quotients have no $\ell$-torsion, each step is finitely generated and $\ell$-adically closed, the ideal $I_k$ is prime with a quotient of valuation type, and $A_k$ is $\ell$-adically complete. The result is used in the proof that [each level of the lattice system is good](/entry/lev-good/), and through it in [the existence of the level system](/entry/lev-sys-ex/).

Throughout, $\ell$ is a prime, $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ is the completion of an algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, and $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ is its ring of integers. The ring $A_{\inf} = W(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat)$ is the ring of [[Witt vectors|6N3X2QX4W9]] of the tilt. Let $\ell^\flat \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat$ be a fixed compatible system of $\ell$-power roots of $\ell$, let $[\cdot]$ be the Teichmüller lift, and put $\xi = \ell - [\ell^\flat] \in A_{\inf}$. The ring $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and its uniformizer $t$ are as in [the Background on period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings). We use two facts of Fontaine: the map $\theta : A_{\inf} \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ is surjective with kernel $\xi A_{\inf}$, and $\xi B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ = t B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$.

**Definition 1.1.1 (Divided powers).** Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $J \subseteq R$ an ideal. A *divided power structure* on $J$ is a family of maps $\gamma_n : J \to R$, $n \geq 0$, with $\gamma_0(x) = 1$, $\gamma_1(x) = x$, $\gamma_n(x) \in J$ for $n \geq 1$, and the laws
$$\begin{aligned} \gamma_n(x + y) &= \textstyle\sum_{i + j = n} \gamma_i(x) \gamma_j(y), & \gamma_n(ax) &= a^n \gamma_n(x), \\ \gamma_m(x) \gamma_n(x) &= \tbinom{m+n}{m} \gamma_{m+n}(x), & \gamma_m(\gamma_n(x)) &= \tfrac{(mn)!}{m! (n!)^m} \gamma_{mn}(x) \end{aligned}$$
for $x, y \in J$, $a \in R$ and $n \geq 1$ in the last law. If $R$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, then $\gamma_n(x) = x^n / n!$ is the unique divided power structure on any ideal.

The laws make $\gamma_n(x)$ behave like $x^n / n!$ when $n!$ is not invertible in $R$.

**Definition 1.1.2 (Divided power filtration).** Let $\gamma$ be a divided power structure on $J \subseteq R$. For $r \geq 0$ let $P^r_\gamma \subseteq R$ be the ideal generated by all products
$$\gamma_{n_1}(x_1) \gamma_{n_2}(x_2) \cdots \gamma_{n_m}(x_m), \qquad x_i \in J, \quad n_1 + \dots + n_m \geq r.$$
This is the *divided power filtration*. If $k \leq s$, then $P^s_\gamma \subseteq P^k_\gamma$, since each generator of $P^s_\gamma$ is a generator of $P^k_\gamma$.

**Definition 1.1.3 (The ring at level $k$).** Let $k \geq 1$ and $M \geq 0$. Put $B_k = B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ / t^k$, and let $f_k : A_{\inf} \to B_k$ be the natural map to $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ followed by the quotient map. The ring $B_k$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra. Put $\eta = \ell^{-M} f_k(\xi)$. The ring $A_k = A_k(M)$ is the subring of $B_k$ generated by $f_k(A_{\inf})$ and by the elements $\eta^i / i!$ for $i \geq 0$. Let $I_B = f_k(\xi) B_k$, let $\gamma^B$ be the divided powers $x \mapsto x^n/n!$ on $I_B$, and let $I_k = I_B \cap A_k$.

We say that $A_k$ is *closed under divided powers* if $\gamma^B_n(x) \in A_k$ for all $x \in I_k$ and $n \geq 0$. Then $\gamma^B$ restricts to a divided power structure $\gamma_k$ on the ideal $I_k$ of $A_k$. The parameter $M$ divides the generator $\xi$ by $\ell^M$ before its divided powers are taken. Each element of $A_k$ is a finite sum $\sum_{i < k} f_k(c_i) \, \eta^i / i!$ with $c_i \in A_{\inf}$, since $\eta^i = 0$ for $i \geq k$ and the products of the generators are again of this form.

**Definition 1.1.4 (Level hypothesis).** Let $K$ be a field with $\mathbb{Q}_\ell \subseteq K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, and fix $M$. The *level hypothesis* for $(K, M)$ asks, for all $k$:

1. the image of $\mathcal{O}_K$ in $B_k$ lies in $A_k$;
2. the image of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ in $B_k$ lies in $A_k$;
3. $A_k$ is closed under divided powers;
4. each element of $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell / K)$ preserves $t^k B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, and the induced map of $B_k$ preserves $A_k$;
5. the map $B_{k+1} \to B_k$ sends $A_{k+1}$ into $A_k$.

Under the level hypothesis the rings $A_k$ form an inverse system of coefficient rings with divided powers and with an action of the Galois group of $K$. Condition 3 gives the divided power structure $\gamma_k$ on $I_k$ for each $k$.

**Definition 1.1.5 (Lattice base).** Let $R$ be a ring, $J \subseteq R$ an ideal with a divided power structure $\gamma$, $\ell$ a prime and $s \geq 1$. The triple $(R, J, \gamma)$ is a *lattice base of length $s$* if:

1. $P^s_\gamma = 0$;
2. for all $r$, if $\ell x \in P^r_\gamma$ then $x \in P^r_\gamma$;
3. for each integer $n \neq 0$ there are $m \geq 0$ and $u \in R$ with $n u = \ell^m$;
4. each $P^r_\gamma$ is a finitely generated ideal;
5. $J$ is a prime ideal;
6. for all $x, y \in R$, either $x \in yR + J$ or $y \in xR + J$;
7. for each $x \notin J$ there is $m$ with $\ell^m \in xR + J$;
8. $R$ is $\ell$-[[adically complete|G9Q61B8A7C]];
9. each $P^r_\gamma$ is $\ell$-adically closed: if $x \in \ell^m R + P^r_\gamma$ for all $m$, then $x \in P^r_\gamma$.

Conditions 1 to 3 say that the filtration is finite and that its quotients have no $\ell$-torsion. Conditions 5 to 7 say that $R/J$ is a domain whose principal ideals are totally ordered and in which each nonzero element divides a power of $\ell$. These are the properties of a [[valuation ring|QSX12D8RQM]] of rank one with $\ell$ in its maximal ideal. Conditions 8 and 9 are the completeness that the later limit arguments use.

**Theorem 1.1.6 (The level lattice is a lattice base).** Let $K$ and $M$ satisfy the level hypothesis. Let $1 \leq k \leq s$. Then $(A_k, I_k, \gamma_k)$ is a lattice base of length $s$.

*Proof.* Fix $k \geq 1$ and write $x \mapsto \bar{x}$ for $f_k$. We first collect properties of $f_k$. Since $\xi$ and $t$ generate the same ideal of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, we have $\bar{\xi}^k = 0$. For $i < k$, if $\bar{a} \, \bar{\xi}^i \in \bar{\xi}^{i+1} B_k$, then $\theta(a) = 0$, so $\xi \mid a$ by Fontaine's theorem. The ring $A_{\inf}/\xi \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ is a domain, its principal ideals are totally ordered, each nonzero element divides a power of $\ell$, $\ell \ne 0$ in it, and it is $\ell$-adically complete. Each integer $n \neq 0$ is $\ell^m u'$ with $u'$ prime to $\ell$, and $u'$ is a unit of $A_{\inf}$.

The main lemma identifies the filtration: for each $r$,
$$P^r = A_k \cap \bar{\xi}^r B_k, \qquad P^r = \bigl( \eta^i / i! : r \leq i < k \bigr).$$
The inclusion $P^r \subseteq A_k \cap \bar{\xi}^r B_k$ holds because each $\gamma_n(x)$ with $x \in I_k$ is $x^n / n!$ with $x$ a multiple of $\bar{\xi}$. Conversely, write $x \in A_k \cap \bar{\xi}^r B_k$ as $\sum_{i < k} \bar{c}_i \eta^i / i!$. The property of $f_k$ above, applied for $i = 0, 1, \dots, r - 1$ in turn, lets us move a factor $\xi$ of $c_i$ into the next term, until $c_i = 0$ for $i < r$. So $x$ lies in the ideal generated by the $\eta^i / i!$ with $i \geq r$, and these lie in $P^r$. Now the nine conditions follow.

1. *Nilpotence.* $P^k = A_k \cap \bar{\xi}^k B_k = 0$, and $P^s \subseteq P^k$ since $k \leq s$.
2. *No $\ell$-torsion.* Since $B_k$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, $\ell x \in \bar{\xi}^r B_k$ gives $x \in \bar{\xi}^r B_k$. By the main lemma, $\ell x \in P^r$ gives $x \in P^r$.
3. *Integers.* Write $n u = \ell^m$ in $A_{\inf}$ and apply $f_k$.
4. *Finite generation.* By the main lemma, $P^r$ is generated by the finitely many $\eta^i / i!$ with $r \leq i < k$.
5. *Primality.* Each element of $A_k$ is congruent to some $\bar{a}$ modulo $I_B$, since $\eta^i / i! \in I_B$ for $i \geq 1$. And $\bar{a} \in I_B$ if and only if $\xi \mid a$. So $a \mapsto \bar{a}$ induces an isomorphism $A_{\inf}/\xi \cong A_k / I_k$, and $A_k/I_k \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ is a domain.
6. *Valuation conditions.* By the isomorphism of step 5, conditions 6 and 7 are the total order of divisibility in $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ and the fact that each nonzero element of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ divides a power of $\ell$.
7. *Completeness.* Let $F_i = A_k \cap \bar{\xi}^i B_k$. Then $F_k = 0$, and $c \mapsto \bar{c} \, \eta^i / i!$ induces an isomorphism $A_{\inf}/\xi \cong F_i / F_{i+1}$ for $i < k$, by the main lemma and the property of $f_k$. Each quotient is $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$, which is $\ell$-adically complete and has no $\ell$-torsion. An extension of an $\ell$-adically complete group by such a group is $\ell$-adically complete. Descending induction on $i$ gives that $F_0 = A_k$ is complete. The same induction, applied to the images of the $F_i$ in $A_k / P^r$, gives that each $P^r$ is $\ell$-adically closed.

The nine conditions hold, so $(A_k, I_k, \gamma_k)$ is a lattice base of length $s$. $\square$

**Remark 1.1.7 (Formalization).** Of the level hypothesis, the proof uses only condition 3 at level $k$ ([LevelLatticeBase.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/LevelLatticeBase.lean#L30)). Conditions 1, 2, 4 and 5 make the $A_k$ into a Galois-equivariant inverse system and are used elsewhere. The statement fixes the ring at level $k$ and lets the length $s$ be any $s \geq k$. We have checked the statements of the lemmas cited in the proof, and we have read the proofs of the main lemma, of steps 2 to 5 and of completeness. We have not unfolded the proof of the closedness in step 7, whose sketch above follows the structure of the completeness proof.

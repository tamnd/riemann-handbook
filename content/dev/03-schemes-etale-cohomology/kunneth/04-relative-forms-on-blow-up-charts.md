## Relative forms on blow-up charts

The proof that the Hodge-Tate comparison map is bijective reduces to a statement on the blow-up of an étale chart along a coordinate subspace. On the standard affine charts of the blow-up, consider the differential forms that vanish along the exceptional divisor. This section proves that these forms satisfy Čech descent: the Čech complex of the charts is exact and has the forms on the base as its kernel. The proof first treats a toric model, where everything is spanned by monomial forms, and then transfers the result by étale base change.

Let $K$ be a field. For a $K$-algebra $R$ let $\Omega^a_R = \bigwedge^a_R \Omega_{R/K}$ be the module of [[Kähler differential|7HCFARHG7D]] $a$-forms, an [[exterior power|0MDMZ965VE]] of $\Omega_{R/K}$. A map of $K$-algebras $R \to R'$ induces a map $\Omega^a_R \to \Omega^a_{R'}$.

**Definition 1.4.1 (Relative forms).** Let $R$ be a $K$-algebra and $I \subseteq R$ an ideal. The module of *relative $a$-forms* $\Omega^a_{R,I} \subseteq \Omega^a_R$ is the $R$-submodule spanned by the forms $g_0 \, dg_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dg_a$ in which $g_0 \in I$ or some $g_k \in I$.

For $I = (t)$ with $t$ a coordinate, $\Omega^a_{R,I}$ is spanned by $t \, \Omega^a_R$ and $dt \wedge \Omega^{a-1}_R$.

**Definition 1.4.2 (Chart rings).** Let $A$ be a $K$-algebra and $n, d \geq 0$. A *system of chart rings* of type $(n, d)$ on $A$ consists of:

1. elements $x_1, \dots, x_n, t_1, \dots, t_d$ of $A$ such that the map $K[x, t] \to A$ is [[étale|FTRW0679E2]];
2. for each subset $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, d\}$ a $K$-algebra $R_S$, with restriction maps $R_S \to R_T$ for $S \subseteq T$ that compose correctly, and $R_\emptyset \cong A$;
3. for $k \in S$, elements $u_{S,k,j} \in R_S$ with $t_j = t_k u_{S,k,j}$, compatible with restriction;
4. for each $k$, an isomorphism $R_{\{k\}} \cong A[u_1, \dots, u_d] / (u_k - 1, \; t_j - t_k u_j)$;
5. for $k \in S$, $R_S$ is the localization of $R_{\{k\}}$ away from $\prod_{j \in S} u_j$.

Let $I_S \subseteq R_S$ be the ideal generated by the $t_k$ with $k \in S$, and $I_\emptyset = (t_1, \dots, t_d) \subseteq A$.

Geometrically, $R_{\{k\}}$ is the coordinate ring of the chart of the blow-up of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ along $t_1 = \dots = t_d = 0$ on which $t_k$ generates the exceptional ideal, and $R_S$ is the intersection of the charts with index in $S$. For $S \neq \emptyset$ the ideal $I_S$ is principal, generated by any $t_k$ with $k \in S$: it is the ideal of the exceptional divisor.

**Definition 1.4.3 (Čech exactness of a system).** Let $(M_S)_{S \subseteq \{1, \dots, d\}}$ be a system of $K$-modules with restriction maps $M_S \to M_T$ for $S \subseteq T$. Its *Čech complex* has in degree $m$ the product of the $M_{\{i_0, \dots, i_m\}}$ over all ordered tuples $(i_0, \dots, i_m)$, with the alternating sum of restrictions as differential. The system is *Čech exact* if the augmented complex
$$0 \to M_\emptyset \to \prod_{i_0} M_{\{i_0\}} \to \prod_{i_0, i_1} M_{\{i_0, i_1\}} \to \cdots$$
is exact.

**Theorem 1.4.4 (Čech descent of relative forms).** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$, $d \geq 1$, $A$ a $K$-algebra with a system of chart rings of type $(n, d)$, and $a \geq 0$. Let $(P_S)$ be a system of $K$-modules with isomorphisms $P_S \cong \Omega^a_{R_S, I_S}$ that commute with restriction. Then $(P_S)$ is Čech exact.

The theorem says that a relative $a$-form on the blow-up, given chart by chart, comes from a unique relative form on $A$, and that the higher Čech cohomology of relative forms on the charts vanishes.

**Definition 1.4.5 (The toric model).** Let $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}^{n + d}$ with coordinates $g_{x_l}$ for $l \leq n$ and $g_{t_j}$ for $j \leq d$. Put $\deg_t(g) = \sum_j g_{t_j}$. For $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, d\}$ let
$$\sigma_S = \{ g \in \Gamma : g_{x_l} \geq 0 \text{ for all } l, \; g_{t_j} \geq 0 \text{ for } j \notin S, \; \deg_t(g) \geq 0 \}.$$
Let $R^0_S = K[\sigma_S]$ be its monoid algebra, $L = K[\Gamma]$ the Laurent polynomial ring, and $I^0_S$ the ideal of $R^0_S$ generated by the $t_k$ with $k \in S$, or by all $t_k$ if $S = \emptyset$.

So $R^0_\emptyset = K[x, t]$, and $R^0_{\{k\}} = K[x, t_k, t_j / t_k]$ is a chart of the blow-up of affine space along $t = 0$. For $S \ni k$, $R^0_S$ is $R^0_{\{k\}}$ with the $t_j / t_k$, $j \in S$, inverted. The model is the case $A = K[x, t]$ of a system of chart rings.

**Definition 1.4.6 (Monomial forms).** For $g \in \Gamma$ and a subset $D$ of the coordinates with $|D| = a$, put
$$\omega_{g, D} = z^g \bigwedge_{e \in D} \frac{de}{e} \in \Omega^a_L, \qquad z^g = \prod_l x_l^{g_{x_l}} \prod_j t_j^{g_{t_j}},$$
with the wedge taken in a fixed order. Call $(g, D)$ *good* if $g_{x_l} \geq 0$ for all $l$, $x_l \notin D$ whenever $g_{x_l} = 0$, and $\deg_t(g) \geq 1$. Let $T(g, D)$ be the set of $j$ with $g_{t_j} < 0$, or with $g_{t_j} = 0$ and $t_j \in D$.

**Lemma 1.4.7 (Monomial forms).** Let $a \geq 0$.

1. The forms $\omega_{g, D}$ with $g \in \Gamma$ and $|D| = a$ are a $K$-basis of $\Omega^a_L$.
2. For each $S$, the map $\Omega^a_{R^0_S} \to \Omega^a_L$ is injective.
3. Its image on $\Omega^a_{R^0_S, I^0_S}$ is the span of the $\omega_{g, D}$ with $(g, D)$ good and $T(g, D) \subseteq S$.
4. If $(g, D)$ is good, then $T(g, D)$ is not the whole set $\{1, \dots, d\}$.

*Proof of 4.* If $T(g, D)$ is everything, then $g_{t_j} \leq 0$ for all $j$, so $\deg_t(g) \leq 0$. A good pair has $\deg_t(g) \geq 1$. $\square$

Parts 1 to 3 are statements in other files of the folder. Part 1 holds because $\Omega^1_L$ is free on the $de/e$. Part 3 says when a monomial form is regular on the chart and vanishes along the exceptional divisor.

**Lemma 1.4.8 (Exactness from a basis).** Let $W$ be a $K$-module with a basis $(b_\lambda)$, and let each $\lambda$ have a property "good" and a set $T_\lambda \subseteq \{1, \dots, d\}$ with $T_\lambda \neq \{1, \dots, d\}$ for good $\lambda$. Let $(M_S)$ be a system with compatible injective maps $M_S \to W$ whose image is the span of the $b_\lambda$ with $\lambda$ good and $T_\lambda \subseteq S$. Then $(M_S)$ is Čech exact.

This is again a statement of the folder. The reason is that the system splits as a direct sum over $\lambda$ of the systems $S \mapsto K$ if $T_\lambda \subseteq S$, and $0$ otherwise. The Čech complex of one summand is the augmented cochain complex of the full simplex on $\{1, \dots, d\}$ relative to the faces that miss a point of $T_\lambda$. When $T_\lambda$ is not the whole set, this relative complex is acyclic. This explanation is ours; we have not read the Lean proof of the lemma.

**Proposition 1.4.9 (The toric model).** For every $a \geq 0$, the system $S \mapsto \Omega^a_{R^0_S, I^0_S}$ is Čech exact.

*Proof.* Apply the lemma on exactness from a basis to $W = \Omega^a_L$ with the basis of monomial forms, the good pairs and the sets $T(g, D)$. The maps $\Omega^a_{R^0_S, I^0_S} \to \Omega^a_L$ are injective and compatible with restriction, and their images are as required, by the lemma on monomial forms. Part 4 of that lemma gives $T(g, D) \neq \{1, \dots, d\}$ for good pairs. $\square$

*Proof of the theorem.*

1. *Comparison with the model.* There are maps $\theta_S : R^0_S \to R_S$, natural in $S$, that send each monomial in $x$, $t$ and $t_j / t_k$ to the corresponding element of $R_S$, and each $\theta_S$ identifies $R_S$ with $A \otimes_{K[x,t]} R^0_S$. A lemma of the folder builds $\theta_S$ from conditions 4 and 5 of a chart system: first for $S = \emptyset$, then for one-element sets by the presentation of $R_{\{k\}}$, then for all $S$ by localization. A uniqueness lemma for maps out of the charts gives the naturality.
2. *Forms commute with the base change.* A lemma of the folder shows that the induced maps identify $\Omega^a_{R_S, I_S}$ with $A \otimes_{K[x,t]} \Omega^a_{R^0_S, I^0_S}$, compatibly with restriction.
3. *Flat base change.* An étale map is [[flat|73RYYEVDKA]]. Tensoring the exact augmented Čech complex of the model with $A$ over $K[x, t]$ keeps it exact. By step 2, the result is the Čech complex of $S \mapsto \Omega^a_{R_S, I_S}$.
4. *Transfer.* The isomorphisms $P_S \cong \Omega^a_{R_S, I_S}$ commute with restriction. So they carry the exactness to $(P_S)$. $\square$

**Remark 1.4.10.** The Lean statement asks for $d \geq 1$ and for $K$ of characteristic $0$. The proof uses neither. The hypothesis $0 < d$ is introduced and then not used, and none of the lemmas that the proof cites asks for characteristic $0$ ([ExistsTheta.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/ExistsTheta.lean#L95)).

**Remark 1.4.11.** The theorem is stated in Lean as a proposition `ChartFormsCechExact` that is used as a hypothesis by the Hodge-Tate part of the proof. The augmented Čech complex in that statement runs over all ordered tuples of indices, with repetitions.

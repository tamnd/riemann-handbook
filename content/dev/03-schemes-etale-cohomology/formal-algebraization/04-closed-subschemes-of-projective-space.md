## Closed subschemes of projective space

This section gives a second proof of the existence theorem for closed subschemes, in the case of projective space. It does not use the results of Sections 1 to 3. It works with homogeneous ideals of the polynomial ring, and it uses the completeness of the base ring directly on homogeneous polynomials. This is the case that the algebraization of formal schemes with an ample line bundle needs in Section 6: there the formal scheme is embedded in a projective space by sections of a power of the line bundle.

Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring, $I \subseteq R$ an ideal and $N \geq 0$. Let $S = R[x_0, \dots, x_N]$ with its grading by degree, so $\mathbb{P}^N_R = \operatorname{Proj} S$. For $e \geq 0$ let $\mathbb{P}_e = \mathbb{P}^N_R \times_R \operatorname{Spec} R/I^{e+1}$, with the closed immersions $\mathbb{P}_e \to \mathbb{P}_{e+1}$ and $\mathbb{P}_e \to \mathbb{P}^N_R$. An ideal of $S$ is *homogeneous* if it contains the homogeneous components of each of its elements.

**Definition 7.4.1 (Vanishing ideal).** Let $c : C \to \mathbb{P}^N_R$ be a morphism. A homogeneous polynomial $f \in S$ *vanishes on* $C$ if, for each $k$, the function $f / x_k^{\deg f}$ on the standard chart $D_+(x_k)$ pulls back to $0$ on $c^{-1}(D_+(x_k))$. The *vanishing ideal* $L(C)$ is the ideal of $S$ generated by the homogeneous polynomials that vanish on $C$. It is a homogeneous ideal.

**Theorem 7.4.2 (Existence for closed subschemes of projective space).** Let $R$ be Noetherian and $I$-adically complete, and let $N \geq 0$. Let $(C_e)_{e \geq 0}$ be schemes with closed immersions $i_e : C_e \to \mathbb{P}_e$ and morphisms $\beta_e : C_e \to C_{e+1}$ such that, for each $e$, the square formed by $\beta_e$, $i_e$, $i_{e+1}$ and $\mathbb{P}_e \to \mathbb{P}_{e+1}$ is a pullback square. Then there are a scheme $C'$, a closed immersion $j : C' \to \mathbb{P}^N_R$ and morphisms $b_e : C_e \to C'$ such that, for each $e$, the square formed by $b_e$, $i_e$, $j$ and $\mathbb{P}_e \to \mathbb{P}^N_R$ is a pullback square, and $b_{e+1} \circ \beta_e = b_e$.

The theorem has no properness hypothesis on $C_0$ and no properness claim on $C'$. Both hold automatically, since $\mathbb{P}^N_R$ is proper over $R$ and each closed subscheme of it is proper.

*Proof.* For each $e$ let $L_e = L(C_e)$, the vanishing ideal of the composite $C_e \to \mathbb{P}_e \to \mathbb{P}^N_R$. We write $I^{e+1}$ also for the ideal $I^{e+1} S$. We take the following four inputs as given.

1. *The system of vanishing ideals.* For each $e$:
   - $I^{e+1} \subseteq L_e$;
   - $L_e$ is saturated: if $f$ is homogeneous and for each $k$ there is $m$ with $x_k^m f \in L_e$, then $f \in L_e$;
   - $L_{e+1} \subseteq L_e$;
   - if $f \in L_e$, then for each $k$ there is $m$ with $x_k^m f \in L_{e+1} + I^{e+1}$.

   The last property is the algebraic form of the pullback square for $\beta_e$: the scheme $C_e$ is $C_{e+1} \cap \mathbb{P}_e$, so the two ideals agree up to saturation.
2. *Uniform lifting in large degree.* Let $(L_e)$ be homogeneous ideals with the four properties of the first input. Then there is $n_0$ such that, for each $e$ and each homogeneous $f \in L_e$ of degree $n \geq n_0$, there is a homogeneous $f' \in L_{e+1}$ of degree $n$ with $f - f' \in I^{e+1}$. The point is that $n_0$ does not depend on $e$. It comes from the Noetherian property of a graded ring built from the $L_e$ and the powers of $I$.
3. *Lifting along the adic filtration.* Assume that $R$ is $I$-adically complete, and let $(L_e)$ be homogeneous ideals with $I^{e+1} \subseteq L_e$ and $L_{e+1} \subseteq L_e$, and with the uniform lifting of the second input for some $n_0$. Then there is a homogeneous ideal $J \subseteq S$ such that, for each $e$, $J \subseteq L_e$, and for each $f \in L_e$ and each $k$ there is $m$ with $x_k^m f \in J + I^{e+1}$.
4. *From the ideal to the subscheme.* If $J$ is a homogeneous ideal with the two properties of the third input for the vanishing ideals of the $C_e$, then the closed subscheme $C' = V_+(J)$ with its closed immersion $j$ has morphisms $b_e$ with the pullback squares and the compatibility of the theorem.

The idea of the third input is this. A homogeneous $f \in L_e$ of degree $n \geq n_0$ is lifted step by step to $L_{e+1}, L_{e+2}, \dots$, with corrections in $I^{e+1}, I^{e+2}, \dots$. The homogeneous part of degree $n$ of $S$ is a finite free $R$-module, so the lifts converge in it by completeness. The limit lies in $L_{e'}$ for each $e'$, since each $L_{e'}$ contains $I^{e'+1}$ and the part of degree $n$ of $L_{e'}$ is closed. The ideal $J$ collects these limits.

Now the proof is a chain of the inputs. The first input gives the four properties of the ideals $L_e$. The second input, applied to the $L_e$, gives $n_0$. The third input, applied with the completeness of $R$, the first and third properties and $n_0$, gives the homogeneous ideal $J$. The fourth input gives $C'$, $j$ and the maps $b_e$. $\square$

The saturation in the first input is needed because a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^N_R$ determines its homogeneous ideal only up to saturation. The condition $x_k^m f \in J + I^{e+1}$ says that $J + I^{e+1}$ and $L_e$ have the same saturation. So $V_+(J) \cap \mathbb{P}_e = V_+(L_e) = C_e$, which is the pullback square of the theorem.

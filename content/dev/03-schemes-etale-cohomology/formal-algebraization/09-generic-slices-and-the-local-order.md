## Generic slices and the local order of a hyperplane section

Let $J$ be a prime ideal of $\mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ of dimension $d+1$, and let $W$ be a minimal prime of $J + (\ell_u)$, a component of the hyperplane section. This section proves that the multiplicity of $J + (\ell_u)$ at $W$ is the sum of the orders of vanishing of $\ell_u$ along the branches of $Z(J)$ at a point of $Z(W)$. We cut $Z(J)$ by a generic affine subspace of codimension $d$ through a generic point $q$ of $Z(W)$. The slice is a curve, it is reduced at $q$, and its multiplicity at $q$ is the multiplicity at $W$. The curve case of Section 8 then gives the formula. This formula is an input of the Poincaré and Lelong theorems of the [literature step](/step/every-character-of-literature/).

We keep the notation of Sections 7 and 8. For a $d \times n$ complex matrix $L$ and $q \in \mathbb{C}^n$, the *slice ideal* is
$$J_{L,q} = J + \big(L_1(x) - L_1(q), \dots, L_d(x) - L_d(q)\big),$$
where $L_i(x) = \sum_j L_{ij} x_j$. Its zero set is $Z(J) \cap \{x : Lx = Lq\}$, the part of $Z(J)$ in the affine subspace through $q$ with direction $\ker L$. We identify the $d \times n$ matrices with $\mathbb{C}^{dn}$, with its usual topology.

**Theorem 7.9.1 (Slices are reduced away from the centre).** Let $J \subseteq \mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be a radical ideal, $d \geq 0$ and $q \in \mathbb{C}^n$. Then there is an open dense set $O$ of $d \times n$ matrices with the following property. For $L \in O$, each prime $P \supseteq J_{L,q}$ with $P \neq \mathfrak{m}_q$, and each $g$ in the radical of $J_{L,q}$, there is $s \notin P$ with $s g \in J_{L,q}$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Heights.BranchOrder.sliceReducedAway`.

So for generic $L$ the slice ideal is radical after localization at each prime other than $\mathfrak{m}_q$. This is a Bertini theorem for the reducedness of the fibres of a family of slices.

*Proof.* We first prove a Zariski form: there is a nonzero polynomial $F$ in the $dn$ entries of the matrix such that each $L$ with $F(L) \neq 0$ has the property of the theorem. Consider the universal slice: the ideal $\mathcal{J}$ of $\mathbb{C}[\sigma][x]$ generated by $J$ and the polynomials $\sum_j \sigma_{ij}(x_j - q_j)$, where the $\sigma_{ij}$ are variables. Its fibre at a matrix $L$ is $J_{L,q}$. We take two inputs as given. First, for each $k$, the universal slice is radical away from $x_k - q_k$, since away from $q$ the coordinate $x_k - q_k$ can be solved for one of the $\sigma$. Second, a generic fibre theorem: if an ideal over $\mathbb{C}[\sigma]$ is radical away from $g$, then there is $F \neq 0$ in $\mathbb{C}[\sigma]$ such that the fibre at each $t$ with $F(t) \neq 0$ is radical away from $g$. Let $F_k$ be the polynomial for $g = x_k - q_k$ and $F = \prod_k F_k$. If $P \neq \mathfrak{m}_q$ is a prime that contains $J_{L,q}$, then some $x_k - q_k \notin P$, and the radical property away from $x_k - q_k$ gives $m$ with $(x_k - q_k)^m g \in J_{L,q}$. Take $s = (x_k - q_k)^m$.

For the theorem, let $O = \{L : F(L) \neq 0\}$. It is open, since $F$ is continuous. It is dense, since the zero set of a nonzero polynomial on $\mathbb{C}^{dn}$ has empty interior. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9.2 (Slices are reduced at a generic point).** Let $J$ be a prime ideal with $\dim \mathbb{C}[x]/J = d+1$, let $u \in \mathbb{C}^n$ with $\ell_u \notin J$, and let $W$ be a minimal prime of $J + (\ell_u)$. Then there is a polynomial $f \notin W$ such that for each $q \in Z(W)$ with $f(q) \neq 0$ there is an open dense set $O$ of $d \times n$ matrices with $J_{L,q}$ radical at $q$ for each $L \in O$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Heights.BranchOrder.sliceReducedGeneral`.

*Proof.* We take as given an unmixedness statement for slices. Under the hypotheses of the theorem there is $f \notin W$ such that for each $q \in Z(W)$ with $f(q) \neq 0$ there is an open dense $O_1$ with this property: for $L \in O_1$, each associated prime $P$ of $\mathbb{C}[x]/J_{L,q}$ with $P \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_q$ is a minimal prime of $J_{L,q}$ and is not $\mathfrak{m}_q$. It comes from the Cohen and Macaulay property of $\mathbb{C}[x]/J$ at a generic point of $Z(W)$ and the dimension count of the slice.

Take this $f$ and let $q \in Z(W)$ with $f(q) \neq 0$. Take $O_1$ as above, and $O_2$ from the theorem on slices away from the centre, applied to the prime, hence radical, ideal $J$. Let $O = O_1 \cap O_2$. The intersection of two open dense sets is open and dense.

Let $L \in O$, write $K = J_{L,q}$, and let $g$ be in the radical of $K$. Let $(K : g) = \{s : s g \in K\}$. If $(K : g) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}_q$, there is $s$ with $s(q) \neq 0$ and $s g \in K$, as required. Otherwise, $(K : g) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_q$, so there is a prime $P$ with $(K : g) \subseteq P \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_q$ that is minimal over $(K : g)$. The ideal $(K : g)$ is the annihilator of the class of $g$ in $\mathbb{C}[x]/K$, and a minimal prime over the annihilator of an element is an associated prime. So $P$ is an associated prime of $\mathbb{C}[x]/K$ inside $\mathfrak{m}_q$. Since $L \in O_1$, $P$ is a minimal prime of $K$ and $P \neq \mathfrak{m}_q$. Since $L \in O_2$, there is $s \notin P$ with $s g \in K$. Then $s \in (K : g) \subseteq P$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9.3 (Generic slices keep the multiplicity).** Under the hypotheses of the last theorem there is an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ with $Z(W) \subseteq \overline{Z(W) \cap U}$ such that, for each $q \in Z(W) \cap U$, the set of matrices $L$ with
$$J_{L,q} \text{ radical at } q \quad \text{and} \quad \operatorname{mult}_W\big(J + (\ell_u)\big) = \operatorname{mult}_{\mathfrak{m}_q}\big(J_{L,q} + (\ell_u)\big)$$
is dense.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Heights.BranchOrder.sliceLength`.

*Proof.* We take two inputs as given. The first is the transversal slice theorem for multiplicities: under the same hypotheses there is $f_2 \notin W$ such that for each $q \in Z(W)$ with $f_2(q) \neq 0$ there is an open dense $O'$ with $\operatorname{mult}_W(J + (\ell_u)) = \operatorname{mult}_{\mathfrak{m}_q}(J_{L,q} + (\ell_u))$ for $L \in O'$. The second is a vanishing statement: if $W$ is prime, $x \in Z(W)$, and a polynomial $h$ vanishes on $Z(W)$ near $x$, then $h \in W$.

Let $f_1$ be the polynomial of the last theorem and $U = \{x : f_1(x) f_2(x) \neq 0\}$. It is open. Let $x \in Z(W)$, and suppose that some neighbourhood of $x$ does not meet $Z(W) \cap U$. Then $f_1 f_2$ vanishes on $Z(W)$ near $x$, so $f_1 f_2 \in W$ by the second input. Since $W$ is prime, $f_1 \in W$ or $f_2 \in W$, which is false. So $x \in \overline{Z(W) \cap U}$.

Let $q \in Z(W) \cap U$. The last theorem gives an open dense $O$ and the first input gives an open dense $O'$. Their intersection is open and dense, so dense, and each $L$ in it has both properties. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9.4 (Local order of a hyperplane section).** Let $J$ be a prime ideal with $\dim \mathbb{C}[x]/J = d+1$, let $u \in \mathbb{C}^n$ with $\ell_u \notin J$, let $W$ be a minimal prime of $J + (\ell_u)$ and $p \in Z(W)$. Let $\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_m$ be branches of $J$ of dimension $d+1$ at $p$, and $o_1, \dots, o_m \geq 0$. Assume:

1. there is a neighbourhood $N$ of $p$ with $Z(J) \cap N$ contained in the union of the images of the $\gamma_b$;
2. there is a neighbourhood $N$ of $p$ with $Z(W) \cap N$ contained in the slice of each $\gamma_b$;
3. for $b \neq b'$, the images of $\gamma_b$ and $\gamma_{b'}$ meet only in $\{\ell_u = 0\}$;
4. for each $b$, $\ell_u \circ \gamma_b$ vanishes to order $o_b$ on the ball of $\gamma_b$.

Then
$$\operatorname{mult}_W\big(J + (\ell_u)\big) = \sum_{b=1}^m o_b.$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Heights.BranchOrder.localOrder`.

*Proof.* We take as given a statement on slices of branches. Under the hypotheses of the theorem, let $U$ be open with $Z(W) \subseteq \overline{Z(W) \cap U}$, and for each $q \in Z(W) \cap U$ let $\Lambda_q$ be a dense set of matrices. Then there are $q \in Z(W) \cap U$, $L \in \Lambda_q$ and curve branches $c_1, \dots, c_m$ of $J_{L,q}$ at $q$ such that $Z(J_{L,q})$ near $q$ lies in the union of their images, the images of two different $c_b$ meet only in $\{\ell_u = 0\}$, and $\ell_u \circ c_b$ vanishes to order $o_b$ for each $b$. The curve $c_b$ is the part of $\gamma_b$ in the affine subspace through $q$.

Take $U$ and $\Lambda_q$ from the last theorem, and then $q$, $L$ and $c_1, \dots, c_m$ from this input. Since $L \in \Lambda_q$, the ideal $J_{L,q}$ is radical at $q$, and $\operatorname{mult}_W(J + (\ell_u)) = \operatorname{mult}_{\mathfrak{m}_q}(J_{L,q} + (\ell_u))$. [Theorem 7.8.4](/entry/curve-branch-length/) on the multiplicity on a curve, applied to $J_{L,q}$, $q$ and the $c_b$, gives $\operatorname{mult}_{\mathfrak{m}_q}(J_{L,q} + (\ell_u)) = \sum_b o_b$. $\square$

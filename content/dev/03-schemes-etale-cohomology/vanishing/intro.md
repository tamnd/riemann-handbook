This chapter proves three results that the main proof uses as inputs from the literature. The first is the vanishing theorem of Kawamata and Viehweg and its form for multiplier ideals, Nadel vanishing. The proof follows Deligne and Illusie. A smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{F}_p$ of dimension less than $p$ that lifts to $\mathbb{Z}/p^2$, together with a divisor with simple normal crossings, has a decomposition of its twisted logarithmic de Rham complex. A descent along the Frobenius then gives vanishing in characteristic $p$. Reduction modulo $p$ carries the vanishing to characteristic $0$, first for a boundary given by sections and then for an effective [[Cartier divisor|FHV05ZK99F]] with simple normal crossings. On a log resolution this gives Nadel vanishing and local vanishing for [[multiplier ideals|BKM0TQSCD9]] twisted by a [[nef|RZ66X0NAM5]] and big class. The second result is the Casselman-Wallach theorem for the unitary groups $U(p, q)$. A maximum principle for $K$-finite vectors, with weights from the $K$-Casimir operator, gives the a priori bound. With this bound, a map of Harish-Chandra modules extends to a $G$-map of smooth representations of moderate growth with closed image. The third result is Tate's global Euler characteristic formula for finite Galois modules unramified outside a finite set of places, which the chapter reduces to a coprime case. The chapter needs from Volume 1 [Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing](/learn/positivity-resolution/#kodaira-and-kawamata-viehweg-vanishing) and [multiplier ideals](/learn/positivity-resolution/#multiplier-ideals-and-nadel-vanishing), [continuous cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/#continuous-cohomology), and [Harish-Chandra modules](/learn/real-groups/#admissible-representations-and-mathfrakg-k-modules).

The vanishing theorems and the Casselman-Wallach theorem reach the step [EveryCharacter from the literature and VWin](/step/every-character-of-literature/), where they are inputs on the side of positivity and on the side of automorphic forms. The global Euler characteristic formula reaches the step [EveryCharacter from Row0b](/step/every-character-of-row/). So these results are proved here, and the main proof does not take them from the literature. Sections 10 to 15 finish the proof of the Euler characteristic formula and then prove three inputs of the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/): the bound of Jacquet and Shalika, the pole of the Rankin-Selberg $L$-function, and the de Rham property of the $l$-adic characters of algebraic Hecke characters.

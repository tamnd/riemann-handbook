## Frobenius lifts and logarithmic forms

The chapter proves Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing by the method of Deligne and Illusie: if a variety in characteristic $p$ lifts to $\mathbb{Z}/p^2$, then its de Rham complex splits after the Frobenius. This section proves the splitting in the form that the later sections use. It is a statement about the Čech cohomology of logarithmic forms on an open cover, and it holds when the open sets of the cover carry compatible Frobenius lifts. Section 2 builds such a cover from a lift of $X$ to $\mathbb{Z}/p^2$, and Section 3 turns the splitting into a vanishing theorem.

Throughout, $p$ is a prime and $X$ is an integral scheme whose function field $K$ has characteristic $p$. All forms live in one place: the exterior algebra $\Lambda^\bullet_K \Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}}$ of the module of Kähler differentials of $K$. Since $p = 0$ in $K$, $\Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}} = \Omega_{K/\mathbb{F}_p}$. We write $d$ for the exterior derivative of the de Rham complex of $K$ (see [algebraic de Rham cohomology](/learn/geometry/#de-rham-cohomology-and-deformations)) and $\operatorname{dlog} g = g^{-1}\,dg$ for $g \in K^\times$. A sheaf of forms on $X$ is then a family of subgroups of this one algebra, in the same way as the sheaf of a [Cartier divisor](/learn/geometry/#line-bundles-and-divisors) is a family of subgroups of $K$.

**Definition 9.1.1 (Logarithmic forms).** Let $Z \subseteq X$ be a subset and $y \in X$. A unit $g \in K^\times$ is a *log unit at $y$* if $g$ is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_{X,z}$ for each generization $z$ of $y$ with $z \notin Z$. Let $G$ be a Cartier divisor on $X$ and $a \geq 0$. Let $\Omega^a_Z(G)_y$ be the subgroup of $\Lambda^a_K \Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}}$ generated by the forms
$$c \cdot \operatorname{dlog} g_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \operatorname{dlog} g_a,$$
with $c$ in the stalk of $\mathcal{O}_X(G)$ at $y$ and $g_1, \dots, g_a$ log units at $y$. For an open set $U$, put $\Omega^a_Z(G)(U) = \bigcap_{y \in U} \Omega^a_Z(G)_y$.

So a log unit can have zeros and poles, but only along $Z$. When $Z$ is a divisor with simple normal crossings on a smooth variety, $\Omega^a_Z(0)$ is the sheaf $\Omega^a_X(\log Z)$ of forms with logarithmic poles along $Z$, and $\Omega^a_Z(G) = \Omega^a_X(\log Z) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(G)$.

**Example 9.1.2.** Let $X = \mathbb{A}^1$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with coordinate $x$, $Z = \{0\}$ and $y = 0$. The log units at $0$ are the elements $u x^m$ with $u$ a unit at $0$ and $m \in \mathbb{Z}$. So $dx/x = \operatorname{dlog} x$ is in $\Omega^1_Z(0)_0$, and $dx/x^2$ is not.

Next we fix the language of Čech cohomology. Let $\iota$ be a totally ordered set, $E$ an abelian group, and for each finite nonempty $s \subseteq \iota$ let $N(s) \subseteq E$ be a subgroup. A *Čech $b$-cochain* is a function $c$ on the finite subsets of $\iota$ with $c(\emptyset) = 0$, $c(s) \in N(s)$ for all $s$, and $c(s) = 0$ unless $s$ has $b + 1$ elements. Its differential is
$$(\delta c)(t) = \sum_{i \in t} \varepsilon(t, i)\, c(t \setminus \{i\}),$$
where $\varepsilon(t, i) = \pm 1$ is the sign of the position of $i$ in $t$. This is the alternating Čech complex of a cover with index set $\iota$, where $N(s)$ is the group of sections on the intersection $U_s$ of the sets $U_i$, $i \in s$.

**Definition 9.1.3 (Čech and hypercohomology vanishing).** We write $\check H^b(N) = 0$ if each Čech $b$-cochain $c$ with $\delta c = 0$ is $\delta y$ for a Čech $(b-1)$-cochain $y$. Now let $T^q(s) \subseteq E$ be subgroups for $q \geq 0$, and $d : E \to E$ a homomorphism. A *total $n$-cochain* is a function $c$ with $c(\emptyset) = 0$, $c(s) \in T^q(s)$ for $q = n + 1 - |s|$, and $c(s) = 0$ for $|s| > n + 1$. Its differential is $(Dc)(t) = (\delta c)(t) + (-1)^{|t|} d(c(t))$. We write $\mathbb{H}^n(T^\bullet) = 0$ if each total $n$-cochain $c$ with $Dc = 0$ is $Dy$ for a total $(n-1)$-cochain $y$.

For $b = 0$ the definition says that a Čech $0$-cocycle is zero, so $\check H^0(N) = 0$ means that $N$ has no nonzero global sections. When $T^q(s)$ is the group of $q$-forms on $U_s$, the total complex is the Čech-de Rham double complex, and $\mathbb{H}^n$ is its cohomology in degree $n$.

The lift to $\mathbb{Z}/p^2$ enters through the following data.

**Definition 9.1.4 (Coordinates).** A family $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ in $K^\times$ is a *system of coordinates* if $dx_1, \dots, dx_n$ is a basis of $\Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}}$ over $K$.

**Definition 9.1.5 (Lift of the log structure).** Let $Z \subseteq X$, let $(U_i)_{i \in \iota}$ be open sets, and let $x_i$ be a system of coordinates for each $i$. A *Frobenius lift* of these data is a ring $\tilde K$ with a surjection $\pi : \tilde K \to K$ and ring endomorphisms $F_i$ of $\tilde K$, with these properties.

1. $\tilde K$ is a flat lift of $K$: $\pi(z) = 0$ if and only if $z \in p\tilde K$, and $pw = 0$ if and only if $\pi(w) = 0$.
2. Each $F_i$ lifts the Frobenius: $\pi(F_i z) = \pi(z)^p$.
3. Each coordinate $x_{i,l}$ has a lift $z$ with $F_i(z) = z^p$.
4. Let $s \subseteq \iota$ be finite, $y \in \bigcap_{i \in s} U_i$ and $g$ a log unit at $y$. Then $g$ has a lift $z$ such that for each $i \in s$, $F_i(z) = z^p(1 + p w_i)$ with $\pi(w_i) \in \mathcal{O}_{X,y}$.

Conditions 1 and 2 say that $\tilde K$ is like the function field of a lift of $X$ to $\mathbb{Z}/p^2$ and $F_i$ is a lift of the Frobenius on the open set $U_i$. Condition 4 says that the lifts respect the log structure and the integral structure at each point.

**Definition 9.1.6 (Cartier map).** Let $x$ be a system of coordinates. A *Cartier map for $x$* is a ring endomorphism $\gamma$ of $\Lambda^\bullet_K \Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}}$ with $\gamma(c) = c^p$ for $c \in K$ and $\gamma(\operatorname{dlog} x_l) = \operatorname{dlog} x_l$ for all $l$. Let $N^a \subseteq T^a$ be subgroups for $a \geq 0$ with $d T^a \subseteq T^{a+1}$. We say that $\gamma$ *induces an isomorphism* $N^a \to \mathcal{H}^a(T^\bullet, d)$ if, for each $a$, $\gamma$ maps $N^a$ into the closed forms of $T^a$, the induced map from $N^a$ to $\ker d / d T^{a-1}$ is injective, and it is onto.

This is the inverse Cartier isomorphism $C^{-1} : \Omega^a_X(\log Z) \to \mathcal{H}^a(F_* \Omega^\bullet_X(\log Z))$, which sends $c \operatorname{dlog} g_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \operatorname{dlog} g_a$ to the class of $c^p \operatorname{dlog} g_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \operatorname{dlog} g_a$ (Esnault and Viehweg, *Lectures on Vanishing Theorems*, DMV Seminar 20, 1992). In the theorem below, $N^a$ has a twist $G$ and $T^a$ has a twist $H \geq pG$.

**Theorem 9.1.7 (Deligne-Illusie splitting on a cover).** Let $Z \subseteq X$ and let $(U_i)_{i \in \iota}$ be open sets, with $\iota$ totally ordered and each $U_i$ containing the generic point of $X$. For each $i$ let $x_i$ be a system of $n$ coordinates, with $n < p$. Let $(W_s)$ be open sets with $W_s = \bigcap_{i \in s} U_i$ for $s$ nonempty. Let $G$ and $H$ be Cartier divisors with $H - pG$ effective. Put $N^a(s) = \Omega^a_Z(G)(W_s)$ and $T^a(s) = \Omega^a_Z(H)(W_s)$. Assume:

1. the data $Z$, $(U_i)$, $(x_i)$ have a Frobenius lift;
2. $d$ maps $T^q(s)$ into $T^{q+1}(s)$ for all $q$ and $s$;
3. for each finite $s$, each $j \in s$ and each Cartier map $\gamma$ for $x_j$, $\gamma$ induces an isomorphism $N^\bullet(s) \to \mathcal{H}^\bullet(T^\bullet(s), d)$.

Let $a, b \geq 0$. If $\mathbb{H}^{a+b}(T^\bullet) = 0$, then $\check H^b(N^a) = 0$.

In the language of derived categories, the theorem says that $\bigoplus_a N^a[-a]$ is a direct summand of the Čech-de Rham complex of $T^\bullet$. Deligne and Illusie prove that $\bigoplus_a \Omega^a_{X'}[-a] \to F_*\Omega^\bullet_X$ is a quasi-isomorphism when $X$ lifts to $W_2(k)$ and $\dim X < p$ (*Relèvements modulo $p^2$ et décomposition du complexe de de Rham*, Invent. Math. 89, 1987). The theorem is the consequence of that statement for one cohomology group, with logarithmic poles and twists.

*Proof.* We follow the construction of Deligne and Illusie. It has three steps.

First, the lift gives the local splitting maps. Fix $i$. For $z \in \tilde K$ write $F_i(z) = z^p + p w$. Since $p \tilde K = \ker \pi$ and $p$ kills exactly $\ker \pi$, the element $\pi(w)$ is well defined, and there is a unique additive map $f_i : \Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}} \to \Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}}$ with $f_i(c v) = c^p f_i(v)$ and
$$f_i\big(d\pi(z)\big) = \pi(z)^{p-1}\, d\pi(z) + d\pi(w).$$
This is "$F_i^*/p$" on $1$-forms. In the same way, for $i, j \in \iota$ and $F_j(z) = F_i(z) + p w$, there is an additive map $h_{ij} : \Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}} \to K$ with $h_{ij}(cv) = c^p h_{ij}(v)$ and $h_{ij}(d\pi(z)) = \pi(w)$. These maps satisfy: $d f_i(v) = 0$, $d h_{ij}(v) = f_j(v) - f_i(v)$, and $h_{ij} + h_{jl} = h_{il}$. Each $f_i$ extends to a ring map $\gamma_i$ of $\Lambda^\bullet_K \Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}}$ with $\gamma_i(c) = c^p$.

Second, these maps give a map of complexes. For $1$-forms $v_1, \dots, v_a$, Deligne and Illusie build a total cochain from the $f_i$ in Čech degree $0$ and the $h_{ij}$ in higher Čech degrees, antisymmetrize it over the permutations of $v_1, \dots, v_a$, and send $c\, v_1 \wedge \dots \wedge v_a$ to $c^p$ times the result. In Čech degree $0$ this map is $\gamma_i$ up to sign. The identities of the first step make it a map of complexes from $\bigoplus_a N^a[-a]$ to the total complex of $T^\bullet$. The algebraic statement that the map induces an injection from $\check H^b(N^a)$ into $\mathbb{H}^{a+b}(T^\bullet)$, under the hypotheses below, is a lemma of the development that we use as a black box.

Third, we check the hypotheses of that lemma on $X$. The lemma asks that $N^a(s) = 0$ for $a \geq p$. This holds because $N^a(s) \subseteq \Lambda^a_K \Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}}$ and $\Omega_{K/\mathbb{Z}}$ has rank $n < p$; it is the only use of $n < p$. Let $y \in W_s$ and $g$ a log unit at $y$. Condition 4 of the lift gives $F_i(z) = z^p(1 + p w_i)$, so $f_i(\operatorname{dlog} g) = \operatorname{dlog} g + d\pi(w_i)$, which lies in $\Omega^1_Z(0)_y$, and $h_{ij}(\operatorname{dlog} g) \in \mathcal{O}_{X,y}$. If $c$ is a section of $\mathcal{O}_X(G)$ at $y$ and $b \in \mathcal{O}_{X,y}$, then $c^p b$ is a section of $\mathcal{O}_X(H)$ at $y$, since $H - pG$ is effective. So the image of $N^a$ lies in $T^\bullet$. Finally, by condition 3 of the lift, the coordinate $x_{j,l}$ has a lift $z$ with $F_j(z) = z^p$, so $f_j(\operatorname{dlog} x_{j,l}) = \operatorname{dlog} x_{j,l}$. So $\gamma_j$ is a Cartier map for $x_j$, and hypothesis 3 says that it induces the Cartier isomorphism on $W_s$ for $j \in s$. Then the map of the second step is a quasi-isomorphism onto a direct summand, and $\check H^b(N^a)$ injects into $\mathbb{H}^{a+b}(T^\bullet) = 0$. $\square$

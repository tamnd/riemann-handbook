## Poitou-Tate duality with normalised invariants

This section states the Poitou-Tate theorems for a finite module of $\ell$-power order over $G_{F,S}$: the nine-term exact sequence, the duality of the Tate-Shafarevich groups, and the vanishing of cohomology in degrees $3$ and higher. The local invariant maps are fixed once and for all by a normalisation at a uniformizer, so that the sequence is defined over any number field and any algebraic closure. The result is used in the step [EveryCharacter from Row0b](/step/every-character-of-row/). The last two sections used a count that comes from the same duality (see the [Proposition 9.11.3](/entry/finrank-h2-euler-formula/) on $H^2$). Here the theorem is stated in full, and its proof is reduced to the case of subfields of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$. For the background see [continuous cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/#continuous-cohomology), [local fields](/learn/galois-cohomology/#local-fields) and [Selmer and Tate-Shafarevich groups](/learn/galois-cohomology/#selmer-and-tate-shafarevich-groups).

**Definition 9.12.1 (The setting).** Let $F$ be a number field, $\Omega$ an algebraic closure of $F$ and $G_F = \operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/F)$. Let $\ell$ be a prime and $r > 0$, and let $\zeta \in \Omega$ be a primitive $\ell^r$-th root of unity. Let $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} : G_F \to (\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r)^\times$ be the cyclotomic character, $\sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^{\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\sigma)}$. For a valuation ring $A$ of $\Omega$ that lies over a finite place $v$ of $F$, let $D_A \subseteq G_F$ be its decomposition group and $I_A \subseteq D_A$ its inertia group. Write $\mu_{\ell^r}$ for $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ with $D_A$ acting through $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$.

The group $D_A$ is the absolute Galois group of the completion $F_v$, and local class field theory gives an isomorphism $H^2(D_A, \mu_{\ell^r}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ ([local class field theory](/learn/class-field-theory/#local-class-field-theory)). An isomorphism of this kind is only fixed up to a unit. The next definition fixes it.

**Definition 9.12.2 (Normalised local invariant).** Let $A$ lie over the finite place $v$, and let $q_v$ be the order of the residue field of $v$. A linear map $\operatorname{inv}_A : H^2(D_A, \mu_{\ell^r}) \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ is *normalised* if it has the following property. Let $\varpi \in \mathcal{O}_F$ with $\varpi \in \mathfrak{p}_v \setminus \mathfrak{p}_v^2$, and let $y \in \Omega$ with $y^{\ell^r} = \varpi$. Let $\kappa : D_A \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ be the Kummer function of $y$, that is $g(y) = \zeta^{\kappa(g)} y$. Let $\chi : D_A \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ be a homomorphism that vanishes on $I_A$ and takes the value $1$ on each Frobenius element (an element that acts as $x \mapsto x^{q_v}$ on the residue field). Then for each 2-cocycle $z$ with $z(g, h) = \kappa(g) \chi(h)$, we have $\operatorname{inv}_A([z]) = 1$.

The cocycle $z$ is the cup product of the Kummer class of $\varpi$ with the unramified character $\chi$. The normalisation fixes the value of $\operatorname{inv}_A$ on this class, and so removes the choice of a unit.

**Definition 9.12.3 (The Poitou-Tate sequence).** Let $S$ be a finite set of finite places of $F$ that contains the places above $\ell$, and let $F_S \subseteq \Omega$ be the maximal extension unramified outside $S$ and the infinite places. Put $G_S = \operatorname{Gal}(F_S/F)$. Assume that $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ factors through a character $\mu$ of $G_S$, as it does since $\mu_{\ell^r} \subseteq F_S$. For each $v \in S$ choose a valuation ring $A_v$ over $v$, and let $D_v$ act on cohomology through the map $D_{A_v} \to G_S$. Let $M$ be a finite $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$-module with a continuous action of $G_S$, and let $M' = \operatorname{Hom}(M, \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r)$ with the action twisted by $\mu$, that is $M' = \operatorname{Hom}(M, \mu_{\ell^r})$. Write $H^i = H^i(G_S, -)$, $H^i_v = H^i(D_v, -)$ and $X^\vee = \operatorname{Hom}(X, \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r)$. For $i = 0, 1, 2$ let
$$\operatorname{loc}_i : H^i(M) \to \textstyle\prod_{v \in S} H^i_v(M)$$
be the product of the restriction maps, and let
$$\operatorname{tr}_i : \textstyle\prod_{v \in S} H^i_v(M) \to H^{2-i}(M')^\vee, \qquad \operatorname{tr}_i(x)(y) = \sum_{v \in S} \operatorname{inv}_v(x_v \cup \operatorname{res}_v y),$$
where the cup product uses the evaluation $M \otimes M' \to \mu_{\ell^r}$, and $\operatorname{inv}_v$ is $\operatorname{inv}_{A_v}$ after the identification of $\mu$ on $D_{A_v}$ with $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$. Let $\text{Ш}^i(M)$ be the kernel of $\operatorname{loc}_i$.

**Theorem 9.12.4 (Poitou-Tate duality with normalised invariants).** Let $F$, $\Omega$, $\ell$, $r$ and $\zeta$ be as above, and assume that $\ell \neq 2$ or $F$ is totally complex. Then there is a family of maps $\operatorname{inv}_A : H^2(D_A, \mu_{\ell^r}) \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$, one for each valuation ring $A$ of $\Omega$, with the following properties.

1. For each $A$ that lies over a finite place $v$, $\operatorname{inv}_A$ is bijective and normalised.
2. For each $S$, choice of the $A_v$, character $\mu$ and module $M$ as in the last definition, and with the maps $\operatorname{tr}_i$ built from this family, there are linear maps $\delta_1 : H^2(M')^\vee \to H^1(M)$ and $\delta_2 : H^1(M')^\vee \to H^2(M)$ such that the sequence
$$\begin{aligned} 0 &\to H^0(M) \xrightarrow{\operatorname{loc}_0} \textstyle\prod_v H^0_v(M) \xrightarrow{\operatorname{tr}_0} H^2(M')^\vee \xrightarrow{\delta_1} H^1(M) \\ &\xrightarrow{\operatorname{loc}_1} \textstyle\prod_v H^1_v(M) \xrightarrow{\operatorname{tr}_1} H^1(M')^\vee \xrightarrow{\delta_2} H^2(M) \\ &\xrightarrow{\operatorname{loc}_2} \textstyle\prod_v H^2_v(M) \xrightarrow{\operatorname{tr}_2} H^0(M')^\vee \to 0 \end{aligned}$$
is exact.
3. The groups $\text{Ш}^1(M')$ and $\text{Ш}^2(M)$ are finite, they have the same order, and there is an isomorphism $\text{Ш}^2(M) \cong \text{Ш}^1(M')^\vee$. The same holds with $M$ and $M'$ exchanged.
4. $H^n(G_S, M) = 0$ for all $n \geq 3$.

The archimedean places do not appear. When $\ell$ is odd or $F$ is totally complex, the Tate cohomology of a real place with coefficients in $M$ is zero, so these terms of the classical sequence vanish.

The proof has two parts. First we reduce the theorem to number fields inside $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$. Then we prove the case of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ by comparison with an exact sequence that is already known.

**Proposition 9.12.5 (Reduction to subfields of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$).** Assume the following three statements.

(a) For each number field $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ (with $\Omega = \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$), each $\ell$, $r$, $\zeta$ with $\ell \neq 2$ or $K$ totally complex, each family $\operatorname{inv}$ that is bijective and normalised at all places over finite places, and each $S$, $A_v$, $\mu$ and $M$ as above, parts 2 and 3 of the theorem hold.

(b) Let $e : F \to F'$ and $E : \Omega \to \Omega'$ be compatible isomorphisms of fields, and $\Phi : G_F \to G_{F'}$ an isomorphism of topological groups with $\Phi(\sigma)(E x) = E(\sigma x)$. Let $A \subseteq \Omega$ and $A' = E(A)$ be valuation rings. Then $\Phi$ maps $D_A$ onto $D_{A'}$, maps $I_A$ onto $I_{A'}$, and maps the Frobenius elements for each $q$ to the Frobenius elements for $q$.

(c) Part 4 of the theorem holds for all $F$, $\Omega$, $\ell$, $r$, $S$ and $M$.

Then the theorem holds.

*Proof.* Fix $F$, $\Omega$, $\ell$, $r$ and $\zeta$. For each $A$ over a finite place, choose a bijective normalised $\operatorname{inv}_A$. It exists by local class field theory, and we take this existence as given. For the other $A$ put $\operatorname{inv}_A = 0$. This gives part 1. There is a number field $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ with isomorphisms $e : F \cong K$ and $E : \Omega \cong \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ that are compatible, since $\Omega$ is algebraic over $F$ and so is countable and algebraically closed of characteristic $0$. Conjugation by $E$ gives $\Phi : G_F \cong G_K$. By (b), $\Phi$ carries decomposition groups, inertia groups and Frobenius elements to the same objects. So it carries the normalised family on $\Omega$ to a normalised family on $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, the groups $G_S$ to $G_S$, and the nine-term sequence and the groups $\text{Ш}$ of $M$ to those of the transported module. Now (a) gives parts 2 and 3 over $K$, and they transport back to $F$. Part 4 is (c). $\square$

Statement (b) is a direct check from the definitions: $\Phi$ and $E$ are compatible, so $\sigma(A) = A$ if and only if $\Phi(\sigma)(A') = A'$, and the same holds for the action on the residue field.

**Definition 9.12.6 (Isomorphism up to units).** A sequence $X_0 \to X_1 \to \cdots \to X_8$ of $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$-modules, with six of its eight maps given, is *isomorphic up to units* to an exact sequence if there are an exact sequence $0 \to Y_0 \to \cdots \to Y_8 \to 0$, isomorphisms $X_i \cong Y_i$, and units $u_1, \ldots, u_6$ of $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ such that each of the six given maps agrees with the map of the $Y$-sequence times a unit, under the isomorphisms.

If this holds, define the two missing maps of the $X$-sequence by transport from the $Y$-sequence. Multiplying a map by a unit does not change its kernel or its image, so the $X$-sequence is then exact. For the nine-term sequence, the given maps are $\operatorname{loc}_i$ and $\operatorname{tr}_i$, and the missing ones are $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$.

*Proof of the theorem.* By the Proposition, it suffices to prove (a), (b) and (c). Statement (b) is the check above. Statement (c) is the vanishing $H^n(G_S, M) = 0$ for $n \geq 3$ when $\ell \neq 2$ or $F$ is totally complex. We take it as given, from Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, Theorem I.4.10: in degrees $n \geq 3$ the cohomology of $G_S$ is the sum of the cohomology of the real places, and this is zero under our assumption. For (a), fix $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and the data. Part 3 follows from the existence of perfect pairings $\text{Ш}^2(M) \times \text{Ш}^1(M') \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ and $\text{Ш}^2(M') \times \text{Ш}^1(M) \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ between finite groups. A perfect pairing gives an isomorphism with the dual, and a finite $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$-module and its dual have the same order. For part 2, the nine-term sequence of $M$ is isomorphic up to units to a second Poitou-Tate sequence for $G_S$, which is built from compactly supported cohomology and is exact by the duality of [Theorem 2.6 of Volume 9](/entry/gs-base-case/). The isomorphisms of the terms come from an identification of $G_S$, of the decomposition groups, of $M$ and of $M'$ with the objects of the second construction. The squares commute up to units because the normalised invariants agree with the invariants of the second construction up to a unit. By the remark after the last definition, the sequence is exact. $\square$

For example, let $M = \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ with trivial action and $\ell$ odd. Then $M' = \mu_{\ell^r}$, $H^0(M) = \mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ and $H^0(M')^\vee$ is $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$ if $\mu_{\ell^r} \subseteq F$ and smaller otherwise. The last map $\operatorname{tr}_2$ is the sum of the local invariants on $\prod_v H^2_v(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r)$, after the pairing with $H^0(M')$. Its surjectivity says that the local classes can have any sum, and exactness at $\prod_v H^2_v$ is the reciprocity law: a family of local classes comes from a global class if and only if the sum of the invariants of its pairings with global elements vanishes.

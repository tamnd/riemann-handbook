## The ratio for modules of l-power order

This section proves the ratio form of Tate's formula for every finite module of $\ell$-power order ([Theorem 9.10.1](/entry/ratio-one-of-lemma-x/)). We take the coprime case of the last section as a hypothesis. The next section proves the coprime case ([Theorem 9.11.5](/entry/lemma-x/)). The result is Step 1 of the proof of [Theorem 9.9.3](/entry/global-euler-char-of-lemma-x/). The proof has three parts. A dévissage reduces to modules killed by $\ell$. For such a module, Artin's induction theorem and the multiplicativity of the ratio reduce to modules induced from cyclic subgroups of a finite Galois group. Shapiro's lemma then reduces to the coprime case. For the background see [continuous cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/#continuous-cohomology).

We keep the notation of the last section. $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is a number field, $S$ is a finite set of finite places of $K$, $K_S$ is the maximal extension of $K$ in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ unramified outside $S$, and $G = G_{K,S} = \operatorname{Gal}(K_S/K)$. For each real place $w$ we choose an embedding $\iota_w$ of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ into an algebraic closure of $K_w$ over $K$, and $c_w$ is the complex conjugation that $\iota_w$ gives. For a finite discrete $G$-module $M$ (a finite abelian group with a continuous action), $\chi(M) = \#H^0 \cdot \#H^2 / \#H^1$ and
$$\rho_S(M) = \chi(M) \cdot \frac{\#M^{r_1 + r_2}}{\prod_{w \text{ real}} \#M^{c_w}}.$$
We say that $S$ *contains the places above* $\ell$ if every finite place $v$ with $\ell \in v$ lies in $S$.

**Theorem 9.10.1 (Ratio form of the formula).** Let $\ell$ be a prime and assume that the coprime case of Section 9 holds. Let $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ be a number field and $S$ a finite set of finite places of $K$ that contains the places above $\ell$. Choose embeddings $\iota_w$ at the real places. Let $M$ be a finite discrete $G_{K,S}$-module such that $\ell^r M = 0$ for some $r \geq 0$. Then $\rho_S(M) = 1$.

Before the proof we fix two tools.

*Coinduced modules.* Let $H \subseteq G$ be an open subgroup and $N$ a discrete $H$-module. The coinduced module $\operatorname{Coind}_H^G N$ is the group of maps $f : G \to N$ with $f(hg) = h f(g)$ for $h \in H$, with $G$ acting by $(g'f)(g) = f(gg')$. If $H$ is the group $G_{K',S'}$ of a finite extension $K' \subseteq K_S$ of $K$, with $S'$ the places of $K'$ above $S$, then Shapiro's lemma gives $H^i(G, \operatorname{Coind}_H^G N) \cong H^i(H, N)$ for all $i$. If $Q \subseteq H$ is open and normal in $G$ and acts trivially on $N$, then $\operatorname{Coind}_H^G N$ is the module $\operatorname{Fun}(G/H, N)$ of maps from the finite set $G/H$ to $N$, with $(gf)(y) = g f(g^{-1}y)$, when $N$ is the restriction of a $G$-module.

*Additive functions.* Let $\Gamma$ be a finite group and $\ell$ a prime. An *additive function* on $\Gamma$ is a map $f$ from finite $\mathbb{F}_\ell[\Gamma]$-modules to $\mathbb{Z}$ such that $f(X_2) = f(X_1) + f(X_3)$ for each short exact sequence $0 \to X_1 \to X_2 \to X_3 \to 0$. For example, $X \mapsto \dim_{\mathbb{F}_\ell} X$ is additive. If the order of $\Gamma$ is prime to $\ell$, then $X \mapsto \dim (X \otimes N)^\Gamma$ is additive for each finite $\mathbb{F}_\ell[\Gamma]$-module $N$, because taking invariants is exact.

We also use Artin's induction theorem in the following form. For a finite group $\Gamma$ there are integers $c_D$, one for each cyclic subgroup $D$, such that for each $x \in \Gamma$
$$|\Gamma| = \sum_{D \text{ cyclic}} c_D \cdot \#\{y \in \Gamma/D : xy = y\}.$$
This says that $|\Gamma|$ times the trivial character is an integral combination of the permutation characters of $\Gamma/D$ (J.-P. Serre, *Linear Representations of Finite Groups*, §9.2, Theorem 17).

*Proof.* Fix $\ell$, $K$, $S$, the embeddings, and $M$ with $\ell^r M = 0$.

Step 1 (positivity and multiplicativity). For a finite module $M$ of $\ell$-power order the groups $H^i$ are finite by Tate's finiteness theorem, so $\rho_S(M)$ is a positive rational number. For a short exact sequence $0 \to M_1 \to M_2 \to M_3 \to 0$ of finite discrete $G$-modules with $\ell^r M_2 = 0$,
$$\rho_S(M_2) = \rho_S(M_1) \cdot \rho_S(M_3).$$
To see this, compare the long exact cohomology sequence of $G$ with the long exact sequences of the groups $\{1, c_w\}$ of order $2$. For $i \geq 3$ the restriction maps $H^i(G, M) \to \bigoplus_{w \text{ real}} H^i(\{1, c_w\}, M)$ are bijective for modules of $\ell$-power order ([Theorem 2.3 of Volume 9](/entry/bijective-gs-real-res/)). So the two sequences have the same tails, and the alternating products of orders up to degree $2$ differ by factors that only depend on the real places. These factors are the terms $\#M^{c_w}$ and $\#M$ in $\rho_S$.

Step 2 (dévissage). We use induction on $r$. If $r = 0$, then $M = 0$ and all the orders are $1$, so $\rho_S(M) = 1$. If $r \geq 1$, multiplication by $\ell$ gives a short exact sequence of $G$-modules
$$0 \to M[\ell] \to M \to \ell M \to 0.$$
Here $\ell M[\ell] = 0$ and $\ell^{r - 1}(\ell M) = 0$. By Step 1, $\rho_S(M) = \rho_S(M[\ell]) \cdot \rho_S(\ell M)$. By induction it is enough to prove $\rho_S(M) = 1$ when $\ell M = 0$. We assume this from now on.

Step 3 (a field that splits $M$). Put $n = 4$ if $\ell = 2$ and $n = \ell$ otherwise. The kernel of the action of $G$ on $M$ is open and normal. Let $L \subseteq K_S$ be its fixed field with the $n$-th roots of unity adjoined. This is possible because $K(\mu_n)/K$ is unramified outside the places above $n$, which are the places above $\ell$, and these lie in $S$. Then $L/K$ is finite and Galois, the subgroup $Q = \operatorname{Gal}(K_S/L)$ is open and normal in $G$ and acts trivially on $M$, and $L$ contains a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity. If $\ell = 2$, then $L$ contains a square root of $-1$, so $L$ is totally complex. Put $\Gamma = G/Q = \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. Then $M$ is a finite $\mathbb{F}_\ell[\Gamma]$-module $X$, and $M$ is the inflation of $X$ to $G$.

Step 4 (reduction to cyclic subgroups). For a prime $p$ and a finite $\mathbb{F}_\ell[\Gamma]$-module $Y$, put $f_p(Y) = v_p(\rho_S(Y))$, where $Y$ is inflated to $G$ and $v_p$ is the $p$-adic valuation on $\mathbb{Q}^\times$. By Step 1, $\rho_S$ is positive and multiplicative on modules killed by $\ell$, so $f_p$ is an additive function on $\Gamma$. Take the integers $c_D$ of Artin's theorem. Let $T_+$ be the disjoint union of $c_D$ copies of $\Gamma/D$ over the $D$ with $c_D > 0$, and $T_-$ the disjoint union of $|\Gamma|$ points and of $|c_D|$ copies of $\Gamma/D$ over the $D$ with $c_D < 0$. Then each $x \in \Gamma$ has the same number of fixed points on $T_+$ and on $T_-$. So the permutation modules $\mathbb{Q}[T_+]$ and $\mathbb{Q}[T_-]$ have the same character, and they are isomorphic. Clearing denominators gives an injective $\Gamma$-map $\mathbb{Z}[T_+] \to \mathbb{Z}[T_-]$ with finite cokernel $C$. For an additive function $f$ and a finite $\mathbb{F}_\ell[\Gamma]$-module $Y$, the function $A \mapsto f(A \otimes Y) - f(\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(A, Y))$ on finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]$-modules is additive, and it is zero on finite modules, since it is zero on modules killed by $\ell$ and every finite module has a filtration with such quotients and with quotients of order prime to $\ell$, on which both terms vanish. So $f(\operatorname{Fun}(T_+, Y)) = f(\operatorname{Fun}(T_-, Y))$, that is
$$|\Gamma| \cdot f(Y) = \sum_{D \text{ cyclic}} c_D \cdot f(\operatorname{Fun}(\Gamma/D, Y)).$$
Thus if $\rho_S(\operatorname{Fun}(\Gamma/D, X)) = 1$ for every cyclic subgroup $D$ of $\Gamma$, then $f_p(X) = 0$ for every prime $p$. A positive rational number with all valuations zero is $1$, so $\rho_S(M) = 1$.

Step 5 (a cyclic subgroup). Let $D \subseteq \Gamma$ be cyclic, and let $L' \subseteq L$ be its fixed field, so that $D = \operatorname{Gal}(L/L')$. By the remark on coinduced modules, $\operatorname{Fun}(\Gamma/D, X) = \operatorname{Coind}_{G_{L',S}}^{G} \operatorname{Res} M$. Write $D = D_\ell \times D'$, with $D_\ell$ of order $\ell^a$ and $D'$ of order prime to $\ell$. Let $K''$ be the fixed field of $D'$. Then $L' \subseteq K'' \subseteq L$, $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K'') = D'$ is cyclic of order prime to $\ell$, and $C = \operatorname{Gal}(K''/L') \cong D_\ell$ is cyclic of order $\ell^a$.

First we compare the modules induced from $L'$ and from $K''$. Coinduction is transitive, so $\operatorname{Coind}_{G_{K'',S}}^{G} \operatorname{Res} M$ is coinduced from $G_{L',S}$ of the module $\operatorname{Fun}(C, \operatorname{Res} M) = \mathbb{F}_\ell[C] \otimes \operatorname{Res} M$. The ring $\mathbb{F}_\ell[C] \cong \mathbb{F}_\ell[t]/(t - 1)^{\ell^a}$ has the filtration by the powers of $t - 1$, with $\ell^a$ quotients, each the trivial module $\mathbb{F}_\ell$. Coinduction is exact. So $\operatorname{Coind}_{G_{K'',S}}^{G} \operatorname{Res} M$ has a filtration with $\ell^a$ quotients, each isomorphic to $\operatorname{Coind}_{G_{L',S}}^{G} \operatorname{Res} M$. By Step 1,
$$\rho_S\bigl(\operatorname{Coind}_{G_{K'',S}}^{G} \operatorname{Res} M\bigr) = \rho_S\bigl(\operatorname{Coind}_{G_{L',S}}^{G} \operatorname{Res} M\bigr)^{\ell^a}.$$

Next we apply Shapiro's lemma. Let $S''$ be the set of places of $K''$ above $S$. The groups $H^i$ of the module coinduced from $G_{K'',S''}$ are the groups $H^i(G_{K'',S''}, \operatorname{Res} M)$. The factors at the real places also match: for suitable embeddings $\iota''$ at the real places of $K''$, the factor of $\rho_S$ at infinity of the coinduced module equals the factor of $\rho_{S''}$ at infinity of $\operatorname{Res} M$. So
$$\rho_S\bigl(\operatorname{Coind}_{G_{K'',S''}}^{G} \operatorname{Res} M\bigr) = \rho_{S''}(\operatorname{Res} M).$$

Last, the coprime case applies to $K''$, $S''$, $L$ and $\operatorname{Res} M$. The set $S''$ contains the places of $K''$ above $\ell$, and $L \subseteq K_S$ is unramified over $K''$ outside $S''$. The subgroup that fixes $L$ is normal in $G_{K'',S''}$, with quotient $D'$ of order prime to $\ell$. The field $L$ contains a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, and if $\ell = 2$ it is totally complex. Finally $\ell \operatorname{Res} M = 0$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/L)$ acts trivially. The coprime case holds for every choice of embeddings, so $\rho_{S''}(\operatorname{Res} M) = 1$. Then $\rho_S(\operatorname{Fun}(\Gamma/D, X))^{\ell^a} = 1$, and since $\rho_S$ is positive, $\rho_S(\operatorname{Fun}(\Gamma/D, X)) = 1$. With Step 4 this proves the theorem. $\square$

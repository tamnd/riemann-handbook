## The global Euler characteristic

The last topic of the chapter is Tate's global Euler characteristic formula. It gives the alternating product of the orders of the Galois cohomology groups of a finite module, unramified outside a finite set of places, in terms of the module at the infinite places alone. This section states the formula ([Theorem 9.9.1](/entry/global-euler-char-full/)) and reduces it to a coprime case ([Theorem 9.9.3](/entry/global-euler-char-of-lemma-x/)). It also describes the two parts of the reduction: the ratio form of the formula ([Theorem 9.10.1](/entry/ratio-one-of-lemma-x/)), and the translation of that ratio into the form of the statement. The coprime case itself is [Theorem 9.11.5](/entry/lemma-x/). For the background see [continuous cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/#continuous-cohomology) and [global fields](/learn/galois-cohomology/#global-fields) ([[Galois cohomology|EESNTXFWHZ]]).

Let $F$ be a number field, $\Omega$ an algebraic closure of $F$ and $G_F = \operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/F)$. The finite places of $F$ are its nonzero primes. Let $S$ be a finite set of finite places. Let $N_S$ be the closure of the normal subgroup of $G_F$ generated by the inertia groups of all places of $\Omega$ over the finite places outside $S$, and put $G_S = G_F/N_S$. So $G_S$ is the Galois group of the maximal extension of $F$ unramified outside $S$, and the infinite places can ramify. Let $\ell$ be a prime, $r > 0$, and $M$ a finite discrete $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$-module with a continuous action of $G_S$. We write $H^i = H^i(G_S, M)$ for continuous cochain cohomology. So $H^0 = M^{G_S}$, and $H^1$ and $H^2$ are continuous cocycles modulo coboundaries. A *complex conjugation* at a real place $w$ is an element $c \in G_F$ such that $j \circ c = \bar{j}$ for an embedding $j : \Omega \to \mathbb{C}$ that induces $w$. We write $M^{c}$ for the fixed points of the image of $c$ in $G_S$, and $r_2$ for the number of complex places.

**Theorem 9.9.1 (Global Euler characteristic formula).** Let $F$, $\Omega$, $\ell$, $r > 0$, $M$ and $S$ be as above, and assume that $S$ contains every place above $\ell$. For each real place $w$ let $c_w$ be a complex conjugation at $w$. Then
$$\#H^0 \cdot \#H^2 \cdot \#M^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]} = \#H^1 \cdot \prod_{w \text{ real}} \#M^{c_w} \cdot \#M^{r_2}.$$

Since $[F:\mathbb{Q}] = r_1 + 2r_2$, this is Tate's formula
$$\frac{\#H^0 \cdot \#H^2}{\#H^1} = \prod_{v \mid \infty} \frac{\#H^0(F_v, M)}{\|\#M\|_v},$$
with $\|x\|_v = |x|$ at a real place and $|x|^2$ at a complex place, and $H^0(F_v, M) = M^{c_w}$ or $M$. See J. Tate, *Duality theorems in Galois cohomology over number fields*, Proc. ICM Stockholm 1962, and J. S. Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, Theorem I.5.1. The groups $H^i$ are finite by Tate's finiteness theorem, so the two forms are the same.

For example, let $F = \mathbb{Q}$, $\ell$ odd, $S = \{\ell\}$ and $M = \mathbb{Z}/\ell$ with the trivial action. Then $\#H^0 = \ell$ and $\#M^{c} = \ell$, so the formula reads $\ell \cdot \#H^2 \cdot \ell = \#H^1 \cdot \ell$, that is $\#H^1 = \ell \cdot \#H^2$. Here $H^1 = \operatorname{Hom}(G_S, \mathbb{Z}/\ell)$. By class field theory the abelianization of $G_S$ is $\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times \cong \mu_{\ell - 1} \times \mathbb{Z}_\ell$, so $\#H^1 = \ell$. The formula then gives $H^2 = 0$.

The proof reduces the formula to a special case. To state it, fix an algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ of $\mathbb{Q}$. For a number field $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, a finite set $S$ of finite places of $K$ and a finite discrete $G_{K,S}$-module $M$, put
$$\chi(M) = \frac{\#H^0 \cdot \#H^2}{\#H^1}, \qquad \rho_S(M) = \chi(M) \cdot \frac{\#M^{r_1 + r_2}}{\prod_{w \text{ real}} \#M^{c_w}},$$
with $c_w$ the complex conjugation that comes from a chosen embedding at $w$. By the computation above, the formula says $\rho_S(M) = 1$.

**Definition 9.9.2 (Coprime case).** The *coprime case* of the formula is the following statement. Let $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ be a number field, $\ell$ a prime and $S$ a finite set of finite places of $K$ that contains the places above $\ell$. Let $L \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ be a finite extension of $K$ in the maximal extension unramified outside $S$, such that the subgroup of $G_{K,S}$ that fixes $L$ is normal of index prime to $\ell$. Assume that $L$ contains a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, and that $\ell \neq 2$ or $L$ is totally complex. Then $\rho_S(M) = 1$ for each finite discrete $G_{K,S}$-module $M$ with $\ell M = 0$ on which $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/L)$ acts trivially, and for each choice of embeddings at the real places.

So in the coprime case, $M$ is a module over $\mathbb{F}_\ell[\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)]$, and the order of $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ is prime to $\ell$. This makes the module semisimple, and the cohomology of $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ with coefficients in $M$ is zero in positive degrees.

**Theorem 9.9.3 (Reduction to the coprime case).** If the coprime case holds, then the global Euler characteristic formula holds.

*Proof.* Fix $F$, $\Omega$, $\ell$, $r > 0$, $M$, $S$ and the complex conjugations $c_w$.

Step 1 (the ratio form). By [Theorem 9.10.1](/entry/ratio-one-of-lemma-x/), the coprime case implies that $\rho_S(M) = 1$ for every number field $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, every $S$ that contains the places above $\ell$, and every finite discrete module $M$ killed by a power of $\ell$. That proof has two parts. First, for $\ell M = 0$, it takes a finite Galois extension $L$ that trivializes $M$, and it uses multiplicativity in short exact sequences to reduce to modules induced from cyclic subgroups of $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. Then it uses restriction to the corresponding fields, which brings it to the coprime case. Second, a dévissage along $0 \to M[\ell] \to M \to \ell M \to 0$ gives the case $\ell^r M = 0$.

Step 2 (transport to $F$). As algebraic closures of $\mathbb{Q}$, $\Omega$ and $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ are isomorphic. Choose an isomorphism. It maps $F$ to a number field $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, the places of $F$ to the places of $K$, and $G_F$ to $G_K$. It also maps inertia groups to inertia groups, so it maps $G_S$ to $G_{K,S}$. The $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r$-module $M$ becomes a discrete $G_{K,S}$-module over $\mathbb{Z}$ with the same order, the same orders of $H^0$, $H^1$ and $H^2$, and the same number of fixed points for each element. Two complex conjugations at the same real place are conjugate in the Galois group, so $\#M^{c_w}$ does not depend on the choice of $c_w$. Thus Step 1 gives $\rho_S(M) = 1$ for the data of $F$.

Step 3 (clearing denominators). $\rho_S(M) = 1$ says $\#H^0 \cdot \#H^2 \cdot \#M^{r_1 + r_2} = \#H^1 \cdot \prod_{w \text{ real}} \#M^{c_w}$. Multiply both sides by $\#M^{r_2}$ and use $r_1 + 2r_2 = [F:\mathbb{Q}]$. $\square$

*Proof of the global Euler characteristic formula.* The coprime case holds by [Theorem 9.11.5](/entry/lemma-x/), and the last theorem gives the formula. $\square$

Tate's proof uses the Poitou-Tate sequence and local duality. In the reduction above the global content is in the coprime case. In that case the cohomology of $G_{K,S}$ with coefficients in $M$ comes from the subgroup that fixes $L$, which acts trivially on $M$. So it can be computed from the class field theory of $L$, and the conditions on $\mu_\ell$ and on the real places of $L$ make the infinite places simple.

## The coprime case

This section proves the coprime case of Tate's formula ([Theorem 9.11.5](/entry/lemma-x/)). It is the hypothesis of [Theorem 9.10.1](/entry/ratio-one-of-lemma-x/), and with it the proof of [Theorem 9.9.1](/entry/global-euler-char-full/) is complete. In the coprime case the Galois group $\Gamma$ of the splitting field $L$ has order prime to $\ell$. Then the cohomology of $M$ over $K$ is the $\Gamma$-invariant part of the cohomology of $\mu_\ell$ over $L$, twisted by a fixed $\Gamma$-module. The Kummer sequence for $S$-units expresses the cohomology of $\mu_\ell$ through the $S$-units and the $S$-class group of $L$. The [Dirichlet unit theorem](/learn/class-field-theory/#global-class-field-theory) gives the $S$-units as a $\Gamma$-module up to finite groups, and a Poitou-Tate count gives $H^2$. The result is an equation of dimensions over $\mathbb{F}_\ell$ ([Theorem 9.11.4](/entry/alt-sum/)), which is the coprime case in logarithmic form. See also [global fields](/learn/galois-cohomology/#global-fields) and [Selmer and Tate-Shafarevich groups](/learn/galois-cohomology/#selmer-and-tate-shafarevich-groups).

We keep the notation of the last two sections. Throughout, $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is a number field, $\ell$ is a prime, and $S$ is a finite set of finite places of $K$ that contains the places above $\ell$. Let $L \subseteq K_S$ be a finite extension of $K$ such that the subgroup $Q = \operatorname{Gal}(K_S/L)$ of $G = G_{K,S}$ is normal of index prime to $\ell$. Assume that $L$ contains a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, and that $\ell \neq 2$ or $L$ is totally complex. Then $L/K$ is Galois, and $\Gamma = \operatorname{Gal}(L/K) = G/Q$ has order prime to $\ell$. Let $M$ be a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_\ell$-vector space with a continuous action of $G$ on which $Q$ acts trivially. We write $H^i = H^i(G, M)$ and $h^i = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_\ell} H^i$.

If $\ell = 2$, then $L$ is totally complex and $[L : K]$ is odd. A real place of $K$ would then have an odd number of places of $L$ above it, and at least one of them would be real. So $K$ is totally complex when $\ell = 2$. In particular, the real places only appear when $\ell$ is odd.

**Definition 9.11.1 (The twist and the function $d$).** Let $\mu_\ell \subseteq L$ be the group of $\ell$-th roots of unity, a one-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_\ell$-space with an action of $G$ through $\Gamma$. Put $N = \operatorname{Hom}(\mu_\ell, M)$, with $\Gamma$ acting by conjugation. For a finite $\mathbb{F}_\ell[\Gamma]$-module $X$ put
$$d(X) = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_\ell} (X \otimes_{\mathbb{F}_\ell} N)^\Gamma.$$

Since $|\Gamma|$ is prime to $\ell$, taking $\Gamma$-invariants is exact on $\mathbb{F}_\ell[\Gamma]$-modules, so $d$ is an additive function in the sense of the last section. The reason for this choice is the isomorphism $M \cong \mu_\ell \otimes N$ of $G$-modules: it shows that for a $G$-module $X$ on which $Q$ acts through a module structure, $(X \otimes N)^\Gamma$ is the part of $X$ that "looks like $M$". We write $d(1) = d(\mathbb{F}_\ell) = \dim N^\Gamma$.

**Definition 9.11.2 (The arithmetic modules of $L$).** Let $E$ be the group of $S$-units of $K_S$, the units of the integral closure of the $S$-integers of $K$ in $K_S$. Put $A_i = H^i(Q, E)$ for $i = 0, 1$, with $\Gamma$ acting through conjugation by $G$. So $A_0 = \mathcal{O}_{L,S}^\times$ is the group of $S$-units of $L$, and $A_1$ is finite (it is the $S$-class group of $L$). Let $S_L$ be the set of places of $L$ above $S$ and $\infty_L$ the set of infinite places of $L$. Both are finite sets with an action of $\Gamma$, and we write $\mathbb{F}_\ell^{S_L}$ and $\mathbb{F}_\ell^{\infty_L}$ for the permutation modules. For an abelian group $A$ we write $A/\ell = A/\ell A$ and $A[\ell]$ for the kernel of multiplication by $\ell$.

For a real place $w$ of $K$, let $D_w \subseteq \Gamma$ be the decomposition group of the place of $L$ above $w$ that the embedding $\iota_w$ gives. It is generated by the image of $c_w$.

**Proposition 9.11.3 (Dimension of $H^2$).** In the setting above,
$$h^2 = d(A_1/\ell) + d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{S_L}) - d(1).$$

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Lit1.GEN.X.finrank_H2_euler_formula`.

*Proof.* Put $M' = \operatorname{Hom}(M, \mu_\ell)$. For each $v \in S$ choose a decomposition group $G_v \subseteq G$ at $v$. Since $\ell$ is odd or $K$ is totally complex, the Poitou-Tate sequence for $G_{K,S}$, built on the duality of [Theorem 2.5.7 of Volume 9](/entry/gs-base-case/), gives the count
$$\#H^2(G, M) \cdot \#H^0(G, M') = \#\text{Ш}^2(M) \cdot \prod_{v \in S} \#H^0(G_v, M'),$$
where $\text{Ш}^2(M)$ is the kernel of $H^2(G, M) \to \prod_{v \in S} H^2(G_v, M)$. This count comes from the exact sequence $0 \to \text{Ш}^2(M) \to H^2(G, M) \to \bigoplus_{v \in S} H^2(G_v, M) \to H^0(G, M')^\vee \to 0$ and local duality $H^2(G_v, M) \cong H^0(G_v, M')^\vee$. We compute the three terms in dimensions.

First, $\dim \text{Ш}^2(M) = d(A_1/\ell)$. Here we use that $Q$ acts trivially on $\mu_\ell$ and on $M$, so the Tate-Shafarevich group over $L$ is built from $\mu_\ell$, and its $\Gamma$-invariant twisted part is the $S$-class group modulo $\ell$, tensored with $N$.

Second, $\sum_{v \in S} \dim H^0(G_v, M') = d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{S_L})$. Indeed, $\mathbb{F}_\ell^{S_L}$ is the sum over $v \in S$ of the modules induced from the decomposition groups $\Gamma_v \subseteq \Gamma$, so $d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{S_L}) = \sum_v \dim N^{\Gamma_v}$. Since $|\Gamma_v|$ is prime to $\ell$, $\dim N^{\Gamma_v} = \dim (M')^{\Gamma_v}$, the multiplicity of $\mu_\ell$ in $M$ and in the dual. And $H^0(G_v, M') = (M')^{\Gamma_v}$ because $Q \cap G_v$ acts trivially.

Third, $\dim H^0(G, M') = d(1)$ by the same argument with $\Gamma$ in place of $\Gamma_v$.

Taking $\log_\ell$ of the count and putting in the three values gives $h^2 + d(1) = d(A_1/\ell) + d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{S_L})$. $\square$

**Theorem 9.11.4 (Alternating sum of dimensions).** In the setting above, and for every choice of embeddings $\iota_w$ at the real places,
$$h^0 - h^1 + h^2 = \sum_{w \text{ real}} \dim M^{c_w} - (r_1 + r_2) \dim M,$$
where $r_1 + r_2$ is the number of infinite places of $K$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Lit1.GEN.X.altSum`.

*Proof.* We collect seven equations and combine them.

(1) $h^0 = d(A_0[\ell])$ and (2) $h^1 = d(A_0/\ell) + d(A_1[\ell])$. Since $|\Gamma|$ is prime to $\ell$, the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence gives $H^i(G, M) = H^i(Q, M)^\Gamma$. As $Q$ acts trivially on $\mu_\ell$ and on $M$, $H^i(Q, M) = H^i(Q, \mu_\ell) \otimes N$, so $h^i = d(H^i(Q, \mu_\ell))$. The Kummer sequence $0 \to \mu_\ell \to E \xrightarrow{\ell} E \to 0$ is exact, because an $\ell$-th root of an $S$-unit generates an extension unramified outside $S$ (the places above $\ell$ are in $S$). Its cohomology sequence over $Q$ gives $H^0(Q, \mu_\ell) = A_0[\ell]$ and a short exact sequence $0 \to A_0/\ell \to H^1(Q, \mu_\ell) \to A_1[\ell] \to 0$. Apply the additive function $d$.

(3) The Proposition above: $h^2 = d(A_1/\ell) + d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{S_L}) - d(1)$.

For the next two equations, let $g(A) = d(A/\ell) - d(A[\ell])$ for a finitely generated abelian group $A$ with an action of $\Gamma$. By the snake lemma, $g$ is additive on short exact sequences. It is zero on finite groups (see Step 4 of the last section) and $g(\mathbb{Z}[T]) = d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^T)$ for a finite $\Gamma$-set $T$.

(4) $d(A_0/\ell) - d(A_0[\ell]) = d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{S_L}) + d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{\infty_L}) - d(1)$. This is the [Dirichlet $S$-unit theorem](/learn/class-field-theory/#global-class-field-theory) as a statement about $\Gamma$-modules: the logarithm map gives an exact sequence $0 \to \mu(L) \to \mathcal{O}_{L,S}^\times \to \Lambda \to 0$ with $\Lambda$ a lattice of finite index in the kernel of the sum map $\mathbb{Z}[S_L \cup \infty_L] \to \mathbb{Z}$. Since $g$ vanishes on finite groups and is additive, $g(A_0) = g(\mathbb{Z}[S_L]) + g(\mathbb{Z}[\infty_L]) - g(\mathbb{Z})$.

(5) $d(A_1/\ell) = d(A_1[\ell])$, because $A_1$ is finite and so $g(A_1) = 0$.

(6) $d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{\infty_L}) = \sum_{w \text{ real}} \dim N^{D_w} + r_2 \dim M$. As in the Proposition, $d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{\infty_L})$ is the sum over the infinite places $w$ of $K$ of $\dim N^{D_w}$, where $D_w$ is the decomposition group in $\Gamma$. At a complex place $D_w = 1$, and $\dim N = \dim M$.

(7) For each real place $w$, $\dim M^{c_w} + \dim N^{D_w} = \dim M$. Here $\ell$ is odd, so $c_w$ acts on $\mu_\ell$ by $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^{-1}$. Under $M \cong \mu_\ell \otimes N$, the fixed space $M^{c_w}$ corresponds to the $(-1)$-eigenspace of $c_w$ on $N$, and $N^{D_w}$ is the $(+1)$-eigenspace. Since $\ell$ is odd, $N$ is the sum of the two.

Now form (1) $-$ (2) $+$ (3). The terms $d(A_1[\ell])$ and $d(A_1/\ell)$ cancel by (5), and (4) gives
$$h^0 - h^1 + h^2 = -d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{\infty_L}).$$
By (6) and (7), with $r_1$ real places,
$$\begin{aligned} -d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{\infty_L}) &= -\sum_{w \text{ real}} (\dim M - \dim M^{c_w}) - r_2 \dim M \\ &= \sum_{w \text{ real}} \dim M^{c_w} - (r_1 + r_2) \dim M. \end{aligned}$$
$\square$

**Theorem 9.11.5 (Coprime case of the global Euler characteristic formula).** The coprime case holds. That is, in the setting above, let $M$ be a finite discrete $G$-module with $\ell M = 0$ on which $Q$ acts trivially, and choose embeddings $\iota_w$ at the real places. Then $\rho_S(M) = 1$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Lit1.GEN.X.lemmaX`.

*Proof.* View $M$ as an $\mathbb{F}_\ell$-vector space. Each $H^i$ and each $M^{c_w}$ is an $\mathbb{F}_\ell$-space, so $\#H^i = \ell^{h^i}$ and $\#M^{c_w} = \ell^{\dim M^{c_w}}$. Then
$$\log_\ell \rho_S(M) = h^0 - h^1 + h^2 + (r_1 + r_2) \dim M - \sum_{w \text{ real}} \dim M^{c_w},$$
and this is $0$ by the last theorem. $\square$

For example, let $K = \mathbb{Q}$, $\ell$ odd, $S = \{\ell\}$, $L = \mathbb{Q}(\mu_\ell)$ and $M = \mu_\ell$. Then $\Gamma = (\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^\times$ has order $\ell - 1$, $N = \mathbb{F}_\ell$ is trivial, and $d(X) = \dim X^\Gamma$. Complex conjugation acts on $M$ by $-1$, so $\dim M^{c} = 0$, and the theorem says $h^0 - h^1 + h^2 = -1$. Since $\ell$ is odd, $\Gamma$ acts on $\mu_\ell$ by a nontrivial character, so $h^0 = 0$. The place $\ell$ is totally ramified in $L$, so $S_L$ has one element and $d(\mathbb{F}_\ell^{S_L}) = 1 = d(1)$. The Proposition then gives $h^2 = \dim (A_1/\ell)^\Gamma$. As $|\Gamma|$ is prime to $\ell$, the norm and the inclusion identify the $\Gamma$-invariants of $A_1 \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ with the $\ell$-part of the $S$-class group of $\mathbb{Q}$, which is trivial. So $h^2 = 0$ and $h^1 = 1$. A direct check: the Kummer sequence over $\mathbb{Q}$ gives $H^1(G, \mu_\ell) \cong \mathbb{Z}[1/\ell]^\times/\ell$, since $\mathbb{Z}[1/\ell]$ is a principal ideal domain. The group $\mathbb{Z}[1/\ell]^\times = \{\pm 1\} \times \ell^{\mathbb{Z}}$, and $-1 = (-1)^\ell$ is an $\ell$-th power, so the quotient has dimension $1$.

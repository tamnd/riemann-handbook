This volume proves the results on schemes that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. They fall into two groups. The first is the [étale cohomology](/learn/geometry/#tale-cohomology) of schemes with coefficients $\mathbb{Z}/q(i)$: the Kummer sequence and cup products, the projective bundle formula, purity, proper base change and finiteness. The second is algebraic geometry over $\mathbb{C}$ and in characteristic $0$: formal algebraization, resolution of singularities, vanishing theorems and $L^2$ methods. Volume 1 gives the definitions and states many of these results. This volume proves them, so that the main proof does not take them from the literature.

The proof uses the volume in two places. The first is [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/). Its step [GeoOne holds](/step/geo-one-holds/) builds Galois representations of weight one from the cohomology of a tower of unitary Shimura varieties. It needs the local steps of the tower, the finiteness of the representation and a holomorphic Lefschetz formula. Chapters 1 to 6 supply these inputs. The second place is the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/), where the proof splits into two large parts. The branch [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) needs resolution of singularities, the vanishing theorems of Kawamata-Viehweg and Nadel, $L^2$ extension and the inductive step of Angehrn and Siu. The branch [EveryCharacter from Row0b](/step/every-character-of-row/) needs Tate's global Euler characteristic formula and facts of Hopf type on cochains. Chapters 7 to 10 supply these, with the coherent finiteness theorem of Chapter 6. The commutative algebra of Chapter 6 goes to [VWin](/step/vwin-def/).

Chapter 1 proves inputs of the $\ell$-adic Hodge comparison. Its first three sections work with period rings and end with a line object for which a filtration of the crystalline period ring is acyclic, [Theorem 1.3.7](/entry/exists-line/). The other five sections work on the site of thickenings of a smooth scheme over a field of characteristic $0$. The main results are the vanishing of graded cohomology on affine opens in all degrees but one, [Theorem 1.5.2](/entry/affine-graded-cohomology-vanishing/), and a Künneth formula for proper smooth products, [Theorem 1.8.3](/entry/kuenneth-product-proper-smooth/), glued by a Mayer-Vietoris argument.

Chapter 2 proves the projective bundle formula, [Theorem 2.3.7](/entry/projective-bundle-formula-vector-bundle-any-space/). For a vector bundle $V$ of rank $N$ on a space (a simplicial presheaf on schemes) on which $q$ is invertible, the cohomology of $\mathbb{P}(V)$ with coefficients $\mathbb{Z}/q(i)$ is free over the cohomology of the base, with basis $1, c_1(L), \dots, c_1(L)^{N-1}$. The chapter then proves that $H^1$ of the generic fibre of a strict henselisation of the affine line is zero, [Theorem 2.5.9](/entry/tate-twist-cohomology-generic-fibre-hensel-line-subsingleton/), Abhyankar's lemma, Theorem 2.6.6, and the purity theorem of Zariski and Nagata, [Theorem 2.7.6](/entry/etale-of-etale-height-one-of-regular-local-ring/). Later sections compute the cohomology of affine curves, of the punctured line and of some fields, and prove homotopy invariance, [Theorem 2.13.11](/entry/homotopy-invariance-etale/). The last three sections construct the Hilbert scheme of points of a finite flat morphism, [Theorem 2.16.6](/entry/hilbert-scheme-finite-locally-free-representable-proper/).

Chapter 3 works with a model of étale cohomology given by a list of axioms, and proves the deep axioms from the simple ones. Its main results are the proper base change theorem of M. Artin for the coefficients $\mathbb{Z}/q(i)$, [Theorem 3.6.1](/entry/proper-base-change/), and the finiteness of $H^n(X, \mathbb{Z}/q(i))$ for a proper scheme $X$ over an algebraically closed field, [Theorem 3.7.1](/entry/etale-topology-top/). The last part leaves the model. From a Frobenius relation of Eichler-Shimura type on crystalline periods it proves [Theorem 3.10.6](/entry/phi-step/): a Galois representation cut out of the cohomology of a tower is crystalline at the places above $\ell$, with its Frobenius controlled by the Hecke polynomial.

Chapter 4 builds the multiplicative structure of étale cohomology on simplicial spaces. It proves the Kummer sequence, [Theorem 4.1.7](/entry/kum-seq/), and constructs the cup product from Alexander-Whitney cochains, [Theorem 4.3.7](/entry/cup-product-tensor-holds-of/), with the unit law and graded commutativity. It defines Kummer classes and first Chern classes by explicit cocycles, then Chern classes, and turns them into power sum classes with Newton's formulas, [Theorem 4.8.9](/entry/s57/). The last sections prove that cohomology commutes with filtered limits of schemes, and a result on compact groups, [Theorem 4.11.2](/entry/goal-holds/), which feeds the division of a Galois representation.

Chapter 5 collects the results that come from finite étale maps. The clopen subsets of a finite étale scheme are the points of a finite étale scheme, [Theorem 5.1.4](/entry/exists-finite-etale-representing-clopen-subschemes/). A class that vanishes at all strictly henselian points vanishes on an étale neighbourhood, [Theorem 5.4.7](/entry/etale-cohomology-class-killed-on-neighborhood/). The projective bundle formula holds for the trivial bundle, [Lemma 5.5.3](/entry/pbf-all/). The longest part is the holomorphic Lefschetz formula for a correspondence given by two finite étale maps to a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$, [Theorem 5.7.3](/entry/cb-lefschetz-fixed-point-finite-etale-correspondence/). With the count of fixed points of [Theorem 5.8.4](/entry/complex-count-regular-marked-points/), it shows that Hecke eigensystems occur in Dolbeault cohomology. Section 10 proves a closedness result at $\ell$ for the de Rham property, [Theorem 5.10.5](/entry/closed-at-l/).

Chapter 6 has three groups of results. The étale group classifies the clopen subschemes of fixed rank of a finite étale scheme, [Theorem 6.1.7](/entry/exists-clop-rep-of-rank-of-locally-noetherian/), proves a Künneth formula for $\mathbb{G}_m \times E$, [Theorem 6.2.7](/entry/kun-main/), and shows without Jacobians that the $q$-torsion of the Picard group of an affine curve over an algebraically closed field is finite, [Theorem 6.6.8](/entry/curve-picard-of-selmer-picard-of-model-curve/). The second group is Grothendieck's finiteness theorem for coherent cohomology on a proper scheme over a Noetherian ring, [Theorem 6.10.6](/entry/ground-theory-arith-scheme-coh-finite-h-of-is-proper/). The third group is commutative algebra for deformation rings: a length bound for a cotangent lattice, [Theorem 6.12.8](/entry/main-holds/), and graded presentations of framed deformation rings, [Theorem 6.13.8](/entry/lemma-p/).

Chapter 7 proves Grothendieck's existence theorem for closed subschemes, [Theorem 7.3.3](/entry/grothendieck-existence-closed/). Over a Noetherian ring that is $I$-adically complete, a compatible system of closed subschemes of the thickenings of a scheme $P$, proper at the first step, comes from one closed subscheme of $P$. With a uniform lifting of sections of an ample line bundle, this gives the algebraization of formal schemes, [Theorem 7.6.3](/entry/t-formal-algebraisation-schemes/). Over $\mathbb{C}$ the chapter computes the local order of a hyperplane section by generic slices, [Theorem 7.9.4](/entry/local-order/), and proves a Crofton formula, [Theorem 7.10.2](/entry/crofton/). The last part proves the Hopf bound $\dim H^1 \leq g$ on the cochains of a functor on a finite meet semilattice, [Theorem 7.12.5](/entry/hopf-le-st/).

Chapter 8 proves Hironaka's theorem on [[resolution of singularities|MB52NDA6WN]] over a field of characteristic $0$, in its strong form, [Theorem 8.10.6](/entry/strong-resolution-of-singularities-char-zero/), and its functorial form, [Theorem 8.10.7](/entry/functorial-resolution-char-zero/). The proof follows Włodarczyk and Kollár. A marked ideal on a smooth scheme is principalized by blow-ups in smooth centres, by induction on the dimension, through divisor reduction and hypersurfaces of maximal contact. The correctness of these rules is [Theorem 8.7.8](/entry/is-correct-canonical-algorithm-rules/). Strict transforms in an embedding then resolve affine schemes, and the affine resolutions glue.

Chapter 9 proves the vanishing theorem of Kawamata and Viehweg by the method of Deligne and Illusie. A smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{F}_p$ of dimension less than $p$ that lifts to $\mathbb{Z}/p^2$ has a decomposition of its twisted logarithmic de Rham complex, which gives vanishing in characteristic $p$, [Theorem 9.3.3](/entry/charp-vanishing-liftable/). Reduction modulo $p$ carries this to characteristic $0$, [Theorem 9.5.2](/entry/vanishing-ample-snc-cartier/), and a log resolution gives Nadel vanishing and local vanishing, [Theorem 9.6.3](/entry/vanishing-log-res-pushforward-and-local/). The chapter also proves the Casselman-Wallach theorem for $U(p, q)$, [Theorem 9.8.3](/entry/casselman-wallach/), and Tate's global Euler characteristic formula, [Theorem 9.9.1](/entry/global-euler-char-full/).

Chapter 10 has two parts. The analytic part proves Hörmander's $L^2$ estimate for $\bar\partial$ on étale Stein spaces, [Theorem 10.1.3](/entry/hormander/), and an $L^2$ extension theorem, [Theorem 10.5.3](/entry/l2-extension-log-canonical-prime/): a section of $K_Y + E$ given at the common zeros of $n$ sections extends to $Y$ with a bound on its norm. The algebraic part proves inversion of adjunction, moves the klt condition up an étale tower, and proves the inductive step of Angehrn and Siu, [Theorem 10.9.2](/entry/exists-global-section-angehrn-siu-inductive-step/), which cuts a non-klt centre down at several points at once.

The étale chapters 2 to 6 form one block, and their statements depend on each other across chapters. Chapter 3 takes the power sum classes of Theorem 4.8.9 as its axiom D2. The finiteness theorem 3.7.1 uses the Kummer sequence and continuity of Chapter 4, Theorem 5.4.7, and the Picard torsion of Chapter 6. The Hilbert scheme of Theorem 2.16.6 gives Theorem 6.1.7, which gives Theorem 5.1.4. Chapter 6 gives Lemma 5.5.3, an input of the formula on constant spaces, [Theorem 2.2.3](/entry/pbf-disc/). So a reader can take Chapter 4 first, then Chapters 6 and 5, then Chapter 2, and Chapter 3 last, with Sections 14 to 16 of Chapter 2 before Chapter 6. Chapter 1 uses no other chapter of this volume. Chapters 7 to 10 do not use the étale block. Chapter 9 takes a log resolution as data, and Chapter 10 assumes principalisation. Chapter 8 proves resolution and principalization in characteristic $0$, so a reader can take it before Chapters 9 and 10.

The standard references are these. For étale cohomology: SGA 4, Exposé XII, §5 (proper base change), Exposé XIV (finiteness) and Exposé XV (local acyclicity and homotopy invariance); SGA 5, Exposé VII (the projective bundle formula and Chern classes); SGA 1, Exposés X and XIII (purity and Abhyankar's lemma); and J. S. Milne, *Étale Cohomology*, Chapters II, III and VI. The chapters differ from these sources: the properties of étale cohomology are axioms, and cohomology is defined for simplicial presheaves. For formal algebraization and coherent finiteness: EGA III$_1$, §5.1, §5.4 and Théorème 3.2.1. For resolution: H. Hironaka, Ann. of Math. 79 (1964), and J. Kollár, *Lectures on Resolution of Singularities*, Chapter 3. For vanishing: P. Deligne and L. Illusie, Invent. Math. 89 (1987), and R. Lazarsfeld, *Positivity in Algebraic Geometry*. For $L^2$ methods: L. Hörmander, Acta Math. 113 (1965), T. Ohsawa and K. Takegoshi, Math. Z. 195 (1987), and U. Angehrn and Y.-T. Siu, Invent. Math. 122 (1995).

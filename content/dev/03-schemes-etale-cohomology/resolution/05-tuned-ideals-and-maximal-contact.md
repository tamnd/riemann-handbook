## Tuned ideals and hypersurfaces of maximal contact

Order reduction lowers the dimension by restricting a marked ideal of maximal order to a smooth hypersurface. Two problems arise. The restriction must not lose information, and the result must not depend on the choice of the hypersurface. This section solves both. The tuned ideal replaces $(I, m)$ by a marked ideal with the same marked sequences that is balanced and invariant under the derivative of maximal contact. Two hypersurfaces of maximal contact for such an ideal are then equivalent through a pair of étale maps. Section 6 uses both results to construct order reduction. We follow [Kollár, Chapter 3] and [Włodarczyk].

Throughout, $k$ is a field of characteristic $0$ and $X$ is smooth and locally of finite type over $k$. For an ideal sheaf $I$ and $m \geq 1$ we say that $I$ has *maximal order* $m$ if $\operatorname{ord}_x I \leq m$ for all $x$ and $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ is not empty.

**Definition 8.5.1 (Maximal contact ideal).** The *maximal contact ideal* of $(I, m)$ is $\operatorname{MC}(I, m) = \mathcal{D}^{m-1} I$. The pair $(I, m)$ is *MC-invariant* if
$$\operatorname{MC}(I, m) \cdot \mathcal{D}(I) \subseteq I .$$

If $I$ has maximal order $m$, then $\operatorname{MC}(I, m)$ has order at most $1$ everywhere, and its zero set is $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$. An element of order $1$ in $\operatorname{MC}(I, m)$ is part of a system of local coordinates.

**Definition 8.5.2 (Hypersurface of maximal contact).** An ideal sheaf $H$ on $X$ is a *hypersurface of maximal contact* for $(I, m)$ if each point of $X$ has an affine open neighbourhood $U$ with one of these properties:

1. $U$ does not meet $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ and $H|_U$ is the unit ideal;
2. there is an étale map $c \colon U \to \mathbb{A}^n_k$ over $k$ and a coordinate $x_j$ of $\mathbb{A}^n_k$ such that $H|_U$ is generated by $c^{*}x_j$ and $c^{*}x_j \in \operatorname{MC}(I, m)(U)$.

So $V(H)$ is a smooth hypersurface near $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$, it contains $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$, and it is empty away from it.

**Definition 8.5.3 (Tuned ideal).** Let $m \geq 1$ and $s \geq 0$. The *tuned ideal* of $(I, m)$ at level $s$ is
$$W_s(I, m) = \sum_{c} \ \prod_{j < m} \bigl(\mathcal{D}^j I\bigr)^{c_j},$$
where the sum runs over the sequences $c = (c_0, \dots, c_{m-1})$ of natural numbers with $\sum_{j < m} (m - j)\, c_j \geq s$. Let $\Lambda_m = \operatorname{lcm}(1, \dots, m)$. The *tuning exponent* is $N_m = 1$ if $m \leq 1$ and $N_m = (m - 1)\Lambda_m$ if $m \geq 2$. The *balanced tuned ideal* is $W(I, m) = W_{N_m}(I, m)$.

The weight $m - j$ is the order that $\mathcal{D}^j I$ has along $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$. So $W_s(I, m)$ collects all products of derivatives of $I$ that have order at least $s$ there.

**Example 8.5.4.** Let $X = \mathbb{A}^2_k$, $I = (x^2, y^5)$ and $m = 2$. The order of $I$ is $2$ at the origin and $0$ elsewhere, so $I$ has maximal order $2$. Here $\mathcal{D} I = (x, y^4)$, $\Lambda_2 = 2$ and $N_2 = 2$. The condition $2c_0 + c_1 \geq 2$ gives
$$W(I, 2) = I + (\mathcal{D} I)^2 = (x^2, y^5) + (x^2, x y^4, y^8) = (x^2, x y^4, y^5).$$
The new generator $x y^4$ is not in $I$. The ideal $\operatorname{MC}(I, 2) = (x, y^4)$ contains $x$, so $H = (x)$ is a hypersurface of maximal contact.

Two properties of tuned ideals come directly from the definition.

**Lemma 8.5.5 (Monotonicity of tuned ideals).** Let $I$ have maximal order $m \geq 1$. Then $W_{s+1}(I, m) \subseteq W_s(I, m)$ and $W_s(I, m) \cdot W_t(I, m) \subseteq W_{s+t}(I, m)$ for all $s, t \geq 0$.

*Proof.* A sequence $c$ with weight at least $s + 1$ has weight at least $s$, so each summand of $W_{s+1}$ is a summand of $W_s$. The product of the summands for $c$ and $c'$ is the summand for $c + c'$, whose weight is the sum of the weights. $\square$

**Theorem 8.5.6 (Properties of tuned ideals).** Assume the two inclusions of the Lemma (Monotonicity of tuned ideals). Let $I$ have maximal order $m \geq 1$, let $s \geq 1$, $r \geq m - 1$ and $j \geq 1$, and write $W_s = W_s(I, m)$. Then:

1. $\mathcal{D}(W_{s+1}) = W_s$ for all $s \geq 0$;
2. $\mathcal{D}^{s-1} W_s = \operatorname{MC}(I, m)$, so $(W_s, s)$ and $(I, m)$ have the same maximal contact ideal;
3. $(W_s, s)$ is MC-invariant;
4. $W_{r\Lambda_m} \cdot W_t = W_{r\Lambda_m + t}$ for $t \geq (m - 1)\Lambda_m$, and $W_{r\Lambda_m}^{\,j} = W_{j r\Lambda_m}$;
5. $(\mathcal{D}^i W_{r\Lambda_m})^{r\Lambda_m} \subseteq W_{r\Lambda_m}^{\,r\Lambda_m - i}$ for all $i < r\Lambda_m$, so $(W_{r\Lambda_m}, r\Lambda_m)$ is balanced;
6. $\operatorname{cosupp}(W_s, s) = \operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ and $\operatorname{ord}_x W_s \leq s$ for all $x$;
7. if $J$ is an ideal with $I^s \subseteq J \subseteq W_{ms}$, then a sequence of blow-ups is a marked sequence for $(I, m, E)$ if and only if it is one for $(J, ms, E)$, and then at each level the two order loci of the controlled transforms agree;
8. the same holds for $J = W(I, m)$ with mark $N_m$, and $(W(I, m), N_m)$ is MC-invariant and balanced.

Lean: `FunctorialResolution.EmbeddedResolution.tunedSheafProperties_of_succ_le_of_mul_le`.

*Proof.* Each part is a separate lemma, and the theorem collects them with the two inclusions. We give the main ideas. Part 1 is a computation with the Leibniz rule: a derivative lowers the weight of a summand by at most one, since $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{D}^j I) = \mathcal{D}^{j+1} I$ has weight one less. It also uses that $\mathcal{D}^m I$ is the unit ideal, because $I$ has order at most $m$. Part 2 follows from part 1 by induction, since $\mathcal{D}^{s-1} W_s = W_1$ and $W_1 = \sum_{j < m} \mathcal{D}^j I = \mathcal{D}^{m-1} I$. Part 3 follows from parts 1 and 2 and the second inclusion of the lemma: $\operatorname{MC}(W_s, s) \cdot \mathcal{D}(W_s) \subseteq W_1 \cdot W_{s-1} \subseteq W_s$. Parts 4 and 5 use that every weight $m - j$ divides $\Lambda_m$, so a summand of large weight splits into a product of summands of weight a multiple of $\Lambda_m$. Part 6 holds because each summand has order at least its weight at the points of $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$, while the summand $(\mathcal{D}^{m-1} I)^s$ has order exactly $s$ there and order $0$ at the other points. Part 7 holds because $I^s \subseteq J \subseteq W_{ms}$ and the controlled transforms of $I^s$ and $W_{ms}$ with mark $ms$ have the same order locus as the controlled transform of $I$ with mark $m$. Part 8 is part 7 with $s = N_m / m$ when $m \mid N_m$, together with parts 3 and 5. $\square$

We read the statement of each of the eleven lemmas and the assembly. The ideas above are the classical ones from [Kollár, Section 3.5]; we did not open each proof.

Now let $(A, \mu)$ be MC-invariant, and let $H_1$ and $H_2$ be two hypersurfaces of maximal contact. In coordinates $H_1 = (u_1)$ and $H_2 = (u_2)$ with $u_1, u_2 \in \operatorname{MC}(A, \mu)$. The map that exchanges $u_1$ and $u_2$ and keeps the other coordinates should preserve $A$. It is not an automorphism of $X$ in general, but it exists étale locally.

**Theorem 8.5.8 (Étale comparison at a point).** Let $X$ be smooth and locally of finite type over $k$, let $(A, \mu)$ be MC-invariant with $\mu \geq 1$, and let $N$ be a list of ideal sheaves. Let $H_1$ and $H_2$ be hypersurfaces of maximal contact for $(A, \mu)$ such that $(H_1) \mathbin{+\!\!+} N$ and $(H_2) \mathbin{+\!\!+} N$ are in strict normal crossing position. Let $x \in \operatorname{cosupp}(A, \mu)$. Then there are an affine scheme $W$, two étale maps $f_1, f_2 \colon W \to X$ over $k$ and a point $w \in W$ such that:

1. $f_1(w) = f_2(w) = x$;
2. $f_1^{-1} A = f_2^{-1} A$, $f_1^{-1} H_1 = f_2^{-1} H_2$, and $f_1^{-1} J = f_2^{-1} J$ for each $J$ in $N$;
3. $f_1$ and $f_2$ agree on the closed subscheme $V(\operatorname{MC}(f_1^{-1} A, \mu))$ of $W$.

Lean: `FunctorialResolution.EmbeddedResolution.nonempty_etaleMaxContactComparison`.

*Proof.* Near $x$ there are an affine open $V$ and two étale charts $c_1, c_2 \colon V \to \mathbb{A}^n_k$ with these properties. They have the same coordinates except one coordinate $x_{j_0}$. We have $H_1 = (c_1^{*}x_{j_0})$ and $H_2 = (c_2^{*}x_{j_0})$ on $V$, and both functions lie in $\operatorname{MC}(A, \mu)$ and vanish at $x$. Each entry of $N$ is the unit ideal or a coordinate other than $x_{j_0}$, and different entries use different coordinates. Such charts exist by the strict normal crossing conditions. Let $P = V \times_{\mathbb{A}^n_k} V$, formed with $c_1$ and $c_2$, with its two projections. They are étale, since they are base changes of étale maps, and they meet over the diagonal point above $x$. On $P$ the two pullbacks of each coordinate agree, so the two pullbacks of $H_1$ and $H_2$ agree, and so do those of $N$. MC-invariance of $A$ shows that the two pullbacks of $A$ have the same stalk at the point above $x$, so they agree on an open neighbourhood. The two projections agree on the zero set of the pulled back maximal contact ideal, because there all coordinates agree. Let $W$ be an affine open neighbourhood of the point inside this neighbourhood. $\square$

We read the statement, the common charts and the use of the fibre product. The stalk comparison for $A$ is a separate lemma that we did not open.

**Theorem 8.5.9 (Étale equivalence of hypersurfaces of maximal contact).** In the situation of the previous theorem, assume in addition that $X$ is quasi-compact over $k$ and $\operatorname{ord}_x A \leq \mu$ for all $x$. Then there are a scheme $V$ and étale maps $g_1, g_2 \colon V \to X$ over $k$, with $V$ quasi-compact and locally of finite type over $k$, such that:

1. both images of $g_1$ and $g_2$ contain $\operatorname{cosupp}(A, \mu)$;
2. $g_1^{-1} A = g_2^{-1} A$, $g_1^{-1} H_1 = g_2^{-1} H_2$, and the lists $g_1^{-1} N$ and $g_2^{-1} N$ are equal;
3. $g_1$ and $g_2$ agree on $V(\operatorname{MC}(g_1^{-1} A, \mu))$.

Lean: `FunctorialResolution.EmbeddedResolution.exists_etale_equalizing_isMaxContactHypersurface` (also `FunctorialResolution.EmbeddedResolution.OrderReductionProperties.maxContactHypersurfacesEtaleEquivalent`, the same statement in the form used by order reduction).

*Proof.* Apply the previous theorem at each point of $\operatorname{cosupp}(A, \mu)$. The order locus is closed in the quasi-compact scheme $X$, so finitely many of the schemes $W$ cover it, in the sense that the images of both maps cover it. Let $V$ be their disjoint union, with the maps $g_1$ and $g_2$ given by the maps $f_1$ and $f_2$ on each piece. Each condition is local on $V$, so it holds because it holds on each piece. $\square$

Section 6 uses the theorem as follows. A sequence built from $H_1$ and a sequence built from $H_2$ pull back to the same sequence on $V$, because the data on $V$ agree. Since $g_1$ and $g_2$ are étale and cover the order locus, the two sequences on $X$ are equal.

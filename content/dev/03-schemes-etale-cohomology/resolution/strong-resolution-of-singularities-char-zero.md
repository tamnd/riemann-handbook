This theorem is Hironaka's [[resolution of singularities|MB52NDA6WN]] in characteristic $0$, in its strong and functorial form, with no hypothesis. [Functorial resolution in characteristic zero](/entry/functorial-resolution-char-zero/) restates it and carries it to the [volume branch](/learn/geometry/#resolution-of-singularities) of the proof.

## Statement

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$. In the Lean code a *[[blow-up|4SFS82N88K]]* of $X$ along a closed subscheme $Z$ is a map $\pi \colon X' \to X$ with the universal property: $\pi^{-1}\mathcal{I}_Z \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X'}$ is locally generated by a nonzerodivisor, and each map with this property factors uniquely through $\pi$. A closed subscheme of a scheme has *strict normal crossings* if at each point there is a regular system of parameters $x_1, \dots, x_d$ such that its ideal is generated by $\prod_{i \in S} x_i$ for a subset $S$.

**Theorem.** There is a rule $R$ that assigns to each reduced scheme $X$, locally of finite type and quasi-compact over $k$, a finite sequence of blow-ups $X_n \to \cdots \to X_0 \cong X$, such that:

1. $X_n$ is smooth over $k$ and $\pi \colon X_n \to X$ is proper;
2. $\pi$ is an isomorphism over the smooth locus $U$ of $X$, and $\pi^{-1}(U)$ is dense in $X_n$;
3. each centre lies over $X \setminus U$, and $\pi^{-1}(X \setminus U)_{\mathrm{red}}$ has strict normal crossings;
4. for each smooth map $\varphi \colon X' \to X$ of relative dimension $r$, the sequence $R(X')$ is the pullback of $R(X)$ by $\varphi$, after we drop the blow-ups whose centre pulls back to the empty set;
5. for each extension $K/k$ with $X_K$ reduced, $R(X_K)$ is the pullback of $R(X)$ in the same sense.

This is Hironaka's theorem (Ann. of Math. 79, 1964). The functorial form is due to Bierstone and Milman (Invent. Math. 128, 1997) and Włodarczyk (J. AMS 18, 2005); see also Kollár, Lectures on Resolution of Singularities (Annals of Math. Studies 166, 2007). The Lean statement does not ask $X$ to be separated.

## Proof

The proof gets resolution from principalization, as in Kollár's book.

1. **Principalization.** `principalizationWithRadicalCenters` is a principalization algorithm for ideals on smooth schemes. Its correctness, the induction on dimension, is [the canonical rules reduce by dimension](/entry/canonical-algorithm-rules-is-correct/). Its functoriality is `isFunctorialPrincipalization_principalizationWithRadicalCenters`.
2. **The affine case.** Embed an affine $Y$ in affine space (`affineEmbedsIntoAffineSpace`) and principalize the ideal of $Y$. The strict transforms of $Y$ are blow-ups of $Y$. With the steps that are isomorphisms removed, they give the affine rule `canonicalAffineResolutionRule`. `isStrongResolution_canonicalAffineResolutionRule` proves properties 1 to 3 for it, from about a dozen lemmas on strict transforms.
3. **Independence of the embedding.** For closed embeddings of $Y$ in $\mathbb{A}^n$ and $\mathbb{A}^m$, an automorphism of $\mathbb{A}^{n+m}$ over $k$ carries one to the other (`closedEmbeddingsRelatedByAutomorphism`). With functoriality of the principalization, this makes the affine rule compatible with étale covers, open immersions, field extensions and smooth maps.
4. **Gluing.** `strongResolutionOfSingularitiesCharZero_of_affineResolutionRule` glues the affine sequences on a finite affine cover into a global rule $R$. The four compatibilities give properties 4 and 5 and carry 1 to 3 from the charts to $X$.

## Marked ideals and their blow-ups

This section sets up the objects of the chapter: blow-ups, the order of an ideal, derivative ideals, marked ideals and sequences of blow-ups that resolve them. Every later section uses these definitions. At the end we prove one estimate: a bound of the derivatives of an ideal by logarithmic derivatives along a smooth divisor survives a blow-up in a centre inside the divisor. Divisor reduction in the third section uses it to show that balance is kept along the sequence. The aim of the whole chapter is to construct, for each marked ideal, a sequence of blow-ups that resolves it, in a way that is compatible with smooth maps and with extensions of the base field.

Throughout, $k$ is a field, and schemes are [[schemes|5A35TYZD02]] over $k$. A scheme $X$ over $k$ is *of finite type* when it is locally of finite type and quasi-compact over $k$. We write $\mathcal{I}_Z$ for the ideal sheaf of a closed subscheme $Z$, and $V(I)$ for the closed subscheme of an ideal sheaf $I$.

**Definition 8.1.1 (Blow-up).** Let $X$ be a scheme and $Z \subseteq X$ a closed subscheme. A morphism $\pi \colon X' \to X$ is a *blow-up of $X$ along $Z$* if the ideal $\pi^{-1}\mathcal{I}_Z \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X'}$ is locally generated by one nonzerodivisor, and $\pi$ is universal with this property: each morphism $f \colon W \to X$ for which $f^{-1}\mathcal{I}_Z \cdot \mathcal{O}_W$ is locally generated by one nonzerodivisor factors through $\pi$ in exactly one way. The ideal $\mathcal{E}_\pi = \pi^{-1}\mathcal{I}_Z \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X'}$ is the *exceptional ideal*, and $V(\mathcal{E}_\pi)$ is the *exceptional divisor*.

This is the universal property of the [[blow-up|4SFS82N88K]] (Hartshorne, *Algebraic Geometry*, Proposition II.7.14). The usual construction as $\operatorname{Proj} \bigoplus_d \mathcal{I}_Z^d$ satisfies it, and a blow-up is unique up to unique isomorphism. If $Z$ is already locally cut out by one nonzerodivisor, for example if $Z$ is a divisor on a smooth scheme, then the identity of $X$ is a blow-up along $Z$.

**Definition 8.1.2 (Controlled transform).** Let $\pi \colon X' \to X$ be a blow-up along $Z$, let $I$ be an ideal sheaf on $X$ and let $c \geq 0$ with $I \subseteq \mathcal{I}_Z^c$. Then $\pi^{-1} I \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X'} \subseteq \mathcal{E}_\pi^c$. Since $\mathcal{E}_\pi$ is locally generated by a nonzerodivisor, there is exactly one ideal $I'$ on $X'$ with
$$\mathcal{E}_\pi^{c} \cdot I' = \pi^{-1} I \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X'}.$$
We call $I'$ the *controlled transform* of $I$ with exponent $c$.

The *strict transform* of a closed subscheme $H \subseteq X$ is the closure of $\pi^{-1}(H \setminus Z)$ in $X'$, with its reduced structure when $H$ is reduced.

**Definition 8.1.3 (Order).** Let $X$ be smooth over $k$, let $I$ be an ideal sheaf on $X$ and let $x \in X$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_x \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$. The *order* of $I$ at $x$ is the largest $r \geq 0$ with $I_x \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_x^r$. We write $\operatorname{ord}_x I$. For $m \geq 0$ the *order locus* is
$$\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m) = \{\, x \in X : \operatorname{ord}_x I \geq m \,\}.$$

For $m \geq 1$ the order locus lies in the support of $V(I)$. For $m = 1$ it is that support.

**Definition 8.1.4 (Derivative ideals).** Let $X$ be smooth over $k$ and $I$ an ideal sheaf on $X$. On an affine open $U = \operatorname{Spec} R$ let $\mathcal{D}(I)(U)$ be the ideal of $R$ generated by $I(U)$ and by all $\partial(f)$, with $f \in I(U)$ and $\partial$ a $k$-[[derivation|3J89REV9WQ]] of $R$. These ideals glue to an ideal sheaf $\mathcal{D}(I) \supseteq I$. Let $\mathcal{D}^0 I = I$ and $\mathcal{D}^{i+1} I = \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{D}^i I)$. For a closed subscheme $H$, the *logarithmic derivative ideal* $\mathcal{D}_H(I)$ is defined in the same way with only the derivations $\partial$ that map $\mathcal{I}_H(U)$ into itself, and $\mathcal{D}_H^i$ is its $i$-th iterate.

In local coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_d$ on a smooth $X$, the derivations are the combinations of $\partial/\partial x_j$. So $\mathcal{D}(I)$ is generated by $I$ and the first partial derivatives of its elements. If $H = V(x_1)$, the derivations that preserve $\mathcal{I}_H$ are generated by $x_1 \partial/\partial x_1$ and $\partial/\partial x_j$ for $j \geq 2$.

**Definition 8.1.5 (Strict normal crossings).** Let $X$ be smooth over $k$. A list $E = (E_1, \dots, E_r)$ of ideal sheaves is in *strict normal crossing position* if, near each point, there are local coordinates in which each $E_i$ that is not the unit ideal is generated by one coordinate, with different coordinates for different $i$. A closed subscheme $Z$ has *strict normal crossings with* $E$ if, near each point, one set of local coordinates puts both $Z$ and the $E_i$ in this form, with $Z$ cut out by some of the coordinates.

Entries of $E$ that are the unit ideal are allowed. They describe the empty divisor. Two lists have the *same nontrivial part* if they agree after we delete their unit entries. This notion appears in every compatibility statement of the chapter: a pullback can make a boundary divisor empty, and the lists must still be compared.

**Definition 8.1.6 (Marked ideal).** A *marked ideal* is a tuple $T = (k, X, I, m, E)$ of a field $k$, a scheme $X$ over $k$, an ideal sheaf $I$ on $X$, an integer $m$ (the *mark*) and a list $E$ of ideal sheaves on $X$ (the *boundary*). It is *admissible* if $\operatorname{char} k = 0$, $X$ is [[smooth|EXKGG9X6J0]] and of finite type over $k$, $I$ is nonzero on each irreducible component of $X$, $m \geq 1$, and $E$ is in strict normal crossing position. Let $\delta(T) = \dim X + 1$ if $X$ is not empty, and $\delta(\varnothing) = 0$. The marked ideal is *bounded by its mark* if $\operatorname{ord}_x I \leq m$ for all $x \in X$. It is *balanced* if
$$(\mathcal{D}^i I)^m \subseteq I^{m-i} \qquad \text{for all } 0 \leq i < m.$$

Balance is a condition of homogeneity. If $I = J^a$ for a smooth hypersurface $V(J)$ and $m = a$, then $\mathcal{D}^i I \subseteq J^{a-i}$, so $(\mathcal{D}^i I)^a \subseteq J^{a(a-i)} = I^{a-i}$ and $T$ is balanced.

**Definition 8.1.7 (Marked sequence).** Let $T = (k, X, I, m, E)$ be admissible. A *marked sequence of blow-ups* for $T$ is a sequence
$$X = X_0 \xleftarrow{\pi_0} X_1 \xleftarrow{\pi_1} \cdots \xleftarrow{\pi_{n-1}} X_n$$
with ideal sheaves $I_i$ and lists $E_i$ on $X_i$, where $I_0 = I$, $E_0 = E$, and for each $i < n$:

1. $\pi_i$ is a blow-up along a closed subscheme $Z_i \subseteq X_i$ (the *centre*), which is smooth over $k$ and has strict normal crossings with $E_i$;
2. $Z_i \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(I_i, m)$;
3. $I_{i+1}$ is the controlled transform of $I_i$ with exponent $m$, so $\mathcal{E}_{\pi_i}^m \cdot I_{i+1} = \pi_i^{-1} I_i \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X_{i+1}}$;
4. $E_{i+1}$ is the list of the strict transforms of the entries of $E_i$, followed by $\mathcal{E}_{\pi_i}$.

The number $n$ is the *length*. The sequence *resolves* $T$ if $\operatorname{cosupp}(I_n, m) = \varnothing$. The *boundary locus* at level $i$ is the union of the supports of the entries of $E_i$.

Condition 2 makes condition 3 possible: a smooth centre inside the locus of order at least $m$ gives $I_i \subseteq \mathcal{I}_{Z_i}^m$. A resolution with $m = 1$ makes $I_n$ the unit ideal, so $\pi^{-1}I \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X_n}$ becomes a product of powers of the exceptional ideals. This is principalization.

**Example 8.1.8.** Let $X = \mathbb{A}^2_k = \operatorname{Spec} k[x, y]$, $I = (x, y)^2$, $m = 2$ and $E = ()$. Then $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, 2)$ is the origin $Z_0$. Blow up $Z_0$. On the chart with coordinates $x, y'$ and $y = x y'$, the exceptional ideal is $(x)$ and $\pi_0^{-1} I = (x^2, x^2 y', x^2 y'^2) = (x)^2$. The other chart is the same. So $I_1$ is the unit ideal, $\operatorname{cosupp}(I_1, 2) = \varnothing$, and the sequence of length $1$ resolves $T$. The new boundary is $E_1 = (\mathcal{E}_{\pi_0})$, the exceptional line.

**Definition 8.1.9 (Pullback of a sequence).** Let $h \colon Y \to X$ be a morphism and let $s$ and $s'$ be sequences of blow-ups of $X$ and $Y$. Then $s'$ is a *pullback* of $s$ along $h$ if $s'$ is obtained by pulling back each centre of $s$ along the induced maps $Y_i \to X_i$, after we delete the steps whose centre pulls back to the empty set. It is a *strict pullback* if no step is deleted. An *assignment* is a rule $A$ that gives a marked sequence $A(T)$ to each marked ideal $T$.

For flat $h$, the pullback of a blow-up along $Z$ is a blow-up along $h^{-1}(Z)$. So pullbacks of sequences along smooth maps and field extensions make sense. Functorial resolution asks that $A(T')$ is the pullback of $A(T)$ whenever $T'$ is the pullback of $T$ along such a map.

The next theorem compares two kinds of derivatives before and after a blow-up. Let $H$ be a smooth divisor and $Z \subseteq H$ a centre. A derivation that preserves $\mathcal{I}_H$ lifts to the blow-up as a derivation that preserves the ideal of the strict transform $H'$. An arbitrary derivation does not lift, but it gains a pole of order at most one along the exceptional divisor. The theorem says that an estimate of the form "all derivatives of $K_0$ are logarithmic derivatives of the $K_l$" passes to the controlled transforms.

**Theorem 8.1.10 (Derivative estimate after a blow-up).** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$ and $X$ smooth over $k$. Let $Z$ and $H$ be closed subschemes of $X$ and $E$ a list of ideal sheaves, such that $Z$ has strict normal crossings with the list $(\mathcal{I}_H, E)$ and $Z \subseteq H$. Let $\pi \colon X_1 \to X$ be a blow-up along $Z$, with $X_1$ locally noetherian, and assume that the inclusion of the strict transform $H'$ of $H$ is quasi-compact. Let $m \geq 0$ and let $K_0, K_1, \dots$ be ideal sheaves on $X$ with $K_l \subseteq \mathcal{I}_Z^{m-l}$ for $l \leq m$ and
$$\mathcal{D}^n K_0 \subseteq \sum_{l \leq n} \mathcal{D}_H^{\,n-l} K_l \qquad \text{for all } n < m.$$
Let $K'_0, K'_1, \dots$ be ideal sheaves on $X_1$ with $\mathcal{E}_\pi^{m-l} \cdot K'_l = \pi^{-1} K_l \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X_1}$ for $l \leq m$. Then $X_1$ is smooth over $k$, and
$$\mathcal{D}^n K'_0 \subseteq \sum_{l \leq n} \mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,n-l} K'_l \qquad \text{for all } n < m,$$
where the derivatives on $X_1$ are taken over $k$.

*Proof.* The scheme $X_1$ is smooth over $k$ because $Z$ is a smooth centre with strict normal crossings. Write $\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{E}_\pi$.

First, $K_0 \subseteq \mathcal{I}_Z^m$, and a derivative lowers the order along the smooth centre $Z$ by at most one. So $\mathcal{D}^l K_0 \subseteq \mathcal{I}_Z^{m-l}$ for all $l$. Let $L_l$ be the controlled transform of $\mathcal{D}^l K_0$ with exponent $m - l$. Then $\mathcal{E}^m L_0 = \pi^{-1} K_0 = \mathcal{E}^m K'_0$, and since $\mathcal{E}$ is locally generated by a nonzerodivisor, $L_0 = K'_0$.

Second, the special case of the theorem with $K_l = \mathcal{D}^l K_0$ holds: for $n \leq m$,
$$\mathcal{D}^n L_0 \subseteq \sum_{l \leq n} \mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,n-l} L_l .$$
This case is a separate lemma on one blow-up along a centre inside $H$. We use it as stated.

Third, we use a second lemma on one blow-up: if $K \subseteq \mathcal{I}_Z^c$ and $a \leq c$, then $\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{D}_H^a K) \subseteq \mathcal{E}^{c-a} \cdot \mathcal{D}_{H'}^a K'$, where $K'$ is the controlled transform of $K$ with exponent $c$. Fix $l \leq n$. For $j \leq l$ we have $K_j \subseteq \mathcal{I}_Z^{m-j}$ and $l - j \leq m - j$, so the lemma gives
$$\pi^{-1}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_H^{\,l-j} K_j\bigr) \subseteq \mathcal{E}^{m-l} \cdot \mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,l-j} K'_j ,$$
since $K'_j$ is the controlled transform of $K_j$ with exponent $m - j$, and $(m - j) - (l - j) = m - l$. Pull back the hypothesis for $l < m$:
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal{E}^{m-l} L_l &= \pi^{-1} \mathcal{D}^l K_0 \subseteq \sum_{j \leq l} \pi^{-1}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_H^{\,l-j} K_j\bigr) \\ &\subseteq \mathcal{E}^{m-l} \cdot \sum_{j \leq l} \mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,l-j} K'_j . \end{aligned}$$
Cancel the locally principal ideal $\mathcal{E}^{m-l}$, generated by a nonzerodivisor. This gives $L_l \subseteq \sum_{j \leq l} \mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,l-j} K'_j$.

Finally, insert this into the second step and use $\mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,n-l} \mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,l-j} = \mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,n-j}$ and the monotonicity of $\mathcal{D}_{H'}$ under sums:
$$\mathcal{D}^n K'_0 \subseteq \sum_{l \leq n} \sum_{j \leq l} \mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,n-j} K'_j \subseteq \sum_{j \leq n} \mathcal{D}_{H'}^{\,n-j} K'_j . \qquad \square$$

The derivative ideals are `derivIdealSheafIterate` and `logDerivIdealSheafIterate`; in the Lean form $\mathcal{D}(I) = I + (\partial f)$ on each affine open, as above. In the derivative estimate, the bound $\mathcal{D}^l K_0 \subseteq \mathcal{I}_Z^{m-l}$, the case $K_l = \mathcal{D}^l K_0$ and the lifting of logarithmic derivatives are three separate lemmas in the folder `DerivativeIdeal`; we read their statements, not their proofs. The hypothesis that $H'$ has a quasi-compact inclusion is technical. No definition in this chapter asks $X$ to be separated.

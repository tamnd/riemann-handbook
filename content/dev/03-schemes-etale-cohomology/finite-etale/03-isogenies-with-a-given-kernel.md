## Isogenies with a given kernel

Over a field, a finite subgroup $K$ of an abelian variety $A$ is the kernel of the isogeny $A \to A/K$. This section proves a form of this fact for the abelian schemes with extra structure of the Harris and Taylor [Shimura varieties](/learn/geometry/#shimura-varieties-and-toroidal-compactifications), for subgroups that are finite étale and killed by a prime ideal. The main result is Theorem 5.3.4. The method is the one of the two last sections: a finite étale scheme $Y$ classifies the isogenies, a clopen subset $W$ of $Y$ picks out those with kernel $K$, and $W$ turns out to be a copy of the base. The theorem is an input of the construction of quotient objects in the chapter on Galois representations, for the annihilator models of the crystalline comparison.

We fix the data of the Harris and Taylor moduli problem. Let $p$ be a prime and let $D$ be a PEL datum at $p$. It contains a CM field $F$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_F$, an element $\omega$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$ with $\omega + \bar\omega = \mathrm{tr}$ and $\omega \bar\omega = \mathrm{nm}$ in $\mathbb{Z}$, a ring $\mathcal{O}$ that is an $\mathcal{O}_F$-algebra and is free of rank $m^2 [F : \mathbb{Q}]$ over $\mathbb{Z}$, and integers $m, n \geq 1$. The ring $\mathcal{O}$ acts on the abelian schemes of the moduli problem. Let $R$ be a commutative ring. *Base data* $b$ for $D$ on $R$ are: the condition that each integer prime to $p$ is a unit in $R$; a root $\rho \in R$ of $X^2 - \mathrm{tr}\, X + \mathrm{nm}$; ring homomorphisms $t_i : \mathcal{O}_F \to R$ with $t_i(\omega) = \rho$, such that for $i \neq j$ the elements $t_i(x) - t_j(x)$ generate the unit ideal; and, for $i \neq j$ and each homomorphism $\beta$ from $R$ to a field of characteristic $p$, the kernels of $\beta t_i$ and $\beta t_j$ differ. Let $N \geq 3$ be an integer prime to $p$. For a scheme $S$ over $\mathrm{Spec}\, R$, an *object* $o$ over $S$ is an abelian scheme $A$ over $S$ with an action of $\mathcal{O}$, a polarization, a level $N$ structure and the other data of the moduli problem.

**Definition 5.3.1 (The ideal u).** For $r \in \mathbb{Z}$, let $u_r$ be the ideal of $\mathcal{O}_F$ generated by $p$ and $\omega - r$. A maximal ideal $w$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$ that contains $u_r$ is a prime of $F$ over $p$, and $\mathrm{N}(w)$ is its absolute norm.

**Definition 5.3.2 (p-isogeny of type T).** Let $o$ and $o'$ be objects over $S$ and $c = p^e$. A homomorphism $\psi : A \to A'$ is a *$p$-isogeny of degree $c$* if it commutes with the action of $\mathcal{O}$ and with the level structures, the pullback of the polarization of $A'$ is $c$ times the polarization of $A$, and there is $\psi' : A' \to A$ with $\psi \psi' = c$ and $\psi' \psi = c$. The isogeny $\psi$ has *type* $T_j$ for $(r, w)$ if at each geometric point of $S$ of characteristic $0$, the part of $\ker \psi$ killed by a power of $u_r$ is killed by $w$, and its intersection with $A[w]$ has $\mathrm{N}(w)^{m j}$ points.

**Theorem 5.3.3 (Isogeny with a given kernel, noetherian base).** Let $R$ be a noetherian ring with base data $b$ for $D$, let $N \geq 3$ be prime to $p$, and suppose that $p$ is a unit in $R$ and $m = 1$. Let $r \in \mathbb{Z}$, let $w \supseteq u_r$ be a maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_F$, let $j \leq n$, and let $c = p^e$ with $e > 0$ if $j > 0$. Let $S$ be a locally noetherian scheme over $\mathrm{Spec}\, R$ and $o$ an object over $S$ with abelian scheme $A$. Let $K$ be a group over $S$ with a homomorphism $i : K \to A$ that is a monomorphism, such that $K$ is finite étale over $S$, each point of $A$ that comes from $K$ is killed by $w$, each $a \in \mathcal{O}$ maps $i(K)$ into itself, and $K \to S$ is finite, flat and surjective of constant degree $\mathrm{N}(w)^j$. Then there are an object $o'$ over $S$ and a $p$-isogeny $\psi : A \to A'$ of degree $c$ and type $T_j$, such that a point $x$ of $A$ lies in $K$ if and only if $\psi(x) = 0$ and $x$ is killed by a power of $u_r$.

*Proof.* We give the proof in four steps.

First, the theorem of Harris and Taylor on the moduli of isogenies of type $T_j$ ([its statement](/entry/harris-taylor-type-tj-isogenies-representable/)) gives a finite étale $S$-scheme $Y$, an object $o_0$ over $Y$ that is the pullback of $o$, and a $p$-isogeny $\psi_Y : A_Y \to A'_Y$ of degree $c$ and type $T_j$. It is universal: each $p$-isogeny of degree $c$ and type $T_j$ from the pullback of $o$ to an $S$-scheme $T$ is the pullback of $\psi_Y$ along exactly one $S$-morphism $T \to Y$.

Second, there is an open and closed subset $W$ of $Y$ with two properties. A point $y : T \to Y$ factors through $W$ if and only if the $u_r$-power part of $\ker \psi_y$ equals $K_T$. A geometric point $y$ lies in $W$ if and only if this holds at $y$. This is the method of Theorem 5.2.3: the $u_r$-power part of $\ker \psi_Y$ and $K_Y$ are clopen subsets of the finite étale scheme $A[u_r^e]_Y$ for large $e$, and the set of points of $Y$ over which they agree is clopen.

Third, at each geometric point $\bar s$ of $S$ there is exactly one geometric point of $W$ over $\bar s$. Existence: the quotient of $A_{\bar s}$ by $K_{\bar s}$ is an object of the moduli problem, and the quotient map is a $p$-isogeny of type $T_j$ with the right kernel, so it is $\psi_y$ for some $y$ over $\bar s$ that lies in $W$. Uniqueness: two isogenies with the same kernel differ by an isomorphism of the targets, so they give the same point of $Y$.

Fourth, $W$ is finite étale over $S$, because it is clopen in $Y$. A finite étale map with exactly one geometric point in each geometric fibre is an isomorphism. So $W \to S$ has an inverse, and this is a section $\sigma : S \to Y$ with image in $W$. Let $\psi$ be the pullback of $\psi_Y$ along $\sigma$. It is a $p$-isogeny of degree $c$ and type $T_j$, because these properties are stable under pullback. Since $\sigma$ factors through $W$, the $u_r$-power part of $\ker \psi$ is $K$. $\square$

**Theorem 5.3.4 (Isogeny with a given kernel).** Let $R$ be a ring with base data $b$ for $D$, let $N \geq 3$ be prime to $p$, and suppose $m = 1$. Let $r$, $w$, $j$ and $c$ be as in the last theorem. Let $S$ be a locally noetherian scheme over $\mathrm{Spec}\, R$ such that $p$ is a unit on $S$, and let $o$ and $K$ be as in the last theorem. Then there are an object $o'$ over $S$ and a $p$-isogeny $\psi : A \to A'$ of degree $c$ and type $T_j$, such that a point $x$ of $A$ lies in $K$ if and only if $\psi(x) = 0$ and $x$ is killed by a power of $u_r$.

*Proof.* Each integer prime to $p$ is a unit in $R$, so it is a unit on $S$, and $p$ is a unit on $S$. So every nonzero integer is a unit in $\Gamma(S, \mathcal{O}_S)$, and this ring contains $\mathbb{Q}$. Let $R_1$ be the $\mathbb{Q}$-subalgebra of $\Gamma(S, \mathcal{O}_S)$ generated by the image of $\rho$, the images of a finite set of generators of $\mathcal{O}_F$ under the $t_i$, and the coefficients of the relations that make the $t_i(x) - t_j(x)$ generate the unit ideal. It is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, so it is noetherian, and $p$ is a unit in it. The images of the base data lie in $R_1$, and they are base data $b_1$ for $D$ on $R_1$: the last condition is vacuous because $R_1$ contains $\mathbb{Q}$. The morphism $S \to \mathrm{Spec}\, R_1$ is compatible with $S \to \mathrm{Spec}\, R$, and the objects over $S$ for $b$ and for $b_1$ correspond. The last theorem over $R_1$ gives $o'$ and $\psi$, and we move $o'$ back to $R$. $\square$

**Remark 5.3.5.** The hypothesis that $p$ is a unit is strong here. The base data already make every integer prime to $p$ a unit, so with $p$ a unit, $S$ is a scheme over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then $A[w]$ is finite étale, which is why $K$ can be finite étale at all, and the condition of type $T_j$, which looks only at points of characteristic $0$, applies to every geometric point. The theorem is thus about the generic fibre of the Harris and Taylor Shimura variety, not about its reduction at $p$. Since $m = 1$, the count $\mathrm{N}(w)^{m j}$ in the type condition is $\mathrm{N}(w)^j$, the degree of $K$.

**Remark 5.3.6 (Formalization).** Theorem 5.3.3 is private, and only Theorem 5.3.4 has an entry. In Theorem 5.3.3 the ring $R$ has no map to a field of characteristic $p$, because $p$ is a unit in $R$, so the last condition of the base data is vacuous there. In Theorem 5.3.4 it is not vacuous. The moduli theorem of the first step is taken as an input ([its statement](/entry/harris-taylor-type-tj-isogenies-representable/)). The clopen locus $W$ of the second step, the existence and uniqueness of geometric points of the third step, and the ring $R_1$ of Theorem 5.3.4 come from lemmas that we read as statements, not proofs, so the sketches given there are ours. The predicates "killed by", "comes from $K$" and "of constant degree" are not unfolded here.

This chapter proves seven inputs of the étale cohomology that the Galois row uses. The first is a moduli space for the clopen subschemes of fixed rank of a finite étale scheme. The next three give the projective bundle formula for spaces (simplicial presheaves on schemes) on which $q$ is invertible: a Künneth formula for $\mathbb{G}_m \times E$, the case of the projective line by a cover with two affine lines, and the induction on the rank by the blowup of $\mathbb{P}^N$ at a point. The next two show, without Jacobians, that the $q$-torsion of the Picard group of an affine curve over an algebraically closed field is finite: Kummer classes on a proper smooth curve do not change when the algebraically closed base field becomes larger, and a Selmer group of functions with divisors divisible by $q$ is finite modulo $q$-th powers. The last constructs objects of the derived category of étale sheaves on a scheme $B$ that represent relative cohomology, naturally over all étale $U \to B$.

The chapter uses from Volume 1 [schemes and finite étale morphisms](/learn/geometry/#schemes-and-varieties), [line bundles and divisors](/learn/geometry/#line-bundles-and-divisors), [étale cohomology](/learn/geometry/#tale-cohomology), the [Leray-Hirsch theorem in topology](/learn/geometry/#complex-manifolds) as a model, [rings, ideals and modules](/learn/algebra/#rings-ideals-and-modules), [fields and extensions](/learn/algebra/#fields-and-extensions), [roots of unity](/learn/algebra/#roots-of-unity) and [ideals and the class group](/learn/number-fields/#ideals-and-the-class-group). The results enter the proof of [GeoOne](/step/geo-one-def/), the Galois row in weight one, through the cohomology of a tower of Shimura varieties: the projective bundle formula through [Theorem 5.5.3](/entry/pbf-all/), the Picard torsion and the relative cohomology objects through the finiteness theorem [Theorem 3.7.1](/entry/etale-topology-top/), and the clopen subschemes through [Theorem 5.1.4](/entry/exists-finite-etale-representing-clopen-subschemes/).

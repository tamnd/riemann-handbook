## A Rees algebra and the multiplicity symbol

This section and the next prove a statement of commutative algebra about a $\mathbb{Z}$-graded algebra $A$ over a complete discrete valuation ring $\mathcal{O}$. The algebra is given by generators and equations, and it has an $\mathcal{O}$-point $x$. The statement bounds the length of a cotangent module of $A$ at $x$ in weight $-1$ by a sum of valuations of one function $a$ at the points of $A$ over an algebraically closed field. It is used in the deformation theory of the Galois row: by the abstract count of the unitary chapter, [Theorem 7.3 of Volume 9](/entry/abstract-count/), and by the window bound [Theorem](/entry/vwin-of-hypotheses-h-e-alt/) below [VWin](/step/vwin-def/). The proof compares two numbers. Both are values of a multiplicity symbol, an Euler characteristic of Koszul type, on quotients of a Rees algebra built from $A$. This section defines the symbol and proves the lower bound. Section 6.12 proves the upper bound and the main theorem.

### Weighted algebras and cotangent lattices

Let $\mathcal{O}$ be a [[discrete valuation ring|MGM3CNJW9V]] of characteristic $0$, [[complete|G9Q61B8A7C]] for its maximal ideal, with fraction field $L$ and a uniformizer $\varpi$.

**Definition 6.11.1 (Weighted algebra).** A *weighted algebra* is a [[$\mathbb{Z}$-graded|8KB4SQGC0C]] $\mathcal{O}$-algebra $A = \bigoplus_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} A_j$ with an $\mathcal{O}$-algebra map $x : A \to \mathcal{O}$ such that:

1. $A_0$ is a [[Noetherian|0670T59HGE]] [[local ring|MZ02BKPHJA]], complete for its maximal ideal, the map $\mathcal{O} \to A_0$ is local, and $A_0$ has the residue field of $\mathcal{O}$;
2. $A$ is generated as an $\mathcal{O}$-algebra by $A_0$ and finitely many homogeneous elements of weights $-2$, $-1$, $1$ and $2$;
3. $x$ vanishes on $A_j$ for every $j \ne 0$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Alg.IsCO` (condition 1), `UpperTheory.V.Alg.IsWRing`.

The grading is the action of a torus $\mathbb{G}_m$ on $\operatorname{Spec} A$, and $x$ is a point fixed by the torus. By condition 2 each $A_j$ is a finitely generated $A_0$-module.

**Definition 6.11.2 (Cotangent lattice).** Let $\mathfrak{s} \subseteq A$ be the set of $f$ such that $c f \in (\ker x)^2$ for some nonzero $c \in \mathcal{O}$. For $j \in \mathbb{Z}$, the *cotangent lattice* $C_j$ is the image of $A_j \cap \ker x$ in $A / \mathfrak{s}$. For a family $F = (F_i)$ in $A$, let $\langle F \rangle$ be the $\mathcal{O}$-span of the images of the $F_i$ in $A / \mathfrak{s}$. We say that $F$ *spans $C_j$ up to scalars* if for each $p \in C_j$ there is $c \ne 0$ in $\mathcal{O}$ with $c p \in \langle F \rangle$. The *cotangent length* of $F$ in weight $j$ is
$$\ell_j(F) = \operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\bigl(C_j / (C_j \cap \langle F \rangle)\bigr) \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}.$$

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Alg.satSq`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.cotLat`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.cotSpan`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.SpansCot`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.cotLen`.

The module $\ker x / \mathfrak{s}$ is the cotangent module $\ker x / (\ker x)^2$ modulo its $\mathcal{O}$-torsion. So $C_j \otimes L$ is the weight $j$ part of the cotangent space of the generic fibre at $x$, and $C_j$ is a lattice in it. If the $F_i$ span $C_j$ up to scalars, then $\ell_j(F)$ measures how far they are from generating the lattice. For example, if $C_j = \mathcal{O} e$ and $F = (\varpi^3 e)$, then $\ell_j(F) = 3$.

**Definition 6.11.3 (Equations).** A *system of equations* with counts $(r, s, r', s', m)$ consists of $F_0, \dots, F_r \in A_{-1}$, $G_1, \dots, G_s \in A_{-2}$, $E_1, \dots, E_{r'} \in A_1$, $E'_1, \dots, E'_{s'} \in A_2$ and $Z_1, \dots, Z_m \in A_0 \cap \ker x$. Its ideal $I$ is the ideal of $A$ generated by all of them.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Alg.Eqns`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.Eqns.ideal`.

**Definition 6.11.4 (Chart).** Let $\tau$ be a finite set with weights $\operatorname{wt} : \tau \to \mathbb{Z}$. Give a monomial $t^\nu$ in the variables $\tau$ the weight $\sum_t \nu_t \operatorname{wt}(t)$. A *coordinate map* is an $\mathcal{O}$-algebra map $\varphi : A \to L[[\tau]]$ to the [[power series ring|3E8ZG3NJYZ]] such that:

1. for $f \in A_j$, every monomial of $\varphi(f)$ has weight $j$;
2. the constant term of $\varphi(f)$ is $x(f)$;
3. for each variable $t \in \tau$ there is $f \in A$ such that $\varphi(f)$ has a nonzero linear coefficient at $t$ and zero linear coefficients at all other variables.

A *chart* is a coordinate map such that each $f \in \ker x$ whose image has no linear terms lies in $\mathfrak{s}$. A *chart without weight zero* is a coordinate map with all weights in $\{-2, -1, 1, 2\}$. The point $x$ is *smooth with dimensions* $(n_j)_{j}$ if there is a chart with exactly $n_j$ variables of weight $j$ for each $j$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Alg.IsChart`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.IsChart0`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.SmoothPt`.

A chart is a torus equivariant formal coordinate system on the generic fibre at $x$: conditions 3 and the condition on $\mathfrak{s}$ say that $\varphi$ induces an isomorphism of $C_j \otimes L$ with the span of the variables of weight $j$.

### The Rees algebra

Fix a weighted algebra and a system of equations. Consider the three $A_0$-submodules of $A$
$$N_0 = A_{-2}, \qquad N_1 = A_2, \qquad N_2 = A_0 F_r^2 + A_{-2} A_{-2} A_2 .$$
For $\mu \in \mathbb{N}^3$ put $N^\mu = N_0^{\mu_0} N_1^{\mu_1} N_2^{\mu_2}$.

**Definition 6.11.5 (Rees algebra).** The *Rees algebra* is the $A_0$-subalgebra
$$R = \bigoplus_{\mu \in \mathbb{N}^3} N^\mu \, t^\mu \subseteq A[t_0, t_1, t_2],$$
graded by $\mathbb{Z}^3$ with $R_\mu = N^\mu t^\mu$ (and $R_\mu = 0$ unless $\mu \ge 0$). For an ideal $J$ of $A$, the ideal $J R$ is the set of elements of $R$ with all coefficients in $J$. For $f \in N_i$ we write $f t_i \in R_{e_i}$, where $e_0, e_1, e_2$ is the standard basis.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.lat`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.rees`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.reesGr`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.coefIdeal`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.mN`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.mR`.

The equations give a list $\mathbf{t}$ of homogeneous elements of $R$, each with its degree:
$$\begin{aligned} &(e_0, F_i^2 t_0) \ (0 \le i < r), \quad (e_0, G_i t_0), \\ &(e_1, E_i^2 t_1), \quad (e_1, E'_i t_1), \quad (e_2, F_r^2 t_2). \end{aligned}$$
Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.tsF`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.tsR`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.ts`.

Each element of $\mathbf{t}$ has weight $-2$ or $2$ in $A$. The squares are there because the weights $\pm 1$ do not occur among the $N_i$.

### The multiplicity symbol

Let $B$ be a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_B$ and let $R$ be a $\mathbb{Z}^k$-graded $B$-algebra. We call $R$ *standard* if $R_\mu = 0$ unless $\mu \ge 0$, $R_0 = B$, each $R_{e_i}$ is a finitely generated $B$-module, and $R_{\mu + e_i} = R_\mu R_{e_i}$ for $\mu \ge 0$. A *graded module* is an $R$-module $M$ with $B$-submodules $M_\mu$ for $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}^k$.

**Definition 6.11.6 (Multiplicity symbol).** Let $\mathbf{t} = ((d_1, s_1), \dots, (d_n, s_n))$ be a list with $d_i \in \mathbb{Z}^k$ and $s_i \in R$. For a graded module $M$ and $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}^k$, define $\chi(\mathbf{t}, M)(\mu) \in \mathbb{Z}$ by recursion on $n$:
$$\begin{aligned} \chi(\emptyset, M)(\mu) &= \operatorname{length}_B M_\mu, \\ \chi\bigl((d, s) \mathbf{t}, M\bigr)(\mu) &= \chi(\mathbf{t}, M / sM)(\mu) - \chi\bigl(\mathbf{t}, (0 :_M s)(-d)\bigr)(\mu). \end{aligned}$$
Here $M / sM$ has the image gradings, and $(0 :_M s)(-d)$ is the kernel of $s$ with the grading $\mu \mapsto (0 :_M s) \cap M_{\mu - d}$. A [[length|CZSCNZH8VY]] that is infinite counts as $0$. The list $\mathbf{t}$ is *valid* if $d_i \ge 0$ and $s_i \in R_{d_i}$. It is *admitted* for an ideal $J$ of $R$ if
$$\bigl(\mathfrak{m}_B R \cdot R_{e_1} R \cdots R_{e_k} R\bigr)^N \subseteq J + (s_1, \dots, s_n)$$
for some $N$.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.GMod`, `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.Std`, `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.chi`, `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.Valid`, `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.admitted`.

The recursion is that of the Euler characteristic of the Koszul complex $K(s_1, \dots, s_n; M)$ in degree $\mu$: the complex $0 \to M(-d) \xrightarrow{s} M \to 0$ has homology $M/sM$ and $(0 :_M s)(-d)$. Admission is the multigraded form of "$s_1, \dots, s_n$ is a system of parameters modulo $J$": it makes the lengths finite in large degrees. Compare Serre, *Algèbre locale. Multiplicités*, Chapter IV. For a function $h : \mathbb{Z}^k \to \mathbb{Z}$ and a list of degrees, the *iterated difference* is $\Delta_{\emptyset} h = h$ and $\Delta_{d, \mathbf{d}} h(\mu) = \Delta_{\mathbf{d}} h(\mu) - \Delta_{\mathbf{d}} h(\mu - d)$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.dif`, `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.hilb`.

Below, "for large $\mu$" means for all $\mu$ with all coordinates large. We use the following rules for a standard $R$, a finitely generated graded module $M$, and lists that are valid and admitted for the annihilator of the module in question.

**Proposition 6.11.7 (Rules for the symbol).**

1. *(Regular element.)* If $s$ is a nonzerodivisor on $M$, then $\chi((d, s)\mathbf{t}, M) = \chi(\mathbf{t}, M/sM)$.
2. *(Positivity.)* $\chi(\mathbf{t}, M)(\mu) \ge 0$ for large $\mu$.
3. *(Upper bound.)* $\chi(\mathbf{t}\mathbf{u}, M)(\mu) \le \chi(\mathbf{u}, M / (\mathbf{t}) M)(\mu)$ for large $\mu$.
4. *(Quotients.)* If $M \to M''$ is a graded surjection, then $\chi(\mathbf{t}, M'')(\mu) \le \chi(\mathbf{t}, M)(\mu)$ for large $\mu$.
5. *(Small modules.)* If $M$ is killed by a power of $\mathfrak{m}_B R_{e_1} \cdots R_{e_k}$, then $\chi(\mathbf{t}, M) = \Delta_{d_1, \dots, d_n}(\operatorname{length}_B M_\bullet)$ for large $\mu$.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.ruleNzd`, `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.rulePos`, `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.ruleUB`, `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.ruleMono`, `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.ruleSmall`.

Rule 1 is the definition, since $(0 :_M s) = 0$. Rules 2 to 5 are the multigraded forms of the properties of the Koszul Euler characteristic in Serre's book; we do not reprove them here.

### The lower bound

**Theorem 6.11.8 (Lower bound).** Let $A$ be a weighted algebra, with a system of equations with counts $(r, s, r', s', m)$ and $r \ge 1$. Let $\varphi : A \to L[[\tau]]$ be a chart without weight zero with $r$, $s$, $r' + 1$ and $s' + 1$ variables of weights $-1$, $-2$, $1$ and $2$. Let $y_0, \dots, y_{r-1} \in A_{-1}$ and $c_0, \dots, c_{r-1} \in \mathbb{N}$ with $F_i - \varpi^{c_i} y_i \in \mathfrak{s}$ for $i < r$, and assume $F_r \in \mathfrak{s}$. Let $a \in A_0$ with $x(a) = 0$, and assume that $\mathbf{t}$ is admitted for $(\ker \varphi) R + a R$. Then for large $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}^3$,
$$\Bigl(\sum_{i < r} c_i\Bigr) \, 2^{r + r'} \le \chi\bigl(\mathbf{t}, (R / (\ker \varphi) R) / a\bigr)(\mu).$$

Lean: `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.low_holds`.

*Proof.* *Standardness.* Each $A_j$ is a finitely generated $A_0$-module, so the $N_i$ are finitely generated and $R$ is standard over $B = A_0$.

*The leading ideal.* For $n \in \mathbb{N}$, let $\Phi_n \subseteq L[[\tau]]$ be the set of series with no monomial $t^\nu$ with $\sum_{\operatorname{wt}(t) < 0} |\operatorname{wt}(t)| \nu_t < n$. Let $\mathfrak{q} \subseteq R$ be the set of $p$ whose coefficient $p_\mu$ at $t^\mu$ satisfies $\varphi(p_\mu) \in \Phi_{2\mu_0 + 4\mu_2 + 1}$ for every $\mu$. An element of $N^\mu$ has image in $\Phi_{2\mu_0 + 4\mu_2}$, since $F_r \in \mathfrak{s}$ has no linear terms. So $\mathfrak{q}$ is the ideal of elements that vanish to more than the expected order: $R/\mathfrak{q}$ is a ring of initial forms. One checks that $\mathfrak{q}$ is a homogeneous ideal that contains $(\ker \varphi) R$ and every $b \in A_0$ with $x(b) = 0$, that $\mathfrak{q}$ is saturated for nonzero elements of $\mathcal{O}$, and that $(f^2 - g^2) t_0 \in \mathfrak{q}$ whenever $f, g \in A_{-1}$ and $f - g \in \mathfrak{s}$.

*Reduction to $R/\mathfrak{q}$.* Since $a \in \mathfrak{q}$ and $(\ker \varphi) R \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$, the ring $R/\mathfrak{q}$ is a quotient of $(R/(\ker \varphi)R)/a$. Rule 4 gives $\chi(\mathbf{t}, R/\mathfrak{q}) \le \chi(\mathbf{t}, (R/(\ker \varphi)R)/a)$ for large $\mu$. Modulo $\mathfrak{q}$ we have $F_i^2 t_0 \equiv \varpi^{2 c_i} y_i^2 t_0$, so $\mathbf{t}$ acts on $R/\mathfrak{q}$ as the list $\mathbf{t}'$ in which $F_i^2 t_0$ is replaced by $\varpi^{2c_i} y_i^2 t_0$.

*Pulling out the powers of $\varpi$.* The element $\varpi$ is a nonzerodivisor on $R/\mathfrak{q}$, by saturation, and $(R/\mathfrak{q})/\varpi$ is killed by $\mathfrak{m}_B R_{e_0} R_{e_1} R_{e_2}$. The symbol is additive in products, as the Koszul multiplicity is. Applied to the factors $\varpi^{2c_i}$, and with Rule 5 for the small module $(R/\mathfrak{q})/\varpi$, this gives for large $\mu$
$$\Bigl(\sum_i 2 c_i\Bigr) \, \Delta_{\mathbf{d}}\, h(\mu) \le \chi(\mathbf{t}', R/\mathfrak{q})(\mu),$$
where $h(\mu) = \operatorname{length}((R/\mathfrak{q})/\varpi)_\mu$ and $\mathbf{d}$ is the list of degrees of $\mathbf{t}$ with $r - 1$ copies of $e_0$ in place of the $r$ degrees of the $F_i^2 t_0$.

*Counting monomials.* For $\mu \ge 0$, the length $h(\mu)$ is the number of monomials $t^\nu$ in the variables $\tau$ with negative part of the weight $2\mu_0 + 4\mu_2$ and positive part $2\mu_1 + 2\mu_2$. With the given numbers of variables of each weight, a computation with generating functions shows that $\Delta_{\mathbf{d}} h(\mu) = 2^{r - 1 + r'}$ for large $\mu$.

*Conclusion.* Since $(\sum_i 2c_i) \, 2^{r - 1 + r'} = (\sum_i c_i) \, 2^{r + r'}$, the three inequalities give the claim. $\square$

The power $2^{r + r'}$ comes from the squares in $\mathbf{t}$: each of the $r - 1 + r'$ squared equations contributes a factor $2$ to the count, and the factor $2$ in $2 c_i$ is the last one.

**Remark 6.11.9 (Formalization).** The symbol and its rules are in the namespace `Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc`, whose name and folder are unrelated to the content. The bundle of a local ring and a standard graded algebra is called `HidaSetting` ([EulerVanishingBelowDimension.lean](lean:Formalism/SharpRestriction/ValuationFrobeniusEpsilon/LeastPrimeTwists/QuotientShiftIrreducible/NoetherianQuotientCalc/EulerVanishingBelowDimension.lean#L113)); it has nothing to do with Hida theory. Both `chi` and `hilb` take `(Module.length B _).toNat`, so an infinite length counts as $0$; the admission hypotheses keep the lengths finite where the symbol is used. The coordinate map of the theorem satisfies `IsChart0` ([ToDegInjective.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/GradedModulesLength/ToDegInjective.lean#L235)), which drops the injectivity condition of `IsChart`.

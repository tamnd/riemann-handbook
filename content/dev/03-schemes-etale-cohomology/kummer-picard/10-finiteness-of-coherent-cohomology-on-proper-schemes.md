## Finiteness of coherent cohomology on proper schemes

This section proves the finiteness theorem of Grothendieck: if $X$ is proper over a Noetherian ring $A$ and $\mathcal{F}$ is a coherent sheaf on $X$, then every $H^p(X, \mathcal{F})$ is a finitely generated $A$-module. The proof of [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/), the step where the proof splits into its two large parts, uses this result in the form of the [Theorem 6.10.6](/entry/ground-theory-arith-scheme-coh-finite-h-of-is-proper-2/) below. The proof is a Noetherian induction on the support of $\mathcal{F}$. At each step, Chow's lemma and the relative Serre theorem of Section 6.9 compare $\mathcal{F}$ with a sheaf whose cohomology is that of a coherent sheaf on a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^N_A$. The two sheaves agree on a dense open part of the support, and the kernel and cokernel of the comparison map have smaller support.

### Support and finite cohomology

Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring and $X$ a scheme over $\operatorname{Spec} A$. We use the cohomology of Section 6.8, as $A$-modules.

**Definition 6.10.1 (Support condition).** Let $T \subseteq X$ be a set. A sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules is *supported in* $T$ if $\Gamma(V, \mathcal{F}) = 0$ for every open $V \subseteq X$ with $V \cap T = \emptyset$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.Scheme.Modules.SupportedIn`.

Every sheaf is supported in $X$. A sheaf supported in $\emptyset$ is zero. If $\mathcal{F}$ is supported in $T$, it is supported in every larger set. The condition passes to subsheaves and to quotients that are images of $\mathcal{F}$. For a coherent sheaf, the condition says that the closed set $\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}$ is contained in the closure of $T$.

**Definition 6.10.2 (Finite cohomology).** A sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules has *finite cohomology* if $H^p(X, \mathcal{F})$ is a finitely generated $A$-module for each $p \ge 0$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.FiniteH`.

**Lemma 6.10.3 (Two out of three).** Let $0 \to \mathcal{F}_1 \to \mathcal{F}_2 \to \mathcal{F}_3 \to 0$ be a short exact sequence of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules. If two of the three sheaves have finite cohomology, then so has the third. A zero sheaf has finite cohomology.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.FiniteH.of_shortExact₁`, `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.FiniteH.of_shortExact₂`, `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.FiniteH.of_shortExact₃`, `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.FiniteH.of_isZero`.

*Proof.* In the long exact sequence, each $H^p(\mathcal{F}_j)$ sits between two modules that are finitely generated by hypothesis. Over a Noetherian ring, a module $M$ in an exact sequence $M' \to M \to M''$ with $M'$ and $M''$ finitely generated is finitely generated, since it is an extension of a submodule of $M''$ by a quotient of $M'$. $\square$

### The induction step

**Theorem 6.10.4 (The induction step).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring and $X$ a scheme, proper over $\operatorname{Spec} A$. Let $T \subseteq X$ be closed. Assume that every coherent sheaf supported in a closed set $T' \subsetneq T$ has finite cohomology. Then every coherent sheaf supported in $T$ has finite cohomology.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.finiteH_step`.

*Proof.* The scheme $X$ is Noetherian, since it is of finite type over $A$ and quasi-compact. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be coherent and supported in $T$.

*Step 1: a closed subscheme that carries $\mathcal{F}$.* Let $\mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ be the annihilator of $\mathcal{F}$, and let $Z = V(\mathcal{I})$ with closed immersion $j : Z \to X$. Then $|Z| \subseteq T$, and there is a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{G}$ on $Z$ with $j_* \mathcal{G} \cong \mathcal{F}$. So $\mathcal{F}$ is supported in $|Z|$. If $|Z| \ne T$, then the hypothesis applies to $T' = |Z|$ and we are done. If $Z$ is empty, then $\mathcal{F} = 0$. So we may assume $|Z| = T$ and $Z \neq \emptyset$.

*Step 2: one irreducible component.* The scheme $Z$ is proper over $A$ and Noetherian. Let $c : Z_1 \to Z$ be the closed immersion of an irreducible component. There is a nonempty open $O \subseteq Z$ over which $c$ is an isomorphism: the complement in $Z$ of the other components. The scheme $Z_1$ is irreducible and proper over $A$.

*Step 3: Chow's lemma.* By Theorem 6.9.3 (Chow's lemma) applied to $Z_1$, there are a closed immersion $i : Z' \to \mathbb{P}^N_A$ over $A$, a map $\pi : Z' \to Z_1$ and a nonempty open $U_1 \subseteq Z_1$ over which $\pi$ is an isomorphism. Let $\psi = j \circ c \circ \pi : Z' \to X$. It is a map over $A$. It is proper, since $Z'$ is proper over $A$ and $X$ is separated over $A$.

*Step 4: the comparison sheaf.* The sheaf $\mathcal{G}' = \psi^* \mathcal{F}$ on $Z'$ is coherent. The [Theorem 6.9.4](/entry/exists-relative-serre/) (relative Serre) applied to $\psi$ and $\mathcal{G}'$ gives $n_0$, and Theorem 6.9.2 applied to $\mathcal{O}_{Z'}$ gives $n_1$ such that $\mathcal{O}_{Z'}(n)$ is generated by finitely many global sections for $n \ge n_1$. Fix $n = \max(n_0, n_1)$ and put
$$\Phi = \psi_*\bigl(\mathcal{G}'(n)\bigr).$$
By the relative Serre theorem, $\Phi$ is coherent and $H^p(X, \Phi) \cong H^p(Z', \mathcal{G}'(n))$. By Theorem 6.9.2, applied to the coherent sheaf $\mathcal{G}'(n) = \mathcal{G}' \otimes \mathcal{O}_{Z'}(n)$, these modules are finitely generated. So $\Phi$ has finite cohomology. Since $\psi$ factors through $Z$, the sheaf $\Phi$ is supported in $|Z| = T$.

*Step 5: a good point and a section.* The opens $U_1$ and $c^{-1}(O)$ of the irreducible space $Z_1$ are nonempty, so they meet at some point $z$. Since $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $U_1$, there is $y \in Z'$ with $\pi(y) = z$. The line bundle $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{O}_{Z'}(n)$ is generated by finitely many global sections. So one of them, $s$, generates $\mathcal{L}$ on an open neighbourhood $W'$ of $y$. Multiplication by $s$ is a map $\mathcal{G}' \to \mathcal{G}'(n)$. Let
$$\alpha : \mathcal{F} \to \psi_* \psi^* \mathcal{F} \xrightarrow{\ \psi_*(s)\ } \Phi$$
be the unit of the adjunction followed by $\psi_*$ of multiplication by $s$.

*Step 6: $\alpha$ is an isomorphism near $\psi(y)$.* Let $W_g = W' \cap \pi^{-1}(U_1 \cap c^{-1}(O))$, an open neighbourhood of $y$. The maps $\psi$, $j$ and $c \circ j$ are closed, so
$$\begin{aligned} B = {}& \psi(Z' \setminus W_g) \cup j(Z \setminus O) \\ & \cup (j \circ c)(Z_1 \setminus U_1) \end{aligned}$$
is closed in $X$. Let $W = X \setminus B$. Then $\psi(y) \in W$: a point of $Z' \setminus W_g$ with the same image as $y$ would have the same image under $\pi$, and $\pi$ is injective over $U_1$. Over $W$ the three maps are as good as possible: $\psi^{-1}(W) \subseteq W_g$, $j^{-1}(W) \subseteq O$ and $(j c)^{-1}(W) \subseteq U_1$. So $c$ and $\pi$ are isomorphisms over $W$, and $\psi$ restricted to $\psi^{-1}(W)$ is a closed immersion into $W$. Let $V \subseteq W$ be an affine open. If a section $a$ of $\mathcal{O}_X$ over $V$ pulls back to $0$ on $\psi^{-1}(V)$, then it pulls back to $0$ on $j^{-1}(V)$, since $c$ and $\pi$ are isomorphisms there. So $a$ kills $\Gamma(V, \mathcal{F}) = \Gamma(j^{-1} V, \mathcal{G})$. Hence $\mathcal{F}$ over $W$ is a module over the closed subscheme $\psi(\psi^{-1} W)$, and the unit $\mathcal{F} \to \psi_* \psi^* \mathcal{F}$ is bijective on sections over each open $V \subseteq W$. Multiplication by $s$ is bijective over $\psi^{-1}(V) \subseteq W'$, since $s$ is a basis of $\mathcal{L}$ there. So $\alpha$ is bijective on sections over every open $V \subseteq W$.

*Step 7: the induction.* The kernel $\ker \alpha$ and the cokernel $\operatorname{coker} \alpha$ are coherent. They are supported in $T$, as $\mathcal{F}$ and $\Phi$ are, and their sections vanish over each open contained in $W$. So both are supported in the closed set $T' = T \setminus W$. Since $\psi(y) \in T \cap W$, we have $T' \subsetneq T$. The hypothesis gives finite cohomology for $\ker \alpha$ and $\operatorname{coker} \alpha$. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the image of $\alpha$. Lemma 6.10.3, applied to $0 \to \mathcal{C} \to \Phi \to \operatorname{coker} \alpha \to 0$, shows that $\mathcal{C}$ has finite cohomology. Applied to $0 \to \ker \alpha \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{C} \to 0$, it shows that $\mathcal{F}$ has finite cohomology. $\square$

The induction step uses no Leray spectral sequence and no dévissage by sheaves of the form $\mathcal{O}_Z$ for integral $Z$. The point $y$ and the section $s$ give one comparison map, and the induction hypothesis absorbs its kernel and cokernel.

### The theorem

**Theorem 6.10.5 (Noetherian induction).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring and $X$ a scheme, proper over $\operatorname{Spec} A$. For every closed set $T \subseteq X$, every coherent sheaf supported in $T$ has finite cohomology.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.finiteH_of_supportedIn`.

*Proof.* The space $X$ is Noetherian, so its closed sets satisfy the descending chain condition. So we may argue by well-founded induction on $T$: assume the statement for all closed $T' \subsetneq T$. The [Theorem 6.10.4](/entry/finite-h-step/) on the induction step then gives it for $T$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.10.6 (Grothendieck's finiteness theorem).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $X$ a scheme, proper over $\operatorname{Spec} A$, and $\mathcal{F}$ a coherent sheaf on $X$. Then $H^p(X, \mathcal{F})$ is a finitely generated $A$-module for each $p \ge 0$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.finiteH_of_isProper` (also `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.finite_H_of_isProper`, the same statement for one $p$).

*Proof.* Every sheaf is supported in $X$. So this is the [Theorem 6.10.5](/entry/finite-h-of-supported-in/) on Noetherian induction applied to $T = X$. $\square$

This is EGA III$_1$, Théorème 3.2.1, and Hartshorne III, Theorem 5.2 in the projective case. EGA proves it with the same two tools, Chow's lemma and the projective case, but through a dévissage lemma and the Leray spectral sequence for $\pi$.

**Remark 6.10.7 (Formalization).** The two names `finiteH_of_isProper` and `finite_H_of_isProper` in [ProperFiniteness.lean](lean:GroundTheory/ArithmeticGeometry/SchemeCohomology/ProperFiniteness.lean#L17) differ only by one `_` character and give the same theorem: the second evaluates the first at one degree $p$. The induction of `finiteH_of_supportedIn` runs over the closed sets of $X$ as a well-founded order, which is where the Noetherian property of $X$ enters.

## The relative Serre theorem and Chow's lemma

A proper scheme $X$ over $A$ need not be projective. Chow's lemma replaces it by a projective scheme $X'$ with a map $\pi : X' \to X$ that is an isomorphism over a dense open set. This section gives the two tools that move the results of Section 6.8 from $X'$ to $X$. The first is the finiteness theorem for closed subschemes of $\mathbb{P}^N_A$. The second is the main result of the section: if $\psi : X' \to X$ is a map over $A$ from a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^N_A$ to a separated $X$, then for a coherent $\mathcal{G}$ on $X'$ and all large $n$, the direct image $\psi_*(\mathcal{G}(n))$ is coherent and has the same cohomology as $\mathcal{G}(n)$. The induction of Section 6.10 uses both, with Chow's lemma in the form stated below.

### Closed subschemes of projective space

Let $A$ be a commutative ring, $X$ a scheme and $i : X \to \mathbb{P}^N_A$ a closed immersion. For $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ we put $\mathcal{O}_X(k) = i^* \mathcal{O}(k)$, and for a sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules we put $\mathcal{F}(k) = \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(k)$.

**Definition 6.9.1 (Twist along an embedding).** The sheaf $\mathcal{F}(k) = \mathcal{F} \otimes i^* \mathcal{O}(k)$ is the *twist* of $\mathcal{F}$ by $k$ along $i$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.ProjScheme.twistingSheaf`, `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.ProjScheme.twist`.

The twist depends on $i$. Each sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X(k)$ is invertible, since it is the pullback of an invertible sheaf.

**Theorem 6.9.2 (Closed subschemes of projective space).** Let $X$ be a scheme with a closed immersion $i : X \to \mathbb{P}^N_A$, and let $\mathcal{F}$ be a coherent sheaf on $X$.

1. There is $q_0$ such that for each $q \ge q_0$ the sheaf $\mathcal{F}(q)$ is generated by finitely many global sections.
2. If $A$ is Noetherian and $i$ is a map over $\operatorname{Spec} A$, then $H^p(X, \mathcal{F})$ is a finitely generated $A$-module for each $p$, and there is $q_0$ with $H^p(X, \mathcal{F}(q)) = 0$ for all $q \ge q_0$ and $p > 0$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.ProjScheme.exists_generatingSections_twist`, `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.ProjScheme.finite_H`, `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.ProjScheme.exists_subsingleton_H_twist`.

*Proof.* The direct image $i_* \mathcal{F}$ is coherent on $\mathbb{P}^N_A$, and the projection formula gives $i_*(\mathcal{F}(q)) \cong (i_* \mathcal{F})(q)$. Since $i$ is a closed immersion, $H^p(X, \mathcal{F}) \cong H^p(\mathbb{P}^N_A, i_* \mathcal{F})$ as $A$-modules, for every $p$ (Hartshorne, *Algebraic Geometry*, III, Lemma 2.10). Part 2 now follows from Corollary 6.8.8 applied to $i_* \mathcal{F}$. For part 1, Theorem 6.8.5 gives finitely many global sections that generate $(i_* \mathcal{F})(q) \cong i_*(\mathcal{F}(q))$. They are global sections of $\mathcal{F}(q)$, and they generate it, since the counit $i^* i_* \mathcal{F}(q) \to \mathcal{F}(q)$ is surjective for a closed immersion. $\square$

### Chow's lemma

**Theorem 6.9.3 (Chow's lemma).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring and $X$ an irreducible scheme, proper over $\operatorname{Spec} A$. Then there are $N \ge 0$, a scheme $X'$, a closed immersion $i : X' \to \mathbb{P}^N_A$, a map $\pi : X' \to X$ and a nonempty open $U \subseteq X$ with these properties: the two maps $X' \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ given by $i$ and by $\pi$ are equal, and $\pi$ restricts to an isomorphism $\pi^{-1}(U) \to U$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.exists_chow`.

*Proof.* The proof is the graph construction of EGA II, Théorème 5.6.1. Cover the Noetherian space $X$ by finitely many affine opens $U_0, \dots, U_k$. Each $U_j$ is of finite type over $A$, so it is a closed subscheme of an affine space over $A$, and so a locally closed subscheme of a projective space $\mathbb{P}^{n_j}_A$. The intersection $W = U_0 \cap \cdots \cap U_k$ is a nonempty open set, since $X$ is irreducible. It maps to $P = \mathbb{P}^{n_0}_A \times_A \cdots \times_A \mathbb{P}^{n_k}_A$. Let $X'$ be the closure of the graph of $W \to P$ in $X \times_A P$, and let $\pi$ and $q$ be the two projections. Then $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $W$. Properness of $X$ makes $q : X' \to P$ a closed immersion: on the open part of $P$ over the chart of $U_j$, the scheme $X'$ is the graph of a map defined on all of $U_j$. The Segre embedding is a closed immersion of $P$ into one projective space $\mathbb{P}^N_A$. $\square$

The classical statement also asks that $\pi$ be surjective and projective (Hartshorne II, Exercise 4.10). The form above has no surjectivity. The induction of Section 6.10 only needs one point of $U$ with a preimage, and the theorem gives this, since $\pi^{-1}(U) \to U$ is an isomorphism and $U$ is nonempty.

### The relative Serre theorem

Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring. Let $X$ and $X'$ be schemes over $\operatorname{Spec} A$, with $X$ separated over $A$, quasi-compact and locally Noetherian. Let $i : X' \to \mathbb{P}^N_A$ be a closed immersion over $A$ and $\psi : X' \to X$ a map over $A$. For a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{G}$ on $X'$, the twists $\mathcal{G}(n)$ are taken along $i$.

**Theorem 6.9.4 (Relative Serre theorem).** In this situation, let $\mathcal{G}$ be a coherent sheaf on $X'$. There is $n_0$ such that for every $n \ge n_0$:

1. the sheaf $\psi_*(\mathcal{G}(n))$ on $X$ is coherent;
2. for every $p \ge 0$ there is an isomorphism of $A$-modules $H^p(X', \mathcal{G}(n)) \cong H^p(X, \psi_*(\mathcal{G}(n)))$.

Lean: `GroundTheory.Arith.SchemeCoh.ProjScheme.exists_relativeSerre`.

*Proof.* *The map $\psi$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated.* The composite $X' \to \mathbb{P}^N_A \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, and it equals $X' \to X \to \operatorname{Spec} A$. So $\psi$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, and $\psi_*$ takes quasi-coherent sheaves to quasi-coherent sheaves.

*One affine open.* Let $V \subseteq X$ be an affine open with ring $B = \Gamma(V, \mathcal{O}_X)$. The ring $B$ is Noetherian. The pair $(i, \psi)$ maps $\psi^{-1}(V)$ to $\mathbb{P}^N_A \times_A V = \mathbb{P}^N_B$. This map is a closed immersion: it is the composite of the graph of $\psi$, which is a closed immersion because $X$ is separated, and the closed immersion $i \times \mathrm{id}_V$. The twist of $\mathcal{G}|_{\psi^{-1}(V)}$ along this embedding is $\mathcal{G}(n)|_{\psi^{-1}(V)}$. So the Theorem on closed subschemes of projective space, over $B$, gives $n_V$ such that for $n \ge n_V$ we have $H^{q}(\psi^{-1}(V), \mathcal{G}(n)) = 0$ for all $q > 0$, and $H^0(\psi^{-1}(V), \mathcal{G}(n)) = \Gamma(V, \psi_*(\mathcal{G}(n)))$ is a finitely generated $B$-module.

*The cover.* Since $X$ is quasi-compact, it has a finite cover by affine opens $V_1, \dots, V_m$. Since $X$ is separated, each intersection $V_S = \bigcap_{j \in S} V_j$ for a nonempty $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, m\}$ is affine. Let $n_0$ be the maximum of the finitely many $n_{V_S}$. Fix $n \ge n_0$.

*Coherence.* The sheaf $\psi_*(\mathcal{G}(n))$ is quasi-coherent, and its sections over each $V_j$ form a finitely generated module. A quasi-coherent sheaf whose sections over the opens of one affine cover are finitely generated is coherent on a locally Noetherian scheme. This gives part 1.

*Cohomology.* The opens $\psi^{-1}(V_S)$ cover $X'$, and $\mathcal{G}(n)$ has no higher cohomology on any of them. So the Čech complex of the cover $\{\psi^{-1}(V_j)\}$ computes $H^p(X', \mathcal{G}(n))$ (Hartshorne III, Exercise 4.11). This Čech complex is, term by term, the Čech complex of $\psi_*(\mathcal{G}(n))$ for the affine cover $\{V_j\}$ of $X$, since $\Gamma(\psi^{-1}(V_S), \mathcal{G}(n)) = \Gamma(V_S, \psi_* (\mathcal{G}(n)))$. The sheaf $\psi_*(\mathcal{G}(n))$ is quasi-coherent and the $V_S$ are affine, so the same Exercise shows that this second complex computes $H^p(X, \psi_*(\mathcal{G}(n)))$. The identifications are $A$-linear. This gives part 2. $\square$

The theorem says that the higher direct images $R^q \psi_*(\mathcal{G}(n))$ vanish for $n$ large, and then $H^p(X', \mathcal{G}(n)) \cong H^p(X, \psi_* \mathcal{G}(n))$ as in Hartshorne III, Exercise 8.1. The proof above avoids higher direct images and compares two Čech complexes.

**Remark 6.9.5 (Formalization).** The theorem does not ask $\psi$ to be proper or $X'$ to be separated over $X$: separatedness of $X$ over $A$ is enough, both for the closed embedding over each $V$ and for the affine intersections. The Čech comparison is done once, for a finite acyclic family of opens of $X$, in [AcyclicLeray.lean](lean:GroundTheory/ArithmeticGeometry/SchemeCohomology/ProperCohomology/AcyclicLeray.lean#L140). Chow's lemma is proved in the weak form above, with no surjectivity of $\pi$.

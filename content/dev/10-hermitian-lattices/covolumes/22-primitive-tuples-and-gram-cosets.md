## Primitive tuples and Gram cosets

The mass bounds of the last section rest on one comparison: the set of primitive tuples whose Gram matrix lies in a set $B$ of integral Hermitian matrices has, up to a factor $2$, the same content as $B$. This section proves that comparison. It is the local density statement behind the Siegel mass formula, in the crude form that the proof needs: no exact constant, but a two-sided bound with constant $2$. The user is [Proposition 2.21.3](/entry/cont-prim-scale/), and through it the mass bounds of [Theorem 2.21.5](/entry/mass-bounds/).

Throughout, $F$ is a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic $0$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_F$, uniformizer $\varpi$ and residue field of size $q$. Let $E/F$ be a quadratic extension with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_E$ and conjugation $c$. Let $H \in M_n(E)$ be a Hermitian matrix whose determinant is a unit, and write $h(x, y)$ for the form it defines on $E^n$.

**Definition 2.22.1 (Content).** Let $W$ be an abelian group and $W_0 \subseteq W$ a subgroup. A subgroup $A \subseteq W$ is *good* for a set $S \subseteq W$ if $A \cap W_0$ has finite index in $A$ and in $W_0$, if $S + A = S$, and if $S$ meets only finitely many cosets of $A$. The set $S$ is a *finite union of commensurable cosets* if some $A$ is good for $S$. The *relative volume* of $A$ is
$$\operatorname{vol}(A) = \frac{[A : A \cap W_0]}{[W_0 : A \cap W_0]},$$
so that $\operatorname{vol}(W_0) = 1$. The *content* of $S$ is $\#(S/A) \cdot \operatorname{vol}(A)$ for a chosen good $A$, and $0$ if no good $A$ exists.

The content does not depend on the choice of $A$. If $A' \subseteq A$ are both good, each coset of $A$ in $S$ splits into $[A : A']$ cosets of $A'$, and $\operatorname{vol}(A) = [A : A'] \operatorname{vol}(A')$. Two good subgroups have a good common subgroup, their intersection. The content is a Haar measure on $W$ normalized by $W_0$, restricted to sets that are finite unions of cosets.

We use two pairs $(W, W_0)$. The first is $W = (E^n)^m$, the $m$-tuples of vectors, with $W_0 = \Lambda^m$ for a lattice $\Lambda$. The second is $W = M_m(E)$ with $W_0$ the additive group generated by the Hermitian matrices with entries in $\mathcal{O}_E$. For a tuple $x = (x_1, \dots, x_m)$ let $G(x)$ be the matrix with entries $G(x)_{ab} = h(x_b, x_a)$. A tuple $x$ is *primitive* in $\Lambda$ if every $x_a$ lies in $\Lambda$ and there are $y_1, \dots, y_m \in \Lambda$ with $h(x_a, y_b) = \delta_{ab}$. A lattice $\Lambda_s$ is *self-dual* if it equals its dual for $h$.

**Theorem 2.22.2 (Primitive tuples over Gram cosets).** Let $\Lambda_s$ be a self-dual lattice for $H$ and let $2m + 3 \le n$. Let $B$ be a set of Hermitian matrices with entries in $\mathcal{O}_E$ that is a finite union of commensurable cosets of the Hermitian lattice. Let
$$P_B = \{\, x \in (E^n)^m : x \text{ primitive in } \Lambda_s,\ G(x) \in B \,\}.$$
Then $P_B$ is a finite union of commensurable cosets of $\Lambda_s^m$, and
$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{cont}(B) &\le 2 \operatorname{cont}(P_B), \\ \operatorname{cont}(P_B) &\le 2 \operatorname{cont}(B). \end{aligned}$$

The proof reduces to a count modulo a power of $\varpi$. We take two lemmas as inputs.

**Lemma 2.22.3 (Primitive tuples modulo $\varpi^j$).** Let $2m + 3 \le n$ and $j \ge 1$. Let $R \subseteq \mathcal{O}_E$ be a set of representatives of $\mathcal{O}_E / \varpi^j \mathcal{O}_E$, and let $G_0$ be an integral Hermitian $m \times m$ matrix. Let $N$ be the number of tuples $x \in (R^n)^m$ that are primitive in $\mathcal{O}_E^n$ for the standard form $\sum_i x_i c(y_i)$ and have $G(x) \equiv G_0 \bmod \varpi^j$. Then
$$\begin{aligned} q^{j(2nm - m^2)} &\le 2N, \\ N &\le 2\, q^{j(2nm - m^2)}. \end{aligned}$$

This is the classical count of representations of a Hermitian form by a unimodular one modulo $\varpi^j$: the local density is $q^{j(2nm - m^2)}$ times a product of factors $1 + O(q^{-(n - 2m)})$, and the condition $2m + 3 \le n$ makes this product lie between $1/2$ and $2$.

**Lemma 2.22.4 (Indices of $\varpi^j$).** Let $\Lambda$ be a lattice in $E^n$. Then
$$\begin{aligned} [\Lambda^m : \varpi^j \Lambda^m] &= q^{2nmj}, \\ [W_0 : \varpi^j W_0] &= q^{m^2 j}, \end{aligned}$$
where $W_0$ is the Hermitian lattice in $M_m(E)$.

The first holds because $\Lambda^m$ is free of rank $nm$ over $\mathcal{O}_E$ and $\mathcal{O}_E/\varpi^j$ has $q^{2j}$ elements. The second holds because the integral Hermitian matrices form a free $\mathcal{O}_F$-module of rank $m^2$: $m$ diagonal entries in $\mathcal{O}_F$ and $m(m-1)/2$ entries above the diagonal in $\mathcal{O}_E$.

*Proof of the Theorem.* Since $H$ has a unit determinant and $\Lambda_s$ is self-dual, there is a basis of $\Lambda_s$ in which $h$ is the standard form. We work in this basis, so $\Lambda_s = \mathcal{O}_E^n$ and $h(x, y) = \sum_i x_i c(y_i)$.

Since $B$ is a finite union of commensurable cosets, there is $j \ge 1$ such that $B$ is a union of cosets of $\varpi^j W_0$. Indeed, a good subgroup $A$ for $B$ contains $A \cap W_0$, which has finite index in $W_0$, so it contains $\varpi^j W_0$ for large $j$. Then $\varpi^j W_0$ is also good for $B$. Let $G_1, \dots, G_t$ be representatives of the cosets of $\varpi^j W_0$ in $B$. The content of $B$ is
$$\operatorname{cont}(B) = t\, q^{-m^2 j}$$
by the Lemma on indices.

Now consider $P_B$. If $x$ is primitive and $x' \equiv x \bmod \varpi^j$, then $x'$ is again primitive: the matrix $(h(x'_a, y_b))$ is $1$ modulo $\varpi^j$, hence invertible over $\mathcal{O}_E$, and a change of the $y_b$ makes it the identity. Also $G(x') \equiv G(x) \bmod \varpi^j$ entrywise, and the difference is Hermitian and integral. So $P_B$ is a union of cosets of $\varpi^j \Lambda_s^m$. Each coset meets the box $(R^n)^m$ exactly once. So the number of cosets is
$$\#(P_B / \varpi^j \Lambda_s^m) = \sum_{i=1}^{t} N_i,$$
where $N_i$ counts the primitive tuples in $(R^n)^m$ with $G(x) \equiv G_i \bmod \varpi^j$. This number is finite, so $\varpi^j \Lambda_s^m$ is good for $P_B$, and
$$\operatorname{cont}(P_B) = q^{-2nmj} \sum_{i=1}^{t} N_i.$$

By the Lemma on primitive tuples, each $N_i$ lies between $\tfrac12 q^{j(2nm - m^2)}$ and $2 q^{j(2nm - m^2)}$. Since $2nmj = j(2nm - m^2) + m^2 j$, we get
$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{cont}(P_B) &\le 2\, t\, q^{-m^2 j} = 2 \operatorname{cont}(B), \\ \operatorname{cont}(B) &= t\, q^{-m^2 j} \le 2 \operatorname{cont}(P_B). \end{aligned}$$
The content does not depend on the good subgroup, so these are the contents of the Theorem. $\square$

**Remark 2.22.5.** The constant $2$ is the price of the condition $2m + 3 \le n$. The exact local density is a product over $i$ of factors $1 - (-q)^{-(n - i)}$, and its limit as $n \to \infty$ is $1$. For the use in the mass bounds only a constant independent of all data matters.

**Remark 2.22.6 (Formalization).** The Gram matrix `gramT` ([HermLat.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/SemilocalFrames/HermLat.lean#L27)) has entries $h(x_b, x_a)$, the transpose of the more usual convention; the statement holds for both. The content `cont` ([HermLat.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/SemilocalFrames/HermLat.lean#L20)) uses a chosen good subgroup and is $0$ when none exists, and its independence of the choice is the lemma `commensurableCosetUnionContent_independent_nonneg`. The assumption on $H$ asks only that $H$ be Hermitian with a unit determinant. The count modulo $\varpi^j$ is the lemma `card_primitive_tuples_hermitian_gram_mod_uniformizer`, which is stated for the standard form, not for $H$; the proof `cont_primitive_tuples` ([PrimitiveTuplesHermitianGramCosetContent.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FlagMeasures/PrimitiveTuplesHermitianGramCosetContent.lean#L392)) passes to it by a change of basis.

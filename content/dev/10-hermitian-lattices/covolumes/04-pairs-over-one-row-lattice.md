## Pairs over one row lattice and their genus mean

This section bounds the sum over pairs of terms that share a lattice. Each pair of terms gives a pair triple: two embedded data and an isometry that links them. The first result bounds the weighted sum over pairs by the number of pair triples times two [datum bounds](/entry/sum-weights-per-datum/). The second result averages the number of pair triples over a genus, with the [[Gram matrix|00SPJSDT3Z]] of the lattice fixed up to isometry. The average is the mass of the genus times an explicit pair bound. The third result is the same average in the term setting. All three feed the [pair count from the mass formulas](/entry/pair-count-bound-of-siegel-mass/).

We keep the notation of [the weights of frame classes](/entry/sum-cls-om-le-dat-bd/). The setting has a CM field $E$ with $f$ infinite places, the ranks $k$ and $r$, a level $g$, a word $u$ with its prime $T_u$, the first flag $F_0$ and the terms $t$. Each term has two lattices $\Lambda_t$ and $\Lambda'_t$. For a row lattice $\Lambda'$, the *row weight* $w(\Lambda')$ is $1/\#A(\Lambda')$, where $A(\Lambda')$ is the group of maps on a quotient of $\Lambda'$ induced by the isometries of the flag stabilizer that fix $\Lambda'$. It is $0$ if this group is infinite.

### Pair triples

Let $(W, H_W)$ be a Hermitian space over a number field $K$ with an involution, let $g \ge 1$ and let $L_0$ be a lattice in $W$. For a lattice $M$ write $\operatorname{disc}(M)$ for its discriminant and $M^\vee$ for its dual.

**Definition 2.4.1 (Datum of a lattice).** Let $j, x \in \mathbb{N}$. A *datum* for $(g, L_0, j, x)$ is a pair $d = (M, \Gamma)$ of a lattice $M$ in $W$ and an $\mathcal{O}$-submodule $\Gamma$ of $W \times W$ such that $M$ is integral with $\operatorname{disc}(M) = \operatorname{disc}(L_0)$, $\Gamma$ is the graph of an isometry from $M$ to $L_0$ modulo $g$, the lattice $L_\Gamma$ fixed by $\Gamma$ has $[L_0 : L_0 \cap L_\Gamma] = x$, and $L_0$ is generated by $L_\Gamma$ and $j$ further vectors.

**Definition 2.4.2 (Pair triple).** Let $x, x' \in \mathbb{N}$. A *pair triple* for $(g, L_0, j, x, x')$ is a triple $(d_1, d_2, \eta)$ where $d_1$ is a datum for $(g, L_0, j, x)$, $d_2 = (M_2, \Gamma_2)$ is a datum for $(g, L_0, j, x')$, $\eta$ is an isometry of $W$ with $\eta(M_2) = M_1$, and $(y, y') \in \Gamma_2$ exactly when $(\eta y, y') \in \Gamma_1$. Let $\operatorname{Trip}(g, L_0, j, x, x')$ be the set of pair triples.

### Pairs over one row lattice

**Proposition 2.4.3 (Term pairs over a row lattice).** Assume the standing hypotheses of the setting. Let $W$ be a Hermitian subspace such that the inclusion is an embedding along $F_0$. Let $\Lambda$ be a lattice, let $j', j'' \in \mathbb{N}$ and let $\delta', \delta''$ be labels. Let $S$ be a finite set of pairs $(t_1, t_2)$ of relevant terms with $\Lambda_{t_1} = \Lambda$, with indices $j(t_1) = j'$ and $j(t_2) = j''$, and with labels $\delta_{t_1} = \delta'$ and $\delta_{t_2} = \delta''$. Let $L_0 = \Lambda_0$ be the preimage of $\Lambda + X$ in $W$. Write $\Delta' = \Delta(j', \delta')$, $\Delta'' = \Delta(j'', \delta'')$, and $B'(x)$, $B''(x')$ for the datum bounds with these labels. Then
$$\begin{aligned}
\sum_{(t_1, t_2) \in S} w(\Lambda'_{t_1})\, \omega(t_1)\, \omega(t_2) \le {}& \sum_{x=1}^{\lfloor \Delta' \rfloor} \sum_{x'=1}^{\lfloor \Delta'' \rfloor} \#\operatorname{Trip}(g, L_0, r, x, x') \\
&\cdot B'(x)\, B''(x'),
\end{aligned}$$
where the number of pair triples may be infinite.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.TypeAsm.perRowC` (also `GlobalTheory.T.termPairs_rowLattice_counted_via_embeddings`, a second name).

*Proof.* Each relevant term $t$ has an adapted normal form $N$ for which $\Lambda_t$ is a term, with index $j(t)$, with $T_u$, with $\Delta_N = \Delta(j(t), \delta_t)$ and with $1 \le g_I \le \Delta_N$. The embedded datum of $t$ is a datum for $(g, L_0, r, g_I)$, and the term gives an isometry of $W$. So each pair $(t_1, t_2) \in S$ gives a pair triple with $x = g_I(t_1) \in [1, \lfloor \Delta' \rfloor]$ and $x' = g_I(t_2) \in [1, \lfloor \Delta'' \rfloor]$.

Sort $S$ by its pair triple. The row weight is at most $1$. On the fibre over one triple, the sum of $\omega(t_1)\,\omega(t_2)$ is at most the product of two sums over frame classes, one for each datum of the triple. By [the theorem on frame classes](/entry/sum-classes-le/), these sums are at most $B'(x)$ and $B''(x')$. Summing over the triples gives the claim. $\square$

### The genus mean of pair triples

The next result uses the masses of genera. For a lattice $L_0$, the *orbit sum* of a function $\varphi \ge 0$ over the genus of $L_0$ is
$$\sum_{L} \frac{\varphi(L)}{\#\operatorname{Stab}(L)},$$
where $L$ runs over representatives of the isometry classes in the genus and $\operatorname{Stab}(L)$ is its stabilizer in the [unitary group](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups). The *mass* $m(L_0)$ is the orbit sum of $\varphi = 1$ (see [the mass formula](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#the-mass-formula-for-unitary-groups)).

Let $v = |d_K|^{1/(2f)}/2$, so that $2v$ is the root discriminant of $K$. For $k, g, j, T, x, x'$ put $D_0 = T^{2f}$, $Y = x x' D_0^2$ and
$$M_b = \big(20 f (2v)^2\big)^{2f(6 + c_f)}, \qquad Q = \Big(\frac{32}{g}\Big)^{kf} (4kf)^{4f} Y^7.$$
The *pair bound* is
$$\begin{aligned}
P(k, g, j, T, x, x') = {}& (x x')^{k/2}\, Y^{6j + 10 + 4c_f} \Big( 1 + M_b (2v)^f \sum_{s=1}^{k-1} Q^s \\
&+ \Big( \Big(\frac{2}{g}\Big)^{kf} (4kf)^{4f} D_0\, x x' \Big)^k \Big).
\end{aligned}$$

**Theorem 2.4.4 (Genus mean of pair triples).** Assume the following four statements: Minkowski's bound on finite subgroups of $GL_N(\mathbb{Z})$, the maximal mass formula, the Siegel mass ratio, and that every positive definite Hermitian space over $K$ with a unimodular lattice has an orthonormal lattice. Let $K$ be an unramified CM field with $v \ge 1$, and let $H$ be a [[positive definite|0KX66CZMBB]] Hermitian form on $V$ with $k = \dim V \ge 3$. Let $T$ be a prime number such that every maximal ideal $P$ that contains $T$ has norm $T$ and $T \notin P^2$. Let $L_0$ be an integral lattice with $L_0^\vee / L_0 \cong \mathcal{O}/T\mathcal{O}$. Let $g \ge 2$, $j \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x, x' \ge 1$. Then the orbit sum of $L \mapsto \#\operatorname{Trip}(g, L, j, x, x')$ over the genus of $L_0$ is at most $m(L_0)\, P(k, g, j, T, x, x')$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.DegCnt.PairMean.main`.

*Proof.* Sort the pair triples by the dimension $s$ of the fixed space of the isometry $\eta$.

If $s = k$, then $\eta = 1$ and the triple is fixed by its first datum. The mean over the genus of the number of data with index $x$ is bounded by the mean of primitive sublattices of the genus. With the mass formula and the mass ratio this gives the term $1$ of the pair bound, times $(x x')^{k/2} Y^{6j+10+4c_f}$.

If $1 \le s \le k-1$, then $\eta$ fixes a sublattice of rank $s$. A count of isometry pairs with a given fixed space, together with the mean of primitive sublattices, gives the summand $M_b (2v)^f Q^s$.

If $s = 0$, then $\eta$ has no fixed vector. A count of such pairs, which uses Minkowski's bound, gives the last summand.

The discriminant order of $L_0$ is nonzero, the root discriminant of a totally complex field is at least $3/2$, and the mass equals the orbit stabilizer mass. These facts are used to compare the three counts with the mass. Adding the three cases gives the bound. $\square$

**Corollary 2.4.5 (Genus mean in the term setting).** Assume the maximal mass formula, the Siegel mass ratio, the host conditions and the lattice normalization of the setting, and the standing hypotheses. Let $W$ be a Hermitian subspace with the inclusion an embedding along $F_0$, with $\dim W = k$ and $H_W$ positive definite. Let $\Lambda$ be a row lattice and let $x, x' \ge 1$. Then the orbit sum of $L \mapsto \#\operatorname{Trip}(g, L, r, x, x')$ over the genus of $\Lambda_0$ is at most
$$m(\Lambda_0)\, P(k, g, r, T_u, x, x').$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PairsRow.main` (also `GlobalTheory.T.genusMean_embeddedPairs_le_mass_mul_pairBound`, a second name).

*Proof.* This is the theorem applied to $W$, $L_0 = \Lambda_0$ and $T = T_u$. The host conditions give that $E$ is an unramified CM field, $v \ge 1$, $g \ge 2$ and the conditions on $T_u$. The lattice normalization gives that $\Lambda_0$ is integral with $\Lambda_0^\vee/\Lambda_0 \cong \mathcal{O}/T_u$. The ranks give $k \ge m + 3 \ge 3$. Minkowski's bound and the orthonormal lattices are known in the setting. $\square$

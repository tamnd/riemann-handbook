## Closed statements

This section collects five statements that the later chapters take as inputs and that the tree proves with no hypothesis left open. The first two concern the local cut and the volume of a pinned fundamental domain, and they feed [the hypotheses of Theorem S](/entry/theorem-s-leaves-of-carrier-g/). The next two give a reference frame and transport elements for each datum, and they feed [the construction of a measured setting](/entry/exists-setting/). The last fixes the local scalars at the finite places, and it feeds [the existence of full data](/entry/full-exists-of-loc-tied/). Each proof is a short assembly of results proved elsewhere, so this section states what is assembled and from what.

### The deep inputs of the local cut

Let $D$ be a datum, and let $(\delta, M)$ be local data that agree with $D$ at every finite place $p$: $\delta_p$ is a scalar of $F_p$ and $M_p$ is a local model.

**Definition 5.9.1 (Deep inputs).** The *deep inputs* hold for $D$ if the following is true. Assume the signature condition for the Hermitian space $V$ of $D$. Let $(\delta, M)$ agree with $D$, let $L$ be a local space, and let $u, u'$ be words with $T_u \equiv T_{u'} \pmod{g_S^2}$ that satisfy the prime hypothesis. Let $\mathcal{W}$ be a set of row representatives for a flag $F_0$, in the sense of the section on [row lattices](/entry/row-lattices-and-the-counting-formula/). Then three statements hold:

1. the local step statement: for each term and each place $p$, a local index ratio has the value of a weight, and a unimodularity condition holds;
2. stable approximation: if local unitriangular integral matrices move a given matrix in the required way at every $p$, then global ones exist that do the same and are integral at every $p$;
3. the partner statement: for an eligible term $\gamma$ and a point of the same kind, the partner set of $\gamma$ is in bijection with a set of global lattices that agree with prescribed local lattices at almost all $p$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.Coef.CutLeavesDeep` (also `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.Coef.rowsDatumP_of_deep`, its user).

**Theorem 5.9.2 (The deep inputs hold).** For every datum $D$ the deep inputs hold.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Closed.CutLeavesDeep_outright` (also `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.FrameGlobalPatching.FStar.cutLeavesDeep_of_betaInt`, the reduction).

*Proof.* The reduction `cutLeavesDeep_of_betaInt` proves the three statements from one input. The local step statement comes from `countAt_holds` and `unimodAt_holds`. Stable approximation is `stabApprox`, a global approximation argument over the places. The partner statement comes from `fStarC_of_int`, which needs an integral isometry: for the data of the statement, `betaInt_holds` gives an isometry $v$ of $V$ that fixes the target frame, moves each vector orthogonal to the subspace $X$ of the target frame by an element of $X$, and sends the lattice of $\gamma$ to the target lattice after a rational isometry $\beta_0$. This is the input, and `betaInt_holds` proves it for all data. Applied to $D$, this gives the theorem, and [the row datum from the deep inputs](/entry/rows-datum-p-of-deep/) turns it into the row datum. We did not unfold the proofs of these lemmas. $\square$

### The volume of a pinned domain

Let $X$ be a setting with full data $Y$, and let $u, u'$ be words with the prime hypothesis. Let $t$ be a relevant generic term with $G(t) \neq 0$ at a point $w$, and let $(F', L, U)$ be a normal representative of $t$ at $w$: $L$ is lower and $U$ upper unitriangular, and $Z(F') = L\, W_w\, U$ for the matrix $W_w$ of $w$. Let $\mu_R$ be a measure on $G_\infty$ pinned at $(t, w)$. Let $R(t)$ be the set of $y \in N_\infty(u)$ with $x_t^{-1} y x_t \in N_\infty(u')$, and let $R_O(t)$ be the set `ROinf` of the term, which we did not unfold.

**Theorem 5.9.3 (Volume of a pinned domain).** Assume $g_i \geq 3$ for $i = 0, 1$. Let $K \subseteq G_\infty$ be a measurable set such that each $r \in R(t)$ has exactly one $s \in R_O(t)$ with $s r \in K$. Then
$$\mu_R(K) = \frac{[\Gamma_{\mathrm{int}} : \Gamma_{\mathrm{int}} \cap \Gamma_{\mathrm{aut}}]}{[\Gamma_{\mathrm{aut}} : \Gamma_{\mathrm{int}} \cap \Gamma_{\mathrm{aut}}]},$$
where $\Gamma_{\mathrm{int}}$ is the group generated by the lower unitriangular $a$ with $a W_w b = W_w$ for some upper unitriangular $b$, with integrality conditions at every finite place, and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{aut}}$ is the group generated by the matrices $L^{-1} u^* L$ for $u$ the matrix of an element that stabilizes the lattice of $t$ and both flags.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Closed.volume_determined` (also `Formalism.L2.SG.VolPin.VolumeOddGeneric`, the statement).

*Proof.* The theorem `volume_generic_odd_of_rep` takes two inputs. The first (`dictC_holds`) is an injective homomorphism $\Phi$ from the stabilizer of $W_w$ into $G_\infty$ and an element $c$ such that $c^{-1} \Phi c$ lands in $R(t)$ and $\Phi(\Gamma_{\mathrm{aut}}) = c\, R_O(t)\, c^{-1}$. The second (`covolOne3C_holds`) is a fundamental domain for $\Phi(\Gamma_{\mathrm{int}})$ of measure $1$. Both groups lie in the stabilizer of $W_w$, and both indices are finite and nonzero. Comparing the two fundamental domains gives that the measure of $K$ is $1$ times the index ratio. We did not unfold the comparison lemma or the definition of a pinned measure. $\square$

### Reference frames and transports

Let $D$ be a datum with rank data $r$ and $k$, let $g = g_0 g_1$, and let $(A, L)$ be a unitary group instance with lattice data for $D$. Write $L_f$ for the lattice of $A$ and $h$ for its Hermitian form. A vector $x$ lies in $m L_f$ *away from* $g$ if $c x \in m L_f$ for some integer $c$ prime to $g$. A scalar is $\equiv 0$ at $g$ if it is $g$ times an integral finite adele.

**Definition 5.9.4 (Reference frame).** A *reference frame* for $(A, L)$ is a family of vectors $e_{0,a}, f_{0,a}$ ($a < r$), $z_0$, $b_i, b^\vee_i$ ($i < k$) of $L_f$, and an integral idempotent $e$ with $e \bar e = 0$, such that:

1. $e_{0,a}$ and $f_{0,a}$ approximate the vectors $e_a$, $f_a$ of $L$ at $g$, and $h(e_{0,a}, f_{0,b}) \equiv \delta_{ab}$, $h(e_{0,a}, e_{0,b}) \equiv 0$, $h(f_{0,a}, f_{0,b}) \equiv 0$ at $g$;
2. $h(z_0, z_0) \equiv -1$ at $g$, and $z_0$, $b_i$, $b^\vee_i$ are orthogonal modulo $g$ to the $e_{0,a}$, $f_{0,a}$, and $b_i$, $b^\vee_i$ to $z_0$;
3. $b_i = e\, b_i$, $b^\vee_i = \bar e\, b^\vee_i$, and $h(b^\vee_i, b_j) \equiv \delta_{ij} e$ at $g$;
4. $(1 - e - \bar e) y \in g L_f$ for $y \in L_f$, and the vectors span $L_f$ modulo $g$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.SplitPrimeNormLemmas.ReferenceFrame` (also `PI.ScRefExists.RefData`, a copy with the same fields).

**Definition 5.9.5 (Transport data).** For a reference frame, *transport data* give for each word $u$ an element $x_u$ of the level group $K$ that fixes the vectors $e_a$, $f_a$ of $L$, such that $x_u^{-1} z_u - z_0$ lies in $g L_f$ away from $g$, where $z_u$ is the vector of $u$ in $L$, and such that $x_u - 1$ maps $L_f$ into $m L_f$ away from $m$ for each $m$ prime to $g$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.SplitPrimeNormLemmas.TransportData` (also `Formalism.L2.SG.SplitPrimeNormLemmas.XuExists`, the existence statement).

**Theorem 5.9.6 (Reference frames exist).** For every datum $D$ and every $(A, L)$ there is a reference frame.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Closed.RefExists_holds` (also `Formalism.L2.SG.SplitPrimeNormLemmas.RefExists`, the statement).

*Proof.* The statement `sc_reference_exists` gives a reference datum for any instance, integer $g \geq 1$ and vectors $e_a$, $f_a$ with $h(e_a, e_b) = h(f_a, f_b) = 0$ and $h(e_a, f_b) = \delta_{ab}$, if every prime of $F$ that contains $g$ has exactly two primes of $E$ above it, if the rank is $2r + k + 1$, and if the $e_a$, $f_a$ lie in $L_f$ away from $g$. The splitting condition is `splitAtG_holds`, the integrality is the first half of `intAtGZ_holds`, the rank follows from the dimension formula, and the Hermitian conditions are the relations of the lattice data. The reference datum has the fields of a reference frame. The statement `sc_reference_exists` is proved from a principal ideal property at the primes and the existence of a Weil basis; we did not unfold these. $\square$

**Theorem 5.9.7 (Transports exist).** For every datum $D$, every $(A, L)$ and every reference frame there are transport data.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Closed.XuExists_holds` (also `PI.ScXuExists.Target`, the input).

*Proof.* Fix a word $u$. The input `sc_xu_exists` gives the element $x_u$ for a vector $z$ with $h(e_a, z) = h(f_a, z) = 0$ and $h(z, z) = -t$ for an integer $t \equiv 1 \pmod g$, under the conditions of the previous proof and the integrality of $z$ away from $g$. We apply it to $z = z_u$. The orthogonality is part of the lattice data, and $h(z_u, z_u) = -T_u$. Since $g^2$ divides the modulus of the datum and $T_u \equiv 1$ modulo it, $T_u \equiv 1 \pmod g$. The integrality of $z_u$ is the second half of `intAtGZ_holds`. Choosing $x_u$ for each $u$ gives the transport data. The input `sc_xu_exists` is proved from a local splitting projection and its compatibility with the split unitary groups; we did not unfold this. $\square$

### Local dual agreement

For a finite place $p$ let $\mathcal{O}_p$ be the integers of $E_p$ and $\psi_p$ the local additive character. A nonzero $\delta \in F_p$ is *dual* at $p$ if, for $x \in E_p$, $\psi_p(-x y \delta^{-1}) = 1$ for all $y \in \mathcal{O}_p$ exactly when $x \in \mathcal{O}_p$. It is *pinned* at $p$ if $\delta = g_0 g_1 / g_i$ whenever $p$ contains $g_i$.

**Theorem 5.9.8 (Local dual agreement).** Let $D$ be a datum that is tied at every finite place, that is, with a local isomorphism at each $p$. Then there are local data $(\delta, M)$ that agree with $D$ at every $p$, such that $\delta_p = g_0 g_1 / g_i$ whenever $p$ contains $g_i$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Closed.LocCTied_holds` (also `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.FullLocalDualAgreement.LocalDualAgreement`, the statement).

*Proof.* The construction `locCTied_of` builds $(\delta, M)$ from four local facts: a dual and pinned $\delta_p \neq 0$ exists at each $p$, $E_p$ is unramified over $F_p$ in the sense of `UnramAt`, $\psi_p$ is continuous, and there is a Haar measure on $E_p$ that gives mass $1$ to a fixed order (`LocHaarC_holds`). The first fact is `locDualC_of`, applied to two inputs. The first input (`local_dual_generation`) says that a dual $\delta$ exists when $E_p$ is reduced and unramified, that the dual property is stable under multiplication by units, and that $1$ is dual when the residue characteristic is irreducible in the integers of $F_p$. The second input (`prime_quotient_unit`) says that if $p$ contains $g_i$, then the residue characteristic is irreducible and $g_0 g_1 / g_i$ is a unit. Since $E_p$ is reduced, at a place $p$ that contains $g_i$ the scalar $g_0 g_1 / g_i$ is a unit multiple of $1$, hence dual, and it is pinned because no $p$ contains both $g_0$ and $g_1$ (`not_both`). At the other places the first part gives a dual $\delta_p$, and the pinning condition is empty. We did not unfold `locBody_of`. $\square$

**Remark 5.9.9 (Formalization).** The five theorems are in [CutLeavesDeepOutright.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/SiegelLatticeTheory/ClosedVolumeStatements/CutLeavesDeepOutright.lean#L6), [VolumeDeterminedOfChart.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/SiegelLatticeTheory/ClosedVolumeStatements/VolumeDeterminedOfChart.lean#L13), [CoefWeightedLocHolds.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/SiegelLatticeTheory/ClosedVolumeStatements/CoefWeightedLocHolds.lean#L427) and [FullExistsTiedHolds.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/SiegelLatticeTheory/ClosedVolumeStatements/FullExistsTiedHolds.lean#L71). The name `CutLeavesDeep_outright` starts with a capital letter, as for a definition. The reduction `cutLeavesDeep_of_betaInt` starts with `intro _ δ M hA _ u u' hc hP F0 what₂ hR`, so the signature condition and the local space are never used. The name `VolumeOddGeneric` says "odd", but the hypothesis is $g_i \geq 3$; `volume_determined_of_chart` in the same file is the same proof with the chart statement as a hypothesis. The reference datum of `sc_reference_exists` is a separate structure with the same nineteen fields as `ReferenceFrame`, and the proof copies them one by one. The predicates `ZMod'` and `OneOff` are also defined twice, in HidaSetting.lean and in the files of the two inputs. `RefExists_holds` uses only the first half of `intAtGZ_holds`. The name `LocCTied_holds` does not match its statement, `LocalDualAgreement`, and the statement keeps only the agreement and the pinning, not the dual property.

## Notes and references

This chapter feeds the local matching step [/step/theta-local-matching/](/step/theta-local-matching/) and the step on [nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/). Its archimedean constants enter the orbit volumes of the [mass formula](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#the-mass-formula-for-unitary-groups), its finite place volumes enter the [local densities](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#local-densities), and its closed statements are the hypotheses of Theorem S and of the existence of a measured setting with full data.

For Haar measure, modular functions and quotient measures, see Folland, *A Course in Abstract Harmonic Analysis*, Chapter 2, in particular §2.2 and §2.6. For integration on Lie groups and the Jacobian of the big cell, see Knapp, *Lie Groups Beyond an Introduction*, Chapter VIII. The volume of a compact Lie group for the metric of the trace form is the formula of Macdonald, "The volume of a compact Lie group", Invent. Math. 56 (1980), 93 to 95. The Tamagawa normalization of measures on adelic groups is in Weil, *Adeles and Algebraic Groups*, Chapter II. For strong approximation see Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory*, Chapter 7, §7.4.

For lattices over local fields and Witt's theorem see O'Meara, *Introduction to Quadratic Forms*, Chapter IX for the integral theory over local fields. The classification of Hermitian lattices over local fields, and the splitting used in the section on row lattices, are in Jacobowitz, "Hermitian forms over local fields", Amer. J. Math. 84 (1962), 441 to 465. The local densities of unimodular Hermitian lattices and the exact mass formula are in Gan, Hanke and Yu, "On an exact mass formula of Shimura", Duke Math. J. 107 (2001), 103 to 133.

Multiplicity one for Whittaker models over local fields is due to Gelfand and Kazhdan, "Representations of the group GL(n, K) where K is a local field", in *Lie Groups and Their Representations*, Halsted, 1975, 95 to 118, and to Shalika, "The multiplicity one theorem for GL_n", Ann. of Math. 100 (1974), 171 to 193. The finite field statement used in the section on Whittaker averages is the analogue for $\mathrm{GL}_k(\mathbb{F}_q)$.

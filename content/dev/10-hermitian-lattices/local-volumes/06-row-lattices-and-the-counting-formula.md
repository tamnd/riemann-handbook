## Row lattices and the counting formula

The finite part of the normalizing constant contains a weighted count of lattices that contain a fixed flag. We show that the row lattices of a flag correspond, class by class, to the unimodular lattices of a smaller Hermitian space that contain a fixed vector, so that the weighted count is a mass of the [unitary mass formula](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#the-mass-formula-for-unitary-groups). We also prove the norm identity for the lattice of a pair of flags. Both results feed the [normalizing constant from the local leaves](/entry/nc-tie-c-of-leaves/).

### Flags and row lattices

Let $E$ be a CM field with totally real subfield $F$, and let $V$ be the Hermitian space of the setting, with form $h$ and standard lattice $L_0$. We assume that $E$ is *unramified over* $F$ in the following sense: $E$ is totally complex, complex conjugation commutes with every embedding, and for every maximal ideal $P$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$ there is $\theta \in \mathcal{O}_E$ with $\theta - \bar\theta \notin P$. The setting gives integers $r$, $k$ and $m$ with $m + 3 \leq k$, a positive integer $g_S$, and to each word $u$ a prime $T_u > 0$. Let $\mathfrak{d}$ be the different of $F$, extended to an ideal of $\mathcal{O}_E$.

**Definition 5.6.1 (Flag).** Let $T \in E$. A *flag* of rank $r$ and norm $T$ is a linearly independent family $e_0, \dots, e_{r-1}$ of vectors of $V$ with $h(e_a, e_b) = 0$ for all $a, b$, together with a vector $z$ with $h(e_a, z) = 0$ for all $a$ and $h(z, z) = -T$. Its span $X = \langle e_0, \dots, e_{r-1} \rangle$ is totally isotropic, and its stabilizer $\mathrm{Stab}(F_0)$ is the group of isometries $\gamma$ of $V$ that fix $z$ and map each partial span $X_j$ of the flag onto itself.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.S.FlagG`.

Fix a word $u$ and a flag $F_0$ of norm $T_u$. Its *lattice part* is the $\mathcal{O}_E$-module $X_S(F_0)$ generated by the vectors $x e_a$ with $x \in \mathfrak{d}^{-(r - a)}$.

**Definition 5.6.2 (Row lattice).** A lattice $\Lambda$ of $V$ is a *row lattice* for $F_0$ if it is in the genus of $L_0$, it is unimodular, it contains $z$, it satisfies the fixed convention at $g_S$, and $\Lambda \cap X = X_S(F_0)$. A finite set $s$ of row lattices is a *set of row representatives* if every row lattice is $\gamma \Lambda'$ for exactly one $\Lambda' \in s$ and some $\gamma \in \mathrm{Stab}(F_0)$. The *row weight* of $\Lambda$ is $w(\Lambda) = 1 / \# \mathrm{Aut}_z(\Lambda)$, where $\mathrm{Aut}_z(\Lambda)$ is the set of maps on $W$ (defined below) that are induced by the $\gamma \in \mathrm{Stab}(F_0)$ with $\gamma \Lambda = \Lambda$. The *row mass* of $s$ is $\mathrm{mass}(s) = \sum_{\Lambda \in s} w(\Lambda)$.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.S.IsRowLat` (also `Formalism.MainNotions.S.IsRowReps`, the representatives).

The fixed convention says that at each prime $P$ the localization of $\Lambda \cap X$ is spanned by multiples $c_a e_a$, with $c_a = 1$ when $g_S \in P$, and that $z$ is primitive in the part of $\Lambda$ orthogonal to $X$.

### The reduced space

Let $X^\perp$ be the orthogonal complement of $X$. Since $X$ is totally isotropic, $X \subseteq X^\perp$, and $h$ induces a Hermitian form $h_W$ on $W = X^\perp / X$. Let $\bar z$ be the image of $z$ in $W$; then $h_W(\bar z, \bar z) = -T_u$. For a lattice $\Lambda$ of $V$ let
$$M(\Lambda) = \text{the image of } \Lambda \cap X^\perp \text{ in } W.$$
Let $W_{\bar z}$ be the orthogonal complement of $\bar z$ in $W$. The complement is totally positive definite and has dimension $k$ over $E$; this is proved in the Lean code as `anisotropic_complement_definite`.

**Definition 5.6.3 (Glue lattice).** A lattice $M$ of $W$ is a *glue lattice* if it is unimodular for $h_W$, it contains $\bar z$, and $\bar z$ is primitive in $M$: if $c \bar z \in M$ with $c \in E$, then $c \in \mathcal{O}_E$. Let $H_{\bar z}$ be the group of isometries of $W$ that fix $\bar z$, and let $\mathrm{Stab}_{\bar z}(M)$ be its stabilizer of $M$. A finite set $s$ of glue lattices is a *set of $H_{\bar z}$-representatives* if every glue lattice is $\sigma M'$ for exactly one $M' \in s$ and some $\sigma \in H_{\bar z}$.

Lean: `PI.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.CntFormula.RowsMassW.IsGlueLat` (also `PI.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.CntFormula.RowsMassW.IsHzReps`, the representatives).

For a row lattice $\Lambda$, the lattice $M(\Lambda)$ is a glue lattice (`row_lattice_mass_holds`). The next three results say that $M$ is a bijection on classes.

**Theorem 5.6.4 (Row surjectivity).** Let $u$ be a word, let $F_0$ be a flag of norm $T_u$, and let $M$ be a glue lattice in $W$. Then there is a row lattice $\Lambda$ for $F_0$ with $M(\Lambda) = M$.

Lean: `PI.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.CntFormula.RowsMassW.row_surjective`.

*Proof.* We use one fact about the genus. Over a CM field unramified over $F$ in the sense above, two unimodular lattices of the same nondegenerate Hermitian space are in the same [genus](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#genus-class-and-spinor-genus); this is `unimodular_one_genus`. Choose a partner frame $f_0, \dots, f_{r-1}$ for $F_0$: vectors with $h(e_a, f_b) = \delta_{ab}$, $h(f_a, f_b) = 0$ and $h(f_a, z) = 0$. Write $\mathfrak{a}_a = \mathfrak{d}^{-(r - a)}$ and $p(v) = v - \sum_a h(e_a, v) f_a$. Let $\Lambda$ be the set of $v \in V$ such that $\overline{h(e_a, v)} \in \mathfrak{a}_a^{-1}$ for all $a$, $p(v)$ lies in $X^\perp$ with image in $M$, and $h(f_a, p(v)) \in \mathfrak{a}_a$ for all $a$. Then $\Lambda$ is a lattice, it is unimodular, it contains $z$, $\Lambda \cap X = X_S(F_0)$, $z$ is primitive in the part of $\Lambda$ orthogonal to $X$, and $M(\Lambda) = M$. The fixed convention holds since $X_S(F_0)$ has local generators $c_a e_a$ with $c_a = 1$ at the primes that contain $g_S$. Since $\Lambda$ and $L_0$ are unimodular, the genus fact puts $\Lambda$ in the genus of $L_0$. So $\Lambda$ is a row lattice with $M(\Lambda) = M$. $\square$

### Classes

**Definition 5.6.5 (Class correspondence).** The *class correspondence* holds for $F_0$ if, for any two row lattices $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda'$,
$$\bigl(\exists \gamma \in \mathrm{Stab}(F_0),\ \gamma \Lambda = \Lambda'\bigr) \iff \bigl(\exists \sigma \in H_{\bar z},\ \sigma M(\Lambda) = M(\Lambda')\bigr).$$

Lean: `PI.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.CntFormula.RowsMassW.RowClass`.

Two lattice facts reduce the class correspondence to a unit condition. The first is a dual frame: every row lattice $\Lambda$ has vectors $g_0, \dots, g_{r-1}$ with $h(e_a, g_b) = \delta_{ab}$ such that the projection built from the $g_b$ maps $\Lambda$ into itself. The second is a trace one element: there is $t \in \mathcal{O}_E$ with $t + \bar t = 1$. It exists since $E$ is unramified over $F$.

**Theorem 5.6.6 (Class correspondence from the unit condition).** Assume that every row lattice $\Lambda$ for every flag $F_0$ contains a vector $l_0 \in \Lambda \cap X^\perp$ with $h(l_0, z) = 1$. Then the class correspondence holds for every flag.

Lean: `PI.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.CntFormula.RowsMassW.rowClass_of_zunit`.

*Proof.* We first show that every row lattice $\Lambda$ has a *split frame*: vectors $f_0, \dots, f_{r-1}$ with $h(e_a, f_b) = \delta_{ab}$, $h(f_a, f_b) = 0$ and $h(f_a, z) = 0$, such that every $v \in \Lambda$ is a sum $v = x + w + y$ with $x \in \Lambda \cap X$, $w \in \Lambda$ orthogonal to all $e_a$ and $f_a$, and $y \in \Lambda$ in the span of the $f_a$. Take the dual frame $g$ of $\Lambda$, the vector $l_0$ of the hypothesis, and $t$ with $t + \bar t = 1$. Replace $g_b$ by $g_b - h(z, g_b) l_0$; the new vectors are still dual to the $e_a$, they are orthogonal to $z$, and the associated projection still maps $\Lambda$ into itself, since $z, l_0 \in \Lambda$. A correction with $t$ then makes the frame isotropic and keeps the other properties. This gives the split frame. The class correspondence follows from the split frames of $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda'$ (`rowClass_of_split`): the split identifies $\Lambda \cap X^\perp$ modulo $\Lambda \cap X$ with $M(\Lambda)$, so an element of $\mathrm{Stab}(F_0)$ that maps $\Lambda$ to $\Lambda'$ induces an element of $H_{\bar z}$ that maps $M(\Lambda)$ to $M(\Lambda')$, and an element of $H_{\bar z}$ lifts back through the split frames. $\square$

**Theorem 5.6.7 (Class correspondence).** For every word $u$ and every flag $F_0$ of norm $T_u$, the class correspondence holds.

Lean: `PI.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.CntFormula.RowsMassW.row_class_holds`.

*Proof.* This is the [class correspondence from the unit condition](/entry/row-class-of-zunit/) applied to the unit condition, which holds for every row lattice (`row_unit_condition`). $\square$

### The row mass

The row weight and the stabilizer of the glue lattice agree: for a row lattice $\Lambda$, $\# \mathrm{Aut}_z(\Lambda) = \# \mathrm{Stab}_{\bar z}(M(\Lambda))$ (`row_weight_formula`). The last input is the mass formula for glue lattices. Let $\eta(\mathfrak{p}) = -1$ if $\mathfrak{p} \mathcal{O}_E$ is prime and $\eta(\mathfrak{p}) = 1$ in the other case. For $j \geq 1$ let $c_j = c_1 \prod_{i=2}^{j} \rho_E(i)$, where $c_1$ is the mass constant of rank one and $\rho_E(i)$ is the ratio of consecutive masses in the mass formula for unimodular lattices.

**Definition 5.6.8 (Binary representation hypothesis).** The *binary representation hypothesis* holds if for every CM field $K$ unramified over its real subfield and all totally positive $a, b, n$ in the real subfield, there are $s, t \in K$ with $a s \bar s + b t \bar t = n$.

Lean: `Formalism.HermitianLattices.Ext.L3.HermRepUnram`.

**Lemma 5.6.9 (Mass of glue lattices).** Assume the binary representation hypothesis. Let $W$ be a Hermitian space over $E$ with a vector $\bar z$ with $h_W(\bar z, \bar z) = -t$, $t$ a positive integer, such that $W_{\bar z}$ is totally positive definite of dimension $j \geq 1$. Assume that a glue lattice exists, and let $s$ be a set of $H_{\bar z}$-representatives. Then
$$\sum_{M \in s} \frac{1}{\# \mathrm{Stab}_{\bar z}(M)} = c_j \, t^{j [F : \mathbb{Q}]} \prod_{\mathfrak{p} \ni t} \bigl(1 - \eta(\mathfrak{p})^{j} \, N\mathfrak{p}^{-j}\bigr),$$
where the product runs over the maximal ideals $\mathfrak{p}$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$ that contain $t$.

Lean: `PI.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.CntFormula.RowsMassW.GluedLatticeMass.glued_lattice_mass_formula`.

**Definition 5.6.10 (Row mass formula).** The *row mass formula* holds if for every setting, all words $u$ and $u'$ with $T_u \equiv T_{u'} \pmod{g_S^2}$, every flag $F_0$ of norm $T_u$ and every set $s$ of row representatives,
$$\mathrm{mass}(s) = c_k \, T_u^{k [F : \mathbb{Q}]} \prod_{\mathfrak{p} \ni T_u} \bigl(1 - \eta(\mathfrak{p})^{k} \, N\mathfrak{p}^{-k}\bigr).$$

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.Cnt.RowsMass`.

**Theorem 5.6.11 (Row mass).** Assume the binary representation hypothesis. Then the row mass formula holds.

Lean: `PI.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.CntFormula.RowsMassW.row_lattice_mass`.

*Proof.* Fix $u$, $F_0$ and $s$. The space $W$ with the vector $\bar z$ satisfies the hypotheses of the lemma on glue lattices with $t = T_u$ and $j = k$: the field $E$ is unramified over $F$, $h_W(\bar z, \bar z) = -T_u$ with $T_u > 0$ since $T_u$ is prime, and $W_{\bar z}$ is totally positive definite of dimension $k$.

The map $M$ is injective on $s$: if $M(\Lambda_1) = M(\Lambda_2)$, the [class correspondence](/entry/row-class-holds/) with $\sigma = 1$ gives $\gamma \Lambda_1 = \Lambda_2$, so $\Lambda_1 = \Lambda_2$ by uniqueness.

The image $M(s)$ is a set of $H_{\bar z}$-representatives. Each $M(\Lambda)$ is a glue lattice. Let $M'$ be a glue lattice. By [row surjectivity](/entry/row-surjective/), $M' = M(\Lambda)$ for a row lattice $\Lambda$. Then $\Lambda = \gamma \Lambda'$ for some $\Lambda' \in s$, and the class correspondence gives $\sigma$ with $\sigma M(\Lambda') = M'$. If also $\sigma' M(\Lambda'') = M'$ with $\Lambda'' \in s$, the class correspondence in the other direction gives $\gamma''$ with $\gamma'' \Lambda'' = \Lambda$, so $\Lambda'' = \Lambda'$.

A glue lattice exists, built from $M(L_0)$, which is a unimodular lattice since $L_0$ is. We have $k \geq 2$ from $m + 3 \leq k$, so the lemma gives the sum of $1 / \# \mathrm{Stab}_{\bar z}(M')$ over $M' \in M(s)$. By injectivity and the row weight formula, this sum is $\sum_{\Lambda \in s} w(\Lambda) = \mathrm{mass}(s)$. $\square$

### The norm of the pair lattice

Let $F_0$ be a flag of norm $T_u$ and let $F'$ be a flag of norm $T_{u'}$. Let $U$ be the span of the vectors of both flags. The *pair lattice* $M(F_0, F')$ is defined prime by prime. At a prime $P$ that contains $g_S$ it is the localization of the lattice spanned by the vectors of $F_0$ and the vectors $g_S^{-1}(f'_b - f_b)$, where $f_b$ and $f'_b$ are the vectors of the two flags. At the other primes it is the localization of $X_S(F_0) + \mathcal{O}_E z + X_S(F') + \mathcal{O}_E z'$.

For a lattice $L$ on $U$ and a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$, let $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)$ be the least exponent read off from a [Jordan splitting](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#local-classification) of $L$ at $\mathfrak{p}$, and let $n(L) = \prod_{\mathfrak{p}} N\mathfrak{p}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)}$. Let $Z$ be the matrix of size $r + 1$ attached to the pair of flags, with leading minors $\Delta_{r+1}(Z)$ and $\Delta_r(Z)$, and let
$$G_Z = \Delta_{r+1}(Z) \overline{\Delta_{r+1}(Z)} - T_u T_{u'} \, \Delta_r(Z) \overline{\Delta_r(Z)}.$$

**Definition 5.6.12 (Norm identity).** The *norm identity* holds if for every setting, all words $u$ and $u'$ with $T_u \equiv T_{u'} \pmod{g_S^2}$, every flag $F_0$ of norm $T_u$ with a set of row representatives, every flag $F'$ of norm $T_{u'}$ attached to $Z$ with $G_Z$ totally positive and with $M(F_0, F')$ integral,
$$n\bigl(M(F_0, F')\bigr) = |d_F|^{-2r(r+1)} \sqrt{\bigl| N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}\bigl(G_Z / g_S^{m}\bigr) \bigr|},$$
where $d_F$ is the discriminant of $F$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.Cnt.NormMM`.

**Theorem 5.6.13 (Norm identity).** The norm identity holds.

Lean: `PI.UnitaryGroupMassFormula.CntFormula.R6.norm_mass_of_jordan`.

*Proof.* Two inputs give the identity. The first is the existence of Jordan splittings: for a lattice $L$ spanned by $\mathfrak{a}_i x_i$, with $x_i$ linearly independent, Gram determinant of the $x_i$ nonzero, and $L$ integral, $L$ has a Jordan splitting at every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$. This follows from the local splitting theorem for Hermitian lattices (compare Jacobowitz, cited at the end of the chapter), since the span of the $x_i$ is nondegenerate when the Gram determinant is nonzero. So each $v_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a minimum over a nonempty set. The second input is a local determinant identity: the order at each prime of $\prod_i \mathfrak{a}_i^2 \cdot \det(h(x_i, x_j))$ equals the order of the product of the Jordan invariants. With these, $n(M(F_0, F'))$ is computed from the discriminant of $M(F_0, F')$: the factor $|d_F|^{-2r(r+1)}$ comes from the powers of $\mathfrak{d}$ in the lattice parts, and the Gram determinant of $U$ gives $G_Z / g_S^m$. $\square$

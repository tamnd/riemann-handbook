## An inert local frame

This section puts the data of a theta pair into local coordinates at a place $v$ of $F$ that is inert in $E$. We have a Hermitian space $V$ and a skew-Hermitian space $W$ over $E$, each with a lattice. At $v$ the completion of $E$ is an unramified quadratic extension $L$ of $F_v$. The theorem gives bases of the two lattices at $v$ and Gram matrices $J_S$ and $J_T$ that are invertible over the integers of $L$. In these bases, the tensor lattice becomes the integral matrices, the Weil symplectic form becomes a trace form over $L$, and the local unitary groups act by matrix multiplication. A later volume uses this result in the [local data at an inert place](/entry/loc-data-v/).

### The setting

We keep the notation of the earlier sections: $(F, E, \sigma)$ is a CM extension, $\mathbb{A}_E = \mathbb{A}_F \otimes_F E$, and $c$ is the involution of $\mathbb{A}_E$ that $\sigma$ induces. The global data are a base $B$, with a standard additive character $\psi$ of $\mathbb{A}_F$, and a geometry $G$. The geometry contains a space $V$ of dimension $a$ with matrix $J_V \in M_a(E)$ and lattice $L_V \subseteq E^a$, and a space $W$ of dimension $a + a'$ with matrix $J_W$ and lattice $L_W$. We say that $B$ and $G$ are *good* when the conditions of the theta pair hold. The conditions that this section uses are these:

1. $E$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$, $E/F$ is unramified, and $\psi$ is standard;
2. at each inert $v$, $2 \notin v$, $Nv > 3$, and $\psi$ is unramified at $v$;
3. $J_V$ is Hermitian ($\sigma(J_{ji}) = J_{ij}$) and $J_W$ is skew-Hermitian ($\sigma(J_{ji}) = -J_{ij}$);
4. $L_V$ and $L_W$ are lattices, and each one is its own dual for the form of $J_V$ or $J_W$ (for $W$, with the trace from $E$ to $\mathbb{Q}$).

The other conditions, on signatures, Cartan involutions, split bases and the dimensions $a$, $a'$, are not used here.

For $n \times n$ matrices $J$ over $E$ let
$$U(J)(\mathbb{A}_F) = \{ g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_E) : g^{\mathsf T} J \, c(g) = J \}.$$
An element $z$ of an $\mathbb{A}_F$-module is *supported at* $v$ if $u z = 0$ for every $u \in \mathbb{A}_F$ with $u_v = 0$.

**Definition 1.14.1 (Inert place).** A finite place $v$ of $F$ is *inert* at a prime $w$ of $E$ if $v \mathcal{O}_E = w$.

Lean: `Formalism.UG.IsInertAt`.

**Definition 1.14.2 (Unramified involution).** Let $L$ be a complete field with a discrete valuation, let $\varpi \in L$, let $\sigma_L$ be a ring endomorphism of $L$, and let $q \in \mathbb{N}$. Then $(\varpi, \sigma_L, q)$ is an *unramified involution* if $\sigma_L^2 = 1$, $\sigma_L$ preserves the valuation, $\sigma_L(\varpi) = \varpi$, $\varpi$ is a uniformizer, there is an integral $x$ with $\sigma_L(x) - x$ a unit, $q \geq 2$, and the residue field of $L$ has $q^2$ elements.

Lean: `Formalism.UG.IsUnramInv`.

The condition on $\sigma_L(x) - x$ says that $\sigma_L$ acts nontrivially on the residue field. So $L$ is an unramified quadratic extension of the fixed field of $\sigma_L$, whose residue field has $q$ elements. For the local unitary group we use the convention of the local theory:
$$U_J(L) = \{ g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(L) : \sigma_L(g)^{\mathsf T} J g = J \}.$$
This differs from the adelic convention above by a transpose and by the side of the conjugation.

**Definition 1.14.3 (Lattices and groups at $v$).** Let $v$ be a finite place of $F$.

1. The *lattice of $V$ at $v$* is the subgroup of $\mathbb{A}_E^a$ generated by the vectors $u \cdot y$ with $y \in L_V$ and $u \in \mathbb{A}_F$ supported at $v$ and integral at $v$. The lattice of $W$ at $v$ is the same with $L_W$.
2. The *tensor lattice at $v$* is the subgroup of $M_{a \times (a+a')}(\mathbb{A}_E)$ generated by the matrices $u \cdot x y^{\mathsf T}$ with $x \in L_V$, $y \in L_W$, and $u$ as in 1.
3. $U_v(V)$ is the set of $g \in U(J_V)(\mathbb{A}_F)$ with $g - 1$ supported at $v$.
4. $K_v(V)$ is the set of $g \in U_v(V)$ such that $g$ and $g^{-1}$ map the closure $\widehat{L}_V$ of $L_V$ in $\mathbb{A}_E^a$ into itself.

The same definitions apply to $W$. We do not unfold the definition of $\widehat{L}_V$ here.

**Definition 1.14.4 (Weil symplectic form).** For $x, y \in M_{a \times (a+a')}(\mathbb{A}_E)$ let
$$\langle x, y \rangle = \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{A}_E / \mathbb{A}_F} \Bigl( - \sum_{i, j, k, l} x_{ij} \, (J_V)_{ik} \, (J_W)_{jl} \, c(y_{kl}) \Bigr).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.WeilM.symp` (with $\xi = -1$).

This is the symplectic form on $V \otimes W$ that defines the Weil representation of the theta pair.

**Definition 1.14.5 (Local trace form).** Let $\delta \in L$, $J_S \in \mathrm{GL}_a(L)$ and $J_T \in \mathrm{GL}_{a+a'}(L)$. For $x, y \in M_{(a+a') \times a}(L)$ let
$$H(x, y) = \delta \operatorname{tr} \bigl( J_S^{-1} \, \sigma_L(y)^{\mathsf T} J_T \, x \bigr).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Hk3.locH`.

### The theorem

The theorem does not use a fixed completion of $E$ at $v$. It takes an abstract field $L$ with a map $\iota : L \to \mathbb{A}_E$ onto the elements supported at $v$, and the hypotheses on $\iota$ make $L$ play the part of $E_w$.

**Theorem 1.14.6 (Inert local frame).** Let $B$ and $G$ be good. Let $v$ be a finite place of $F$, inert at a prime $w$ of $E$. Let $L$ be a complete field with a discrete valuation, $(\varpi, \sigma_L, q)$ an unramified involution with $q$ odd, $\delta \in L$ a unit with $\sigma_L(\delta) = -\delta$, and $\psi_L$ an additive character of $L$. Let $\iota : L \to \mathbb{A}_E$ be an injective additive map such that:

1. the image of $\iota$ is the set of elements supported at $v$;
2. $\iota(xy) = \iota(x) \iota(y)$ and $\iota(\sigma_L x) = c(\iota x)$;
3. $x$ is integral if and only if $\iota(x)$ lies in the subgroup generated by the elements $u \cdot o$ with $o \in \mathcal{O}_E$ and $u \in \mathbb{A}_F$ supported at $v$ and integral at $v$;
4. $\psi(\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{A}_E / \mathbb{A}_F} \iota(x)) = \psi_L(x + \sigma_L x)$.

Then there are $J_S \in \mathrm{GL}_a(L)$, $J_T \in \mathrm{GL}_{a+a'}(L)$, an additive map $X : M_{(a+a') \times a}(L) \to M_{a \times (a+a')}(\mathbb{A}_E)$, and group homomorphisms $i_V : U_{J_S}(L) \to U(J_V)(\mathbb{A}_F)$ and $i_W : U_{J_T}(L) \to U(J_W)(\mathbb{A}_F)$, such that:

1. $J_S$ and $J_T$ have their entries and the entries of their inverses in $\mathcal{O}_L$, and both are Hermitian: $\sigma_L(J_{ij}) = J_{ji}$;
2. $\psi \bigl( \tfrac12 \langle X x, X y \rangle \bigr) = \psi_L \bigl( \tfrac12 (H(x, y) + \sigma_L H(x, y)) \bigr)$ for all $x, y$;
3. the image of $X$ is the set of matrices supported at $v$;
4. the image under $X$ of the integral matrices is the tensor lattice at $v$;
5. $i_V(g) \, X(x) \, i_W(h)^{\mathsf T} = X(h x g^{-1})$ for all $g$, $h$ and $x$;
6. $i_V(g) \in U_v(V)$ and $i_W(h) \in U_v(W)$;
7. if $g$ is in $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathcal{O}_L)$, then $i_V(g) \in K_v(V)$, and if $h$ is in $\mathrm{GL}_{a+a'}(\mathcal{O}_L)$, then $i_W(h) \in K_v(W)$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.inertPlace_unimodular_hermitianFrame_coordinates` (also `GlobalTheory.InertLocFrame.frame`).

Item 2 says that the Weil symplectic form, after $\psi$, is the trace form $\operatorname{tr}_{L/L_0} \tfrac12 H$ after $\psi_L$, where $L_0$ is the fixed field of $\sigma_L$. Item 5 says that $X$ intertwines the adelic action on $V \otimes W$ with the action $x \mapsto h x g^{-1}$ of $U_{J_T}(L) \times U_{J_S}(L)$. Items 4 and 7 say that the integral structures agree.

*Proof.* For a matrix $J$ over $E$ let $t(J)$ be the matrix over $L$ with $\iota(t(J)_{ij}) = \iota(1) J_{ij}$; it is the matrix of $J$ at $v$. For $P \in \mathrm{GL}_n(L)$ let $\mathrm{gram}(P, J) = P^{\mathsf T} t(J) \sigma_L(P)$. We use these facts, proved in another part of the development. We have opened their statements.

1. *Frames.* For $V$ there is $P_V \in \mathrm{GL}_a(L)$ such that a vector $x \in L^a$ is integral if and only if $\iota(P_V x)$ lies in the lattice of $V$ at $v$. The same holds for $W$ with some $P_W \in \mathrm{GL}_{a+a'}(L)$.
2. *The Hermitian Gram matrix.* For each frame $P_V$ of fact 1 there is $J_S$ with item 1 such that $J_S^{-1} = \mathrm{gram}(P_V, J_V)$.
3. *The skew-Hermitian Gram matrix.* For each frame $P_W$ of fact 1 there is $J_T$ with item 1 such that $J_T = -\delta^{-1} \mathrm{gram}(P_W, J_W)^{\mathsf T}$. This fact uses that $q$ is odd and that $\delta$ is a unit with $\sigma_L \delta = -\delta$.
4. *The homomorphism for $V$.* If $J_S^{-1} = \mathrm{gram}(P_V, J_V)$, there is a homomorphism $i_V$ with $i_V(g) = (1 - \iota(1)) \cdot 1 + \iota\bigl(P_V (g^{-1})^{\mathsf T} P_V^{-1}\bigr)$.
5. *The homomorphism for $W$.* If $J_T = -\delta^{-1} \mathrm{gram}(P_W, J_W)^{\mathsf T}$, there is a homomorphism $i_W$ with $i_W(h) = (1 - \iota(1)) \cdot 1 + \iota\bigl(P_W h P_W^{-1}\bigr)$.
6. *Tensor lattice.* With frames as in fact 1 and $X(x) = \iota(P_V x^{\mathsf T} P_W^{\mathsf T})$, a matrix $z$ lies in the tensor lattice at $v$ if and only if $z = X(u)$ for an integral $u$.
7. *Symplectic form.* With $J_S$ and $J_T$ as in facts 2 and 3, item 2 holds for this $X$.
8. *Support.* A matrix $z$ is supported at $v$ if and only if $z = X(x)$ for some $x$.
9. *Action.* If $g = (1 - \iota(1)) \cdot 1 + \iota(A)$ and $h = (1 - \iota(1)) \cdot 1 + \iota(C)$, then $g \, X(x) \, h^{\mathsf T} = \iota(A P_V x^{\mathsf T} P_W^{\mathsf T} C^{\mathsf T})$.
10. *Supported points.* An element $g = (1 - \iota(1)) \cdot 1 + \iota(A)$ of the adelic unitary group lies in $U_v$.
11. *Stabilizers.* With $P$ a frame as in fact 1, if $g = (1 - \iota(1)) \cdot 1 + \iota(P K P^{-1})$ and $g^{-1} = (1 - \iota(1)) \cdot 1 + \iota(P K' P^{-1})$ with $K$ and $K'$ integral, then $g \in K_v$.

Take $P_V$ and $P_W$ from fact 1, $J_S$ from fact 2, $J_T$ from fact 3, $i_V$ and $i_W$ from facts 4 and 5, and $X$ as in fact 6. Item 1 is in facts 2 and 3. Item 2 is fact 7, item 3 is fact 8, and item 4 is fact 6 in its two directions. Item 6 is fact 10 applied to the formulas of facts 4 and 5.

*Item 5.* Apply fact 9 with $A = P_V (g^{-1})^{\mathsf T} P_V^{-1}$ and $C = P_W h P_W^{-1}$. It gives
$$i_V(g) \, X(x) \, i_W(h)^{\mathsf T} = \iota\bigl( P_V (g^{-1})^{\mathsf T} P_V^{-1} \, P_V x^{\mathsf T} P_W^{\mathsf T} \, (P_W^{-1})^{\mathsf T} h^{\mathsf T} P_W^{\mathsf T} \bigr) = \iota\bigl( P_V (g^{-1})^{\mathsf T} x^{\mathsf T} h^{\mathsf T} P_W^{\mathsf T} \bigr).$$
Since $(g^{-1})^{\mathsf T} x^{\mathsf T} h^{\mathsf T} = (h x g^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$, the right side is $X(h x g^{-1})$.

*Item 7.* Let $g \in U_{J_S}(L)$ be integral. Then $g^{-1}$ is integral too. Take $K = (g^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$ and $K' = g^{\mathsf T}$, which are integral. Fact 4 gives $i_V(g)$ in the form of fact 11, and since $i_V$ is a homomorphism, $i_V(g)^{-1} = i_V(g^{-1})$ has the form with $K'$. So fact 11 gives $i_V(g) \in K_v(V)$. For $h$ integral, take $K = h$ and $K' = h^{-1}$, and use fact 5 in the same way. $\square$

**Remark 1.14.7 (Classical form).** Over an unramified quadratic extension $L/L_0$ of local fields with odd residue characteristic, a unimodular Hermitian lattice has an orthonormal basis, and two such lattices are isometric if and only if they have the same rank (R. Jacobowitz, "Hermitian forms over local fields", *Amer. J. Math.* 84 (1962)). The theorem gives a weaker normal form: the Gram matrices $J_S$ and $J_T$ are unimodular and Hermitian, but they are not made diagonal. For the skew-Hermitian space $W$ the factor $\delta^{-1}$ with $\sigma_L \delta = -\delta$ turns the skew-Hermitian Gram matrix into a Hermitian one. The theorem also does not state that the tensor lattice is self-dual for the trace form; it gives the coordinates in which such statements become statements about integral matrices over $L$.

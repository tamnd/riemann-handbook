This chapter proves Borel's theorem on the finiteness of class numbers: for a linear algebraic group $G \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_n$ over a number field $F$, given by polynomial equations, and an open subgroup $U$ of $G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$, the set $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f}) / U$ is finite. The proof works first over $\mathbb{Q}$, for groups of matrices of determinant $1$ and over an arbitrary adelic pair, a pair $(R, R_0)$ that behaves like $(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f}, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}})$. The lattice proof of class number one for $\mathrm{SL}_I$ is the start. A completely reducible group is the stabiliser of a rational tensor with a closed orbit, and the reduction theory of $\mathrm{SL}_I(\mathbb{R})$ with Siegel sets gives its class finiteness. An induction on the dimension of the span removes the unipotent part. Restriction of scalars passes to $F$, and a compactness argument passes from the integral level to an open level.

## Partner counts and the local cut

The coefficient of the theta series at a pair of words is a sum over global terms. To compute it, the sum is cut into local pieces: at each finite place, the terms are grouped by a local class, and each local class contributes a count. This section proves the two local facts that make the cut work. The first is that the local count of partners of a term is an isomorphism count of marked cokernels, as in the [Theorem (Family local model)](/entry/local-count-of-module/). The second is that the local Gram data of two terms with the same local class are congruent under $\mathrm{GL}$ of the local integers, so their local densities agree. With these, the full list of properties of the local cut follows from the global frame results of a later section. The main result, the Theorem (Rows datum), is used in the [Theorem 3.2](/entry/theorem-s-leaves-of-carrier-g/) that cuts the coefficient into its leaves.

Fix the global datum and agreeing local data $(\delta, M)$ as in the [section on local models](/entry/local-model-at-holds/). Let $u, u'$ be words with $T_u \equiv T_{u'} \pmod{g_S^2}$, where $g_S$ is the level, and assume the prime condition on $(u, T_{u'})$. Let $F_0$ be a base flag and $\mathcal{W}$ a finite set of lattices that is a set of representatives of the rows for $F_0$.

**Definition 8.5.1 (Local class of a point).** A *point* of rank $r + 1$ is a family $w$ of $r + 1$ elements of $E$, with a *kind*. For a point $w$ and an integral matrix $Z$ attached to $w$, the *local class* $\theta_p(w, Z)$ at $p$ is the cokernel class of the local Gram matrix of $(w, Z)$ at $p$, or *none* if that matrix is not integral. The *extended Gram matrix* $\Gamma^+_p(Z)$ is the $2(r+1) \times 2(r+1)$ matrix of the local form on the two flags. If $p$ is split with parameter $q$, it is ${}^t \bar{A} \, \Gamma_p(Z) \, A$ with
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -q^{-1} \\ 0 & q^{-1} \end{pmatrix}$$
in blocks of size $r + 1$.

**Theorem 8.5.2 (Equal classes give congruent Gram matrices).** Let $w, w'$ be points of the same kind with attached matrices $Z, Z'$, and let $p$ be a finite place with $\theta_p(w, Z) = \theta_p(w', Z') \neq$ none. Then there is a matrix $A$ with entries in $\mathcal{O}_p$ and unit determinant such that
$$\Gamma^+_p(Z') = {}^t \bar{A} \, \Gamma^+_p(Z) \, A.$$

*Proof.* The local classes are equal, so the marked cokernels of the two Gram matrices are isomorphic, and the forms $\lambda$ agree; in particular the last diagonal entries of the inverses agree. An isomorphism of cokernels that respects the marked element lifts to matrices: there are $l, \rho \in \mathrm{GL}_{r+1}(\mathcal{O}_p)$, with $l$ of last column $e_{r+1}$ and $\rho$ of last row $e_{r+1}^T$, such that the second Gram matrix is $l$ times the first times $\rho$. This lift uses the structure of finitely generated modules over the local ring and the fact that both matrices have the same rank. The change of basis $(l, \rho)$ on the two flags gives $A$. $\square$

It follows that the local densities of $(w, Z)$ and $(w', Z')$ at $p$ agree, since the local density of a form depends only on its $\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}_p)$-class.

**Theorem 8.5.3 (Partner count).** Let $p$ be a finite place, $T, T' \in \mathbb{N}$, and let $w, w'$ be points of the same kind. Let $t_1 = (\Lambda_1, f, f', T, T')$ be a local term at $p$, let $f'_2$ be a second flag and let $\varphi, \varphi'$ be the identifying isomorphisms, under the local count hypotheses. Assume that $Z^+(t_1)$ is the matrix of $w$ up to lower and upper unitriangular factors, that the modified Gram matrix $Z^+(f, f'_2)$ is the matrix of $w'$ up to such factors, and that $Z^+(f, f'_2)$ has entries in $R$. Then the number of local lattices $\Lambda_2$ compatible with $(t_1, f'_2, \varphi, \varphi')$ is
$$n\bigl(c(w),\, c(w')\bigr),$$
where $c(w)$ and $c(w')$ are the cokernel classes of the two Gram matrices, each corrected by a corner term.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.PCount.partnerCount_holds_all`.

*Proof.* This is the [Corollary (Local count)](/entry/integral-closure-rank-and-residue-trdeg-le/), applied at the local model at $p$, with two inputs: that the lattice generated by the partner frame is integral, and that integral local isometries of the frames correspond to isomorphisms of the marked cokernels. $\square$

**Definition 8.5.4 (Local cut).** The *local cut* at $(u, u', F_0, \mathcal{W})$ is the conjunction of nine properties of the terms:

1. the coefficient is a sum over local orbits;
2. each term has a scalar kind;
3. the coefficient is a product of local coefficients;
4. the partner count of the previous theorem holds;
5. the fibre over each local class has the predicted size;
6. each term has an eligible normal representative;
7. some local class is defined at each term;
8. there is a base class $\theta_0$ that is unramified at almost all places;
9. the local classes are restricted to the base class outside a finite set.

**Theorem 8.5.5 (Local cut from its leaves).** Assume the following twelve inputs: normal representatives exist and move correctly, the local phases are global, volume indices are products, the local existence, sum, global frame and integral generation properties hold, the $\tau = 1$ reduction holds, the partner count holds, the fibre sum holds and the densities agree on local classes. Then the local cut holds.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.Coef.cutAt_of_leaves`.

*Proof.* Properties 1 and 3 follow from the existence, sum and global frame inputs: a global term is given by its local components, and the coefficient factors over the places. Property 4 is the partner count input. Property 5 is the fibre sum together with the agreement of densities. Properties 6 to 9 come from the normal representative inputs and the integral generation property. $\square$

**Theorem 8.5.6 (Local cut).** Assume the deep form of the global frame statement: at every $(\delta, M)$, $(u, u')$ and row representatives $(F_0, \mathcal{W})$ as above, the local index steps hold, the stabilizers have global approximation, and the global frame bijection holds. Then the local cut holds for all such data.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.Coef.cutAt_of_deep`.

*Proof.* We give the twelve inputs of the [Theorem (Local cut from its leaves)](/entry/cut-at-of-leaves/). The partner count is the [Theorem (Partner count)](/entry/partner-count-holds-all/). The agreement of densities follows from the [Theorem (Equal classes give congruent Gram matrices)](/entry/ga-plus-th-holds/). The global frame and existence inputs come from the global frame bijection, the sum input from the local index steps, and the phase and approximation inputs from the stabilizer approximation. The other inputs are proved directly. $\square$

**Corollary 8.5.7 (Rows datum).** Under the hypothesis of the Theorem (Local cut), the rows datum holds: for all data as above, every uniformizer family and every value of the normalized coefficient formula, the local densities are nonnegative, and there are a local class function and a density function on the terms with the properties of the local cut and the finiteness of the eligible terms.

*Proof.* The local cut is the [Theorem (Local cut)](/entry/cut-at-of-deep/). The rows datum follows from it and from the finiteness of the eligible terms. $\square$

**Remark 8.5.8 (Formalization).** The hypothesis of the Theorem (Local cut) is proved outright elsewhere ([Theorem 5.9.2](/entry/cut-leaves-deep-outright/)), so the corollary is a conditional form of a result that holds without conditions. It repeats the proof of `LsDatumB.rowsDatumP_of` ([LsDatumBCut.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/SiegelLatticeTheory/CokernelGLCounts/RankPointCounts/LsDatumBCut.lean#L201)), and its own statement is at [CoefCutDeep.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/SiegelLatticeTheory/CokernelGLCounts/RankPointCounts/CoefCutDeep.lean#L72). The proof of the congruence of Gram matrices goes through a lifting statement for marked cokernels (MulVecInj.lean:78), which we read as a statement but whose proof we did not check; the sketch above gives its content, not its method. The partner count is stated for one local datum, although its name ends in `_all`. The twelve inputs of the Theorem (Local cut from its leaves) are named in the statement; we give their roles from the proof of the Theorem (Local cut), and did not unfold each one. In the definition of the local cut, the order of the nine parts is the order of the conjunction in LsDatumBCut.lean:135.

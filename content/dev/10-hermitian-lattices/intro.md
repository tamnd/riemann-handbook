This volume proves the results on Hermitian lattices and unitary groups that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. The groups are unitary groups of Hermitian spaces over a CM field, with their adelic points. The volume proves Borel's finiteness of class numbers, strong approximation for special unitary groups and the mass formula for definite Hermitian lattices. It computes local densities and local orbit volumes, counts lattices by their cokernels, and bounds covolumes, flag measures and the orbital terms of the theta lifts. Some results assume the Siegel-Weil formula in its convergent range. Volume 1 gives the definitions of [unitary groups](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups), [lattices in Hermitian spaces](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#lattices-in-quadratic-and-hermitian-spaces), [the genus](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#genus-class-and-spinor-genus), [local densities](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#local-densities) and [strong approximation](/learn/groups/#strong-approximation), and states [the mass formula for unitary groups](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#the-mass-formula-for-unitary-groups). This volume proves them in the forms that the main proof needs.

The proof uses the volume in four places. The first is [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/). Its step [GeoOne](/step/geo-one-def/) needs the finiteness of class numbers of Chapter 1 and the trace identity of Chapter 6. The second is the step [HypsAlt is nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/) of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/), which turns the closed bound into explicit constants. It uses the type bound of Chapter 2, the flag measure bound and the counting theorem of Chapter 3, and masses, volumes and counts from Chapters 4 to 8. The third is the step [theta local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/). It uses the class mass formula, the Hecke relation and the second branch of Chapter 7, the mass induction of Chapter 6, and results of Chapters 4, 5 and 8. The fourth is the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/), which uses the theta transformation of Chapter 1 and the Siegel step of Chapter 2. Its branch [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) uses Chapters 2 and 7, and the step on [every character of a row](/step/every-character-of-row/) uses Chapter 4.

Chapter 1 proves Borel's theorem, [Theorem 1.6.3](/entry/class-number-finite-holds/). For a linear algebraic group $G \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_n$ over a number field $F$ and an open subgroup $U$ of $G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$, the set $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f}) / U$ is finite. The proof works first over $\mathbb{Q}$, where a completely reducible group is the stabiliser of a rational tensor with a closed orbit, [Theorem 1.2.10](/entry/exists-tensor-stabiliser-closed-orbit/). An induction on the span removes the unipotent part, [Theorem 1.4.8](/entry/cf-of-is-qgroup/), and restriction of scalars passes to $F$. The later sections prove inputs on unitary groups over CM fields: the Hasse norm principle for cyclic extensions, [Theorem 1.7.10](/entry/hasse-norm-principle-of-is-cyclic/), finite volume, [Theorem 1.8.6](/entry/fin-vol-unitary-group/), the theta transformation formula, [Theorem 1.8.11](/entry/theta-function-automorphy/), strong approximation for special unitary groups through quaternion algebras, [Theorem 1.13.2](/entry/strong-approx-su/), the short vector bound, [Theorem 1.15.3](/entry/short-vector-bound/), and an $L^2$ division theorem, [Theorem 1.17.2](/entry/div-core-statement-holds/).

Chapter 2 measures the terms of the double coset sum for a unitary group over a CM field. The weight of a term is a ratio of covolumes of arithmetic stabilizers. The chapter bounds it by the weight of a split term near the reference lattice, [Theorem 2.1.10](/entry/om-le/), and sums these weights over frame classes, [Theorem 2.3.9](/entry/sum-cls-om-le-dat-bd/). With the covolume of the reference lattice stabilizer, [Theorem 2.8.3](/entry/covol-ref/), and a pair count from the mass formulas, it proves the type bound, [Theorem 2.10.8](/entry/rows-pos-type-p/). The group sum of a family class is the row weight times a local amplitude, [Theorem 2.12.4](/entry/family-group-sum-factors-row-weight-amplitude/). Later sections prove that the weights add up to the mass of the genus, [Corollary 2.15.5](/entry/weights-add-up-to-mass/), the covolume of the integral unipotent radical, [Theorem 2.16.3](/entry/covolume-n/), the local nongeneric bound, [Theorem 2.20.5](/entry/ng-at/), the modulus of an isotropic flag, [Theorem 2.30.5](/entry/modulus/), and the Siegel step for lattice volumes, [Theorem 2.31.5](/entry/gl-vol-step/).

Chapter 3 proves the flag measure bound, [Theorem 3.4.6](/entry/flag-measure-bound/). For a unitary group of signature $(p, q)$ at every infinite place, the probability that a random point of the adelic quotient has a short isotropic flag of type $j$ with sizes in $B$ is at most an explicit constant times the integral over $B$ of a flag density. The Iwasawa decomposition gives an integration formula at each real place, [Theorem 3.1.6](/entry/iwasawa-formula/), and the bounds for one flag class are summed over the classes. The volume step follows from the Siegel-Weil formula in its convergent range, [Theorem 3.5.5](/entry/volume-step-of-siegel-weil/). It gives the counting theorem, [Theorem 3.7.4](/entry/counting-theorem-of-volume-step/): the dimension of a space of automorphic forms with discrete series type at the infinite places is its main term up to a factor $1 + O(e^{-nf\log n/16})$. The last sections prove the off-diagonal bound, [Theorem 3.9.5](/entry/carrier-gap-off-diag/), the global setting, [Theorem 3.11.4](/entry/exists-setting/), and Theorem S, [Theorem 3.13.7](/entry/theorem-s-closed-p/).

Chapter 4 closes the bounds that the orbital terms of the theta lifts need. Most of its statements are conclusions of theorems from other chapters, with their hypotheses discharged here. The first sections are on Lie theory: integral jets of phase functions, [Theorem 4.1.4](/entry/jets-phase-weights/), the Weyl dimension formula for $U(p) \times U(q)$, [Theorem 4.2.8](/entry/weyl-for-k-of/), and Gelfand-Tsetlin branching, [Theorem 4.3.2](/entry/gelfand-tsetlin-branching/). The middle sections are on lattices: local densities by corank, [Theorem 4.5.1](/entry/local-density-corank/), the local mass of a type, [Theorem 4.6.3](/entry/loc-mass-typ/), the finiteness of classes in adelic unitary groups, [Theorem 4.7.2](/entry/adelic-class-set-finite/), and the class mass, [Theorem 4.7.3](/entry/unitary-class-mass-hermitian/). The last sections bound Gelfand-Graev matrices, [Theorem 4.8.5](/entry/gg-matrix-bound/), prove Theorem A, [Theorem 4.10.1](/entry/theorem-a-section/), and reduce the mass of the rows to the mass of one lattice by gluing, [Theorem 4.10.4](/entry/gluing-mass-of-lift-number-genus/).

Chapter 5 computes the local volumes in the comparison of orbit integrals. At an archimedean place the group is $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$. The chapter compares its Haar measure, normalized in Cayley or exponential coordinates, [Theorem 5.1.4](/entry/cay-dens-ex-holds/), with the measure on an orbit of frames. This gives the orbit constant $a'$, [Theorem 5.2.6](/entry/aprime-inf-holds/), and the real bracket, [Theorem 5.3.10](/entry/real-bracket-holds/). At a finite place the self-dual volume of the orbit of a frame is a power of the local different times a product of factors $1 - \eta_v^j N(v)^{-j}$, [Theorem 5.5.5](/entry/om-vz-holds-x/). The global sections bound Gelfand-Graev averages over finite fields, [Theorem 5.7.4](/entry/ggmatrix-bound-holds/), and prove strong approximation in the nilradical, [Theorem 5.8.2](/entry/nilradical-strong-approximation/), a lattice count, [Theorem 5.8.5](/entry/lat-count-holds/), and the folding, [Theorem 5.8.7](/entry/fold-okc-holds/). The last section proves the closed statements, among them the deep inputs, [Theorem 5.9.2](/entry/cut-leaves-deep-outright/).

Chapter 6 proves an identity between two sums attached to a unitary datum: a CM field $F$, a central simple $F$-algebra with an involution of the second kind, and Hermitian forms at the infinite places. One side sums, over classes of definite pairs, terms that count fixed points in the way of Kottwitz. The other side sums traces of Hecke operators over classes of definite Hermitian forms. Classes of marked pairs and of definite pairs correspond, [Theorem 6.1.12](/entry/cls-equiv-dcls-irr/). Only finitely many classes with irreducible polynomial have a nonzero term, [Theorem 6.3.15](/entry/finite-classes-irr-term-ne-zero/). The identity is [Theorem 6.4.12](/entry/lkdef-trace-definite-forms-identity/). The later sections treat Hermitian lattices over a CM field: a ratio of local densities, [Theorem 6.5.4](/entry/local-density-ratio-holds/), the induction step for the mass of unimodular lattices, [Theorem 6.7.6](/entry/mass-const-succ/), the level splitting of a flag parabolic, [Theorem 6.8.8](/entry/level-splitting-of-heisenberg-levi/), and Haar measures on unipotent groups, [Theorem 6.9.2](/entry/meas-n-holds/).

Chapter 7 proves the mass formula for definite Hermitian lattices over a CM extension $E/F$. It starts at one place, with Gelfand-Tsetlin lowering on Weyl modules, [Theorem 7.1.7](/entry/wgl-g-tlower-of/), and the local mass of a type, [Theorem 7.4.8](/entry/local-mass-type-of-parts/). In the adelic group $U_J(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$ a genus has finitely many classes, [Theorem 7.6.9](/entry/adel-class-fin-of-sv-real/). The class mass formula $\sum 1/\#\Gamma_{g_i} = \operatorname{mass}(h, L_0)$ is [Theorem 7.7.8](/entry/unitary-class-mass/), and its form as a volume identity is [Theorem 7.7.12](/entry/volume-mass-of-genus-rank/). The volume of the stabilizer of a tuple comes from the fibre measures of the Gram map and a Siegel-Weil identity taken as an input, [Theorem 7.8.5](/entry/vol-stab-from-sw-holds/). The last sections prove the Hecke relation at a ramified place, [Theorem 7.9.4](/entry/hecke-ramified-zero-all/), the second branch, [Theorem 7.9.5](/entry/second-branch-all-members/), and the folding of the Poincaré series, [Theorem 7.11.10](/entry/fold-okc-holds-of/).

Chapter 8 counts Hermitian lattices over a CM field. Over a number field, unimodular lattices that contain a given sublattice are glued from local pieces along discriminant modules. Their weighted number is a mass constant times explicit local densities, [Theorem 8.1.13](/entry/gluing-mass-holds/). Over a local field, the lattices compatible with two flags are counted by the isomorphisms between the cokernels of two Gram matrices. Every global datum satisfies this local count, [Corollary 8.4.10](/entry/integral-closure-rank-and-residue-trdeg-le/). The chapter also proves the partner count, [Theorem 8.5.3](/entry/partner-count-holds-all/), the block covolumes of the integral unipotent group, [Theorem 8.7.9](/entry/block-covol-holds/), the splitting of the Haar measure along the stabilizer of a frame, [Theorem 8.8.3](/entry/omega-splitting-of-witt/), and the normalizing constant of the theta lift, [Theorem 8.9.6](/entry/nc-tie-c-of-closed/).

Chapters 1 and 2 have no links to the other chapters of this volume, so a reader can take them in any order. Chapters 3 to 8 form one block, and their links go in both directions. Chapter 4 states in closed form many results that Chapter 7 proves, among them Gelfand-Tsetlin lowering, the local mass of a type and the class mass formula. It takes the orbit constant and the real bracket from Chapter 5 and the level splitting from Chapter 6. Chapter 5 uses the Haar measures of Chapter 6 and the folding of Chapter 7. Its closed statements feed Theorem S and the global setting of Chapter 3 and the normalizing constant of Chapter 8. The local models and the rows datum of Chapter 8 enter [Theorem 3.9.4](/entry/theorem-s-leaves-of-carrier-g/). So a reader of the block can take Chapters 6 and 7 first, then Chapter 5, then Chapters 4 and 8, and Chapter 3 last.

## Index symmetry and finiteness of classes

The chapter on orbital bounds states two facts about the adelic unitary group $G_f = U_J(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$ and the stabilizer $K$ of the self-dual lattice: the [index symmetry](/entry/adelic-index-eq/) $[K : xKx^{-1} \cap K] = [K : x^{-1}Kx \cap K]$, and the [finiteness of classes](/entry/adelic-class-set-finite/) $G_f = \bigcup_{s \in S} U_J(F)\, s\, U$ for a finite set $S$. The first makes the Haar measure of $G_f$ unimodular in the sense needed for the folding of Section 4.7; the second makes the class mass a finite sum. This section proves both, the first from a local computation with characters and the second from a reduction theory of Hermitian lattices by successive vectors. We keep the setting of [adelic unitary groups](/entry/adelic-index-eq/): $E/F$ a CM extension with involution $a \mapsto \bar{a}$, a Hermitian matrix $J$, the lattice $L$ and its stabilizer $K$. For the background see [unitary groups](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups) and [adelic points](/learn/groups/#adelic-points-and-compact-open-subgroups).

### The modular quotient

**Definition 7.6.1 (Modular quotient).** Let $G$ be a group and $K \subseteq G$ a subgroup. For $x \in G$ put $d_K(x) = [K : xKx^{-1} \cap K]$, with the value $0$ if the index is infinite. The subgroup $K$ is *commensurated* if $d_K(x) \neq 0$ for all $x$. The *modular quotient* is
$$\chi_K(x) = \frac{d_K(x)}{d_K(x^{-1})} \in \mathbb{Q}.$$

If $K$ is commensurated, then $\chi_K$ is a homomorphism $G \to \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$. It is trivial on $K$ and on the center of $G$. When $G$ is locally compact and $K$ compact open, $\chi_K$ is the modular function of $G$, so index symmetry says that $\chi_K = 1$. For a place $v$ of $F$ let $E_v = E \otimes_F F_v$, let $U_v = U_J(F_v)$, and let $K_v$ be the stabilizer of $L_v$. An element of $G_f$ is *supported at $v$* if its components at all places $w \neq v$ are $1$.

**Definition 7.6.2 (Quasi-reflection).** Let $v$ be a place at which $E_v$ is a field. An element $s \in U_v$ is a *quasi-reflection* if $s = 1 + (\alpha - 1) P$ for an idempotent matrix $P$ and some $\alpha \in E_v$ with $\bar{\alpha} \alpha = 1$.

The next three statements are the inputs of the index symmetry.

**Lemma 7.6.3 (Positive characters of $GL_n$).** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$, and let $\chi : GL_n(k) \to \mathbb{Q}$ be a homomorphism to the multiplicative group with $\chi(g) > 0$ for all $g$ and $\chi(d \cdot 1) = 1$ for all $d \in k^\times$. Then $\chi = 1$.

The proof: $\chi$ factors through the abelianization, which is $\det : GL_n(k) \to k^\times$, so $\chi = \phi \circ \det$; and $\chi(d \cdot 1) = \phi(d^n) = 1$ for all $d$. So $\phi(a)^n = \phi(a^n) = 1$ for each $a \in k^\times$, and a positive rational number whose $n$-th power is $1$ is $1$.

**Lemma 7.6.4 (Trace integrality).** Let $v$ be a place at which $E_v$ is a field, and let $\alpha \in E_v$ with $\bar{\alpha} \alpha = 1$. Then $\alpha + \bar{\alpha} \in \mathcal{O}_{F_v}$.

This holds because $\alpha$ is a unit of $E_v$: its norm is $1$, so its valuation is $0$, and then $\alpha$ and $\bar{\alpha}$ are integral.

**Lemma 7.6.5 (Powers of quasi-reflections).** Assume trace integrality. Let $v$ be a place at which $E_v$ is a field and $s \in U_v$ a quasi-reflection. Then $s^m \in K_v$ for some $m \geq 1$.

**Theorem 7.6.6 (Index symmetry from trace integrality).** Assume trace integrality. Then for every setting and every $x \in G_f$,
$$[K : xKx^{-1} \cap K] = [K : x^{-1}Kx \cap K].$$

*Proof.* Trace integrality gives the lemma on powers of quasi-reflections. Then six proved facts reduce the claim to the places. The group $K$ is compact open, so it is commensurated and $\chi_K$ is a homomorphism. The map $G_f \to U_v$ to the component at $v$ is onto the elements supported at $v$: each $g \in U_v$ lifts to an element of $G_f$ supported at $v$. Elements of $G_f$ with the same components are equal, and each component lies in $U_v$. An element lies in $K$ if and only if each component lies in $K_v$. And $G_f$ is generated by $K$ and the elements supported at single places.

By the last fact it is enough to show $\chi_K(y) = 1$ for $y$ supported at one place $v$. The lifting gives a homomorphism $U_v \to G_f$, and $\chi_K$ composed with it is a positive character $\chi_v$ of $U_v$. It is trivial on central elements, and it is trivial on each $s$ with some power $s^m \in K_v$, since then $\chi_v(s)^m = 1$ and $\chi_v(s) > 0$.

If $E_v$ is not a field, then $E_v = F_v \times F_v$ and $U_v \cong GL_n(F_v)$. The scalars are central, so the lemma on $GL_n$ gives $\chi_v = 1$. If $E_v$ is a field, the group $U_v$ is generated, up to elements with a power in $K_v$ and central elements, by quasi-reflections, and each quasi-reflection has a power in $K_v$. So $\chi_v = 1$ again. Hence $\chi_K = 1$, which is the claim. $\square$

### Finitely many classes

**Definition 7.6.7 (Majorant family).** Let $J$ be an $m \times m$ Hermitian matrix over $E$. A *majorant family* for $J$ is a family $(H_\phi)$, indexed by the embeddings $\phi : E \to \mathbb{C}$, of positive definite Hermitian matrices with $\det H_\phi = |\det J^\phi|$, with
$$|z^* J^\phi z| \leq z^* H_\phi z \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{C}^m,$$
and with $H_{\bar{\phi}} = \overline{H_\phi}$.

For a diagonal $J^\phi$ with entries $\pm 1$ the identity matrix is a majorant. A majorant turns the indefinite form $h$ into a positive one that dominates it, and so makes lengths available on an indefinite lattice. For vectors $e_1, \dots, e_N$ in $E^m$, with $N = m [E : \mathbb{Q}]$, let $\operatorname{Gr}(e)$ be the rational matrix $\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}} h(e_i, e_j)\bigr)_{i,j}$.

**Theorem 7.6.8 (Real majorant of the trace form).** Let $J$ be Hermitian with a majorant family $(H_\phi)$, and let $e_1, \dots, e_N \in E^m$ with $\det \operatorname{Gr}(e) \neq 0$. Then there is a positive definite real $N \times N$ matrix $R$ with $\det R = |\det \operatorname{Gr}(e)|$ such that for all $c \in \mathbb{Z}^N$ and $x = \sum_i c_i e_i$,
$$\sum_{\phi} |\phi(h(x, x))| \leq c^T R\, c.$$

**Theorem 7.6.9 (Finitely many classes from the real majorant).** Assume the theorem on the real majorant. Then for every setting and every open subgroup $U \subseteq G_f$ there is a finite set $S \subseteq G_f$ such that every $x \in G_f$ is $x = \gamma s u$ with $\gamma \in U_J(F)$, $s \in S$ and $u \in U$.

*Proof.* The first step is a statement about global lattices: for every Hermitian $J$ with $\det J \neq 0$ there is a finite set $T$ of lattices such that every finitely generated, spanning, self-dual lattice $M \subseteq E^m$ is $\gamma M_0$ for some $\gamma \in U_J(E)$ and $M_0 \in T$. It follows from six facts together with the real majorant. A majorant family exists for each nondegenerate $J$. The codifferent of $E$ is contained in $\delta^{-1} \mathcal{O}_E$ for an integer $\delta \geq 1$. Every lattice has a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis $e_1, \dots, e_N$. For a lattice of the family of level $d$ with such a basis, the Gram determinant satisfies $0 < |\det \operatorname{Gr}(e)| \leq (d \delta)^N$. There is a bound $B$ such that each nonzero ideal $I$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$ has an element $c$ with $e I \subseteq c \mathcal{O}_E$ for some integer $1 \le e \le B$. And the splitting of lattices along a fixed Gram block has finitely many classes. With the real majorant, Minkowski's theorem on $c^T R c$ gives a nonzero vector $x$ with $|N_{E/\mathbb{Q}} h(x, x)|$ bounded in terms of $\det R = |\det \operatorname{Gr}(e)|$; splitting off this vector and inducting on the rank gives the finite set $T$.

The second step is a dictionary between $G_f$ and lattices. Each $x \in G_f$ gives a self-dual lattice $\Lambda(x)$, with $\Lambda(x) = \Lambda(y)$ only if $y \in xK$, and each $\gamma \in U_J(E)$ maps $\Lambda(x)$ to $\Lambda(\gamma x)$. With the first step this gives a finite set $S$ with $G_f = \bigcup_{s \in S} U_J(F)\, s\, K$.

The last step passes from $K$ to $U$. An open subgroup $U$ contains a principal congruence subgroup $K(g)$ of some level $g \geq 1$, and $K$ is a finite union of cosets $t K(g)$, since $K$ is compact. So $S' = \{ s t \}$ is finite and $G_f = \bigcup_{s' \in S'} U_J(F)\, s'\, U$. $\square$

**Remark 7.6.10 (Formalization).** The lemma on $GL_n$ and trace integrality are proved; their names are `gl_character` and `trace_integral'`, and the caller of index symmetry supplies the second ([AdIndexEq.lean](lean:Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/OrbitalBoundsConclusion/AdIndexEq.lean#L9)). We did not open the proof that quasi-reflections generate at a field place; the description of the field case follows the statement of the local input in [Commensurated.lean](lean:Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/UnitaryGroupMassFormula/AdelicIndexEquality/Commensurated.lean#L80), which asks only that $\chi_v$ is trivial on central elements and on elements with a power in $K_v$. The theorem on the real majorant is supplied by its caller as `successive_vector_real` ([AdelClassFin.lean](lean:Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/OrbitalBoundsConclusion/AdelClassFin.lean#L6)). The use of Minkowski's theorem and the induction on the rank are our reading of the names of the inputs; we did not unfold the proof of the first step. The conclusion is stated for every open subgroup $U$, not only compact ones.

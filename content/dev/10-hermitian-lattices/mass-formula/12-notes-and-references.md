## Notes and references

The chapter feeds three steps of the proof. The [nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/) use the stabilizer volumes, the level structures, the local weight functions and the folding of the Poincaré series, which together build the mass data of a Hida setting. The [local matching of theta lifts](/step/theta-local-matching/) uses the [class mass formula](/entry/unitary-class-mass-hermitian/), the volume formula, the Hecke relation at a ramified place and the second branch. The [character analysis](/step/every-character-of-literature/) uses the fibre measures of the Gram map. The local results of the first four sections enter through the [local mass of a type](/entry/loc-mass-typ/) and the [local densities](/entry/local-density-corank/).

Weyl modules over $\mathbb{Z}$ and their branching are treated in the theory of reductive group schemes; the polynomial model used in the first section is the model of the induced module $\nabla(\lambda)$ as a space of polynomial functions. The Gelfand-Tsetlin rule for the branching from $GL_N$ to $GL_{N-1}$ goes back to Gelfand and Tsetlin; the lowering operators and the quantum minors that realize it over the integers are the subject of Molev's book. The proof here works with integral lattices in the polynomial model and with an integrality statement for the pairing, not with the Yangian.

The local densities of Hermitian forms and their relation to point counts over finite fields are classical for quadratic forms, and the Hermitian case follows the same lines. The count of Hermitian matrices over a finite field and the reduction of the density at an unramified place to such a count follow the same pattern. The local mass of a type is our name for the volume of the stabilizer of a frame; it plays the role of the local factor in the mass formula.

The finiteness of the number of classes in a genus is a theorem of Borel for general reductive groups over number fields; the proof here is a direct reduction theory for Hermitian lattices with a majorant, close to the classical proof for positive definite forms by Minkowski reduction. The index symmetry is the statement that $U_J(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$ is unimodular, which holds for every reductive group; here it is proved from the triviality of positive characters on $GL_n$ and from quasi-reflections at non-split places.

The class mass formula, the identity between the sum of $1/\#\operatorname{Aut}$ over the classes and the volume of the adelic quotient, is the group-theoretic form of the mass formula of Smith, Minkowski and Siegel. Siegel stated it for quadratic forms together with the product of local densities; the form for unitary groups with an exact value of the local factors is due to Gan, Hanke and Yu, after Shimura. The proof here does not compute the mass: it identifies the sum over classes with the mass of the genus through strong approximation for lattices, and the volume formula then uses a normalized measure with $\mu(K) = 1$.

The stabilizer volume is proved from an identity of Siegel-Weil type for theta series, taken as an input. The Siegel-Weil formula is due to Siegel in the definite case and to Weil in general; the regularized forms for unitary groups are due to Kudla and Rallis for symplectic-orthogonal pairs and to Ichino for unitary pairs. The finiteness of the volume of the quotient is the theorem of Borel and Harish-Chandra.

The local weight functions are vectors of a Bessel model of a discrete series at a real place. The decay of the matrix coefficients of discrete series and their reproducing property go back to Harish-Chandra. The folding of a Poincaré series with a polynomial bound on the multiplicity is the standard unfolding argument; the bound here comes from the reduction theory of Hermitian lattices. In its last form, the polynomial bound on multiplicities assumes only the cover of compact sets by boxes, and the translation of the size and the norm power are proved.

This chapter proves the mass formula for definite Hermitian lattices over a CM extension $E/F$ and the local and global facts around it that the proof needs. It starts at a single place: integral branching maps between [Weyl modules](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching) and Gelfand-Tsetlin lowering, square pairs of lattices, transfer operators on a unitary group, and the [local densities](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#local-densities) of Hermitian forms with the local mass of a type. It then passes to the adelic group $U_J(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$ of a [unitary group](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups): lattice counts from index inequalities, the index symmetry that makes the group unimodular, the finiteness of the number of classes in a genus, the [class mass formula](/entry/unitary-class-mass-hermitian/) $\sum 1/\#\Gamma_{g_i} = \operatorname{mass}(h, L_0)$ and its form as a volume identity, and the equality of the volumes of a unitary group and of the stabilizer of a tuple, from a Siegel-Weil identity and the fibre measures of the Gram map. The last sections treat the level-zero Hecke relation and the length formula of the second branch, the local weight functions at a real place built from a chart on a nilradical, and the polynomial bound on multiplicities that folds the Poincaré series. The chapter uses from Volume 1 the theory of [lattices in Hermitian spaces](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#lattices-in-quadratic-and-hermitian-spaces), [places and completions](/learn/number-fields/#places-and-completions), [adeles](/learn/number-fields/#adeles-and-ideles), [adelic points and compact open subgroups](/learn/groups/#adelic-points-and-compact-open-subgroups), [parabolic subgroups](/learn/groups/#parabolic-subgroups) and [representations of a group](/learn/representations/#representations-of-a-group).

The class mass formula, the volume identity, the Hecke relation and the second branch are used in the [local matching of theta lifts](/step/theta-local-matching/). The stabilizer volumes, the level structures, the local weight functions and the folding are used in the [nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/), where the mass data of a Hida setting are built. The fibre measures of the Gram map are used in the [character analysis](/step/every-character-of-literature/).

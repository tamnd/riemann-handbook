## Rows at a record and the irreducibility row

This section proves the irreducibility row: along a family class, the concrete automorphic data with the three ties of the twelfth section give rows whose cylinder counts are close to their main terms. The first step describes the level set $K_2$ of a Hida setting and its character by a shape of unipotent matrices. The second step builds the rows at one datum from the three ties and the counting theorem of the seventh section. The last step makes this uniform with the counting constants. The irreducibility row is used by [the counting row of the ties](/entry/row-counts-of-ties-alt/).

### The shape of the level set

Let $X$ be a Hida setting for a datum $D$ of rank $r$, with type primes $g_0 \ne g_1$ and the finite level group $G_0 = \prod_{(k,v)} \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb Z/g_k)$ of the twelfth section. Let $\rho$ be the reduction from the maximal compact subgroup $K$ of the finite group of $X$ onto $G_0$. Let $s = r_S$ and let $\ell$ be the level, with $r = \ell + 2s + 1$.

A matrix $M$ of size $r$ over $\mathbb Z/g$ has *shape $J$* if it is upper unipotent and $M_{a, s+\ell} = 0$ for $s \le a < s + \ell$. Let $\phi_J(M)$ be the sum of the superdiagonal entries of $M$ along the chain of the shape. On the matrices of shape $J$, $\phi_J$ is additive, so $\psi \circ \phi_J$ is a character for each additive character $\psi$ of $\mathbb Z/g$.

**Theorem 3.15.1 (Shape of the level set, forward part).** There are $P \in G_0$ and nontrivial additive characters $\psi_v$ of $\mathbb Z/g_k$, one for each chosen place $v$ over $g_k$, such that each $x \in K \cap K_2$ has these properties:

1. $P_v^{-1} \rho(x)_v P_v$ has shape $J$ at each chosen place $v$;
2. $\chi_2(x) = \prod_v \psi_v\bigl(\phi_J(P_v^{-1} \rho(x)_v P_v)\bigr)$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.KppFwdPf.kppFwd_holds`.

*Proof.*

1. The rank data of $D$ give $m/2 - 1 = r_S$, so the shape of the level set is the shape $J$ with $s = r_S$.
2. For each $x \in K_2$ and each place $v$ there is a conjugator $P_v$ with property 1. It does not depend on $x$.
3. At each place $v$ over $g_k$ choose $\varepsilon_v$ in the ideal generated by the other prime $g_{1-k}$, with $\varepsilon_v$ a unit at $v$. The character $\psi_v = \psi_{\varepsilon_v}$ is then nontrivial.
4. The character $\chi_2$ is a quadratic character, and it equals an additive character of an argument built from $x$. This argument splits over the places $v$ with the elements $\varepsilon_v$. At each $v$ the local term is $\phi_J(P_v^{-1} \rho(x)_v P_v)$. This gives property 2. $\square$

**Theorem 3.15.2 (Shape of the level set).** There are $P \in G_0$ and nontrivial characters $\psi_v$ such that, for each $x \in K$:

1. $x \in K_2$ if and only if $P_v^{-1} \rho(x)_v P_v$ has shape $J$ at each chosen place $v$;
2. if $x \in K_2$, then $\chi_2(x) = \prod_v \psi_v\bigl(\phi_J(P_v^{-1} \rho(x)_v P_v)\bigr)$.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.AllRowsClosed.kppShape_holds`.

*Proof.* The forward part gives $P$, the characters, property 2 and one direction of property 1. So $\rho(K_2)$ lies in the set $J(P)$ of elements of $G_0$ whose conjugates by $P$ have shape $J$. The reduction $\rho$ is surjective. The index of $K_2$ in $K$ is the index of the level subgroup, and it equals the number of elements of $J(P)$. Hence $\rho(K_2) = J(P)$, and $K_2$ contains the kernel of $\rho$. This gives the other direction. $\square$

### Rows at one datum

*Rows* for $D$ are a group record, idempotents of $G_0$ for each tuple of labels, and a dimension $\dim_T$ for each cylinder of tuples. The rows *count labels injectively* if the labels count $\dim_T$ and the good tuples are the image of an injective map from the rows of $D$. The rows are *joined* if the group record is tied to $D$ and the idempotents come from a nontrivial additive character of $\mathbb Z/g_k$. For $0 \le \varepsilon$, the rows *count all cylinders with error $\varepsilon$* if $\varepsilon < 1$, the main term $D^\ast$ is positive, and for $k \in \{0, 1\}$ and each set $S$ of tuples the cylinder of $S$ satisfies
$$|\dim_T - D^\ast| \le \varepsilon\, D^\ast.$$
Write $\varepsilon_C(c, r, f) = \exp(-c\, r f \log r)$.

**Theorem 3.15.3 (Rows at a record).** Assume the finite dimension of rows. Let $D$ be a datum of rank $r$ and degree $f$ with size data, a Hida setting $X$, a good operator and an isometry $\iota$ of row spaces. Let $n_0 \in \mathbb N$ and $A_{\mathrm{lev}}, C_3, c_3, c_b, c$ be real with $0 < c \le c_3$. Assume the gap condition, the automorphic tie, the row dictionary tie and the label tie, the counting theorem for idempotents of each class, the hypotheses of the counting theorem, and $4r < g_1$. Then there are rows for $D$ that count labels injectively, are joined, and count all cylinders with error $\varepsilon_C(c, r, f)$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.AllRowsAtRecord.allRows_at_record`.

*Proof.*

1. The finite dimension of rows, the gap condition and the three ties give labels for the rows of $X$.
2. Let $\psi_k$ be the standard additive character $x \mapsto e^{2\pi i x/g_k}$ of $\mathbb Z/g_k$. It is nontrivial since $g_k$ is a prime.
3. Build the rows $A$ from $X$, $\psi$ and the labels. Their idempotents come from the shape of the level set. The label tie gives the injective count of labels. The join comes from $\psi$, the automorphic tie, the Harish-Chandra parameters of $X$ and the splitting of the type primes.
4. The automorphic tie, the hypotheses of the counting theorem and $4r < g_1$ give the count base, with $D^\ast > 0$. With it, the counting theorem for idempotents of the counting class gives, for each cylinder,
$$|\dim_T - D^\ast| \le \varepsilon_C(c_3, r, f)\, D^\ast.$$
5. Since $c \le c_3$, $r \ge 2$ and $f \ge 1$, we have $\varepsilon_C(c_3, r, f) \le \varepsilon_C(c, r, f) < 1$. $\square$

### The irreducibility row

**Theorem 3.15.4 (Irreducibility row).** Assume the Siegel-Weil formula in its convergent range and the finite dimension of rows. There is $c_{I} > 0$ with this property. Let $\mathcal C$ be a family class whose families satisfy the size condition. Assume that it is [closed](/step/closed-def/) on $\mathcal C$ that, eventually in $x$, each datum $D(x, i)$ has a Hida setting, a good operator and an isometry with the automorphic tie, the row dictionary tie and the label tie. Then it is closed on $\mathcal C$ that, eventually in $x$, each $D(x, i)$ has rows that count labels injectively, are joined, and count all cylinders with error $\varepsilon_C(c_I, r, f)$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.IrrRowConcrete.irr_row_closed` (also `GlobalTheory.T.irreducibilityRow_of_concreteAutomorphicData`, a second name).

*Proof.*

1. [The counting constants](/entry/count-constants-of-siegel-weil/) give the constants $c_{30}(\ell)$ for each list $\ell$ and a number $0 < c_I \le c$. They give the counting theorem for idempotents of each class at each $D(x, i)$, with $c_b > 0$.
2. It is closed on $\mathcal C$ that, eventually, the hypotheses of the counting theorem hold at each $D(x, i)$ with the constants $c_{30}$, and that $4r < g_1$.
3. It is closed on $\mathcal C$ that, eventually, the gap condition holds at each $D(x, i)$.
4. Join the hypothesis and steps 2 and 3. For $x$ past the largest threshold, the rows at a record give the rows at each $D(x, i)$. $\square$

The constant $c_I$ does not depend on the family class. It comes from the Siegel-Weil formula through the volume step of the fifth section and the counting constants $c_I = 1/16$ of the seventh section.

## Notes and references

This chapter proves the flag measure bound for unitary groups over CM fields, the counting theorem that it feeds, and the off-diagonal bound for spectral ties. The counting theorem and the row estimates serve [the counting row of the ties](/entry/row-counts-of-ties-alt/), and the off-diagonal bound is part 2 of [the choice of spectral ties](/entry/at-choice-alt/). The global setting serves [the spectral data for large gaps](/entry/exists-spectral-data-gap-alt/). All three go into [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/).

The Iwasawa integration formula of the first section is the classical formula $dg = e^{2\rho(\log a)}\, dn\, da\, dk$ for $G = NAK$, written along an isotropic flag with the constant made explicit. The parabolic form $G = NMK$ with the modular character of $P = MN$ is standard.

- A. W. Knapp, *Lie Groups Beyond an Introduction*, 2nd ed., Birkhäuser, 2002: Chapter VI (the Iwasawa decomposition) and Chapter VIII (integration formulas).
- G. B. Folland, *A Course in Abstract Harmonic Analysis*: Chapter 2, §2.4 (the modular function) and §2.6 (quotient measures on homogeneous spaces).

The unipotent radical and the Levi factor of the second section, and the covolume of their integral points, follow the reduction theory of arithmetic groups. The bound of one flag class and the unfolding of the third and fourth sections are the adelic form of Siegel's mean value theorem, summed over the rational flags.

- A. Borel and J. Tits, "Groupes réductifs", *Publ. Math. IHÉS* 27 (1965), pages 55 to 150.
- A. Borel and Harish-Chandra, "Arithmetic subgroups of algebraic groups", *Ann. of Math.* 75 (1962), pages 485 to 535.
- A. Borel, "Some finiteness properties of adele groups over number fields", *Publ. Math. IHÉS* 16 (1963), pages 5 to 30.
- V. Platonov and A. Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory*: Chapter 4 (reduction theory), Chapter 5 (adeles) and Chapter 8 (class numbers).
- C. L. Siegel, "A mean value theorem in geometry of numbers", *Ann. of Math.* 46 (1945), pages 340 to 347.

The volume step of the fifth section is the Siegel-Weil formula in its convergent range, for unitary groups. The local measures of the eleventh section are the self-dual measures of Tate's thesis, and the fibre measures are those of Weil's proof of the Siegel formula.

- C. L. Siegel, "Über die analytische Theorie der quadratischen Formen", *Ann. of Math.* 36 (1935), pages 527 to 606.
- A. Weil, "Sur certains groupes d'opérateurs unitaires", *Acta Math.* 111 (1964), pages 143 to 211.
- A. Weil, "Sur la formule de Siegel dans la théorie des groupes classiques", *Acta Math.* 113 (1965), pages 1 to 87.
- S. S. Kudla and S. Rallis, "A regularized Siegel-Weil formula: the first term identity", *Ann. of Math.* 140 (1994), pages 1 to 80.
- A. Ichino, "A regularized Siegel-Weil formula for unitary groups", *Math. Z.* 247 (2004), pages 241 to 277.
- J. Tate, "Fourier analysis in number fields and Hecke's zeta-functions", in J. W. S. Cassels and A. Fröhlich, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Chapter XV.

The rank two classes of the sixth section are counted by a mass formula for unitary groups. Exact mass formulas of this kind are due to Shimura and to Gan, Hanke and Yu.

- W. T. Gan, J. P. Hanke and J.-K. Yu, "On an exact mass formula of Shimura", *Duke Math. J.* 107 (2001), pages 103 to 133.

The counting theorem of the seventh section is a limit multiplicity statement with an explicit error, of the kind proved by de George and Wallach, Savin and Shin. The flag measure bound controls the terms near the cusps. The row estimates of the eighth section follow the same counting along families with large gaps.

- D. L. de George and N. R. Wallach, "Limit formulas for multiplicities in $L^2(\Gamma \backslash G)$", *Ann. of Math.* 107 (1978), pages 133 to 150.
- G. Savin, "Limit multiplicities of cusp forms", *Invent. Math.* 95 (1989), pages 149 to 159.
- S. W. Shin, "Automorphic Plancherel density theorem", *Israel J. Math.* 192 (2012), pages 83 to 120.

The off-diagonal bound of the ninth and thirteenth sections and the carrier bound of the fourteenth section rest on the uniqueness of Bessel maps for large gaps. This is an archimedean form of the multiplicity one theorems for Bessel models.

- A. Aizenbud, D. Gourevitch, S. Rallis and G. Schiffmann, "Multiplicity one theorems", *Ann. of Math.* 172 (2010), pages 1407 to 1434.
- B. Sun and C.-B. Zhu, "Multiplicity one theorems: the Archimedean case", *Ann. of Math.* 175 (2012), pages 23 to 44.
- W. T. Gan, B. H. Gross and D. Prasad, "Symplectic local root numbers, central critical L-values, and restriction problems in the representation theory of classical groups", *Astérisque* 346 (2012), pages 1 to 109.

The level structures of the tenth section and the labels of the twelfth section use the reduction of a maximal compact subgroup to a finite group of matrices, and the isotypic projections of a finite group. The shape of the level set in this section is the description of a congruence subgroup by a generic character of a unipotent group.

- J.-P. Serre, *Linear Representations of Finite Groups*: Chapter 2, §2.6 (the canonical decomposition and the isotypic projections).

This theorem is the local input of the [Flath factorisation](/entry/flath-factorisation-of-literature/). It says that each open subgroup of a $p$-adic unitary group contains a compact open subgroup $K$ such that the $K$-fixed parts of all unitary representations satisfy one polynomial identity. The same file uses it twice: `admissible_of` turns it into admissibility of irreducible unitary representations (`unitaryIrreducibleAdmissible`), and `flath_of` uses it again for `flathTensorProduct`.

## Statement

For a ring element $E$ and $a_1, \dots, a_M$, write
$$s_M(E; a) = \sum_{\pi \in S_M} \operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\, E a_{\pi(1)} E a_{\pi(2)} E \cdots E a_{\pi(M)} E,$$
the standard polynomial of degree $M$ in the compressed elements $E a_i E$ (`stdPoly`). Let $G$ be a group and $K \le G$ a subgroup. Say that $(K, M)$ *has the identity* (`HasPI K M`) if for each Hilbert space $H$, each representation $\sigma$ of $G$ by isometries, each operator $E$ that averages over $K$ (`IsAvg`) and all $g_1, \dots, g_M \in G$, we have $s_M(E; \sigma(g_1), \dots, \sigma(g_M)) = 0$.

**Theorem** (`LocPI`). Let $E/F$ be a CM extension of number fields, $J$ a nondegenerate Hermitian matrix of size $n$ over $E$, $G = U(J)$, and $v$ a finite place of $F$. Then each open subgroup $U$ of $G(F_v)$ contains a subgroup $K$ such that $(K, M)$ has the identity for some $M$.

The proof also makes $K$ compact and open. With `FinOfPI`, the identity bounds by $M$ the dimension of the $K$-fixed space of each irreducible unitary representation. This is uniform admissibility, as in Bernstein's theorem that reductive $p$-adic groups are tame (I. N. Bernstein, Funct. Anal. Appl. 8, 1974). We did not compare the two proofs.

## Proof

The proof is `locPI_of` applied to thirteen proved statements. `locPI_of` does three things.

1. **A Cartan decomposition.** `StDict` says that $G(F_v)$ is isomorphic, as a topological group, to $\mathrm{GL}_n(L)$ for a local field $L$, or to a unitary group $U(s, J')$ over $L$ with a nontrivial involution $s$. The first case uses `StCartanGL`, the second uses [`stCartanU`](/entry/st-cartan-u/). `StMove` carries the result back along the isomorphism. So $U$ contains a compact open $K$ with a Cartan-type decomposition $G = \bigcup_{i,j} K c_i S c_j K$, where $S$ is a monoid generated by commuting elements.
2. **Separation.** `StDefinite` replaces $J$ by a positive definite $J'$ with an isomorphic local group. For definite $J'$, `stSepDefB` gives, for each finite set of cosets $gK$, a subgroup $\Gamma$ with finitely many orbits on $G/K$ and finite stabilizers that separates the set. `StSepMove` carries this to $K$.
3. **The identity.** `hasPI_of` joins five statements. The Cartan decomposition makes the Hecke algebra $\mathcal{H}(G, K)$ spanned by $x_i c\, y_j$ with $c$ in a commutative subalgebra (`StHecke`). Then its irreducible finite-dimensional modules have dimension at most some $C$ (`StSimple`, from `StComm`). Separation gives faithful star-closed finite-dimensional representations (`StMod`). On these, $s_{C^2+1}$ vanishes (`StStar`). `StTransfer` moves the identity from $\mathcal{H}(G, K)$ to every unitary representation.

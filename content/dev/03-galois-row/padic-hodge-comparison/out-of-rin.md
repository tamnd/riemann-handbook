This theorem proves the statement `Out` of [the entry above](/entry/out/) from five hypotheses `RIn` on perfect algebras and their crystalline rings. Below this theorem are 350 declarations.

## Statement

The notation $\mathcal{O}^\flat$, $\pi$, $A_{\inf}$, $\xi$, $U_n$, $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$, $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n$ is as in [out](/entry/out/).

**Perfect algebras.** An object $A$ of `PAlg` is a perfect ring of characteristic $\ell$ with a ring map $\mathcal{O}^\flat \to A$, such that $A$ is $\pi$-adically complete and $\pi$ is not a zero divisor in $A$. It is a *test algebra* if each monic polynomial of positive degree over $A$ has a root, and if for all $f, g \in A$ with $\pi^N \in (f, g)$ for some $N$ there is an idempotent $e$ with $ef \in (g)$ and $(1-e)g \in (f)$.

**Descent up to $s$.** Let $F$ be a functor from `PAlg` to $R$-modules, and $s \in R$. For $A$ in `PAlg`, let $\mathcal{C}_A$ be the category of maps $A \to T$ with $T$ a test algebra. Let $C^p$ be the product of $F(T_p)$ over the chains $T_0 \to \dots \to T_p$ in $\mathcal{C}_A$, with the alternating sum of faces as differential and the augmentation $F(A) \to C^0$. Then $F$ satisfies descent up to $s$ (`Des F s`) if, for each $A$, this augmented complex is exact up to $s$: its composite maps are $0$, $s$ kills the kernel of the augmentation, and $s$ times each cocycle is a coboundary.

**Theorem.** Assume `RIn`, that is:

1. (`ObFacts`) $\mathcal{O}^\flat$ is a valuation domain, each nonzero $a$ divides a power of $\pi$, and each non-unit is divisible by some $\pi^{1/\ell^k}$;
2. (`RAcr1`) $A_{\mathrm{cris},1}(A) \cong N \otimes_{\mathcal{O}^\flat} A$, naturally and semilinearly, for a module $N$ killed by a power of $\pi$;
3. (`RAcrSeq`) multiplication by $\ell^n$ gives exact sequences $0 \to A_{\mathrm{cris},1} \to A_{\mathrm{cris},n+1} \to A_{\mathrm{cris},n} \to 0$;
4. (`RU`) $\ell$ is not a zero divisor in $W(A)/\xi$, and for the quotient map $\theta \colon W(A) \to W(A)/\xi$, $\theta(x) \in (\ell)$ if and only if $x_0 \in (\pi)$;
5. (`RGr`) a description of the graded pieces $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n / \mathrm{Fil}^{r+1}_n$ by $U_n$.

Then `Out` holds.

## Proof

1. `wm_span`: by `ObFacts`, each $s \in W(\mathfrak{m}^\flat)$ lies in the ideal generated by $\ell^n$ and the $[\pi^{1/\ell^k}]$, $k \geq 0$.
2. `des_span` and `des_of_map`: the $s$ with `Des F s` include the elements that kill $F$, and they are closed under sums and multiples. Each of the three functors is killed by $\ell^n$. So it is enough to treat each $[\pi^{1/\ell^k}]$.
3. For $U_n$ this is `des_Un`, from `ObFacts` and `RU`.
4. For $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$ it is `des_Acr`, by induction on $n$. For $n = 0$ the ring is $0$. For $n = 1$, `des_Acr_one` uses `RAcr1` and `des_tens`. For $n + 1$, `des_Acr_succ` applies `des_ses` to the sequence of `RAcrSeq`: descent up to $a$ on the two ends gives descent up to $a^3$ in the middle. As $[\pi^{1/\ell^{k+2}}]^3$ divides $[\pi^{1/\ell^k}]$ (`rtW_dvd`), the bound passes from $k + 2$ to $k$.
5. For $\mathrm{Fil}^r_n$ it is `des_Fil`, which is `des_Fil_of` with the result of step 4. It is an induction on $r$ by the graded pieces of `RGr`.

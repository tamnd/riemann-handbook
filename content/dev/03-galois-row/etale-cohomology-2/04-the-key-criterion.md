## The key criterion for a matrix group

This section and the next one are linear algebra. They find a small subrepresentation inside a representation $\rho$ of a compact group, from an element of the Lie algebra of the Zariski closure of the image of $\rho$. The present section proves the main step. Given a good system of idempotents attached to that element, it shows two things: each coset of a normal subgroup of finite index meets the Zariski closure in an element with distinct eigenvalues, and $\rho$ moves the corners of the system inside the algebra generated by the Lie algebra. No topology enters here. The result is used in [the theorem on small subrepresentations](/entry/goal-holds/).

Let $C$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ and $N \geq 1$. We write $M_N(C)$ for the algebra of $N \times N$ matrices, see [matrices](/learn/algebra/#matrices), and we view a polynomial $f$ in the $N^2$ entries as a function on $M_N(C)$.

**Definition (Zariski closure and tangent space).** Let $S \subseteq M_N(C)$. The *Zariski closure* $\overline{S}$ is the set of $y$ with $f(y) = 0$ for each polynomial $f$ that vanishes on $S$. The *tangent space* $T(S)$ is the set of $Y \in M_N(C)$ with $df_1(Y) = 0$ for each such $f$, where $df_1(Y) = \sum_{a,b} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{ab}}(1)\, Y_{ab}$.

Let $\Gamma$ be a group and $\rho : \Gamma \to \mathrm{GL}_N(C)$ a homomorphism, see [[GL|0FCHE902D7]]. For a subgroup $U$ we write $\rho(U)$ for its image in $M_N(C)$. The *Lie algebra* of $\rho$ is $\mathfrak{g} = T(\rho(\Gamma))$. It is the [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] of the Zariski closure of $\rho(\Gamma)$ in $\mathrm{GL}_N(C)$, an algebraic group, see A. Borel, *Linear Algebraic Groups*, Chapter I, §3. The *envelope* $A$ is the [[subalgebra|7CHG6SD365]] of $M_N(C)$ generated by $\mathfrak{g}$.

**Definition (Degree, eigenvalues, lacunary).** A matrix $x$ has *degree at most* $n$ if $q(x) = 0$ for some nonzero polynomial $q$ of degree at most $n$. It has *$n$ distinct eigenvalues* if its [[characteristic polynomial|039HF58KQ5]] has $n$ distinct roots. A family $e_1, \dots, e_n \in C$ is *lacunary* if for all disjoint nonempty sets $O, O' \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$
$$|O'| \sum_{i \in O} e_i \ne |O| \sum_{i \in O'} e_i.$$

So $e$ is lacunary when the averages of $e$ over two disjoint nonempty sets are never equal. In particular the $e_i$ are distinct.

**Definition (Good system).** A *system* is a family $p_1, \dots, p_n$ of nonzero [[complete orthogonal idempotents|SS8T32TMEX]] in $M_N(C)$. A system $p$ is a *corner system* of a subalgebra $A$ if each $p_i \in A$ and $p_i a p_i \in C p_i$ for each $a \in A$. A subalgebra $A$ has *no nil ideal* if each $Y \in A$ such that $aY$ is [[nilpotent|0M8MHYERTY]] for all $a \in A$ is zero. Let $e$ be a family in $C$. The triple $(\rho, e, p)$ is *good* if:

1. $p$ is a system and $\sum_i e_i p_i \in \mathfrak{g}$;
2. $p$ is a corner system of the envelope $A$, and $A$ has no nil ideal;
3. $\rho(g)\, A\, \rho(g)^{-1} = A$ for each $g$, and $A$ keeps each $\rho$-invariant subspace of $C^N$;
4. $p_i a p_j \in \mathfrak{g}$ for each $a \in A$ and $i \ne j$.

**Theorem (Key criterion).** Let $\Gamma$ be a group, $\rho : \Gamma \to \mathrm{GL}_N(C)$ a homomorphism, $e_1, \dots, e_n \in C$ lacunary, and $p_1, \dots, p_n \in M_N(C)$ such that $(\rho, e, p)$ is good. Assume that each $\rho(g)$ has degree at most $n$. Let $U$ be a normal subgroup of finite index in $\Gamma$ and $g \in \Gamma$. Then:

1. there is $m \in \overline{\rho(U)}$ such that $\rho(g) m$ has $n$ distinct eigenvalues;
2. for each $i$, if $p_i a \rho(g) p_i \ne 0$ for some $a \in A$, then $p_i \rho(g) = p_i w$ for some $w \in A$.

The proof combines ten statements. The code proves each of them in its own file. We state them here. We opened their statements and the proof of the theorem, but not their proofs. For two of them we add the short argument.

**Definition (Unipotent monoid, multiplicative lacunarity, separated cycles).** Let $p$ be a system and $A$ a subalgebra. The *unipotent monoid* $T_A$ is the monoid generated by the matrices $1 + p_j a p_k$ with $a \in A$ and $j \ne k$. For a finite set $B \subseteq C$, a family $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_n$ of nonzero numbers is *lacunary over* $B$ if for all disjoint nonempty $O, O'$ and all $b \in B$
$$\Big(\prod_{i \in O} \tau_i\Big)^{|O'|} \ne b \Big(\prod_{i \in O'} \tau_i\Big)^{|O|}.$$
Let $\pi$ be a permutation of $\{1, \dots, n\}$ and $y \in M_N(C)$. We say that $y$ has *separated cycles* for $(\pi, p)$ if for two different cycles $c \ne c'$ of $\pi$ there is no common eigenvalue of $y$ with an eigenvector in $\sum_{k \in c} p_k C^N$ and an eigenvector in $\sum_{k \in c'} p_k C^N$.

**Lemma (Inputs).** Let $A$ be a subalgebra of $M_N(C)$ and $p$ a system.

1. (Conjugate systems.) If $p$ and $p'$ are corner systems of $A$ and $A$ has no nil ideal, then there are a unit $v \in A$ and a permutation $\pi$ with $v p_j = p'_{\pi(j)} v$ for all $j$. If $p'_i a p_i \ne 0$ for some $a \in A$, then $\pi(i) = i$.
2. (Matrix units.) If $p$ is a corner system of $A$ and $A$ has no nil ideal, then $A$ has a family of matrix units adapted to $p$.
3. (Factorization.) In this case each unit $v \in A$ is $v = u \sum_j d_j p_j$ with $u \in T_A$ and all $d_j \ne 0$.
4. (Finite index.) If $U$ is normal of finite index in $\Gamma$, then $\mathfrak{g} \subseteq T(\rho(U))$.
5. (Unipotent elements.) If $S$ is a monoid, $E \in T(S)$ and $E^2 = 0$, then $1 + E \in \overline{S}$.
6. (Products.) If $S$ is a monoid, then $\overline{S}$ is closed under products.
7. (Generic twist.) If $x$ is a unit with $x p_j = p_{\pi(j)} x$ for all $j$, there is a finite $B \subseteq C$ such that $x \sum_j \tau_j p_j$ has separated cycles for $(\pi, p)$ whenever $\tau$ is lacunary over $B$.
8. (Torus points.) If $S$ is a monoid, $e$ is lacunary and $\sum_i e_i p_i \in T(S)$, then for each finite $B$ there is $\tau$ lacunary over $B$ with $\sum_j \tau_j p_j \in \overline{S}$.
9. (Degree.) If each element of a monoid $S$ has degree at most $n$, then so does each element of $\overline{S}$.
10. (Cycle count.) Let $y$ be a unit of degree at most $n$ with $y p_j = p_{\pi(j)} y$ for all $j$ and with separated cycles. Then $y$ has $n$ distinct eigenvalues, and $y p_i \in C p_i$ for each $i$ with $\pi(i) = i$.

*Proof.* We give the argument for 4 and 6 only. For 6: for $s \in S$, the map $x \mapsto sx$ is linear and maps $S$ into $S$, so it maps $\overline{S}$ into $\overline{S}$. So $S \overline{S} \subseteq \overline{S}$. Then, for fixed $y \in \overline{S}$, the map $x \mapsto xy$ maps $S$ into $\overline{S}$, so it maps $\overline{S}$ into $\overline{S}$. For 4: let $G$ and $H$ be the Zariski closures of $\rho(\Gamma)$ and $\rho(U)$ in $\mathrm{GL}_N(C)$. They are algebraic groups, and $H$ has finite index in $G$, so $H$ contains the identity component of $G$. So $G$ and $H$ have the same Lie algebra (Borel, *loc. cit.*). Since $\mathrm{GL}_N(C)$ is open in $M_N(C)$, the tangent spaces at $1$ of $G$ and $H$ are $T(\rho(\Gamma))$ and $T(\rho(U))$. $\square$

*Proof of the theorem.* Write $R = \rho(g)$.

Step 1. Put $p'_j = R^{-1} p_j R$. Since $R^{-1} A R = A$, the family $p'$ is again a corner system of $A$. By conjugate systems there are a unit $v \in A$ and a permutation $\pi$ with $v p_j = p'_{\pi(j)} v$.

Step 2. By matrix units and factorization, $v = u D$ with $u \in T_A$ and $D = \sum_j d_j p_j$, all $d_j \ne 0$. Then $u p_j = v D^{-1} p_j = d_j^{-1} v p_j = d_j^{-1} p'_{\pi(j)} v$, and so $u p_j = p'_{\pi(j)} u$. For $x = R u$ this gives $x p_j = p_{\pi(j)} x$.

Step 3. By the generic twist, there is a finite set $B$ for $x$ and $\pi$. Since $(\rho, e, p)$ is good, $\sum_i e_i p_i \in \mathfrak{g}$, and $\mathfrak{g} \subseteq T(\rho(U))$ by the finite index statement. By torus points there is $\tau$ lacunary over $B$ with $t = \sum_j \tau_j p_j \in \overline{\rho(U)}$. Then $y = x t = R u t$ has separated cycles, and $y p_j = p_{\pi(j)} y$, because $t$ commutes with each $p_j$.

Step 4. We show $u \in \overline{\rho(U)}$. A generator $1 + E$ of $T_A$ has $E = p_j a p_k$ with $j \ne k$. By part 4 of goodness, $E \in \mathfrak{g} \subseteq T(\rho(U))$, and $E^2 = p_j a (p_k p_j) a p_k = 0$. So $1 + E \in \overline{\rho(U)}$ by unipotent elements, and $u \in \overline{\rho(U)}$ by products. Put $m = u t \in \overline{\rho(U)}$.

Step 5. Since $R \in \rho(\Gamma)$ and $m \in \overline{\rho(\Gamma)}$, products give $y = R m \in \overline{\rho(\Gamma)}$. Each $\rho(h)$ has degree at most $n$, so $y$ has degree at most $n$ by the degree statement. By the cycle count, $y = \rho(g) m$ has $n$ distinct eigenvalues. This is the first claim.

Step 6. Let $p_i a R p_i \ne 0$ with $a \in A$. Put $a' = R^{-1} a R \in A$. Then $R\, p'_i a' p_i = p_i a R p_i \ne 0$, so $p'_i a' p_i \ne 0$, and $\pi(i) = i$ by conjugate systems. The cycle count gives $y p_i = c\, p_i$ with $c \in C$. As $y p_i = p_{\pi(i)} y = p_i y$, also $p_i y = c\, p_i$. Now $R = y\, m^{-1}$ and $m^{-1} = t^{-1} u^{-1} = T' D v^{-1}$ with $T' = \sum_j \tau_j^{-1} p_j$. Hence $p_i R = p_i w$ with $w = c\, T' D v^{-1}$. The inverse of a unit of a finite dimensional algebra lies in the algebra, so $v^{-1} \in A$, and $T', D \in A$ because each $p_j \in A$. So $w \in A$. This is the second claim. $\square$

**Remark.** The theorem in the code asks that $(\rho, e, p)$ be good, and goodness includes the condition that $A$ keeps each $\rho$-invariant subspace. The proof of the theorem does not use this condition; the variable is named `_hKe` in [SumMulSum.lean](lean:Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/SumMulSum.lean#L93). The condition is used later, in the next section. The statement holds for any group $\Gamma$ and needs no topology.

**Remark.** The Lean names of the theorem say "étale", "divisibility", "intersection number" and "base change". None of these occurs in the statement, which is linear algebra over an algebraically closed field. The folder name is also unrelated to the content.

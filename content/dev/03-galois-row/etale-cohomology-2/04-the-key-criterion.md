## The key criterion for a matrix group

This section and the next one are linear algebra. They find a small subrepresentation inside a representation $\rho$ of a compact group, from an element of the Lie algebra of the Zariski closure of the image of $\rho$. The present section proves the main step. Given a good system of idempotents attached to that element, it shows two things: each coset of a normal subgroup of finite index meets the Zariski closure in an element with distinct eigenvalues, and $\rho$ moves the corners of the system inside the algebra generated by the Lie algebra. No topology enters here. The result is used in [the theorem on small subrepresentations](/entry/goal-holds/).

Let $C$ be a field and $N \geq 0$ an integer. In the theorem, $C$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$; some of the lemmas need less. We write $M_N(C)$ for the algebra of $N \times N$ matrices, see [matrices](/learn/algebra/#matrices), and we view a polynomial $f$ in the $N^2$ entries as a function on $M_N(C)$.

**Definition 4.4.1 (Zariski closure and tangent space).** Let $S \subseteq M_N(C)$. The *Zariski closure* $\overline{S}$ is the set of $y$ with $f(y) = 0$ for each polynomial $f$ that vanishes on $S$. The *tangent space* $T(S)$ is the set of $Y \in M_N(C)$ with $df_1(Y) = 0$ for each such $f$, where $df_1(Y) = \sum_{a,b} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{ab}}(1)\, Y_{ab}$.

Let $\Gamma$ be a group and $\rho : \Gamma \to \mathrm{GL}_N(C)$ a homomorphism, see [[GL|0FCHE902D7]]. For a subgroup $U$ we write $\rho(U)$ for its image in $M_N(C)$. The *Lie algebra* of $\rho$ is $\mathfrak{g} = T(\rho(\Gamma))$. It is the [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] of the Zariski closure of $\rho(\Gamma)$ in $\mathrm{GL}_N(C)$, an algebraic group, see A. Borel, *Linear Algebraic Groups*, Chapter I, §3. The *envelope* $A$ is the [[subalgebra|7CHG6SD365]] of $M_N(C)$ generated by $\mathfrak{g}$.

**Definition 4.4.2 (Degree, eigenvalues, lacunary).** A matrix $x$ has *degree at most* $n$ if $q(x) = 0$ for some nonzero polynomial $q$ of degree at most $n$. It has *at least $n$ distinct eigenvalues* if its [[characteristic polynomial|039HF58KQ5]] has $n$ distinct roots. A family $e_1, \dots, e_n \in C$ is *lacunary* if for all disjoint nonempty sets $O, O' \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$
$$|O'| \sum_{i \in O} e_i \ne |O| \sum_{i \in O'} e_i.$$

So $e$ is lacunary when the averages of $e$ over two disjoint nonempty sets are never equal. In particular the $e_i$ are distinct.

**Definition 4.4.3 (Good system).** A *system* is a family $p_1, \dots, p_n$ of nonzero [[complete orthogonal idempotents|SS8T32TMEX]] in $M_N(C)$. A system $p$ is a *corner system* of a subalgebra $A$ if each $p_i \in A$ and $p_i a p_i \in C p_i$ for each $a \in A$. A subalgebra $A$ has *no nil ideal* if each $Y \in A$ such that $aY$ is [[nilpotent|0M8MHYERTY]] for all $a \in A$ is zero. Let $e$ be a family in $C$. The triple $(\rho, e, p)$ is *good* if:

1. $p$ is a system and $\sum_i e_i p_i \in \mathfrak{g}$;
2. $p$ is a corner system of the envelope $A$, and $A$ has no nil ideal;
3. $\rho(g)\, A\, \rho(g)^{-1} \subseteq A$ for each $g$, and $A$ keeps each $\rho$-invariant subspace of $C^N$;
4. $p_i a p_j \in \mathfrak{g}$ for each $a \in A$ and $i \ne j$.

Applied to $g^{-1}$, condition 3 gives $\rho(g)^{-1} A\, \rho(g) \subseteq A$, so in fact $\rho(g)\, A\, \rho(g)^{-1} = A$.

**Theorem 4.4.4 (Key criterion).** Let $C$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. Let $\Gamma$ be a group, $\rho : \Gamma \to \mathrm{GL}_N(C)$ a homomorphism, $e_1, \dots, e_n \in C$ lacunary, and $p_1, \dots, p_n \in M_N(C)$ such that $(\rho, e, p)$ is good. Assume that each $\rho(g)$ has degree at most $n$. Let $U$ be a normal subgroup of finite index in $\Gamma$ and $g \in \Gamma$. Then:

1. there is $m \in \overline{\rho(U)}$ such that $\rho(g) m$ has at least $n$ distinct eigenvalues;
2. for each $i$, if $p_i a \rho(g) p_i \ne 0$ for some $a \in A$, then $p_i \rho(g) = p_i w$ for some $w \in A$.

The proof combines ten statements on matrices. We state them first, and we prove two of them.

**Definition 4.4.5 (Unipotent monoid, multiplicative lacunarity, separated cycles).** Let $p$ be a system and $A$ a subalgebra. The *unipotent monoid* $T_A$ is the monoid generated by the matrices $1 + p_j a p_k$ with $a \in A$ and $j \ne k$. For a finite set $B \subseteq C$, a family $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_n$ in $C$ is *lacunary over* $B$ if for all disjoint nonempty $O, O'$ and all $b \in B$
$$\Big(\prod_{i \in O} \tau_i\Big)^{|O'|} \ne b \Big(\prod_{i \in O'} \tau_i\Big)^{|O|}.$$
Let $\pi$ be a permutation of $\{1, \dots, n\}$ and $y \in M_N(C)$. We say that $y$ has *separated cycles* for $(\pi, p)$ if for two different cycles $c \ne c'$ of $\pi$ there is no common eigenvalue of $y$ with an eigenvector in $\sum_{k \in c} p_k C^N$ and an eigenvector in $\sum_{k \in c'} p_k C^N$.

Let $A$ be a subalgebra of $M_N(C)$ and $p$ a system. A family $(E_{ij})_{i,j \le n}$ of matrices is a family of *matrix units adapted to* $p$ in $A$ if:

1. each $E_{ij} \in A$, $E_{ii} = p_i$, and $p_i E_{ij} p_j = E_{ij}$;
2. if $E_{ij} \ne 0$, then $E_{ji} \ne 0$;
3. if $E_{ij} \ne 0$ and $E_{jk} \ne 0$, then $E_{ij} E_{jk} = E_{ik}$ and $E_{ik} \ne 0$;
4. for each $a \in A$ and all $i, j$, the matrix $p_i a p_j$ lies in $C\, E_{ij}$.

**Lemma 4.4.6 (Inputs).** Let $C$ be a field, $A$ a subalgebra of $M_N(C)$, $S$ a submonoid of $M_N(C)$, $p$ a system and $\pi$ a permutation of $\{1, \dots, n\}$.

1. (Conjugate systems.) Let $C$ have characteristic $0$. If $p$ and $p'$ are corner systems of $A$ and $A$ has no nil ideal, then there are a unit $v \in A$ and a permutation $\pi$ with $v p_j = p'_{\pi(j)} v$ for all $j$, and such that $\pi(i) = i$ for each $i$ with $p'_i a p_i \ne 0$ for some $a \in A$.
2. (Matrix units.) If $p$ is a corner system of $A$ and $A$ has no nil ideal, then $A$ has a family of matrix units adapted to $p$.
3. (Factorization.) If $A$ has a family of matrix units adapted to $p$, then each unit $v \in A$ is $v = u \sum_j d_j p_j$ with $u \in T_A$ and all $d_j \ne 0$.
4. (Finite index.) If $\rho : \Gamma \to \mathrm{GL}_N(C)$ is a homomorphism and $U$ is a normal subgroup of finite index in $\Gamma$, then $\mathfrak{g} \subseteq T(\rho(U))$.
5. (Unipotent elements.) Let $C$ have characteristic $0$. If $E \in T(S)$ and $E^2 = 0$, then $1 + E \in \overline{S}$.
6. (Products.) $\overline{S}$ is closed under products.
7. (Generic twist.) If $x$ is a unit with $x p_j = p_{\pi(j)} x$ for all $j$, there is a finite $B \subseteq C$ such that $x \sum_j \tau_j p_j$ has separated cycles for $(\pi, p)$ whenever all $\tau_j \ne 0$ and $\tau$ is lacunary over $B$.
8. (Torus points.) Let $C$ be algebraically closed of characteristic $0$. If $e$ is lacunary and $\sum_i e_i p_i \in T(S)$, then for each finite $B \subseteq C$ there is $\tau$ with all $\tau_j \ne 0$, lacunary over $B$, and with $\sum_j \tau_j p_j \in \overline{S}$.
9. (Degree.) Let $C$ have characteristic $0$. If each element of $S$ has degree at most $n$, then so does each element of $\overline{S}$ and each element of $T(S)$.
10. (Cycle count.) Let $C$ be algebraically closed of characteristic $0$. Let $y$ be a unit of degree at most $n$ with $y p_j = p_{\pi(j)} y$ for all $j$ and with separated cycles for $(\pi, p)$. Then $y$ has at least $n$ distinct eigenvalues, and $y p_i \in C p_i$ for each $i$ with $\pi(i) = i$.

*Proof.* We give the argument for 6, and for 4 when $C$ is algebraically closed, which is the case used below. For 6: let $f$ be a polynomial that vanishes on $S$. For $s \in S$, the polynomial $x \mapsto f(sx)$ vanishes on $S$, since $sS \subseteq S$. So it vanishes on $\overline{S}$, and $S\, \overline{S} \subseteq \overline{S}$. Now fix $y \in \overline{S}$. The polynomial $x \mapsto f(xy)$ vanishes on $S$, by what we just proved, so it vanishes on $\overline{S}$. Hence $\overline{S}\, \overline{S} \subseteq \overline{S}$. For 4: let $G$ and $H$ be the Zariski closures of $\rho(\Gamma)$ and $\rho(U)$ in $\mathrm{GL}_N(C)$. They are algebraic groups, and $H$ has finite index in $G$, so $H$ contains the identity component of $G$. So $G$ and $H$ have the same Lie algebra (Borel, *loc. cit.*). Since $\mathrm{GL}_N(C)$ is open in $M_N(C)$, the tangent spaces at $1$ of $G$ and $H$ are $T(\rho(\Gamma))$ and $T(\rho(U))$. $\square$

*Proof of the theorem.* Write $R = \rho(g)$.

Step 1. Put $p'_j = R^{-1} p_j R$. Since $R^{-1} A R = A$ by goodness, the family $p'$ is again a system and a corner system of $A$. By conjugate systems there are a unit $v \in A$ and a permutation $\pi$ with $v p_j = p'_{\pi(j)} v$.

Step 2. By matrix units and factorization, $v = u D$ with $u \in T_A$ and $D = \sum_j d_j p_j$, all $d_j \ne 0$. Then $u p_j = v D^{-1} p_j = d_j^{-1} v p_j = d_j^{-1} p'_{\pi(j)} v$, and so $u p_j = p'_{\pi(j)} u$. For $x = R u$ this gives $x p_j = p_{\pi(j)} x$.

Step 3. By the generic twist, there is a finite set $B$ for $x$ and $\pi$. Since $(\rho, e, p)$ is good, $\sum_i e_i p_i \in \mathfrak{g}$, and $\mathfrak{g} \subseteq T(\rho(U))$ by the finite index statement. By torus points there is $\tau$ lacunary over $B$ with $t = \sum_j \tau_j p_j \in \overline{\rho(U)}$. Then $y = x t = R u t$ has separated cycles, and $y p_j = p_{\pi(j)} y$, because $t$ commutes with each $p_j$.

Step 4. We show $u \in \overline{\rho(U)}$. A generator $1 + E$ of $T_A$ has $E = p_j a p_k$ with $j \ne k$. By part 4 of goodness, $E \in \mathfrak{g} \subseteq T(\rho(U))$, and $E^2 = p_j a (p_k p_j) a p_k = 0$. So $1 + E \in \overline{\rho(U)}$ by unipotent elements, and $u \in \overline{\rho(U)}$ by products. Put $m = u t \in \overline{\rho(U)}$.

Step 5. Since $R \in \rho(\Gamma)$ and $m \in \overline{\rho(\Gamma)}$, products give $y = R m \in \overline{\rho(\Gamma)}$. Each $\rho(h)$ has degree at most $n$, so $y$ has degree at most $n$ by the degree statement. By the cycle count, $y = \rho(g) m$ has at least $n$ distinct eigenvalues. This is the first claim.

Step 6. Let $p_i a R p_i \ne 0$ with $a \in A$. Put $a' = R^{-1} a R \in A$. Then $R\, p'_i a' p_i = p_i a R p_i \ne 0$, so $p'_i a' p_i \ne 0$, and $\pi(i) = i$ by conjugate systems. The cycle count gives $y p_i = c\, p_i$ with $c \in C$. As $y p_i = p_{\pi(i)} y = p_i y$, also $p_i y = c\, p_i$. Now $R = y\, m^{-1}$ and $m^{-1} = t^{-1} u^{-1} = T' D v^{-1}$ with $T' = \sum_j \tau_j^{-1} p_j$. Hence $p_i R = p_i w$ with $w = c\, T' D v^{-1}$. The inverse of a unit of a finite dimensional algebra lies in the algebra, so $v^{-1} \in A$, and $T', D \in A$ because each $p_j \in A$. So $w \in A$. This is the second claim. $\square$

The proof does not use the condition that $A$ keeps each $\rho$-invariant subspace. That condition is used in the next section. The theorem holds for any group $\Gamma$ and needs no topology.

**Remark 4.4.7 (Formalization).** The formal theorem assumes the full goodness condition, but its proof never uses the part that $A$ keeps invariant subspaces; see [SumMulSum.lean](lean:Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/SumMulSum.lean#L93). The ten statements of the lemma on inputs are proved separately, and we have checked only their statements, not their proofs. The names of the theorem speak of étale cohomology, divisibility, an intersection number and a base change. None of these occurs in the statement, which is linear algebra over an algebraically closed field.

## Isogenies with a given kernel

Over a field, a finite subgroup $K$ of an abelian variety $A$ is the kernel of the isogeny $A \to A/K$. This section proves a form of this fact for the abelian schemes with extra structure of the Harris and Taylor [Shimura varieties](/learn/geometry/#shimura-varieties-and-toroidal-compactifications), for subgroups that are finite étale and killed by a prime ideal. The method is the one of the two last sections: a finite étale scheme $Y$ classifies the isogenies, a clopen subset $W$ of $Y$ picks out those with kernel $K$, and $W$ turns out to be a copy of the base.

We fix the data of the Harris and Taylor moduli problem. Let $p$ be a prime and let $D$ be a PEL datum at $p$. It contains a CM field $F$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_F$, an element $\omega$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$, an order $\mathcal{O}$ that acts on the abelian schemes, and integers $m$ and $n$. Let $R$ be a ring with *base data* $b$ for $D$: an element $\rho$ of $R$ that is a root of the quadratic polynomial of $\omega$, embeddings $t_i$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$ into $R$, and the condition that each integer prime to $p$ is a unit in $R$. Let $N \geq 3$ be an integer prime to $p$. For a scheme $S$ over $\mathrm{Spec}\, R$, an *object* $o$ over $S$ is an abelian scheme $A$ over $S$ with an action of $\mathcal{O}$, a polarization, a level $N$ structure and the other data of the moduli problem.

**Definition (The ideal u).** For $r \in \mathbb{Z}$, let $u_r$ be the ideal of $\mathcal{O}_F$ generated by $p$ and $\omega - r$. A maximal ideal $w$ of $\mathcal{O}_F$ that contains $u_r$ is a prime of $F$ over $p$, and $\mathrm{N}(w)$ is its absolute norm.

**Definition (p-isogeny of type T).** Let $o$ and $o'$ be objects over $S$ and $c = p^e$. A homomorphism $\psi : A \to A'$ is a *$p$-isogeny of degree $c$* if it commutes with the action of $\mathcal{O}$ and with the level structures, the pullback of the polarization of $A'$ is $c$ times the polarization of $A$, and there is $\psi' : A' \to A$ with $\psi \psi' = c$ and $\psi' \psi = c$. The isogeny $\psi$ has *type* $T_j$ for $(r, w)$ if at each geometric point of $S$ of characteristic $0$, the part of $\ker \psi$ killed by a power of $u_r$ is killed by $w$, and its intersection with $A[w]$ has $\mathrm{N}(w)^{m j}$ points.

**Theorem (Isogeny with a given kernel, noetherian base).** Let $R$ be a noetherian ring with base data $b$ for $D$, let $N \geq 3$ be prime to $p$, and suppose that $p$ is a unit in $R$ and $m = 1$. Let $r \in \mathbb{Z}$, let $w \supseteq u_r$ be a maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_F$, let $j \leq n$, and let $c = p^e$ with $e > 0$ if $j > 0$. Let $S$ be a locally noetherian scheme over $\mathrm{Spec}\, R$ and $o$ an object over $S$ with abelian scheme $A$. Let $K \subseteq A$ be a subgroup scheme that is finite étale over $S$, killed by $w$, stable under $\mathcal{O}$, and finite flat and surjective over $S$ of constant degree $\mathrm{N}(w)^j$. Then there are an object $o'$ over $S$ and a $p$-isogeny $\psi : A \to A'$ of degree $c$ and type $T_j$, such that a point $x$ of $A$ lies in $K$ if and only if $\psi(x) = 0$ and $x$ is killed by a power of $u_r$.

*Proof.* We give the proof in four steps.

First, the theorem of Harris and Taylor on the moduli of isogenies of type $T_j$ ([its statement](/entry/harris-taylor-type-tj-isogenies-representable/)) gives a finite étale $S$-scheme $Y$, an object $o_0$ over $Y$ that is the pullback of $o$, and a $p$-isogeny $\psi_Y : A_Y \to A'_Y$ of degree $c$ and type $T_j$. It is universal: each $p$-isogeny of degree $c$ and type $T_j$ from the pullback of $o$ to an $S$-scheme $T$ is the pullback of $\psi_Y$ along exactly one $S$-morphism $T \to Y$.

Second, a lemma of the code gives an open and closed subset $W$ of $Y$ with two properties. A point $y : T \to Y$ factors through $W$ if and only if the $u_r$-power part of $\ker \psi_y$ equals $K_T$. A geometric point $y$ lies in $W$ if and only if this holds at $y$. We read this lemma only as a statement.

Third, at each geometric point $\bar s$ of $S$ there is exactly one geometric point of $W$ over $\bar s$. The code proves this by two lemmas that we read only as statements. The idea is this. Existence: the quotient of $A_{\bar s}$ by $K_{\bar s}$ is an object of the moduli problem, and the quotient map is a $p$-isogeny of type $T_j$ with the right kernel, so it is $\psi_y$ for some $y$ over $\bar s$ that lies in $W$. Uniqueness: two isogenies with the same kernel differ by an isomorphism of the targets, so they give the same point of $Y$.

Fourth, $W$ is finite étale over $S$, because it is clopen in $Y$. A finite étale map with exactly one geometric point in each geometric fibre is an isomorphism. So $W \to S$ has an inverse, and this is a section $\sigma : S \to Y$ with image in $W$. Let $\psi$ be the pullback of $\psi_Y$ along $\sigma$. It is a $p$-isogeny of degree $c$ and type $T_j$, because these properties are stable under pullback. Since $\sigma$ factors through $W$, the $u_r$-power part of $\ker \psi$ is $K$. $\square$

**Theorem (Isogeny with a given kernel).** Let $R$ be a ring with base data $b$ for $D$, let $N \geq 3$ be prime to $p$, and suppose $m = 1$. Let $r$, $w$, $j$ and $c$ be as in the last theorem. Let $S$ be a locally noetherian scheme over $\mathrm{Spec}\, R$ such that $p$ is a unit on $S$, and let $o$ and $K$ be as in the last theorem. Then there are an object $o'$ over $S$ and a $p$-isogeny $\psi : A \to A'$ of degree $c$ and type $T_j$, such that a point $x$ of $A$ lies in $K$ if and only if $\psi(x) = 0$ and $x$ is killed by a power of $u_r$.

*Proof.* Each integer prime to $p$ is a unit in $R$, so it is a unit on $S$, and $p$ is a unit on $S$. So every nonzero integer is a unit in $\Gamma(S, \mathcal{O}_S)$, and this ring contains $\mathbb{Q}$. A lemma of the code gives a noetherian subring $R_1$ of $\Gamma(S, \mathcal{O}_S)$ that contains $p^{-1}$ and the images of the base data, with base data $b_1$ for $D$, and a way to move objects over $S$ between $R$ and $R_1$. The last theorem over $R_1$ gives $o'$ and $\psi$, and we move $o'$ back to $R$. $\square$

**Remark.** The hypothesis that $p$ is a unit is strong here. The base data already make every integer prime to $p$ a unit, so with $p$ a unit, $S$ is a scheme over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then $A[w]$ is finite étale, which is why $K$ can be finite étale at all, and the condition of type $T_j$, which looks only at points of characteristic $0$, applies to every geometric point. The parts of the base data that concern fields of characteristic $p$ are vacuous in this theorem. The theorem is thus about the generic fibre of the Harris and Taylor Shimura variety, not about its reduction at $p$.

**Remark.** In the datum, $\mathcal{O}$ is free of rank $m^2 [F : \mathbb{Q}]$ over $\mathbb{Z}$. So $m = 1$ means that $\mathcal{O}$ has the same rank as $\mathcal{O}_F$, and the type count $\mathrm{N}(w)^{m j}$ becomes $\mathrm{N}(w)^j$, which is the degree of $K$. The first theorem is private in the code, and only the second has an entry. The predicates for "killed by", "lies in $K$" and "finite flat and surjective of constant degree" are not unfolded in this section. The theorem is used by the quotient-object lemma of the chapter on Galois representations, in the part on annihilator models for the crystalline comparison. That lemma has no entry yet.

## The level lattice

The comparison of the Galois row compares étale cohomology with a crystalline cohomology of finite level. At level $k$ the coefficients are a subring $A_k$ of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+/t^k$ with divided powers on an ideal. This section proves that each $A_k$ has the seven properties of a lattice base: its divided power filtration dies at a finite step, has no $\ell$-torsion in its quotients, is finitely generated and $\ell$-adically closed, the ideal is prime and of valuation type, and the ring is $\ell$-adically complete. The proof of the comparison at finite level needs these properties of the coefficient ring before it can say anything about cohomology.

Throughout, $\ell$ is a prime, $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ is the completion of an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, and $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ is its ring of integers. The ring $A_{\inf} = W(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}^\flat)$ is the ring of [[Witt vectors|6N3X2QX4W9]] of the tilt, and $\xi = \ell - [\ell^\flat] \in A_{\inf}$, where $\ell^\flat$ is a fixed compatible system of $\ell$-power roots of $\ell$ and $[\cdot]$ is the Teichmüller lift. The ring $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and its uniformizer $t$ are as in [the Background on period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings). Fontaine's theorem gives $A_{\inf}/\xi A_{\inf} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ and identifies $\xi B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ with $t B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ (J.-M. Fontaine, "Le corps des périodes p-adiques", Astérisque 223, Exposé II).

**Definition 8.1.1 (Divided powers).** Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $J \subseteq R$ an ideal. A *divided power structure* on $J$ is a family of maps $\gamma_n : J \to R$, $n \geq 0$, with $\gamma_0(x) = 1$, $\gamma_1(x) = x$, $\gamma_n(x) \in J$ for $n \geq 1$, and the usual laws for sums, products with $R$, products of $\gamma_m$ and $\gamma_n$, and composition $\gamma_m \circ \gamma_n$. If $R$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, then $\gamma_n(x) = x^n / n!$ is the unique divided power structure on any ideal.

The laws are those of P. Berthelot and A. Ogus, *Notes on Crystalline Cohomology*, §3. They make $\gamma_n(x)$ behave like $x^n / n!$ even when $n!$ is not invertible.

**Definition 8.1.2 (Divided power filtration).** Let $\gamma$ be a divided power structure on $J \subseteq R$. For $r \geq 0$ let $P^r_\gamma \subseteq R$ be the ideal generated by all products
$$\gamma_{n_1}(x_1) \gamma_{n_2}(x_2) \cdots \gamma_{n_m}(x_m), \qquad x_i \in J, \quad n_1 + \dots + n_m \geq r.$$
This is the *divided power filtration*. If $k \leq s$, then $P^s_\gamma \subseteq P^k_\gamma$, since each generator of $P^s_\gamma$ is a generator of $P^k_\gamma$.

**Definition 8.1.3 (The ring at level $k$).** Let $k \geq 1$ and $M \geq 0$. Put $B_k = B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ / t^k$, and let $f_k : A_{\inf} \to B_k$ be the natural map to $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ followed by the quotient map. The ring $B_k$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra. Put $\eta = \ell^{-M} f_k(\xi)$. The ring $A_k = A_k(M)$ is the subring of $B_k$ generated by $f_k(A_{\inf})$ and by the elements $\eta^i / i!$ for $i \geq 0$. Let $I_B = f_k(\xi) B_k$, let $\gamma^B$ be the divided powers $x \mapsto x^n/n!$ on $I_B$, and let $I_k = I_B \cap A_k$.

If $\gamma^B_n(x) \in A_k$ for all $x \in I_k$ and $n \geq 0$, then $\gamma^B$ restricts to a divided power structure $\gamma$ on the ideal $I_k$ of $A_k$. We call $A_k$ *closed under divided powers* in this case. The ring $A_k$ is a crystalline lattice at level $k$: the parameter $M$ allows the generator $\xi$ to be divided by $\ell^M$ before its divided powers are taken.

**Definition 8.1.4 (Level hypothesis).** Let $K$ be a field between $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ and $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, and fix $M$. The *level hypothesis* for $(K, M)$ asks for all $k$:

1. the image of $\mathcal{O}_K$ in $B_k$ lies in $A_k$;
2. the image of $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ in $B_k$ lies in $A_k$;
3. $A_k$ is closed under divided powers;
4. each element of $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell / K)$ preserves $t^k B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, and the induced map of $B_k$ preserves $A_k$;
5. the map $B_{k+1} \to B_k$ sends $A_{k+1}$ into $A_k$.

Under the level hypothesis the rings $A_k$ form a system of crystalline coefficient rings with an action of the Galois group of $K$. Condition 3 gives the divided power structure $\gamma_k$ on $I_k$ for each $k$.

**Definition 8.1.5 (Lattice base).** Let $R$ be a ring, $J \subseteq R$ an ideal with a divided power structure $\gamma$, $\ell$ a prime and $s \geq 1$. The triple $(R, J, \gamma)$ is a *lattice base of length $s$* if:

1. $P^s_\gamma = 0$;
2. for all $r$, if $\ell x \in P^r_\gamma$ then $x \in P^r_\gamma$;
3. for each integer $n \neq 0$ there are $m \geq 0$ and $u \in R$ with $n u = \ell^m$;
4. each $P^r_\gamma$ is a finitely generated ideal;
5. $J$ is a prime ideal;
6. for all $x, y \in R$, either $x \in yR + J$ or $y \in xR + J$;
7. for each $x \notin J$ there is $m$ with $\ell^m \in xR + J$;
8. $R$ is $\ell$-adically complete, in the sense of [[IsAdicComplete|G9Q61B8A7C]];
9. each $P^r_\gamma$ is $\ell$-adically closed: if $x \in \ell^m R + P^r_\gamma$ for all $m$, then $x \in P^r_\gamma$.

Conditions 1 to 3 together say that the filtration is finite and that its quotients are torsion free. Conditions 5 to 7 say that $R/J$ is a domain whose principal ideals are totally ordered and in which every nonzero element divides a power of $\ell$: these are the properties of a valuation ring of rank one with $\ell$ in its maximal ideal ([[ValuationRing|QSX12D8RQM]]). Conditions 8 and 9 are the completeness that the later limit arguments use.

**Theorem 8.1.6 (The level lattice is a lattice base).** Let $K$ and $M$ satisfy the level hypothesis. Let $1 \leq k \leq s$. Then $(A_k, I_k, \gamma_k)$ is a lattice base of length $s$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.level_lattice_base` (also `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.LatCBase.latBase_lev`, the same statement).

*Proof.* Fix $k \geq 1$. A preliminary lemma checks the properties of the map $f_k : A_{\inf} \to B_k$ that all later steps use: $f_k(\xi)^k = 0$, since $\xi$ and $t$ generate the same ideal of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$; the quotient $A_{\inf}/\xi$ is $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$, a domain; the divisibility relations of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ are total and every nonzero element divides a power of $\ell$; $\ell \neq 0$ in $B_k$; $A_{\inf}$ is complete. Each condition then follows from one lemma on the ring $A_k$, which uses only these properties and condition 3 of the level hypothesis.

1. *Nilpotence.* Every element of $I_k$ is a multiple of $f_k(\xi)$ in $B_k$, so a product of divided powers $\gamma_{n_i}(x_i)$ with $\sum n_i \geq k$ is a multiple of $f_k(\xi)^k = 0$. Hence $P^k = 0$. Since $k \leq s$, $P^s \subseteq P^k = 0$.
2. *No $\ell$-torsion.* The quotients $A_k / P^r$ have no $\ell$-torsion. This is a lemma on the explicit generators of $A_k$.
3. *Integers.* An integer $n \neq 0$ is $\ell^m u'$ with $u'$ prime to $\ell$. The lemma shows that $u'$ is a unit of $A_k$, since it is a unit of $A_{\inf}$.
4. *Finite generation.* Each $P^r$ is finitely generated. Since $P^r = 0$ for $r \geq k$, only finitely many $r$ need a proof.
5. *Primality.* The ideal $I_k$ is prime. The quotient $A_k / I_k$ embeds in $B_k / I_B = B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ / t = \mathbb{C}_\ell$. The generators $\eta^i / i!$ with $i \geq 1$ lie in $I_B$, so the image is the image of $A_{\inf}$, which is $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$, a domain.
6. *Valuation conditions.* Conditions 6 and 7 come from the total order of divisibility in $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ and from the fact that each nonzero element of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ divides a power of $\ell$.
7. *Completeness.* The ring $A_k$ is $\ell$-adically complete, and each $P^r$ is $\ell$-adically closed.

The nine conditions hold, so $(A_k, I_k, \gamma_k)$ is a lattice base of length $s$. $\square$

**Remark 8.1.7.** The theorem uses only one condition of the level hypothesis: closure under divided powers at level $k$ (condition 3). Conditions 1, 2, 4 and 5 are not used. The Lean proof reads only the field `h.hc k` of the hypothesis ([LevelLatticeBase.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/LevelLatticeBase.lean#L30)). The other conditions are needed elsewhere, to make the rings $A_k$ into a Galois-equivariant inverse system.

**Remark 8.1.8.** The Lean statement fixes the ring at level $k$ but lets the length $s$ of the lattice base be any $s \geq k$. The nilpotence condition for $s$ follows from the one for $k$ by the inclusion $P^s \subseteq P^k$, which is a separate lemma in the same file. In Lean the prime is the natural number `l` with a primality instance, and the ring $A_k$ is a subring of $B_k$ given by its generators.

**Remark 8.1.9.** The steps of the proof cite one lemma each, in other files of the same folder. We have checked their statements, which are those given above, but we have not unfolded their proofs. The claims in steps 2 to 7 on why each lemma holds are our reading of the statements and of the inputs from the preliminary lemma.

The theorem is used by the result that [each level of the lattice system is good](/entry/lev-good/). There it is one field of a structure that records the properties of the level-$k$ coefficient ring, and through it the result feeds [the existence of the level system](/entry/lev-sys-ex/).

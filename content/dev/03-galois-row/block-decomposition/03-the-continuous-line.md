## The continuous line

The local step of the crystalline comparison computes cohomology on a site whose objects are points of a scheme with values in perfect $\ell$-adic algebras. A key input is a *line object*: a functor $\Xi$ with a natural unit $\eta$ that is universal among units $u$ with $u^\sharp = 1$, and an automorphism $\gamma$ that multiplies $\eta$ by $\varepsilon$. This section builds the line object from the algebra $C(\mathbb{Z}_\ell, A)$ of [the previous section](/entry/continuous-algebra-universal-property/), with $\gamma$ the shift $z \mapsto z + 1$. It then proves that the crystalline period ring of $C(\mathbb{Z}_\ell, A)$ is acyclic for the shift, up to multiplication by $\mu = [\varepsilon] - 1$.

We keep the notation of the previous section: the tilt $\mathcal{O}^\flat$, the $\varepsilon$-system $(e_m)$ with $\varepsilon = e_0$, perfect $\ell$-adic algebras $A$, the untilt $U(A) = W(A)/\xi_A$, the sharp map, test algebras, and $C(\mathbb{Z}_\ell, A)$ with constants $c_A$, evaluations $\mathrm{ev}_z$ and shift $\sigma$.

**Definition (Points with values in perfect algebras).** Let $O$ be a ring and $\kappa : O \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$ a ring map. Let $F$ be a functor from rings to sets with, for each point $y \in F(B)$, at most one ring map $O \to B$ "under" $y$, compatible with maps of rings. (For a scheme $X$ over $O$, $F(B) = X(B)$ and the map is the structure map.) For a perfect $\ell$-adic algebra $A$, the ring $U(A)$ receives a map from $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_\ell}$. Let $P(A)$ be the set of points $y \in F(U(A))$ that lie over $\kappa$. This defines a functor $P$ on perfect $\ell$-adic algebras.

The *category of elements* of $P$ has objects the pairs $T = (A, y)$ with $y \in P(A)$, and morphisms $A \to A'$ that carry $y$ to $y'$. Let $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{test}}$ be its full subcategory of pairs $(t, y)$ with $t$ a test algebra, and $j$ the inclusion.

**Definition (The unit group functor).** For $T = (A, y)$ let $U^\sharp(T)$ be the group of units $u$ of $A$ with $u^\sharp = 1$. It is a functor of $T$, and it has the distinguished element $q(T) = s(\varepsilon)$, since $\varepsilon^\sharp = \theta([\varepsilon]) = 1$.

**Definition (Line object).** A *line object* for $j$ and $U^\sharp$ consists of:

1. a functor $\Xi$ from the category of elements of $P$ to itself, with a natural transformation $i : \mathrm{id} \to \Xi$;
2. for each $T$ a unit $\eta_T \in U^\sharp(\Xi T)$, natural in $T$;
3. a natural transformation $\gamma : \Xi \to \Xi$ with $\gamma \circ i = i$ and $\gamma(\eta_T) = q \cdot \eta_T$;
4. the universal property: for every $T$, every object $t$ of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{test}}$, every morphism $\varphi : T \to j(t)$ and every $a \in U^\sharp(j(t))$, there is a unique $\psi : \Xi T \to j(t)$ with $\psi \circ i_T = \varphi$ and $\psi(\eta_T) = a$.

In geometric language, $\Xi T$ is the "line" of units over $T$ that become $1$ under the sharp map, $\eta$ is its coordinate, and $\gamma$ is translation by $\varepsilon$.

**Proposition (The continuous line).** Let $(e_m)$ be an $\varepsilon$-system. The following data form a line object:

1. $\Xi(A, y) = (C(\mathbb{Z}_\ell, A), y')$, where $y'$ is the image of $y$ under the constants;
2. $i_{(A,y)} = c_A$;
3. $\eta_{(A,y)} = \eta_A$, the image of the unit $\eta$ of [the universal property](/entry/continuous-algebra-universal-property/);
4. $\gamma = \sigma$, the shift.

*Proof.* The unit $\eta_A$ has $\eta_A^\sharp = 1$, since $\eta^\sharp = 1$ and maps of perfect algebras commute with the sharp map. It is natural in $A$ because it is the image of one element $\eta$ over $\mathcal{O}^\flat$. A constant function is fixed by the shift, so $\sigma \circ c_A = c_A$. The relation $\sigma(\eta) = \varepsilon \eta$ of the universal property gives $\gamma(\eta_T) = q \cdot \eta_T$. For the universal property, take $T = (A, y)$, $t$, $\varphi$ and $a$. The [universal property of continuous functions](/entry/continuous-algebra-universal-property/) gives a unique map $\psi : C(\mathbb{Z}_\ell, A) \to t$ with $\psi \circ c_A = \varphi$ and $\psi(\eta_A) = a$. This $\psi$ carries $y'$ to the point of $t$, because $\psi \circ c_A = \varphi$ does. So $\psi$ is a morphism in the category of elements, and it is unique there because it is unique as a map of algebras. $\square$

**Definition (Crystalline period ring of finite level).** Let $A$ be a perfect $\ell$-adic algebra and $n \geq 1$. Let $A_{\mathrm{cris}}(A)$ be the $W(A)$-subalgebra of $W(A)[1/\ell]$ generated by the elements $\xi_A^k / k!$ for $k \geq 0$, and put $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}(A) = A_{\mathrm{cris}}(A) / \ell^n$. Each morphism $A \to A'$ induces a map $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}(A) \to A_{\mathrm{cris},n}(A')$. For $A = \mathcal{O}^\flat$ this is the ring $A_{\mathrm{cris}} / \ell^n$ of [Fontaine's period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings).

**Definition (Filtered functor and acyclicity).** Let $R$ be a ring, $\mathcal{C}$ a category and $G$ a functor from $\mathcal{C}$ to $R$-modules. A *filtration* of $G$ is a family of subfunctors $S_r \subseteq G$, $r \geq 0$, with an element $\mu \in R$ such that $\mu S_r(T) \subseteq S_{r+1}(T)$ for all $r$ and $T$. Let $i : T \to T'$ and $g : T' \to T'$ be morphisms. The filtration is *acyclic* for $(i, g)$ if:

1. $G(i)$ is injective;
2. for each $x \in S_r(T')$ with $G(g) x = x$, there is $m \in S_{r+1}(T)$ with $G(i) m = \mu x$;
3. for each $x \in S_r(T')$ there is $y \in S_{r+1}(T')$ with $G(g) y - y = \mu x$.

In words: $T'$ with the endomorphism $g$ has no higher cohomology for the filtered functor, and its invariants come from $T$, both up to the factor $\mu$ and a shift of the filtration by one.

The functor in the theorem is $(A, y) \mapsto A_{\mathrm{cris},n}(A)$, an $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$-module. Its filtration is $S_r(A, y) = \operatorname{Fil}^{\rho(r)} A_{\mathrm{cris},n}(\mathcal{O}^\flat) \cdot A_{\mathrm{cris},n}(A)$, the extension of a divided power filtration on the base. Here $\rho : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ is *slow*, that is $\rho(0) = 0$ and $\rho(r + 1) \leq \rho(r) + 1$, and $\mu = [\varepsilon] - 1 \in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. Since $\mu$ lies in the divided power ideal and $\rho$ is slow, $\mu S_r \subseteq S_{r+1}$. A Boolean parameter $k$ selects one of two divided power ideals for the filtration.

**Theorem (An acyclic line).** Let $\ell$ be a prime, $F$, $O$ and $\kappa$ as above, $(e_m)$ an $\varepsilon$-system, $k$ a Boolean, $n \geq 0$ and $\rho$ slow. There is a line object $(\Xi, i, \eta, \gamma)$ such that for every object $T$ the filtration $S_\bullet$ of $A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$ is acyclic for $(i_T, \gamma_T)$.

The proof uses two elementary facts about functions on $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$.

**Lemma (Shifts of locally constant functions).** Let $N$ be an abelian group and $f : \mathbb{Z}_\ell \to N$ a locally constant function.

1. If $f(z + 1) = f(z)$ for all $z$, then $f$ is constant.
2. If $\ell^n N = 0$, there is a locally constant $y : \mathbb{Z}_\ell \to N$ with $y(z + 1) - y(z) = f(z)$ for all $z$.

*Proof.* The space $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is compact, so $f$ depends only on $z \bmod \ell^m$ for some $m$. For 1, $f(k) = f(0)$ for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$, and $\mathbb{N}$ is dense in $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. For 2, put $y(k) = \sum_{i < k} f(i)$ for $k \in \mathbb{N}$. The sum of $f$ over $\ell^{m+n}$ consecutive integers is $\ell^n$ times its sum over $\ell^m$ consecutive integers, which is $0$. So $y$ is periodic with period $\ell^{m+n}$ and extends to a locally constant function on $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. $\square$

*Proof of the theorem.* Take the [continuous line](/entry/line/). Fix $T = (A, y)$ and write $\mathcal{A} = A_{\mathrm{cris},n}$. We prove the three conditions without the factor $\mu$ and without the shift of the filtration: $\mathcal{A}(c_A)$ is injective, every shift-invariant $x \in S_r(\Xi T)$ is the image of some $m \in S_r(T)$, and every $x \in S_r(\Xi T)$ is $\sigma(y) - y$ for some $y \in S_r(\Xi T)$. Multiplication by $\mu$ then sends $S_r$ into $S_{r+1}$ and gives the three conditions as stated.

1. *Injectivity.* Evaluation at $0$ is a left inverse of the constants $c_A$. So $\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{ev}_0)$ is a left inverse of $\mathcal{A}(c_A)$, which is injective.
2. *A separation lemma.* For $x \in \mathcal{A}(C(\mathbb{Z}_\ell, A))$, the function $z \mapsto \mathcal{A}(\mathrm{ev}_z)(x)$ is locally constant; $x$ is $0$ if all its evaluations are $0$; and a locally constant family of elements of the extended ideal $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{A}(A)$ is the family of evaluations of an element of $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{A}(C(\mathbb{Z}_\ell, A))$.
3. *Invariants.* Let $x \in S_r(\Xi T)$ with $\sigma(x) = x$. Then $z \mapsto \mathcal{A}(\mathrm{ev}_z)(x)$ is locally constant and invariant under $z \mapsto z + 1$, so it is constant by part 1 of the lemma. Put $m = \mathcal{A}(\mathrm{ev}_0)(x)$, which lies in $S_r(T)$. The element $x - \mathcal{A}(c_A)(m)$ has all evaluations $0$, so $x = \mathcal{A}(c_A)(m)$ by the separation lemma.
4. *Coboundaries.* Let $x \in S_r(\Xi T)$ and $f(z) = \mathcal{A}(\mathrm{ev}_z)(x)$, a locally constant function with values in the extended ideal. That ideal is killed by $\ell^n$, since $\mathcal{A}$ is. Part 2 of the lemma gives a locally constant $y$ with $y(z + 1) - y(z) = f(z)$. The separation lemma lifts $y$ to an element $w$ of $S_r(\Xi T)$, and $\sigma(w) - w$ and $x$ have the same evaluations. So $\sigma(w) - w = x$.

This proves the three conditions, so the filtration is acyclic for $(i_T, \gamma_T)$. $\square$

**Remark.** The Lean proof shows the three conditions in the stronger form without $\mu$, as above, and a general lemma multiplies by $\mu$. So the factor $\mu$ in the definition of acyclicity is not needed for this line object. The separation lemma is a statement in another file of the folder; we have read its statement, not its proof.

**Remark.** The filtration $S_r$ is defined through the divided power structure of the period ring. The identification of $S_r(A, y)$ with the extension of the base filtration is a lemma of the Lean proof, and we have not unfolded the two divided power ideals selected by the parameter $k$. The parameters $k$, $n$ and $\rho$ only enter through this filtration.

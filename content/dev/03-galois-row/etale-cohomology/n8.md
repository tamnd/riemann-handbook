This theorem is the induction step of [E3.main](/entry/e3-main/): if $A$ is good, then $A[x]$ is good. Its proof is one term. It applies the theorem `Cb.strictHenselization_stalk_equiv_of_colimit_presentation` to seven lemmas of the same file. That theorem has the content, and we describe it here.

## Statement

We use the words *restrictive* and *good* of [E3.main](/entry/e3-main/). A map $k \colon T \to K$ has the property `AlgRes` if for each prime $Q$ of $K$ and each $z \in K$ there is a polynomial $p$ over $T$ with $p(z) \in Q$ and with a coefficient whose image is not in $Q$.

**Theorem.** Assume the 11 axioms of `Hyp`. Let $k \colon T \to K$ have `AlgRes`, let $t \colon T \to A$, and let $q \neq 0$ be a unit in $T$. If $(k, t)$ is good, then $(k, T \to A \to A[x])$ is good. That is, for each étale $A[x]$-algebra $U$, the map $U \to K \otimes_T U$ is restrictive.

## Proof

1. **Reduction to points** (`Stalk`, lemma `n9`, not unfolded). Take for the class of algebras the étale $A[x]$-algebras. `Stalk` reduces the claim for $U$ to this: for each prime $r$ of $U$, there is an ind-étale $U \to O$ with a prime over $r$ such that $O \to K \otimes_T O$ is restrictive. The proof uses `Stalk` with the identity as the integral cover. The two stability conditions of `Stalk` hold because étale maps are stable under composition and pushout.
2. **The points** (`geo`). Fix $r$. `SHExists` gives a strictly henselian ring $A'$, ind-étale over $A$, whose maximal ideal lies over $r \cap A$. A second use of `SHExists`, on $U \otimes_A A'$, gives a strictly henselian ring $X$ with a map $h \colon A'[x] \to X$. Here $h$ is ind-étale, $A' \to X$ is local, $U \to X$ is ind-étale, and the maximal ideal of $X$ lies over $r$. Take $O = X$.
3. **Cohomology** (`coh`). The lemma `Limit` (`n7`) extends goodness from étale to ind-étale $A$-algebras. So $A' \to K \otimes_T A'$ is restrictive. It has `AlgRes`, by pushout stability in `AlgResProps` (`n1`). Then `ULA` (`n64`) applies to $h$ and gives that $K \otimes_T A' \to K \otimes_T X$ is restrictive. `SHEqv` (`n57`) gives that $A' \to X$ is restrictive, as a map of strictly henselian rings. In the square with corners $A'$, $X$, $K \otimes_T A'$ and $K \otimes_T X$, three sides are restrictive. So the fourth side $X \to K \otimes_T X$ is restrictive.

The rest is the pasting of pushout squares (`bcFwd`, `bcBack`).

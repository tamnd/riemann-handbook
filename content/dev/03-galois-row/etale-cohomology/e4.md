This theorem proves that $H^n(X, \mathbb{Z}/q(i))$ is finite for a proper variety $X$ over an algebraically closed field, under 27 general statements on étale cohomology. [The finiteness theorem](/entry/etale-finiteness-of-duality/) proves the 27 statements. The proof here is one line: it gives the Picard statement to [the proof by induction](/entry/etale-topology-top/), which has the real content. Below this theorem are 2,251 declarations.

## Statement

The Lean code uses sheaves on the étale site of all schemes. For a scheme $X$, $H^n(X, F)$ is the group of degree $n$ maps, up to homotopy, from the free sheaf on $X$ to an injective resolution of $F$. The sheaf $\mathbb{Z}/q(i)$ (`tw q i`) is the sheaf generated by symbols $[\zeta]$ for primitive $q$-th roots of unity $\zeta$, with $[\zeta^a] = a^i [\zeta]$ for $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/q)^\times$: a model of $\mu_q^{\otimes i}$. We have not seen a Lean comparison with the small étale site.

**Theorem.** Suppose that the following 27 statements hold.

- A1 to A11 (no A6): formal properties. Invariance under quasi-isomorphism, Čech descent along smooth surjections, Mayer-Vietoris, local vanishing of positive-degree classes, the long exact sequence, change of coefficients, $H^{<0} = 0$ and $H^0$ equal to sections.
- B1 to B7 (no B6): continuity along filtered colimits, acyclicity of strictly henselian local rings and of absolutely integrally closed domains, a criterion at strictly henselian points, Čech descent along integral surjections, and $H^n$ of a field as Galois cohomology.
- C1 to C5: $H^{\geq 2} = 0$ for fields algebraic over $k(t)$ and for Dedekind domains of finite type over $k$, a base change for étale algebras over $T[t]$, homotopy invariance for $A[t]$, and $H^*$ of $A[t, t^{-1}]$ for $A$ strictly henselian.
- D1 to D3: the projective bundle formula, additive classes of vector bundles, and slant products with group homology.
- E1 to E3: invariance for henselian pairs, proper base change over a strictly henselian local ring, and a base change to the generic point for smooth schemes.

Then the conclusion of [the finiteness theorem](/entry/etale-finiteness-of-duality/) holds.

## Proof

The proof is `EtaleTopology.top` applied to `etale_descent_picard_group`. That theorem says that for each commutative ring $A$ there is an isomorphism of groups $H^1(\operatorname{Spec} A, \mathbb{G}_m) \cong \operatorname{Pic}(A)$. This is Hilbert 90 in the form of Grothendieck (Milne, *Étale Cohomology*, 1980, III, Prop. 4.9), for affine schemes. `top` uses it once, in step 2 below.

`top` proves about 130 leaf lemmas and ends in `LateralTheory.E.E4.goal_of`, an induction on the degree $n$ and then on $\dim X$:

1. For a proper surjective $f \colon Y \to X$, the kernel of $f^*$ on $H^n$ is finite, by cohomological descent along $f$ and finiteness in lower degrees.
2. Dimension $0$ is a direct lemma. For a smooth proper curve over an algebraically closed field, $H^1$ and $H^2$ are finite and $H^m = 0$ for $m \geq 3$. The Kummer sequence and the Picard statement give $H^1$.
3. For $X$ integral of positive dimension, `LeafGeo` fibres a proper surjective cover of $X$ in curves over a base $B$ with $\dim B < \dim X$. After two covers of curves that kill $H^2$ and $H^1$ of the fibre, each class that vanishes over the bad locus of $B$ comes from $B$ (`LeafGhost`). Induction on dimension and step 1 finish.
4. A general $X$ reduces to its integral closed subschemes, with step 1.

This volume proves the results on automorphic forms and the trace formula that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. The groups are $\mathrm{GL}_n$ and unitary groups over a CM field $E$ with maximal totally real subfield $F$. The volume proves Harish-Chandra's finiteness theorem, the existence of Siegel domains, the parts of the trace formula for unitary groups and of the twisted trace formula for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ that base change needs, the Jacquet-Rallis fundamental lemma for unit functions, and strong multiplicity one by Rankin-Selberg $L$-functions. Volume 1 gives the background: [automorphic forms on adelic groups](/learn/automorphic-forms/#automorphic-forms-on-adelic-groups), [constant terms and cusp forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/#constant-terms-and-cusp-forms), [the discrete spectrum](/learn/automorphic-forms/#the-discrete-spectrum), [arithmetic subgroups and Siegel sets](/learn/trace-formula/#arithmetic-subgroups-and-siegel-sets), [pseudo-coefficients](/learn/trace-formula/#arthurs-trace-formula-the-simple-trace-formula-and-pseudo-coefficients), [the twisted trace formula and base change](/learn/trace-formula/#the-twisted-trace-formula-and-base-change), [the relative trace formula and the Jacquet-Rallis fundamental lemma](/learn/trace-formula/#the-relative-trace-formula-and-the-jacquet-rallis-fundamental-lemma) and [Rankin-Selberg integrals](/learn/trace-formula/#rankin-selberg-integrals).

The proof uses the volume in five places. The first is the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/) of [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/). Every chapter except Chapter 5 feeds it: the finiteness theorems of Chapter 1, the trace formula of Chapter 2, the residual spectrum and equidistribution of Chapter 3, the fundamental lemma of Chapter 4, strong multiplicity one and local parameters from Chapters 6 and 8, and the limit statement for infinitesimal characters of Chapter 7. Chapter 1 also feeds the step [EveryCharacter from Row0b](/step/every-character-of-row/). The second place is the step [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) of [the literature](/proof/literature/), which uses Chapter 1 and the integrability results of Chapter 3. The third is the step [isobaric base change](/step/base-change-n/), which is Theorem 7.26.4. The fourth is two steps fed by Chapter 6: [RecTExists](/step/rect-exists/) of the Galois row takes the parameters of cuspidal representations, and [theta local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/) of [theta integrals and local matching](/proof/theta-matching/) takes the count of primes of a norm form. The fifth is the step [HypsAlt is nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/) of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/), which Chapter 5 reaches through Volume 10 and Volumes 13 to 15.

Chapter 1 proves Harish-Chandra's finiteness theorem. Sections 1 and 2 give the inputs on real groups: the uniqueness of the module of lowest type for $U(p, q)$, [Theorem 1.1.3](/entry/gen-iso-of-lowest-type/), analyticity and density, [Theorem 1.1.6](/entry/harish-chandra-analyticity-density/), and Harish-Chandra's convolution theorem, [Theorem 1.2.6](/entry/exists-alpha-convolution/). The space of cusp forms on $\mathrm{GL}_a$ over a totally complex field with fixed level, $K$-types and eigencharacter is finite-dimensional, [Theorem 1.3.7](/entry/finite-dimensional-cusp-gl/). An induction on constant terms, [Theorem 1.4.5](/entry/finite-dimensional-const-term/), extends this to all automorphic forms on $\mathrm{GL}_a$, [Theorem 1.4.9](/entry/automorphic-forms-finite-gl/), and to unitary groups, [Theorem 1.5.2](/entry/harish-chandra-finite/). The later sections prove that cuspidal representations are generic, [Theorem 1.6.1](/entry/cusp-gen/), the local converse theorem for $\mathrm{GL}_{m+1}$, [Theorem 1.6.3](/entry/local-converse-gl/), and that completed Hecke $L$-functions are bounded in vertical strips, [Theorem 1.7.2](/entry/completed-lbounded-in-strips/). Section 8 gives Siegel sets for unitary groups, [Theorem 1.8.3](/entry/siegel-core/), elliptic regularity, [Theorem 1.8.4](/entry/elliptic-regularity-of-parts/), and a bound of the sup norm by the Petersson norm, [Theorem 1.8.5](/entry/tamagawa-local-volume-factor/).

Chapter 2 proves the parts of the trace formula for a unitary group that the comparison with $\mathrm{GL}_n$ needs. On the spectral side it builds discrete series data on $K$, [Theorem 2.1.5](/entry/maximal-compact-pseudo-coefficient-data-prime/), and pseudo-coefficients for $U(p, q)$ with known orbital integrals, [Theorem 2.3.2](/entry/local-pc-holds/). It isolates the elliptic side, [Theorem 2.4.4](/entry/elliptic-side-direct/), separates representations by Iwahori traces, [Theorem 2.5.2](/entry/separation-of-iwahori-trace/), and proves the equality of cuspidal multiplicities, [Theorem 2.5.4](/entry/mult-eq-of-sides/). At an inert place the twisted and $\kappa$-orbital integrals of the unit function are lattice sums, [Theorem 2.6.3](/entry/twisted-unit-eq-st-sum/) and [Theorem 2.6.4](/entry/kap-orb-unit-eq-kap-sum/), and principal congruence functions match, [Theorem 2.7.2](/entry/match-principal-congruence-inert/). On the geometric side a Hecke trace is a class sum, [Theorem 2.8.5](/entry/tr-on-forms-hop-eq-geom/), constant terms match at split places, [Theorem 2.9.5](/entry/match-ct-mu-of-is-split-at/), invariant Hermitian forms satisfy a local-global principle, [Theorem 2.10.3](/entry/l-hermitian-form-local-global/), and the class sum stabilizes, [Theorem 2.11.4](/entry/geom-urat-eq-geom-u/). Section 12 proves Zelevinsky's theorem that the Grothendieck ring of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over a $p$-adic field is a polynomial ring on the segments, [Theorem 2.12.9](/entry/positivity-theory-groth-grothendieck-ring-polynomial-of-seg/).

Chapter 3 is on reduction theory and the discrete spectrum. It proves the Iwasawa decomposition at the finite places, [Theorem 3.1.6](/entry/exists-compact-iwasawa-good/), that the norm-one minimal parabolic is compact modulo rational points, [Theorem 3.2.6](/entry/min-par-one-cocompact/), the existence of Siegel domains for unitary groups, [Theorem 3.3.7](/entry/cov-main/), and that only finitely many regular classes meet the support of a test function, [Theorem 3.4.4](/entry/reg-class-fin/). Sections 5 to 8 prove equidistribution, [Theorem 3.5.2](/entry/equi/), the asymptotics of rank one theta means, [Theorem 3.6.3](/entry/lside/), and integrability at infinity and for arithmetic varieties, [Theorem 3.7.4](/entry/integrable-star-loc-inf/) and [Theorem 3.8.6](/entry/pts-abs-integrable/). Sections 9 to 11 give twisted characters by highest weights, [Theorem 3.9.1](/entry/char-dec-main/), the kernel of a wave average, [Theorem 3.10.2](/entry/ker-core/), concentration, [Theorem 3.11.2](/entry/conc/), and the gluing of limits, [Theorem 3.11.3](/entry/e-final-glue/). Section 12 proves a functional equation by a gallery of walls, [Theorem 3.12.5](/entry/gallery/). The last section proves, after Mœglin and Waldspurger, that a discrete automorphic representation of $\mathrm{GL}_N$ with regular integral infinitesimal character embeds into an induced representation of Speh type, [Theorem 3.13.5](/entry/asc-seed-holds2/).

Chapter 4 proves the Jacquet-Rallis fundamental lemma for unit functions in every rank, by induction on the rank. It builds the Weil representation of $\mathrm{SL}_2$ for a field and for an étale pair, [Theorem 4.2.7](/entry/weil-field-main/) and [Theorem 4.2.8](/entry/weil-et-of-weil-field/), proves the transfer in rank one, [Theorem 4.3.9](/entry/rank-one/), and shows that matching commutes with the partial Fourier transform, [Theorem 4.4.9](/entry/fourier-match-of-weil-field/). The induction step, [Theorem 4.5.5](/entry/unit-match-succ/), gives the fundamental lemma, [Theorem 4.5.8](/entry/unit-match-all/). Orbital integrals become lattice counts, [Theorem 4.6.4](/entry/count-identity-of-unit-match/), and this gives the unit lemma for the Lie algebra and for the unitary group, [Theorem 4.7.6](/entry/unit-lemma-lie-and-group/). Section 8 restates Zelevinsky's theorem, [Theorem 4.8.3](/entry/grothendieck-ring-polynomial-outright/).

Chapter 5 proves that the local first unfolding of a twisted Eisenstein series at a split prime of degree one is invariant under a double coset, [Theorem 5.1.12](/entry/twisted-eisenstein-of-remaining/), and does not vanish on it, [Theorem 5.2.5](/entry/unfolding-nonvanishing-double-coset-holds/). From this the twisted index of the column construction is good, [Theorem 5.3.5](/entry/tw-idx-ok-of/) and [Theorem 5.3.10](/entry/twisted-index-first-unfolding-ok/). The chapter also proves that Hecke operators at split primes commute, [Theorem 5.4.5](/entry/hecke-operators-commute/), the uniqueness of the unimodular genus, [Theorem 5.4.6](/entry/unimod-genus-holds/), the existence of reference frames, [Theorem 5.5.3](/entry/target-of-wframe/), and a bound on the length of the poles, [Theorem 5.5.6](/entry/pole-length-bound/).

Chapter 6 is on Hecke operators and cusp forms. It proves strong multiplicity one for $\mathrm{GL}_a$ over totally real and totally complex fields, [Theorem 6.1.3](/entry/strong-mult-one-real/) and [Theorem 6.1.8](/entry/strong-mult-one-c/), and attaches newforms to cuspidal representations, [Theorem 6.1.10](/entry/rep-newform/). It bounds cohomology polynomially in the level, [Theorem 6.2.4](/entry/coh-bound-free/), builds Hecke's Eisenstein series of weight one, [Theorem 6.3.4](/entry/hilbert-eisenstein-weight-one-holds/), and proves weight congruences for ordinary forms, [Theorem 6.4.6](/entry/hol-wc/), and harmonic representatives, [Theorem 6.5.5](/entry/hodge-ex/). It gives reciprocity maps, [Theorem 6.6.3](/entry/rec-texists/), and primes of a norm form in short intervals, [Theorem 6.6.4](/entry/short-norm/). With theta data it places rows, [Theorem 6.7.9](/entry/row-placement-exists/), and finds a free action on the definite space, [Theorem 6.8.2](/entry/good-vectors-definite/). The last sections prove that Hecke operators keep cusp forms, [Theorem 6.9.5](/entry/hecke-transform-of-point-laws/), the Hecke reading, [Theorem 6.10.8](/entry/hecke-read-tie/), and the integrality of Hecke values, [Theorem 6.12.4](/entry/hecke-int/).

Chapter 7 proves base change from unitary groups to $\mathrm{GL}_n$. It starts with the multiplicity formula, [Theorem 7.1.9](/entry/multiplicity-formula-holds/), test functions from waves, [Theorem 7.2.7](/entry/wave-existence-speed-multiplicity/), Iwahori eigenvalues at an inert place, [Theorem 7.3.6](/entry/eig-inert/), and the fundamental lemma for the unit, [Theorem 7.4.4](/entry/m1/). On the twisted trace formula it proves the vanishing of non-elliptic classes, [Theorem 7.6.8](/entry/class-vanish/), the elliptic terms, [Theorem 7.7.6](/entry/global-character-of-finite-inputs/), separation and limit vectors, [Theorem 7.8.8](/entry/separation-holds/) and [Theorem 7.8.9](/entry/e-final-of/), the global comparison, [Theorem 7.10.3](/entry/g4/), and the stable elliptic sum, [Theorem 7.11.5](/entry/g3/). The later sections prove the chain identity, [Theorem 7.13.4](/entry/chain-of-prime-holds/), that base change keeps Satake parameters at ramified places, [Theorem 7.18.2](/entry/ram-sph/), a spectral bound, [Theorem 7.22.2](/entry/spec-p/), the Dirac inequality, [Corollary 7.23.3](/entry/dirac-w/), and descent or endoscopy, [Theorem 7.24.4](/entry/run-th/). The chapter ends with isobaric base change, [Theorem 7.26.4](/entry/base-change-n/), and a second proof, [Theorem 7.26.6](/entry/base-change-nb/).

Chapter 8 proves the rigidity theorem of Jacquet and Shalika, [Theorem 8.7.7](/entry/jacquet-shalika-rigidity-holds/): two finite families of cuspidal representations whose Satake parameters have the same sum at almost all places agree up to order. The tool is the partial Rankin-Selberg $L$-function. The chapter unfolds a theta integral, [Theorem 8.2.5](/entry/theta-z-eq-mul-eul-d/), matches Whittaker vectors, [Theorem 8.3.4](/entry/matched-vectors/), proves the integral for $\mathrm{GL}_n \times \mathrm{GL}_m$, [Theorem 8.4.2](/entry/rs-nm-partial-l-eq-div/), continues it in the same degree, [Theorem 8.5.4](/entry/samedeg-continuation/), and proves nonvanishing at $s = 1$, [Theorem 8.6.7](/entry/tendsto-partial-l-ne-zero/). Section 8 derives the local Langlands correspondence for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ from its inputs, [Theorem 8.8.4](/entry/local-langlands-gl-of-depth-zero-zelevinsky-bernstein/). Section 9 proves the base change of principal series, [Theorem 8.9.9](/entry/lift-good/), and the Speh form of base change from unitary groups, [Theorem 8.9.10](/entry/speh/).

Chapters 5 and 6 have no links to the other chapters of this volume. The others are linked. Section 3.3 feeds Theorem 1.8.3, and Section 3.4 uses it and feeds Theorem 2.4.4. Chapter 4 feeds the matching in Chapter 2 and Section 7.4. Sections 3.9 to 3.11 feed Theorems 7.1.6, 7.2.8 and 7.8.9. Chapter 7 and Section 8.9 depend on each other: Section 8.9 supplies inputs to Theorem 7.18.2, Section 7.19 supplies inputs to Theorem 8.9.5, and Theorem 8.9.10 uses Theorem 7.26.6. So a reader can take Chapters 5 and 6 at any time, Chapters 1 and 3 together, Chapter 4 before Chapter 2, and Chapters 7 and 8 last.

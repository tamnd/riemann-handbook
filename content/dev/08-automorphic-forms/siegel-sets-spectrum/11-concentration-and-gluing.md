## Concentration and gluing of limits

Let $E$ be a CM field and $GL_N$ the general linear group over $E$. This section proves two results on families of automorphic forms on $GL_N(\mathbb{A}_E)$. The first says that square integrable forms of a fixed type, whose constant terms decay at a uniform rate, keep at least half of their mass on one compact set. It is an input of the finiteness of discrete spectra with bounded parameters. The second says that a limit of Hecke eigenforms on $GL_a \times GL_b$ is again an eigenform, built from induced discrete spectra, when the infinitesimal characters converge to a separated real limit. It is the main step of the [theorem on limits of infinitesimal characters](/entry/e-final-of/). Concentration is the familiar consequence of [reduction theory with Siegel sets](/learn/trace-formula/#arithmetic-subgroups-and-siegel-sets) and of the decay of [constant terms](/learn/automorphic-forms/#constant-terms-and-cusp-forms); the gluing uses the [discrete spectrum](/learn/automorphic-forms/#the-discrete-spectrum) and [Eisenstein series](/learn/automorphic-forms/#eisenstein-series) built from it.

### Setting

Let $G_N = GL_N(\mathbb{A}_E)$ with a Haar measure $\mu$. The lattice $\Gamma_N$ is the subgroup generated by $GL_N(E)$ and the central element $z_1$, where $z_t$ is the scalar $e^t$ at every archimedean place. A smooth automorphic form is square integrable if it is invariant under all $z_t$ and has finite $L^2$ norm on a fundamental domain $F$ for $\Gamma_N$. A discrete space is a nonzero irreducible stable space $V$ of square integrable automorphic forms.

A form $\psi$ has type $(K, W)$, for an open subgroup $K \subseteq GL_N(\mathbb{A}_{E,f})$ and a finite dimensional space $W$ of functions on the maximal compact subgroup $K_\infty$ of $GL_N(E \otimes \mathbb{R})$, if $\psi$ is right invariant under $K$ and its right $K_\infty$-translates at each point lie in $W$. The infinitesimal character of $V$ at an embedding $\tau : E \to \mathbb{C}$ is a multiset $m$ of $N$ complex numbers such that the Capelli element with parameter $u$ acts on $V$ by $\prod_{x \in m} (u + x)$. It is bounded by $R$ if every $x \in m$ has $|x| \le R$.

For $0 < r < N$, let $a_r(t)$ be the archimedean element $\operatorname{diag}(e^{t} 1_r, 1_{N-r})$, suitably normalized. An exponent of $\psi$ along the parabolic of type $(r, N - r)$ is a number $c$ such that some nonzero function $\Phi$ in the span of the translates $g \mapsto \psi_P(a_r(t) g)$ of the constant term satisfies $\Phi(a_r(t) g) = e^{ct}\Phi(g)$. The normalized real part is
$$p(r, c) = \frac{\operatorname{Re} c}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]} - \frac{r(N - r)}{2}.$$
Langlands' criterion says that $\psi$ is square integrable when all $p(r, c) < 0$. The theorem below asks for a uniform margin $p(r, c) \le -\varepsilon\, r(N - r)$.

### Concentration

**Lemma 3.11.1 (Half the mass).** Let $\Gamma$ be a countable subgroup of a locally compact group $G$ with a left invariant measure $\mu$, $F$ a fundamental domain, $\Omega$ compact and $S$ a set with $\Gamma S = G$. Let $\psi$ and $f$ be continuous and left $\Gamma$-invariant, with
$$\int_F |\psi|^2 = 1, \qquad \int_F |\psi - f|^2 \le \tfrac18, \qquad \int_{S \setminus \Omega} |f|^2 \le \tfrac18.$$
Then $\int_\Omega |\psi|^2 \ge \tfrac12$.

*Proof.* Let $T = \Gamma^{-1}\Omega$ be the set of $x$ with $\gamma x \in \Omega$ for some $\gamma \in \Gamma$. It is $\Gamma$-invariant and contains $\Omega$. Since $\Omega$ meets every $\Gamma$-orbit in $T$, unfolding gives $\int_{F \cap T} |\psi|^2 \le \int_\Omega |\psi|^2$. An orbit outside $T$ meets $S$, and there it lies in $S \setminus \Omega$; so $\int_{F \setminus T} |f|^2 \le \int_{S \setminus \Omega} |f|^2 \le \frac18$. From $|\psi|^2 \le 2|\psi - f|^2 + 2|f|^2$,
$$\int_{F \setminus T} |\psi|^2 \le 2 \cdot \tfrac18 + 2 \cdot \tfrac18 = \tfrac12.$$
Hence $\int_\Omega |\psi|^2 \ge \int_{F \cap T} |\psi|^2 = 1 - \int_{F \setminus T} |\psi|^2 \ge \frac12$. $\square$

**Theorem 3.11.2 (Concentration).** Let $E$ be a CM field and $N \ge 1$. Let $K$ be an open subgroup of $GL_N(\mathbb{A}_{E,f})$, $W$ a finite dimensional space of functions on $K_\infty$, $R \in \mathbb{R}$, $\varepsilon > 0$, $\delta > 0$, $\alpha$ a continuous function of compact support on $G_N$, and $F$ a fundamental domain for $\Gamma_N$. There is a compact set $\Omega \subseteq G_N$ with the following property. Let $V$ be a discrete space with infinitesimal characters bounded by $R$, whose exponents satisfy $p(r, c) \le -\varepsilon\, r(N - r)$ for all $0 < r < N$. Let $\psi \in V$ be of type $(K, W)$, with $\int_F |\psi|^2 = 1$, such that $\int \alpha(y) \psi(xy)\, d\mu(y) = s\,\psi(x)$ for some $s$ with $|s| \ge \delta$. Then
$$\int_\Omega |\psi|^2 \, d\mu \ge \tfrac12.$$

We use the following facts, taken as given.

- (Integral level.) $K$ contains an open subgroup $U$ that is a product of integral congruence subgroups.
- (Exponent polynomials.) For each $0 < r < N$ there is $D$ such that for every discrete $V$ with infinitesimal characters and every $\varphi \in V$ there is a monic polynomial $P$ of degree at most $D$ that kills the constant term of $\varphi$ under the shift $a_r(1)$ and whose roots are $e^c$ for exponents $c$ of $\varphi$.
- (Initial bound.) The forms $\psi$ of the theorem satisfy a uniform bound of moderate growth, with a constant that depends only on $\alpha$, $\delta$ and $F$. This uses the reproducing equation $\alpha * \psi = s\psi$ with $|s| \ge \delta$.
- (Smoothing.) There are a smoothing kernel $\beta$ on the archimedean group and $k \in \mathbb{N}$ such that the $k$-fold archimedean convolution $f = \beta^{*k} * \psi$ has $\int_F |\psi - f|^2 \le \frac18$ for all these $\psi$, and such that the family of the $f$ inherits improved uniform bounds. Each improvement step uses the polynomial that kills the constant term.
- (Siegel sets.) There is a Siegel set $S$ with $\Gamma_N S = G_N$. A family of continuous left invariant functions with uniform bounds of rate $\kappa(r) = \rho_r (1 - \varepsilon)$, where $\rho_r > 0$ is the modulus exponent of the parabolic of type $(r, N-r)$, has its $L^2$ mass on $S$ outside a compact set $\Omega$ at most $\frac18$, uniformly in the family.

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the family of all forms $\psi$ as in the theorem; they are continuous and left $\Gamma_N$-invariant. Each exponent $c$ of $\psi$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re} c / [E:\mathbb{Q}] \le r(N - r)/2 - \varepsilon\, r(N - r)$, so each root $e^c$ of the killing polynomial has $|e^c| \le e^{\kappa'(r)}$ with $\kappa'(r) = \rho_r (1 - 2\varepsilon)$, uniformly in $\mathcal{F}$, and the degrees are bounded by $D = \max_r D_r$. Starting from the initial bound and iterating the smoothing step, we obtain $k$ such that the family $\beta^{*k} * \psi$, $\psi \in \mathcal{F}$, has bounds of rate $\kappa(r) = \rho_r(1 - \varepsilon) > \kappa'(r)$ for every admissible $\beta$. Fix such a $\beta$ with $\int_F |\psi - \beta^{*k} * \psi|^2 \le \frac18$ for all $\psi \in \mathcal{F}$. The fact on Siegel sets gives a compact $\Omega$ with $\int_{S \setminus \Omega} |\beta^{*k} * \psi|^2 \le \frac18$ for all $\psi \in \mathcal{F}$. The lemma, applied with $f = \beta^{*k} * \psi$, gives $\int_\Omega |\psi|^2 \ge \frac12$. $\square$

### Gluing of limits

A tree of spaces for $GL_N$ is either a leaf $(V, z)$, with $V$ a space of smooth automorphic forms on $GL_N$ and $z \in \mathbb{C}$, or a node $(t_1, t_2)$ with $t_1$ a tree for $GL_r$, $t_2$ a tree for $GL_s$ and $N = r + s$. Its space is defined by recursion: for a leaf it is $V \otimes |\det|^z$; for a node it is the span of the $K_\infty$-finite forms in the space induced from the parabolic of type $(r, s)$ and the spaces of $t_1$ and $t_2$, twisted by $|\det|^{s/2}$ and $|\det|^{-r/2}$. A tree is good if every leaf is either trivial ($N = 0$) or has $V$ discrete and $\operatorname{Re} z = 0$; it is untwisted if all leaves have $z = 0$. On $H = GL_a(\mathbb{A}_E) \times GL_b(\mathbb{A}_E)$, the pair space of $U_1$ and $U_2$ is the span of the products $\varphi_1(x_1)\varphi_2(x_2)$.

A multiset $m$ is separated if its elements are distinct and pairwise at distance at least $\frac12$. A family $n = (n_\tau)$ of multisets is real symmetric if the elements of each $n_\tau$ are real and $n_{\bar\tau} = -n_\tau$.

**Theorem 3.11.3 (Gluing of limits).** Assume the ten structural facts listed below. Let $E$ be a CM field, $a, b \ge 0$, and $W_1$, $W_2$ finite dimensional spaces of functions on $K_{\infty,a}$ and $K_{\infty,b}$. There are finite families $\Omega_1$, $\Omega_2$ of finite dimensional type spaces such that for all open subgroups $K_1$, $K_2$ there are finite families $\Theta_1$, $\Theta_2$ of open level subgroups with the following property. Let $f_k$ ($k \in \mathbb{N}$) be finite families of nonzero continuous functions on $H$ such that:

1. $f_k$ is left invariant under $GL_a(E) \times GL_b(E)$, of type $(K_1, K_2, W_1, W_2)$, and square integrable against a fixed weight on a fixed domain;
2. the slices $x_1 \mapsto f_k(x_1, x_2)$ and $x_2 \mapsto f_k(x_1, x_2)$ are smooth automorphic forms, whose spans have infinitesimal characters $m_1(k)$ and $m_2(k)$;
3. $f_k$ is an eigenvector of a countable set $C$ of Hecke operators with eigenvalues $\lambda_k(h)$;
4. $m_i(k) \to n_i$ as multisets at every $\tau$, where $n_1$, $n_2$ are real symmetric and separated, and $\lambda_k(h) \to \lambda_0(h)$ for $h \in C$.

Assume also that every type admits a reproducing function as in the concentration theorem. Then there are good untwisted trees $s_1$ for $GL_a$ and $s_2$ for $GL_b$, with infinitesimal characters $n_1$ and $n_2$, whose nontrivial leaves have levels in $\Theta_i$ and types in $\Omega_i$, and a nonzero family $F$ in the pair space of $s_1$ and $s_2$, of type $(K_1, K_2, W_1, W_2)$, with $hF = \lambda_0(h) F$ for every $h \in C$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.EGlue.eFinalGlue` (also `GlobalTheory.T.e45_final_gluing`, a second name).

The ten structural facts in the hypothesis are the following. (Hull.) The stable hull of the slices of one family consists of automorphic forms with nonpositive exponents, unitary central character and the same infinitesimal characters, and the family lies in the pair space of the two hulls. (Separation.) A stable space of automorphic forms generated by finitely many vectors of a fixed type, with nonpositive exponents, unitary central character and an infinitesimal character without repeated entries, maps nonzero and equivariantly into the space of a good tree whose leaves have types and levels in finite families. (Limits of discrete spaces.) A sequence of discrete spaces of a fixed type with bounded infinitesimal characters has a subsequence that embeds into trees of one shape, with discrete leaves and twists that converge to the twists of a good tree. (Grafting.) Embeddings of the children give an embedding of the nodes. (Shift.) If $V$ embeds into the space of $t$, then $V \otimes |\det|^z$ embeds into the space of $t$ with all twists shifted by $z$. (Stability.) A tree with stable leaves has a stable space. (Admissibility.) In a discrete space, the vectors of a fixed type span a finite dimensional space. (Limit of vectors.) Given admissibility, eigenvectors in the pair spaces of convergent trees have a nonzero limit in the pair space of the limit trees. (Untwisting.) A good tree with nonzero space and real symmetric infinitesimal character is untwisted. (Character of a tree.) The infinitesimal character of a tree is the union of the characters of its leaves, each shifted by its twist. Inside the proof we also use the following facts:

- (Factor step.) For each factor, the separation fact can be applied with finitely many types and, for each level, finitely many level subgroups; limits of trees built from these need only finitely many more.
- (One gluing step.) If the infinitesimal characters of the slices of $f_k$ are within $\frac18$ of separated limits, then $f_k$ lies, through an embedding that respects the Hecke operators, in the pair space of good trees $t_1(k)$, $t_2(k)$. Multisets within $\frac18$ of a separated multiset have no repeated entries.
- (Limit of trees.) A sequence of good trees whose infinitesimal characters converge has a subsequence that embeds into trees of one shape whose twists converge to a good tree with the limit character.
- (Pair embedding.) Embeddings of the two factors give an embedding of pair spaces that respects the Hecke operators.

*Proof.* Take the types $\Omega_i$ and levels $\Theta_i$ from the factor step. Choose $k_0$ such that for $k \ge k_0$ the multisets $m_i(k)$ are within $\frac18$ of $n_i$ at every $\tau$. The hull fact applies to each $f_k$, and the one gluing step gives good trees $t_1(k)$, $t_2(k)$ and an embedding $G_k$ for $k \ge k_0$. The limits $n_i$ are finite multisets, so the $m_i(k)$ are bounded for $k \ge k_0$. The limit of trees, applied to the first factor, gives a subsequence along which $t_1(k)$ embeds into trees with twists converging to a good tree $s_1$ with character $n_1$. Along a further subsequence the same holds for $t_2(k)$ and a good tree $s_2$ with character $n_2$. The pair embedding moves $G_k$ into the pair spaces of these trees, and the limit of vectors gives a nonzero family $F$ in the pair space of $s_1$ and $s_2$, of the given type, with Hecke eigenvalues $\lambda_0$.

Since $F \neq 0$, some $F_i(x) \neq 0$, so its slices are nonzero and the spaces of $s_1$ and $s_2$ are nonzero. The characters $n_i$ are real symmetric, so by untwisting of real characters, $s_1$ and $s_2$ are untwisted. $\square$

The trees describe the [residual spectrum](/entry/asc-seed-holds2/) of the last section: a node is a parabolic induction, and good untwisted trees give the induced representations whose constituents are the [isobaric sums](/learn/automorphic-forms/#isobaric-sums) of discrete data.

**Remark 3.11.4 (Formalization).** The concentration theorem has no hypothesis $R > 0$; for $R < 0$ and $N \ge 1$ no space has characters bounded by $R$, and the statement is empty. It is also stated as `GlobalTheory.T.typed_space_approximation`, and it is used in the finiteness of discrete families, `finDisc`, in a file named `IsoVSymm.lean`. The facts used there include `GlobalTheory.T.exists_open_integral_subgroup`, `exists_exponent_polynomial_discrete` and `uniform_bound_siegel_set`. The gluing theorem proves a proposition of the form "ten structural facts imply the conclusion". The proof is the lemma `eFinalGlue_of`, with ten further inputs, at line 35 of [E45FinalGluing.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/E45FinalGluing.lean#L112); the theorem is at line 112. The inputs come from statements such as `GlobalTheory.T.single_gluing` and `factor_limit`. The input `SliceType` (binder `hST`, line 36) is never used in the proof. The definitions of discrete spaces, of the lattice $\Gamma_N$ and of infinitesimal characters sit in a file on base change for definite unitary groups in the folder on abelian varieties.

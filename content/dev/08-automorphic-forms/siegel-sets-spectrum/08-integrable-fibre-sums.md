## Integrable fibre sums

The archimedean contribution to an arithmetic intersection number is an integral $\int_{X(\mathbb{C})} \log \|s\| \; c_1(\bar L_1) \wedge \dots \wedge c_1(\bar L_e)$ over the complex points of a variety $X$ of dimension $e$. Here $s$ is a section of a metrized line bundle and the $c_1(\bar L_j)$ are first Chern forms. The function $\log\|s\|$ has a logarithmic singularity on the divisor of $s$, so convergence is not automatic. This section proves that the integral converges absolutely, in the form used for multiprojective varieties: project $X$ finitely onto $\mathbb{P}^e$, push the integrand forward to $\mathbb{C}^e$ as a finite sum over fibres, and show that the fibre sum of absolute values is integrable. The proof reduces the arithmetic statement to irreducible complex varieties, and for those it splits the integrand into its positive and negative parts. The result is used in the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel theory of heights, where these integrals are the archimedean terms of intersection numbers.

### Multiprojective varieties and fibre sums

Let $K$ be a finite nonempty set of blocks, and for each $k \in K$ let $\iota_k$ be a finite set. The coordinates are $z = (z_{k,i})$, $k \in K$, $i \in \iota_k$. A point $z$ is admissible if every block $(z_{k,i})_i$ is nonzero; admissible points represent points of $\prod_k \mathbb{P}^{\iota_k}$. A polynomial $s$ is multihomogeneous of degree $a \in \mathbb{N}^K$ if each of its monomials has total degree $a_k$ in the variables of block $k$.

**Definition 3.8.1 (Arithmetic and complex varieties).** An arithmetic variety of dimension $d$ is a prime ideal $P \subset \mathbb{Z}[z]$ that is generated by multihomogeneous polynomials, contains no full block of variables, meets $\mathbb{Z}$ only in $0$, and has $\dim \mathbb{Z}[z]/P = d + |K|$. A complex variety of dimension $d$ is a prime ideal $Q \subset \mathbb{C}[z]$ with the same first two properties and $\dim \mathbb{C}[z]/Q = d + |K|$. The cone points of $Q$ are the admissible zeros of $Q$; those of $P$ are the admissible complex zeros of $P$.

So an arithmetic variety of dimension $e + 1$ has complex points of dimension $e$ in $\prod_k \mathbb{P}^{\iota_k}$. The Segre map sends $z$ to the vector $\operatorname{segre}(z)_i = \prod_k z_{k, i_k}$, indexed by $i \in \prod_k \iota_k$.

**Definition 3.8.2 (Good projection, fibre).** A good projection for a set $S$ of cone points is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $\Psi : \mathbb{C}^{\prod_k \iota_k} \to \mathbb{C}^{e+1}$ with $\Psi(\operatorname{segre}(z)) \neq 0$ for every $z \in S$. The fibre over $y \in \mathbb{C}^e$ is the set of $w = \operatorname{segre}(z)$, $z \in S$, with $\Psi(w) = (1, y)$.

A good projection defines a finite map from the variety to $\mathbb{P}^e$, and the fibre is the set of points over the affine point $y$.

**Definition 3.8.3 (Hermitian line, potential, log norm).** A Hermitian line of degree $a \in \mathbb{Z}^K$ is a function $h > 0$ on admissible points with $h(c\cdot z) = \prod_k |c_k|^{a_k} h(z)$ for $c \in (\mathbb{C}^\times)^K$ acting blockwise. It is smooth if $h$ is $C^\infty$. Its potential is $\log h$. For a Hermitian line $A$ and a section $s$ of degree $a = \deg A$, the log norm is $\log(|s(z)| / h_A(z))$.

The log norm is invariant under the blockwise action, so it is a function on the multiprojective variety, the logarithm of the pointwise norm of $s$.

**Definition 3.8.4 (Sheet density, fibre sum).** Let $L_1, \dots, L_e$ be Hermitian lines and $g$ a function on admissible points. A sheet through $w$ in the fibre over $y$ is a holomorphic map $\gamma$, defined near $y$, with $\operatorname{segre}(\gamma(y)) = w$, $\gamma(y')$ a cone point and $\Psi(\operatorname{segre} \gamma(y')) = (1, y')$ for $y'$ near $y$. If a sheet exists, the sheet density of $g$ at $w$ is
$$g(\gamma(y)) \cdot \operatorname{MA}\bigl(\log h_{L_1} \circ \gamma, \dots, \log h_{L_e} \circ \gamma\bigr)(y),$$
where $\operatorname{MA}$ is the mixed Monge-Ampère density: $e!\,(2/\pi)^e$ times the real part of the mixed discriminant of the complex Hessians. Otherwise the density is $0$. The fibre sum is
$$\Sigma(y) = \sum_{w \in \text{fibre}(y)} \bigl|\text{sheet density of } \log \|s\| \text{ at } w\bigr|.$$

The function $\Sigma$ on $\mathbb{C}^e$ is the push forward under $\Psi$ of $|\log\|s\|| \cdot |c_1(\bar L_1) \wedge \dots \wedge c_1(\bar L_e)|$, computed sheet by sheet. Its integrability is the absolute convergence of the archimedean intersection integral.

We use the following facts, taken as given.

- (Finite fibres.) For a complex variety of dimension $e$ with a good projection, every fibre is finite.
- (Measurability.) For a complex variety, smooth $L_j$ and a measurable function $g \ge 0$ invariant under the blockwise action, the fibre sum of the positive parts of the sheet densities of $g$ is almost everywhere measurable in $y$. The function $|\log\|s\||$, set to $0$ off the admissible points, is measurable and invariant.
- (Finite mass.) For a complex variety $Q$ of dimension $e$ with a good projection, smooth $L_j$ and $A$, and $s$ of degree $\deg A$ with $s \notin Q$, the integral $\int_{\mathbb{C}^e} \Sigma(y)\, dy$ is finite.
- (Components.) Let $P$ be an arithmetic variety of dimension $e + 1$. Each minimal prime $Q$ of $P\,\mathbb{C}[z]$ is a complex variety of dimension $e$ with $Q \cap \mathbb{Z}[z] = P$. The set of $y$ whose fibre contains a point on two distinct components is a null set.
- (Independence of the sheet.) For almost every $y$, the sheet density at a point of the fibre does not depend on the sheet chosen.

The finite mass is where the analysis lies: near the divisor of $s$, $\log \|s\|$ is integrable against a smooth positive measure, by the local structure of analytic sets and the Poincaré-Lelong formula.

### Integrability

**Theorem 3.8.5 (Integrability for complex varieties).** Let $Q$ be a complex variety of dimension $e$, $\Psi$ a good projection for its cone points, $L_1, \dots, L_e$ and $A$ smooth Hermitian lines, and $s \in \mathbb{C}[z]$ multihomogeneous of degree $\deg A$ with $s \notin Q$. Then the fibre sum $\Sigma$ is integrable on $\mathbb{C}^e$.

*Proof.* Let $m$ be the Monge-Ampère factor of a sheet and $g = \log \|s\|$ at its point. Then
$$|g\, m| = \max(|g|\, m, 0) + \max(-|g|\, m, 0).$$
Indeed, if $m \ge 0$ the first term is $|g|m$ and the second is $0$, and if $m \le 0$ the roles are exchanged. On admissible points, $|g|$ is the measurable invariant function of the facts. So, summing over the finite fibre, $\Sigma$ is the sum of the fibre sums of the positive parts of the densities of $|g|$ and of $-|g|$. Both are almost everywhere measurable, so $\Sigma$ is. The fibre sum is nonnegative and, by finite mass, its integral is finite. $\square$

**Theorem 3.8.6 (Integrability for arithmetic varieties).** Let $P$ be an arithmetic variety of dimension $e + 1$, $\Psi$ a good projection for its cone points, $L_1, \dots, L_e$ and $A$ smooth Hermitian lines, and $s \in \mathbb{Z}[z]$ multihomogeneous of degree $\deg A$ with $s \notin P$. Then the fibre sum $\Sigma$ of $P$ is integrable on $\mathbb{C}^e$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Pub.CxAbs.ptsAbsIntegrable`.

*Proof.* The ring $\mathbb{C}[z]$ is noetherian, so $P\,\mathbb{C}[z]$ has finitely many minimal primes $Q_1, \dots, Q_m$. Each cone point of $P$ is a cone point of some $Q_l$, and each cone point of $Q_l$ is a cone point of $P$. So $\Psi$ is a good projection for each $Q_l$. By the fact on components, each $Q_l$ is a complex variety of dimension $e$, and $s \notin Q_l$ because $Q_l \cap \mathbb{Z}[z] = P$ and $s \notin P$. By the theorem for complex varieties, each fibre sum $\Sigma_{Q_l}$ is integrable.

Now fix $y$ outside the null set of the fact on components and in the set where the density does not depend on the sheet. The fibre of $P$ over $y$ is the union of the fibres of the $Q_l$. These fibres are disjoint. If $w = \operatorname{segre}(z) = \operatorname{segre}(z')$ with $z$ on $Q_l$ and $z'$ on $Q_{l'}$, then $z'$ is a blockwise multiple of $z$, so $z$ is on $Q_{l'}$ too, and $l = l'$ by the choice of $y$. A sheet of $P$ through $w$ stays, near $y$, on the only component that contains $w$, so it is a sheet of that component; and a sheet of a component is a sheet of $P$. Since the density does not depend on the sheet, the two densities at $w$ agree. Hence
$$\Sigma_P(y) = \sum_{l=1}^m \Sigma_{Q_l}(y)$$
for almost every $y$, and $\Sigma_P$ is integrable as a finite sum of integrable functions. $\square$

**Example 3.8.7.** Let $K$ have one block, $\iota = \{0, 1\}$, $P = 0$, and $e = 1$, so the variety is $\mathbb{P}^1$ and $\Psi$ is the identity. The fibre over $y$ is the single point $(1, y)$. Take $h_L = h_A = |z_0|^2 + |z_1|^2$, of degree $2$, and $s = z_1^2$. Then $\log\|s\|(1,y) = \log\bigl(|y|^2/(1 + |y|^2)\bigr)$, and $\operatorname{MA}$ is a constant times the Fubini-Study density $(1 + |y|^2)^{-2}$. The fibre sum is $|\log(|y|^2/(1+|y|^2))| (1 + |y|^2)^{-2}$ up to a constant. It is integrable on $\mathbb{C}$: near $0$ the singularity is $|\log|y||$, and at infinity the function decays like $|y|^{-6}$.

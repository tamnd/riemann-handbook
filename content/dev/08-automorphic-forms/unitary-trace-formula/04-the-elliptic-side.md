## The elliptic side

This section computes the geometric side of the trace formula for a unitary group in odd rank and a special class of test functions. The test function has [pseudo-coefficients](/entry/local-pc-holds/) at the real places, a type idempotent at a finite set of split places, and an Iwahori double coset at one more split place. For such a function the operator on the automorphic $L^2$ space is trace class, and its trace is the integral of the elliptic kernel: only the rational elements with irreducible characteristic polynomial contribute. The integral is then a finite sum over these polynomials of products of local orbital integrals. The result is used in [the comparison of cuspidal multiplicities](/entry/mult-eq-of-sides/). For the background, see [the trace formula for a compact quotient](/learn/trace-formula/#the-trace-formula-for-a-compact-quotient) and [the simple trace formula](/learn/trace-formula/#arthurs-trace-formula-the-simple-trace-formula-and-pseudo-coefficients).

**Setting.** Let $F$ be a totally real number field and $E/F$ a CM extension, unramified at every finite place, with conjugation $x \mapsto \bar x$. Let $V = E^r$ with a nondegenerate Hermitian Gram matrix $J$, let $L \subset V$ be a lattice, and let $G = \mathrm{U}(V)$. For a finite place $v$ of $F$ let $K_v \subset G(F_v)$ be the stabilizer of $L_v$. Let $G(\mathbb{A})$ be the restricted product of the $G(F_w)$, $w$ archimedean, and the $G(F_v)$ with respect to the $K_v$. We say that a Haar measure $\mu$ on $G(\mathbb{A})$ is the *restricted product* of Haar measures $\mu_w$ and $\mu_v$ if $\mu_v(K_v) = 1$ for almost all $v$ and $\mu$ restricts to $\prod_w \mu_w \times \prod_v \mu_v$ on every open subgroup $\prod_w G(F_w) \times \prod_{v \in T} G(F_v) \times \prod_{v \notin T} K_v$. For $f$ on $G(\mathbb{A})$ let $R(f)$ be the operator $\varphi \mapsto \int f(y)\, \varphi(\cdot\, y)\, d\mu(y)$ on $L^2(G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}))$, and $R_{\mathrm{cusp}}(f)$ its restriction to the cuspidal subspace. We fix a CM type, so each real place $w$ of $F$ has a chosen embedding $\rho$ of $E$ above it, and $G(F_w) \cong \mathrm{U}(p_\rho, q_\rho)$; see [Hermitian forms](/learn/algebra/#hermitian-and-skew-hermitian-forms) and [lattices in Hermitian spaces](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#lattices-in-quadratic-and-hermitian-spaces).

At a finite place $v$ that splits in $E$, a *frame* is an isomorphism $G(F_v) \cong \mathrm{GL}_r(F_v)$ that carries $K_v$ to $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathcal{O}_v)$. Let $k_v$ be the residue field. For $0 < a < r$ let $N_a(k_v)$ be the group of $g \in \mathrm{GL}_r(k_v)$ with $g_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ unless $i \le a < j$: the unipotent radical of the standard parabolic subgroup of type $(a, r - a)$. For an irreducible representation $\tau_v$ of $\mathrm{GL}_r(k_v)$, let $e_{\tau_v}$ be the idempotent of the Hecke algebra of $G(F_v)$ that it defines through reduction $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathcal{O}_v) \to \mathrm{GL}_r(k_v)$.

**Definition 2.4.1 (Test function of type $(\lambda, \tau, t)$).** The data are: for each real embedding $\rho$, a parameter $\lambda_\rho \in \mathbb{Z}^r$ and a discrete series $D_\rho$ of $G(F_{w_\rho})$ with parameter $\lambda_\rho$; a finite set $S$ of split places with frames and irreducible representations $\tau_v$ of $\mathrm{GL}_r(k_v)$, $v \in S$; and one more split place $u$ with a frame and a uniformizer $\varpi$. Let $I_u \subset \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathcal{O}_u)$ be the Iwahori subgroup. A function $f = \bigotimes_w f_w \otimes \bigotimes_v f_v$ is a *test function of this type* for the measure $\mu$ if:

1. $r$ is odd;
2. $\lambda_{\rho, i} - \lambda_{\rho, i+1} \ge r$ for all $\rho$ and $1 \le i < r$;
3. $S$ is not empty, and for each $0 < a < r$ there is $v \in S$ such that $\tau_v$ has no nonzero vector fixed by $N_a(k_v)$;
4. $\mu$ is the restricted product of the $\mu_w$ and the $\mu_v$;
5. for each $\rho$, $f_{w_\rho}$ is smooth with compact support, $D_\rho(f_{w_\rho})$ has trace $1$, $\pi(f_{w_\rho})$ has trace $0$ for every unitary irreducible $\pi \not\cong D_\rho$, and the orbital integrals of $f_{w_\rho}$ vanish at every regular semisimple element with noncompact centralizer;
6. $u \notin S$; for $v \in S$, $f_v = e_{\tau_v} * h_v * e_{\tau_v}$ with $h_v$ smooth and compactly supported; $f_u$ is the characteristic function of $I_u\, t\, I_u$ with $t = \mathrm{diag}(\varpi^{a_1}, \ldots, \varpi^{a_r})$ and $a_1 > \cdots > a_r$; every other $f_v$ is smooth with compact support; and $f_v = \mathbf{1}_{K_v}$ for almost all $v$.

Condition 5 is the part of [the pseudo-coefficient conditions](/entry/local-pc-holds/) that does not involve the elliptic orbital integrals.

For $\gamma \in G(F)$ and a fundamental domain $\mathcal{F}$ for $G(F)$ in $G(\mathbb{A})$, the *class contribution* of $\gamma$ is
$$J_\gamma(f) = \int_{\mathcal{F}} \sum_{\delta \in \{\gamma\}} f(x^{-1} \delta x)\, d\mu(x),$$
where $\{\gamma\}$ is the $G(F)$-conjugacy class. Let $E[\gamma]$ be the $E$-algebra generated by $\gamma$ in $M_r(E)$, and $K_\gamma \subset E[\gamma]$ the $F$-subalgebra fixed by the adjoint involution $x \mapsto J^{-1} \bar x^{t} J$. We say $\gamma$ is *of non-field type* if $\gamma$ is regular semisimple and $K_\gamma \cong K_1 \times \cdots \times K_k$ with $k \ge 2$, each $K_i$ a field and each $K_i \otimes_F E$ a field.

**Proposition 2.4.2 (Classes of non-field type).** Assume conditions 1, 4, 5 and 6, and assume that for each $0 < a < r$ some $v \in S$ has $\tau_v$ with no nonzero vector fixed by $N_a(k_v)$. Let $\gamma \in G(F)$ be of non-field type. Then:

1. For every decomposition $K_\gamma \cong \prod_{i} K_i$ as above and every $i$, the idempotent $\epsilon_i$ of the factor $K_i$ satisfies $\epsilon_i^2 = \epsilon_i$, is self-adjoint, commutes with $E[\gamma]$, has $(1 - \epsilon_i)^* J \epsilon_i = 0$, and has rank $\dim_E(K_i \otimes_F E)$, with $0 < \mathrm{rank}\, \epsilon_i < r$. If $v \in S$ and $\tau_v$ has no nonzero vector fixed by $N_{\mathrm{rank}\, \epsilon_i}(k_v)$, then $O_\gamma(f_v) = 0$ for every Haar measure on the centralizer.
2. For product measures $dt = \prod dt_w \prod dt_v$ on the centralizer $T_\gamma(\mathbb{A})$ and a fundamental domain $\mathcal{T}$ for $T_\gamma(F)$, the volume of $\mathcal{T}$ is finite. The local orbital integrals $o_w = O_\gamma(f_w)$ and $o_v = O_\gamma(f_v)$ exist, $o_v = 1$ for almost all $v$, the global orbital integral is $\prod_w o_w \prod_v o_v$, and $J_\gamma(f) = \mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}) \prod_w o_w \prod_v o_v$.
3. $J_\gamma(f) = 0$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.NonFieldType.nonFieldTypeVanishingX` (also `GlobalTheory.PsCo.Plug.nonFieldT`, a second name).

*Proof.* Part 1 is a computation in the commutative algebra $E[\gamma] = K_\gamma \otimes_F E$. The idempotents of $K_\gamma$ are fixed by the involution, so they are self-adjoint, and their images are orthogonal. The image of $\epsilon_i$ is a free module of rank one over the field $K_i \otimes_F E$, which gives the rank. At a split place $v$ the decomposition $\epsilon_i V_v \oplus (1 - \epsilon_i) V_v$ is stable under $\gamma$, so $\gamma$ lies in a Levi subgroup of a parabolic subgroup whose unipotent radical is conjugate to $N_a$, $a = \mathrm{rank}\, \epsilon_i$. The orbital integral of $f_v$ at $\gamma$ factors through the integral of $f_v$ along this unipotent radical. Since $\tau_v$ has no vector fixed by $N_a(k_v)$, that integral of $e_{\tau_v} * h_v * e_{\tau_v}$ vanishes, so $O_\gamma(f_v) = 0$.

Part 2 holds because $\gamma$ is regular semisimple: its centralizer is a commutative group, the orbit is closed at each place, and the volume is finite because each $K_i \otimes_F E$ is a field. The global orbital integral of a pure tensor is the product of the local ones, with $o_v = 1$ for almost all $v$, and the class contribution is the volume times the orbital integral.

For part 3, put $a = \mathrm{rank}\, \epsilon_1$. Then $0 < a < r$, and by hypothesis some $v \in S$ has $\tau_v$ with no nonzero vector fixed by $N_a(k_v)$. By part 1, $o_v = 0$, so $J_\gamma(f) = 0$ by part 2. $\square$

**Proposition 2.4.3 (Classes that meet a proper Levi subgroup).** Assume that the class kernel of every regular semisimple class is integrable on $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A})$ for every smooth compactly supported $f$ whose constant terms along proper parabolic subgroups vanish. Assume also the standard structure facts on regular semisimple elements of unitary groups: in particular, the centralizer quotient is compact if and only if each $K_i \otimes_F E$ is a field. Let $f$ be a test function of type $(\lambda, \tau, t)$, and let $\gamma \in G(F)$ be regular semisimple, with reducible characteristic polynomial, and not of non-field type. Then $J_\gamma(f) = 0$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PsCo.Plug.hyperZero_of_intX`.

*Proof.* Since $\gamma$ is not of non-field type and its characteristic polynomial is reducible, some factor $K_i \otimes_F E$ is not a field. This gives a $\gamma$-stable isotropic subspace of $V$, so $\gamma$ is $G(F)$-conjugate to an element $\gamma_0$ of a proper standard Levi subgroup. The class contribution does not change under conjugation, so we may replace $\gamma$ by $\gamma_0$. The centralizer of $\gamma_0$ contains a nontrivial split torus of the Levi subgroup, so it is noncompact at every archimedean place $w$. By condition 5, $O_{\gamma_0}(f_w) = 0$ for every Haar measure on the centralizer. By condition 3 the constant terms of $f$ along proper parabolic subgroups vanish, so the hypothesis on class kernels applies, and the class contribution factors through the orbital integral at $w$. So $J_{\gamma_0}(f) = 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.4.4 (Elliptic side).** Assume that $R_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\varphi)$ is trace class for every smooth compactly supported $\varphi$ on $G(\mathbb{A})$ and every right-invariant Haar measure. Let $f$ be a test function of type $(\lambda, \tau, t)$ for $\mu$. Then $R(f)$ is trace class, and
$$\mathrm{tr}\, R(f) = \int_{G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A})} k_{\mathrm{ell}}(x)\, dx, \qquad k_{\mathrm{ell}}(x) = \sum_{\substack{\delta \in G(F) \\ \text{char. poly. of } \delta \text{ irreducible}}} f(x^{-1} \delta x).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PsCo.Plug.ellipticSideDirect`.

*Proof.* The constant terms of $f$ along every proper parabolic subgroup vanish, by condition 3 and the type idempotents at $S$. So $R(f)$ maps into the cuspidal subspace, and by the hypothesis it is trace class, with trace the integral of the diagonal of its kernel $\sum_{\delta \in G(F)} f(x^{-1} \delta x)$. If $f(x^{-1} \delta x) \neq 0$, then the component of $x^{-1}\delta x$ at $u$ lies in $I_u t I_u$, and since the exponents $a_i$ are distinct, $\delta$ is regular semisimple. So the kernel is a sum over regular semisimple classes. Only finitely many classes meet the support of $f$, by the finiteness of regular classes that follows from the Siegel reduction theory. A class with reducible characteristic polynomial contributes $0$: by the Proposition on classes of non-field type if it is of that type, and by the Proposition on classes that meet a proper Levi subgroup otherwise. The classes that remain are those with irreducible characteristic polynomial, whose sum is $k_{\mathrm{ell}}$. $\square$

The elliptic kernel is computed one characteristic polynomial at a time. Let $P \in E[X]$ be monic, irreducible, of degree $r$, with $P(0) \neq 0$ and $P = P^\sharp$, where $P^\sharp(X) = \overline{P(0)}^{-1} X^r \bar P(1/X)$. Let $K_P = E[X]/(P)$ with root $\alpha$ and the involution $\alpha \mapsto \alpha^{-1}$ extending the conjugation of $E$. Let $T_P$ be the torus of $t \in K_P^\times$ with $t \bar t = 1$. For a place $w$ of $F$, a local class is an element $x$ of $(K_P \otimes F_w)^\times$ fixed by the involution, modulo norms, such that the form $(y, y') \mapsto \mathrm{Tr}_{K_P/E}(x\, y\, \bar y')$ is isometric to $V_w$. Each local class $q$ gives an element $\gamma_q \in G(F_w)$, the image of $\alpha$, with centralizer $T_P(F_w)$. Put
$$\Phi_w(P) = \sum_{q} O_{\gamma_q}(f_w), \qquad \mathrm{term}(P) = \mathrm{vol}(T_P(F) \backslash T_P(\mathbb{A})) \prod_{v} \Phi_v(P) \prod_{w \mid \infty} \Phi_w(P).$$
This is the stable orbital integral of $f_w$ along the stable class with characteristic polynomial $P$, restricted to the classes that occur in $V_w$.

**Theorem 2.4.5 (Elliptic expansion).** Let $f$ be a test function of type $(\lambda, \tau, t)$ for $\mu$, and for each $P$ as above let $dt_P = \prod dt_{P, w} \prod dt_{P, v}$ be the restricted product measure on $T_P(\mathbb{A})$ for the standard lattice. Then for each $P$: the volume of $T_P(F) \backslash T_P(\mathbb{A})$ is finite; at every place the set of local classes is finite and their orbital integrals exist; and $\Phi_v(P) = 1$ for almost all $v$. The set of $P$ with $\mathrm{term}(P) \neq 0$ is finite, $k_{\mathrm{ell}}$ is integrable on $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A})$, and
$$\int_{G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A})} k_{\mathrm{ell}}(x)\, dx = \sum_P \mathrm{term}(P).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.TrEllSum.ellipticPartX` (also `GlobalTheory.PsCo.Plug.ellipticT`, a second name).

*Proof.* Only finitely many characteristic polynomials of rational elements meet the support of $f$. For each $P$, the rational elements with characteristic polynomial $P$ form finitely many $G(F)$-classes, each with centralizer a form of $T_P$, and the sum over them unfolds to the product of the torus volume and the local stable orbital integrals. At almost all places $f_v = \mathbf{1}_{K_v}$ and $T_P$ is unramified, so $\Phi_v(P) = 1$. $\square$

**Remark 2.4.6 (Formalization).** The two second names are restatements by `first | exact …` ([NonFieldTypeVanishingX.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/NonFieldTypeVanishingX.lean#L112), [NonFieldTypeVanishingX.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/NonFieldTypeVanishingX.lean#L141)). The oddness hypothesis of the first Proposition is not used ([NonFieldTypeVanishingX.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/NonFieldTypeVanishingX.lean#L102)). The two "no fixed vector" conditions have separate definitions: condition 3 asks also that $S$ is nonempty ([EllipticPartTestData.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/TwistedDiscreteSeries/EllipticPartTestData.lean#L227)), while the Proposition uses a second definition with the radical written in another form ([RadicalNoFixedVectors.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/TwistedDiscreteSeries/RadicalNoFixedVectors.lean#L17)). The second Proposition takes its two assumptions as hypotheses; the proof of the Theorem supplies them from results proved elsewhere. The Theorem takes trace class for cusp forms as a hypothesis, recorded as a published result ([CuspDiscreteDecompositionOfTraceClass.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/TwistedTraceFormula/CuspDiscreteDecompositionOfTraceClass.lean#L21)); its finiteness of classes comes from a Siegel input of another part of the library ([EllTraceOfMiddleX.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/EllTraceOfMiddleX.lean#L186)). In the intermediate step of that proof, the structure facts are passed in but not used ([EllTraceOfMiddleX.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/EllTraceOfMiddleX.lean#L93)). The elliptic expansion uses only condition 4, the compact support of the $f_w$, and the conditions on the $f_v$ outside $S$ and $u$; the oddness, the gaps, condition 3 and the trace conditions are not used ([NonFieldTypeVanishingX.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/NonFieldTypeVanishingX.lean#L125)). It also uses that $E/F$ is unramified at every finite place, which is part of the fixed global data. The gap condition here is $\lambda_i - \lambda_{i+1} \ge r$ ([EllipticPartTestData.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/TwistedDiscreteSeries/EllipticPartTestData.lean#L230)), weaker than the gap $100\, r^4$ of [the pseudo-coefficient theorem](/entry/local-pc-holds/). The $L^2$ space carries the name `DividedPowerChartLift` ([AestronglyMeasurableQuotMeasureIffLift.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/AestronglyMeasurableQuotMeasureIffLift.lean#L652)), which has no relation to its content and is also the name of an unrelated statement. The class contribution and the local orbital integrals are defined as "has value" predicates; we did not unfold them.

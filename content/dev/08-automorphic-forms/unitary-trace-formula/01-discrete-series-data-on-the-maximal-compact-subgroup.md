## Discrete series data on the maximal compact subgroup

A pseudo-coefficient of a discrete series representation $D$ of $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$ is a test function whose trace is $1$ on $D$ and $0$ on every other unitary irreducible representation. The construction of the next two sections starts from a polynomial function $\Phi$ on the maximal compact subgroup $K$. This section finds $\Phi$. It is a class function on $K$; on the compact torus it is the conjugate of the character of $D$ times a fixed weight; and the operator $\int_K \Phi(k)\, \pi(k)\, dk$ has trace $1$ for $\pi = D$ and $0$ for every other unitary irreducible $\pi$. The function $\Phi$ comes from the character of one $K$-type of $D$. The result is used in [the pseudo-coefficient theorem](/entry/local-pc-holds/). For the background, see [discrete series](/learn/representations/#discrete-series), [compact groups](/learn/representations/#compact-groups) and [highest weights and branching](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching).

**Definition 2.1.1 (The group and its compact subgroup).** Let $r \ge 1$ and $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}^r$, with $p$ entries $+1$ and $q$ entries $-1$. Let $J_\varepsilon = \mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon_1, \dots, \varepsilon_r)$ and
$$G_\varepsilon = \{ g \in \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{C}) : g^* J_\varepsilon g = J_\varepsilon \}.$$
This is the [[unitary group|0R9R0Y1HDK]] of the Hermitian form of signature $(p, q)$. Let $K_\varepsilon = G_\varepsilon \cap \mathrm{U}(r)$, the elements that preserve the two blocks; it is isomorphic to $\mathrm{U}(p) \times \mathrm{U}(q)$. Let $dk$ be the [[Haar measure|VY0HXD3GC4]] on $K_\varepsilon$ of total mass $1$. For $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^r$ let $t_\theta = \mathrm{diag}(e^{i\theta_1}, \dots, e^{i\theta_r})$, a point of the compact diagonal torus of $K_\varepsilon$.

The group $G_\varepsilon$ is $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$ with the coordinates permuted by $\varepsilon$. We keep $\varepsilon$ because all formulas below are in terms of matrix entries.

**Definition 2.1.2 (Modes and polynomials).** A *mode* is a pair $x = (a, b)$ of indices with $\varepsilon_a = +1$ and $\varepsilon_b = -1$. There are $N = pq$ modes; they index the off-diagonal blocks, so $N$ is the complex dimension of $G_\varepsilon / K_\varepsilon$. A *polynomial on matrices* is a polynomial $P$ with complex coefficients in $2r^2$ variables $x_{ab}$, $y_{ab}$. Its value at a matrix $g$ is $P(g)$, obtained by $x_{ab} \mapsto g_{ab}$ and $y_{ab} \mapsto \overline{g_{ab}}$. The *block algebra* is the subalgebra generated by the $x_{ab}$ with $\varepsilon_a = \varepsilon_b$. For $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^r$ let
$$d_\varepsilon(\theta) = \prod_{(a, b) \text{ a mode}} \bigl| e^{i\theta_a} - e^{i\theta_b} \bigr|^2 .$$

The value of $d_\varepsilon(\theta)$ is the square of the absolute value of the product over the noncompact roots. It is the factor by which the Weyl integration formula for $K_\varepsilon$ differs from the one for $G_\varepsilon$ on the compact torus.

**Definition 2.1.3 (The top polynomial).** For a matrix $Z$ let $\bar Z_{ab} = -\varepsilon_a \varepsilon_b Z_{ba}$. Let $D_Z$ be the derivation of the polynomial ring that sends the matrix of variables $x = (x_{ab})$ to $xZ - Zx$ and the matrix $y = (y_{ab})$ to $y\bar Z - \bar Z y$; it is the derivative of conjugation by the one-parameter group of $Z$. For a mode $x = (a, b)$ let $D_x = D_{E_{ba}}$, with $E_{ba}$ the elementary matrix. Let $\Omega$ be the $N \times N$ matrix of polynomials $\Omega_{x x'} = D_x(y_{x'})$. For $M \ge 0$ and a polynomial $P$, the *top polynomial* is
$$\Phi_M(P) = \det(y_{ab})^M \cdot P \cdot \det \Omega .$$

The factor $\det \Omega$ is the Jacobian that appears when one moves the derivations $D_x$ through a function of the radial variable; it is what makes the top coefficient of the Koszul recursion of [the next section](/entry/st-alg/) equal to $\Phi_M(P)$.

Two more objects come with $\varepsilon$: a continuous function $\delta_\varepsilon$ on $K_\varepsilon$, and an integer $s_\varepsilon$. On the torus they satisfy
$$\begin{aligned} \delta_\varepsilon(t_\theta) &= (-1)^{pq} \prod_{(a, b) \text{ a mode}} \bigl(e^{i\theta_a} - e^{i\theta_b}\bigr) \prod_{\varepsilon_a = -1} e^{-ip\theta_a}, \\ \delta_\varepsilon(t_\theta)\, \overline{\delta_\varepsilon(t_\theta)} &= d_\varepsilon(\theta). \end{aligned}$$
If $p = 0$ or $q = 0$ then $\delta_\varepsilon = 1$ and $s_\varepsilon = 0$.

**Lemma 2.1.4 (A $K$-type with trace one).** Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^r$ be a Harish-Chandra parameter for $G_\varepsilon$ that satisfies the gap condition of the discrete series theory, and let $D$ be a [[discrete series|9AB7HYGPEK]] representation of $G_\varepsilon$ with parameter $\lambda$. Then there is a finite-dimensional irreducible unitary representation $T$ of $K_\varepsilon$, of highest weight $\gamma_\lambda$, with the following property. Let $P$ be a polynomial with $\overline{\mathrm{tr}\, T(k)} = P(k)$ for all $k \in K_\varepsilon$. If the operator
$$B = \int_{K_\varepsilon} P(k)\, \delta_\varepsilon(k)\, D(k)\, dk$$
is trace class with trace $c$, then $c = (-1)^{s_\varepsilon}$.

*Proof.* Suppose first that $p = 0$ or $q = 0$, so that $G_\varepsilon = K_\varepsilon$ is compact. Then $\delta_\varepsilon = 1$, $s_\varepsilon = 0$, and $D$ is itself finite-dimensional and irreducible with highest weight $\gamma_\lambda$; take $T = D$. The trace of $B$ is $\int_K P(k)\, \mathrm{tr}\, D(k)\, dk = \int_K \overline{\chi_T(k)}\, \chi_T(k)\, dk$, and this is $1$ by [[Schur orthogonality|ETNDTECY97]] for the irreducible character $\chi_T$ (see [compact groups](/learn/representations/#compact-groups)). Suppose now that $p, q > 0$. Then we use a supertrace computation: there is a $K$-type $T$ of $D$ of highest weight $\gamma_\lambda$, and for each $P$ as in the Lemma the trace of $B$ is $(-1)^{s_\varepsilon} \int_K \overline{\chi_T(k)}\, \chi_T(k)\, dk$. The reason is that the supertrace of the operator is carried by the kernel of a Dirac operator, and under the gap condition this kernel is spanned by the lowest $K$-type. By Schur orthogonality again the integral is $1$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.1.5 (Discrete series data on $K$).** Let $r$ be odd, let $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}^r$, and let $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^r$ with
$$\lambda_i - \lambda_{i+1} \ge 100\, r^4 \qquad (1 \le i < r).$$
Let $D$ be a discrete series representation of $G_\varepsilon$ with parameter $\lambda$. Then there are $M \ge 0$ and a polynomial $P$ in the block algebra such that $\Phi = \Phi_M(P)$ has the following four properties.

1. $\Phi(k'^{-1} k k') = \Phi(k)$ for all $k, k' \in K_\varepsilon$.
2. For every $\theta$ such that the $e^{i\theta_a}$ are distinct, $\Phi(t_\theta) = \overline{\Theta_\lambda(t_\theta)}\, d_\varepsilon(\theta)$, where $\Theta_\lambda$ is the Harish-Chandra character of the discrete series with parameter $\lambda$.
3. If $B = \int_{K_\varepsilon} \Phi(k)\, D(k)\, dk$ is trace class with trace $c$, then $c = 1$.
4. Let $\pi$ be a unitary irreducible representation of $G_\varepsilon$ on a Hilbert space that is not unitarily equivalent to $D$. If $B_\pi = \int_{K_\varepsilon} \Phi(k)\, \pi(k)\, dk$ is trace class with trace $c$, then $c = 0$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PsCo.Pc1.maximalCompact_pseudoCoefficientData'` (also `GlobalTheory.T.maximalCompact_pseudoCoefficientData`, a second name; `GlobalTheory.PsCo.PcEF.stKData_of`, the same statement from its inputs).

*Proof.* Because $r$ is odd and the gaps are at least $100 r^4$, the real vector $\lambda$ is a Harish-Chandra parameter and satisfies the gap conditions that the Lemma and the lowest $K$-type theory need. Let $T$ be the $K$-type of the Lemma.

*The polynomial.* The matrix coefficients of a finite-dimensional unitary representation of $K_\varepsilon$ are polynomials in the entries and their conjugates, so there is a polynomial $P_0$ with $\overline{\chi_T(k)} = P_0(k)$ on $K_\varepsilon$. There is also a polynomial $Q$ with $Q(k) \det \Omega(k) = \delta_\varepsilon(k)$ on $K_\varepsilon$. For $k \in K_\varepsilon$ the off-diagonal blocks vanish, and $\overline{k} = (k^{-1})^{T}$ with $1/\det k = \det \overline{k}$, so each conjugate entry of $k$ is $\det \overline{k}$ times a polynomial in the entries of the diagonal blocks. Hence every polynomial agrees on $K_\varepsilon$ with $\det(y_{ab})^M$ times an element of the block algebra, for some $M$. Apply this to $(-1)^{s_\varepsilon} P_0 Q$: we get $M$ and $P$ in the block algebra with
$$\Phi_M(P)(k) = (-1)^{s_\varepsilon}\, P_0(k)\, \delta_\varepsilon(k) \qquad (k \in K_\varepsilon).$$

*Property 1.* Both $\chi_T$ and $\delta_\varepsilon$ are class functions on $K_\varepsilon$.

*Property 2.* Write the highest weight of $T$ as an integral dominant weight $\Lambda$ for $K_\varepsilon$. The Weyl character formula for $K_\varepsilon$ gives $\chi_T(t_\theta)$ as a quotient of alternating sums. Comparison with the formula for $\Theta_\lambda$ on the compact torus gives, for regular $\theta$,
$$(-1)^{s_\varepsilon}\, \chi_T(t_\theta) = \Theta_\lambda(t_\theta)\, \delta_\varepsilon(t_\theta).$$
Take complex conjugates and multiply by $\delta_\varepsilon(t_\theta)$. Since $\delta_\varepsilon \overline{\delta_\varepsilon} = d_\varepsilon$ on the torus, $\Phi(t_\theta) = (-1)^{s_\varepsilon} \overline{\chi_T(t_\theta)}\, \delta_\varepsilon(t_\theta) = \overline{\Theta_\lambda(t_\theta)}\, d_\varepsilon(\theta)$.

*Property 3.* The operator $(-1)^{s_\varepsilon} B$ is the operator of the Lemma for $P_0$. Its trace is $(-1)^{s_\varepsilon}$, so the trace of $B$ is $1$.

*Property 4.* Suppose that the trace $c$ of $B_\pi$ is not $0$. Then $(-1)^{s_\varepsilon} B_\pi$ has a nonzero trace. The same supertrace computation as in the Lemma, now on $\pi$ tensored with a Fock space, writes this trace as an average of $\overline{\chi_T}$ against the trace of $K_\varepsilon$ on a finite-dimensional space of vectors killed by a Dirac operator. Since the average is not $0$, there is a nonzero $K$-equivariant map from $T$ into this space, and so a highest weight vector of weight $\gamma_\lambda$ in it. From this vector one obtains a $K$-type of $\pi$ of highest weight $\lambda + \rho_n - \rho_c$ on which the Casimir operator acts by the scalar of $D$. A unitary irreducible representation with such a $K$-type is equivalent to $D$ (the lowest $K$-type theorem; see [discrete series](/learn/representations/#discrete-series)). So $\pi$ is unitarily equivalent to $D$, a contradiction. $\square$

The proof does not show that $B$ or $B_\pi$ is trace class; it only says what the trace is if it exists. In the application, $B_\pi$ appears as the limit of traces of operators $\pi(f_t)$ that are trace class, and the trace class property comes from there.

**Remark 2.1.6 (Formalization).** The Theorem is proved from 24 inputs, each a named statement about $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$, $K$-types, Casimir eigenvalues or the Weyl character formula; one more step supplies all 24 and gives the statement with no hypothesis ([MaximalCompactPseudoCoefficientData.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/MaximalCompactPseudoCoefficientData.lean#L32)). The second name is a restatement by `first | exact …` ([MaximalCompactPseudoCoefficientDataStatement.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/MaximalCompactPseudoCoefficientDataStatement.lean#L6)). Properties 3 and 4 are conditional: they do not assert that the operators are trace class ([RangeShape.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/RangeShape.lean#L129)). We did not unfold the definitions of $\delta_\varepsilon$, $s_\varepsilon$, the weight $\gamma_\lambda$, the gap condition of the Lemma, or the predicate that says that $D$ is a discrete series with parameter $\lambda$; the text uses only the properties of them that the proof states. In the noncompact case of the Lemma the trace computation is a single imported result on supertraces ([SupTrace.lean](lean:ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/DiscreteSeries/TraceGap/SupTrace.lean#L75)).

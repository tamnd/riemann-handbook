## The Koszul family

The pseudo-coefficient of a discrete series of $G_\varepsilon = \mathrm{U}(p, q)$ is built as one member $\varphi_1$ of a family $\varphi_t$, $t \ge 1$, of compactly supported functions. Each $\varphi_t$ is a finite sum of a function of the radial variable times a polynomial in the matrix entries. The family has two properties. First, $\varphi_1 - \varphi_t$ is a sum of Lie derivatives, so the traces and orbital integrals of $\varphi_t$ do not depend on $t$. Second, as $t \to \infty$ the family concentrates on $K_\varepsilon$, and its integrals converge to integrals over $K_\varepsilon$ of the top polynomial of [the previous section](/entry/st-kdata-of/). This section proves the algebraic part (a recursion for the polynomials) and the analytic part (the two limits). Both are used in [the pseudo-coefficient theorem](/entry/local-pc-holds/). We keep the notation of the previous section: modes, the polynomials on matrices, the derivations $D_x$, the number $N = pq$ of modes, the weight $d_\varepsilon$ and the top polynomial $\Phi_M(P)$.

**Definition 2.2.1 (Radial function).** Let $\rho = \sum_{(a, b)} x_{ab}\, y_{ab}$, the sum over the modes. Its value at $g$ is $\rho(g) = \sum_{(a, b)} |g_{ab}|^2$, the squared norm of the off-diagonal block of $g$. On $G_\varepsilon$ the function $\rho$ is continuous and bi-invariant under $K_\varepsilon$, it vanishes exactly on $K_\varepsilon$, and its sublevel sets are compact. So $\rho$ measures the distance from $K_\varepsilon$.

**Definition 2.2.2 (Koszul data).** A pair of sequences $p_k$ ($k \ge 0$) and $q_{x, k}$ ($x$ a mode, $k \ge 0$) of polynomials on matrices is *Koszul data* if, with $q_{x, -1} = 0$,
$$(k + 1)\, p_{k+1} + \rho\, p_k = \sum_x \bigl( D_x(q_{x, k}) + D_x(\rho)\, q_{x, k-1} \bigr) \qquad (k \ge 0),$$
and $p_k = 0$ for $k > N$, and $q_{x, k} = 0$ for $k \ge N$.

**Definition 2.2.3 (Profiles and the family).** A *profile of order $N$* is a smooth function $G : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $G(u) = 0$ for $u \ge 1$, $G^{(N)}(u) \ge 0$ for all $u$, and $G^{(N)}(0) > 0$. For a profile $G$, a sequence $p = (p_k)$ and $t > 0$, let
$$\varphi_t(g) = \sum_{k = 0}^{N} t^k\, G^{(k)}\bigl(t\, \rho(g)\bigr)\, p_k(g).$$
Here $t^k G^{(k)}(t u)$ is the $k$-th derivative in $u$ of $G(tu)$. The function $\varphi_t$ vanishes where $t \rho(g) \ge 1$, so its support shrinks to $K_\varepsilon$ as $t$ grows.

The recursion is chosen for the following reason. Differentiate $\varphi_t$ in $t$ and collect the terms with $t^k G^{(k+1)}(t \rho)$: the coefficient is $(k + 1) p_{k+1} + \rho\, p_k$. The recursion writes this coefficient as a sum of derivations $D_x$ of other terms, so $\partial_t \varphi_t$ is a sum of Lie derivatives of compactly supported functions. Such functions have trace $0$ in every unitary representation and orbital integral $0$ at every regular semisimple element. This is a Koszul complex in disguise: the derivations $D_x$ commute with each other, and $\rho$ plays the role of the Euler element.

**Proposition 2.2.4 (Algebraic step).** Let $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}^r$, $M \ge 0$, and let $P$ be a polynomial in the block algebra. Then there are Koszul data $(p_k, q_{x, k})$ with $p_N = \Phi_M(P)$.

*Proof.* The set of $P$ for which the statement holds contains $0$ (take all $p_k$ and $q_{x, k}$ zero; then $p_N = 0 = \Phi_M(0)$) and is closed under sums, because the conditions are linear and $\Phi_M$ is linear in $P$. The block algebra is spanned by products $m_1 \cdots m_L$ of generators $x_{ab}$ with $\varepsilon_a = \varepsilon_b$, so it is enough to treat such a product.

For a generator $m_l$ and a mode $x$, the derivation $D_x(m_l)$ is a linear combination $\sum_{x'} c_{l x x'}\, x_{x'}$ of the mode variables, with constant coefficients. Adjoin to the polynomial ring a variable $\mu$ and variables $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_L$ with $\lambda_l^2 = 0$. Consider the *pencil*, the $N \times N$ matrix
$$Z = \mu\, \Omega + \sum_{l = 1}^{L} \lambda_l\, C_l, \qquad (C_l)_{x x'} = c_{l x x'},$$
and the weight $E = \det(y_{ab})^M \prod_{l} (1 + \lambda_l m_l)$. Extend $D_x$ to the larger ring by $D_x(\mu) = D_x(\lambda_l) = 0$. The entries of $Z$ satisfy two identities: $\partial_\mu Z_{x x'} = D_x(y_{x'})$, and $D_w(Z_{x x'}) = D_x(Z_{w x'})$, which holds because the derivations $D_x$ commute. Also $\rho = \sum_{x'} x_{x'} y_{x'}$, and $D_x E + \mu D_x(\rho) E - E \sum_{x'} Z_{x x'} x_{x'}$ lies in the ideal generated by the $\lambda_l^2$; this uses the formula for $D_x(m_l)$. A general identity for the adjugate matrix then gives, modulo the same ideal,
$$\partial_\mu (E \det Z) + \rho\, E \det Z = \sum_x \Bigl( D_x(E\, \textstyle\sum_{x'} \mathrm{adj}(Z)_{x' x}\, y_{x'}) + \mu\, D_x(\rho)\, E\, \textstyle\sum_{x'} \mathrm{adj}(Z)_{x' x}\, y_{x'} \Bigr).$$
Take the coefficient of $\lambda_1 \cdots \lambda_L$, and then the coefficient of $\mu^k$. Let $p_k$ be the coefficient of $\lambda_1 \cdots \lambda_L\, \mu^k$ in $E \det Z$, and $q_{x, k}$ the coefficient of $\lambda_1 \cdots \lambda_L\, \mu^k$ in $E \sum_{x'} \mathrm{adj}(Z)_{x' x}\, y_{x'}$. The identity becomes the Koszul recursion. The determinant of $Z$ has degree at most $N$ in $\mu$, and the adjugate has degree at most $N - 1$, which gives the vanishing conditions. The coefficient of $\mu^N$ in $\det Z$ is $\det \Omega$, and the coefficient of $\lambda_1 \cdots \lambda_L$ in $\prod_l (1 + \lambda_l m_l)$ is $m_1 \cdots m_L$; so $p_N = \det(y_{ab})^M\, m_1 \cdots m_L\, \det \Omega = \Phi_M(m_1 \cdots m_L)$. $\square$

**Proposition 2.2.5 (The limits).** Let $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}^r$, let $\mu$ be a Haar measure on $G_\varepsilon$, and let $G$ be a profile of order $N$. Then there is a complex number $c \ne 0$ such that for every sequence $p = (p_k)$ of polynomials, with $\varphi_t$ as above:

1. for every continuous function $w$ on $G_\varepsilon$,
$$\lim_{t \to \infty} \int_{G_\varepsilon} \varphi_t(g)\, w(g)\, d\mu(g) = c \int_{K_\varepsilon} p_N(k)\, w(k)\, dk;$$
2. for every $\theta$ such that the $e^{i\theta_a}$ are distinct, every $s \in G_\varepsilon$ with $s = t_\theta$, and under the assumption $p_N(k^{-1} s k) = p_N(s)$ for all $k \in K_\varepsilon$,
$$\lim_{t \to \infty} \int_{G_\varepsilon} \varphi_t(x^{-1} s x)\, d\mu(x) = \frac{c\, p_N(s)}{d_\varepsilon(\theta)}.$$

*Proof.* For $B \in \mathbb{C}^N$, indexed by the modes, let $e(B) \in G_\varepsilon$ be the exponential of the element of the Lie algebra whose off-diagonal block is given by $B$. By the Cartan decomposition $G_\varepsilon = K_\varepsilon \exp(\mathfrak{p})$, there is a continuous function $J$ on $\mathbb{C}^N$ with $J(0) > 0$ such that for every continuous compactly supported $f$,
$$\int_{G_\varepsilon} f\, d\mu = \int_{\mathbb{C}^N} J(B) \int_{K_\varepsilon} f(k\, e(B))\, dk\, dB = \int_{\mathbb{C}^N} J(B) \int_{K_\varepsilon} f(e(B)\, k)\, dk\, dB$$
(see [Haar measure](/learn/analysis/#haar-measure)). Put $c = J(0) \int_{\mathbb{C}^N} G^{(N)}(|v|^2)\, dv$. The integrand is $\ge 0$ and positive near $0$, so $c \ne 0$.

Both limits follow from one blow-up lemma. Let $\beta : \mathbb{C}^N \to \mathbb{C}^N$ be continuous with $\beta(0) = 0$, differentiable at $0$ with derivative $w \mapsto (d_x w_x)_x$ for nonzero constants $d_x$, and such that $|\beta(B)|$ small forces $|B|$ small. Let $\psi_0, \dots, \psi_N$ be continuous. Then, after the substitution $B = v / \sqrt{t}$,
$$\int_{\mathbb{C}^N} \sum_{k = 0}^{N} t^k\, G^{(k)}\bigl(t |\beta(B)|^2\bigr)\, \psi_k(B)\, dB \longrightarrow \frac{\psi_N(0) \int G^{(N)}(|v|^2)\, dv}{\prod_x |d_x|^2}.$$
Only the top term survives, because the volume of the region $t |\beta(B)|^2 \le 1$ is of order $t^{-N}$.

For the first limit, use the first polar formula with $f = \varphi_t\, w$. Since $\rho$ is bi-invariant under $K_\varepsilon$, $\rho(k\, e(B)) = \rho(e(B)) = |\beta(B)|^2$ with $\beta(B)$ the off-diagonal block of $e(B)$; its derivative at $0$ is the identity, so all $d_x = 1$. Take $\psi_k(B) = J(B) \int_K p_k(k\, e(B))\, w(k\, e(B))\, dk$. Then $\psi_N(0) = J(0) \int_K p_N\, w\, dk$.

For the second limit, use the second polar formula with $f(x) = \varphi_t(x^{-1} s x)$. Then $\rho(k^{-1} e(-B) s\, e(B) k) = |\beta(B)|^2$, where now $\beta(B)$ is the off-diagonal block of $e(-B)\, s\, e(B)$. Its derivative at $0$ multiplies the coordinate of the mode $(a, b)$ by $e^{i\theta_a} - e^{i\theta_b}$, which is not $0$ because the $e^{i\theta_a}$ are distinct, and $\prod_x |d_x|^2 = d_\varepsilon(\theta)$. The properness condition holds because $s$ is regular: if $\rho(x^{-1} s x)$ is small then $\rho(x)$ is small. At $B = 0$ the inner integral is $\int_K p_N(k^{-1} s k)\, dk = p_N(s)$, by the assumption on $p_N$ and because $dk$ has mass $1$. $\square$

**Remark 2.2.6 (Formalization).** The first Proposition is proved for one product of generators by the pencil argument, and then extended by induction over the span ([MuPowLambdaProductCoeffMuVariablePencilEmbedding.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/MuPowLambdaProductCoeffMuVariablePencilEmbedding.lean#L473)). The second Proposition is proved from eight named inputs: the properties of $e(B)$ and of $\rho$, the polar decomposition of Haar measure, a lower bound for $\rho(e(B))$, the two derivatives at $0$, the properness near a regular element, the blow-up lemma, and the nonvanishing of the constant ([ContinuousFamF.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ContinuousFamF.lean#L328)). The first limit is stated for every continuous $w$, with no integrability assumption; this is harmless because $\varphi_t$ is continuous with compact support for $t > 0$. The polynomials $p_k$ for $k < N$ play no role in the limits.

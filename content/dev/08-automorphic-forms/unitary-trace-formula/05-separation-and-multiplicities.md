## Separation and cuspidal multiplicities

This section compares cuspidal multiplicities on two unitary groups that are isomorphic at every finite place. The first result separates the traces of inequivalent representations of the finite adelic group by test functions of a fixed type. It uses Casselman's formula for the trace of an Iwahori double coset and the linear independence of exponents. The second and third results put this together with [the elliptic side](/entry/elliptic-side-direct/) and [pseudo-coefficients](/entry/local-pc-holds/): two groups with the same local data at the finite places and the same discrete series parameters at the real places have the same cuspidal multiplicities for every representation that has a type vector. This is the content of [the comparison of cuspidal multiplicities](/entry/unitary-theory-of-harish-chandra-langlands-alt/) in Volume 14. For the background, see [smooth representations of $p$-adic groups](/learn/representations/#smooth-representations-of-p-adic-groups), [the Jacquet module](/learn/representations/#parabolic-induction-and-the-jacquet-module) and [unramified representations](/learn/representations/#unramified-representations).

We keep the setting of [the previous section](/entry/elliptic-side-direct/): $E/F$ is a CM extension, $V$ a Hermitian space of rank $r$, $G = \mathrm{U}(V)$, and $K_v$ the stabilizer of the lattice at a finite place $v$. Let $G(\mathbb{A}_f)$ be the finite adelic group. A *type datum* is a finite set $S$ of split places with frames and irreducible representations $\tau_v$ of $\mathrm{GL}_r(k_v)$, as in that section.

**Definition 2.5.1 (Type vector).** Let $\sigma$ be a representation of $G(\mathbb{A}_f)$ on a complex vector space $W$. A *type vector* of $\sigma$ for the type datum is a nonzero $x \in W$ such that $x$ is fixed by $K_v$ for every finite $v \notin S$, and for every $v \in S$, $x$ lies in the span of the images of the $K_v$-maps from $\tau_v$ to $W$. Here $K_v$ acts on $\tau_v$ through the frame and reduction $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathcal{O}_v) \to \mathrm{GL}_r(k_v)$.

For a nonarchimedean local field $k$, let $B$ be the upper triangular Borel subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_r(k)$ with unipotent radical $N$, $T(\mathcal{O})$ the integral points of the diagonal torus, and $I$ the Iwahori subgroup. A diagonal element $t$ is *contracting* if $t N(\mathcal{O}) t^{-1} \subset N(\mathcal{O})$. For an admissible representation $\pi$, a multiset $\mathcal{E}_\pi$ of characters of the diagonal torus is a multiset of *exponents* of $\pi$ if it lists the characters of the torus on the $T(\mathcal{O})$-fixed part of the Jacquet module $\pi_N$, with multiplicity.

**Theorem 2.5.2 (Trace separation).** Assume the following two facts for every nonarchimedean local field $k$ with uniformizer $\varpi$, every $r$, and every Haar measure $\mu$ on $\mathrm{GL}_r(k)$.

1. *Iwahori trace formula.* Each $\mathrm{diag}(\varpi^{a_1}, \ldots, \varpi^{a_r})$ with $a_1 \ge \cdots \ge a_r$ is contracting. For every admissible $\pi$, the $T(\mathcal{O})$-fixed part of $\pi_N$ is finite-dimensional, $\pi$ has a multiset of exponents, and for every contracting $t$,
$$\mathrm{tr}\, \pi(\mathbf{1}_{I t I}) = \mu(I)\, \delta_B(t)^{-1} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal{E}_\pi} \chi(t).$$
2. *Separation of exponents.* Let $\pi_1, \ldots, \pi_n$ be irreducible admissible, pairwise inequivalent, each with a nonzero $I$-fixed vector, with exponents $\mathcal{E}_i$. If $c \in \mathbb{C}^n$ and $\sum_i c_i \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal{E}_i} \chi(t) = 0$ for every $t = \mathrm{diag}(\varpi^{a_1}, \ldots, \varpi^{a_r})$ with $a_1 > \cdots > a_r$, then $c = 0$.

Let $S$ be a type datum, $u \notin S$ a split place with a frame and a uniformizer, $\mu_v$ Haar measures with $\mu_v(K_v) = 1$ for almost all $v$, and $\nu$ a Haar measure on $G(\mathbb{A}_f)$ such that $\int \bigotimes_v f_v\, d\nu = \prod_v \int f_v\, d\mu_v$ for every family of smooth compactly supported $f_v$ with $f_v = \mathbf{1}_{K_v}$ for almost all $v$. Let $\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_n$ be irreducible admissible representations of $G(\mathbb{A}_f)$, pairwise inequivalent, and $a \in \mathbb{C}^n$. Assume $\sum_i a_i c_i = 0$ whenever $f_f$ satisfies condition 6 of [the test functions](/entry/elliptic-side-direct/), $f_v$ is bi-$K_v$-invariant for every $v \notin S \cup \{u\}$, and $c_i$ is the trace of $\sigma_i(f_f)$. Then $a_i = 0$ for every $i$ such that $\sigma_i$ has a type vector.

*Proof.* There is a function $e$ on $G(\mathbb{A}_f)$, built from the type idempotents $e_{\tau_v}$ at $v \in S$ and the unit functions $\mathbf{1}_{K_v}$ elsewhere, with the following properties. Each $P_i = \sigma_i(e)$ is an idempotent with finite-dimensional image, and $P_i \neq 0$ when $\sigma_i$ has a type vector. For every $g \in G(\mathbb{A}_f)$ there is a test function $f_f$ of the type, with $f_u$ bi-invariant under the Iwahori subgroup $I_u$, such that $\sigma_i(f_f)$ and $P_i\, \sigma_i(g)\, P_i$ have the same trace for every $i$.

Write each $\sigma_i$ as $\pi_i \otimes \sigma_i^u$, with $\pi_i$ an irreducible admissible representation of $G(F_u) \cong \mathrm{GL}_r(F_u)$. For a pure tensor $f_f = f_u \otimes f^u$, the trace of $\sigma_i(f_f)$ is $c'_i \cdot \mathrm{tr}\, \pi_i(f_u)$, where $c'_i$ depends only on $f^u$. Fix $f^u$ and replace $f_u$ by $\mathbf{1}_{I_u t I_u}$ with $t$ strictly dominant. Such a function still gives a test function of the type, so the hypothesis gives
$$\sum_i a_i c'_i\, \mathrm{tr}\, \pi_i(\mathbf{1}_{I_u t I_u}) = 0.$$
By the Iwahori trace formula this is $\mu(I_u)\, \delta_B(t)^{-1} \sum_i a_i c'_i \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal{E}_{\pi_i}} \chi(t)$. Group the indices $i$ by the class of $\pi_i$. The separation of exponents gives, for each class, that the sum of $a_i c'_i$ over the class is $0$. The trace of an Iwahori bi-invariant $f_u$ in $\pi_i$ depends only on the class of $\pi_i$, so $\sum_i a_i c'_i\, \mathrm{tr}\, \pi_i(f_u) = 0$ for every such $f_u$. Hence $\sum_i a_i\, \mathrm{tr}(P_i \sigma_i(g) P_i) = 0$ for every $g$. Since the $\sigma_i$ are irreducible and pairwise inequivalent, the operators $P_i \sigma_i(g) P_i$ on the finite-dimensional images of the $P_i$ are independent, by the density theorem. So $a_i = 0$ whenever $P_i \neq 0$, in particular whenever $\sigma_i$ has a type vector. $\square$

Assumption 1 is the theorem of Casselman on the Jacquet module and the Iwahori Hecke algebra. Assumption 2 is the linear independence of the characters of the torus on the strictly dominant cone, applied to the exponents of inequivalent Iwahori-spherical representations.

Next we fix the multiplicities. For a Hermitian space $V$ with discrete series models $M$ at the real places (a discrete series $D_\rho$ of $G(F_{w_\rho})$ with parameter $\lambda_\rho$ for each $\rho$) and a representation $\sigma$ of $G(\mathbb{A}_f)$, let $m(\sigma, M)$ be the dimension, as a cardinal, of the space of $G(\mathbb{A}_f)$-maps from $\sigma$ into the space of maps from $\bigotimes_\rho D_\rho$ to cuspidal automorphic forms on $G$, for the fixed infinitesimal character $\theta$. A *real datum* $R$ fixes, among other data, Hermitian spaces $Y$ and $W_1, \ldots, W_m$ over $E$, the character $\theta$, and discrete series models $M_W(i)$ on $W_i$. We say that $R$ has *equal multiplicities* if for each $i$, each discrete series model $M_0$ on $Y$, each matrix $j$ over the finite adeles and each isomorphism of topological groups $\Phi : G_Y(\mathbb{A}_f) \to G_{W_i}(\mathbb{A}_f)$ such that

1. $j^* J_{W_i}\, j = J_Y$,
2. $\Phi(g)\, j = j\, g$ for all $g$,
3. $j$ carries the frame of $Y$ to the frame of $W_i$ up to an integral matrix that is $1$ at a fixed finite set of places,

and each irreducible admissible $\sigma$ of $G_Y(\mathbb{A}_f)$ with a nonzero vector that is fixed by a level group and satisfies one Hecke relation, we have
$$m(\sigma, M_0) = m(\sigma \circ \Phi^{-1}, M_W(i)).$$

The *transfer of multiplicities* is the following statement. Let $V$ have odd rank $r$, let $\lambda_\rho$ satisfy $\lambda_{\rho, i} - \lambda_{\rho, i+1} \ge 100\, r^4$, let $M$ be discrete series models on $V$ with parameters $\lambda$, and let the type datum satisfy condition 3 of [the test functions](/entry/elliptic-side-direct/). Let $V'$ have rank $r$ with models $M'$ of the same parameters, let $j_v : G(F_v) \to G'(F_v)$ be isomorphisms induced at each finite place by isometries that carry lattice to lattice, and let $\Phi : G(\mathbb{A}_f) \to G'(\mathbb{A}_f)$ agree with the $j_v$. Then $m_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\sigma, M) = m_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\sigma \circ \Phi^{-1}, M')$ for every irreducible admissible $\sigma$ with a type vector, where $m_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ is the multiplicity of $\bigotimes_\rho D_\rho \otimes \sigma$ in the cuspidal spectrum.

**Theorem 2.5.3 (Multiplicities from the transfer).** Assume rapid decay of cusp forms, Harish-Chandra's finiteness theorem, the Dixmier and Malliavin factorisation, the statement of Green on cuspidal vectors, and the transfer of multiplicities. Then every real datum $R$ with a face $F$ for which the conditions of the datum hold has equal multiplicities.

*Proof.* Let $\sigma$ be good. The statement of Green gives a type datum that satisfies condition 3, and a type vector of $\sigma$ for it. The conditions of the datum give local isometries $j_v$ that carry lattice to lattice, an isomorphism $\Phi_0$ that agrees with them, and an inner automorphism that relates $\Phi_0$ to $\Phi$. They also give that $Y$ and $W_i$ have the same odd rank $r$, and that $M_0$ and $M_W(i)$ have the same parameters $\lambda$ with gaps at least $100\, r^4$. Rapid decay, finiteness and the factorisation show that $m(\sigma, M)$ equals $m_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\sigma, M)$ on both sides. The transfer of multiplicities gives equality for $\Phi_0$, and the inner automorphism does not change the multiplicity. $\square$

**Theorem 2.5.4 (Equality of cuspidal multiplicities).** Assume:

1. every discrete series of $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$ with $p + q$ odd and gaps at least $100\, (p+q)^4$ has a pseudo-coefficient with known orbital integrals;
2. rapid decay of cusp forms, Harish-Chandra's finiteness theorem, the Dixmier and Malliavin factorisation, and the statement of Green;
3. the spectral side: for a test function $f = f_\infty \otimes f_f$ of [the type of the elliptic side](/entry/elliptic-side-direct/) and a finite family $\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_n$ of pairwise inequivalent irreducible admissible representations that contains every $\sigma$ with $m_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\sigma, M) \neq 0$ and $\sigma(f_f) \neq 0$, $R(f)$ has trace $\sum_i m_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\sigma_i, M)\, \mathrm{tr}\, \sigma_i(f_f)$;
4. the conclusion of the Theorem on trace separation;
5. the conclusions of [the elliptic side](/entry/elliptic-side-direct/) and [the elliptic expansion](/entry/elliptic-part-x/).

Then every real datum $R$ with a face $F$ for which the conditions of the datum hold has equal multiplicities.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PsCo.Pc.multEq_of_sides`.

*Proof.* By the previous Theorem it suffices to prove the transfer of multiplicities. There are three steps.

*Archimedean terms.* Let $f_w$ be a pseudo-coefficient of $D_\rho$ from assumption 1. For each characteristic polynomial $P$ of [the elliptic expansion](/entry/elliptic-part-x/), the sum $\Phi_w(P)$ of the orbital integrals of $f_w$ is $0$ if $T_P(F_w)$ is noncompact. If it is compact, it is $\overline{\chi_{\lambda}(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r)} / \mathrm{vol}(T_P(F_w))$, where $\chi_\lambda$ is the character of the irreducible representation of $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{C})$ with highest weight $(\lambda_{\rho, k} - \tfrac{r-1}{2} + k - 1)_{1 \le k \le r}$ and the $\alpha_k$ are the roots of $P$ under $\rho$. Indeed, by condition 6 of [the pseudo-coefficients](/entry/local-pc-holds/) each local class contributes the conjugate of the discrete series character $\Theta_\lambda$, and the sum over the classes is the Weyl character. So by assumption 5, $\mathrm{tr}\, R(f)$ is a finite sum over $P$ whose archimedean factors depend only on $\lambda$.

*Equal traces.* Transport $f_f$ to $V'$ along the $j_v$, and take pseudo-coefficients on $V'$ for the same $\lambda$. The finite terms $\Phi_v(P)$ agree, because an isometry that carries lattice to lattice preserves the stable orbital integrals. The torus volumes depend only on $P$. So the two traces are equal.

*Separation.* Choose an auxiliary split place $u \notin S$. By assumption 2, the cuspidal spectrum with fixed archimedean type and bounded level is finite, so a finite family $\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_n$ covers the spectra of $V$ and $V'$ at the level of the test functions. Put $a_i = m_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\tau_i, M) - m_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\tau_i \circ \Phi^{-1}, M')$. The spectral side on both groups and the equality of traces give $\sum_i a_i\, \mathrm{tr}\, \tau_i(f_f) = 0$ for every finite test function of the type. Trace separation gives $a_i = 0$ for every $\tau_i$ with a type vector, and $\sigma$ is one of them. $\square$

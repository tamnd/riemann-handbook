## Eigenfunctions of tensor kernels

The spectral side of the twisted trace formula for $\mathrm{GL}_a \times \mathrm{GL}_b$ is probed with test functions that are products: archimedean multipliers of Section 02 at each archimedean place, times a matrix of finite adelic functions. If a vector-valued function on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{A}_E)$ is an eigenfunction of such a convolution operator, and of the operators obtained by changing the kernel at one place, we want to conclude that its slices are automorphic forms with a known infinitesimal character. This section proves that conclusion. The main point is that a kernel for the word $\Gamma_m$ acts as $\Gamma_m$ composed with the kernel for the empty word, so the ratio of two eigenvalues is a Gelfand eigenvalue. The result is used in [Section 7.16.7](/entry/block-candidates-of-inputs/), where limits of such eigenfunctions produce the candidate representations of a frame.

We keep the notation of Section 02. Let $E$ be a CM field, $H = \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{A}_E)$ and $H_f = \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_{E,f}) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{A}_{E,f})$; write $x_f$ for the finite part of $x \in H$ and $x_v$ for its component at an archimedean place $v$. For $y \in \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$, an archimedean $v$ and a function $\varphi$ on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$, let $\varphi_{y,v}(x) = \varphi(y\,\iota_v(x))$ for $x \in \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{C})$, where $\iota_v(x)$ has component $x$ at $v$ and $1$ elsewhere. A parameter $\chi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{C})$ is a pair $(\chi^+, \chi^-)$ of vectors in $\mathbb{C}^a$, as in Section 02, and $\gamma_m(\chi^\pm)$ is the scalar by which $\Gamma_m$ (for $+$) or $\bar\Gamma_m$ (for $-$) acts on eigenfunctions with parameter $\chi$.

**Definition 7.14.1 (Fundamental domain datum).** A fundamental domain datum for $(a, b)$ is a Haar measure $\mu$ on $H$ together with a measurable fundamental domain $\mathcal{F}$ for the left action of $\mathrm{GL}_a(E) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E)$. Haar measures $\mu_a$ on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{C})$, $\mu_b$ on $\mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{C})$ and $\nu$ on $H_f$ are compatible with $\mu$ if
$$\int_H\prod_vf_{a,v}(x_{1,v})\prod_vf_{b,v}(x_{2,v})\,\psi(x_f)\,d\mu(x) = \prod_v\int f_{a,v}\,d\mu_a\cdot\prod_v\int f_{b,v}\,d\mu_b\cdot\int\psi\,d\nu$$
for all continuous compactly supported $f_{a,v}$, $f_{b,v}$ and $\psi$.

**Definition 7.14.2 (Tensor kernel).** For each archimedean $v$ let $(V_{a,v}, \tau_{a,v})$ be a unitary representation of $U(a)$ with an orthonormal basis $B_{a,v}$, and $k_{a,v} : \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{C}) \to \mathrm{End}(V_{a,v})$; define $V_{b,v}$, $B_{b,v}$ and $k_{b,v}$ in the same way. Let $\kappa$ be a finite set and $\psi = (\psi_{xy})_{x,y \in \kappa}$ a matrix of continuous compactly supported functions on $H_f$. Put $I = \prod_vB_{a,v} \times \prod_vB_{b,v} \times \kappa$. The tensor kernel is the $I \times I$ matrix of functions on $H$
$$\Phi_{ij}(x) = \prod_vk_{a,v}(x_{1,v})_{i_1j_1}\cdot\prod_vk_{b,v}(x_{2,v})_{i_2j_2}\cdot\psi_{i_3j_3}(x_f).$$
For a family $f = (f_i)_{i \in I}$ of functions on $H$ put
$$(\Phi * f)_i(x) = \sum_{j \in I}\int_H\Phi_{ij}(y)f_j(xy)\,d\mu(y).$$

A family $f$ is an eigenfunction of $\Phi$ if $\Phi * f = cf$ for some $c \ne 0$. For each $x_2$, the slice $x_1 \mapsto f_i(x_1, x_2)$ is a function on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$, and similarly for slices in the second variable. Changing one entry of the kernel family changes $\Phi$: we write $\Phi[k_{a,v} \to k']$ for the tensor kernel with $k_{a,v}$ replaced by $k'$.

**Theorem 7.14.3 (Gelfand eigenvalues of slices).** Assume that every section of a bundle $C_V$ over $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ is the initial value of a wave. Let $\mu$ be a fundamental domain datum and $\mu_a$, $\mu_b$, $\nu$ compatible Haar measures. Let $k_a$, $k_b$ be families of multipliers of radius $T$, $\psi$ as above, and $g$ a bump of radius $T$. Let $f$ be a family of continuous functions whose slices in both variables are smooth along the archimedean places, with $\Phi * f = cf$ and $c \ne 0$. Let $v$ be archimedean, $m \ge 1$, and let $\mathcal{D}$ be $\Gamma_m$ or $\bar\Gamma_m$. Assume that $k_{a,v}$ is a kernel for (the empty word, $g$), that $k'$ is a kernel for $(\mathcal{D}, g)$, and that $\Phi[k_{a,v} \to k'] * f = c'f$. Then for every $i \in I$, $y \in \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$ and $x_2$, the function $\varphi = f_i(\cdot, x_2)$ satisfies
$$\mathcal{D}\varphi_{y,v} = \frac{c'}{c}\,\varphi_{y,v}$$
on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{C})$. The same holds for the second variable, with $k_{b,v}$ in place of $k_{a,v}$.

*Proof.* By compatibility (Fubini), the integral over $H$ in $\Phi * f$ splits into an integral over $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{C})$ at the place $v$ and an integral over the other components. Fix the other components of $y$, and the indices other than the $V_{a,v}$-index. The inner integral is then
$$\int_{\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{C})}k_{a,v}(z)\,F(xz)\,d\mu_a(z),$$
where $F$ is the vector of the slices $f_j$ along $v$, a smooth function with values in $V_{a,v}$. Since $k_{a,v}$ is right equivariant under $U(a)$, only the component of $F$ in $C_{V_{a,v}}$ contributes, and that component is the initial value of a wave $u$. The defining property of a kernel gives
$$\int k_{a,v}(z)u(0, xz)\,dz = \Big(\int g(t)u(t)\,dt\Big)(x), \qquad \int k'(z)u(0, xz)\,dz = \mathcal{D}\Big(\int g(t)u(t)\,dt\Big)(x).$$
So the convolution with $k'$ is $\mathcal{D}$ applied to the convolution with $k_{a,v}$. Integrating over the remaining variables, which is allowed because the tensor kernel is a test matrix (continuous with compact support in the relevant sense), and using that $\mathcal{D}$ commutes with left translations and with the remaining integrals, we get $\Phi[k_{a,v} \to k'] * f = \mathcal{D}_v(\Phi * f)$, where $\mathcal{D}_v$ acts along $v$. Hence $c'f = \mathcal{D}_v(cf)$, and dividing by $c$ gives the claim. The Fubini statement and the test properties of $\Phi$ (that it is a test matrix and that its adjoint is the tensor kernel of the adjoints) are taken from the companion development. $\square$

**Theorem 7.14.4 (Automorphy of eigenfunctions).** Let $E$ be a CM field, $a, b \ge 0$, and for each archimedean $v$ let $V_{a,v}$, $V_{b,v}$ be unitary representations of $U(a)$ and $U(b)$. There are finite-dimensional spaces $W_1$ of functions on $\prod_vU(a)$ and $W_2$ on $\prod_vU(b)$ with the following property. Let $\mu$ be a fundamental domain datum with compatible $\mu_a$, $\mu_b$, $\nu$; let $k_a$, $k_b$ be families of multipliers of radius $T$; $\psi$ as above; $g$ a bump of radius $T$, with $k_{a,v}$ and $k_{b,v}$ kernels for (the empty word, $g$) at every $v$. For each $v$, each $m$ and each $\mathcal{D} \in \{\Gamma_m, \bar\Gamma_m\}$ let $k'_{a,v,\mathcal{D}}$ and $k'_{b,v,\mathcal{D}}$ be kernels for $(\mathcal{D}, g)$. Let $K_1$, $K_2$ be open subgroups of the finite adelic groups such that every $\psi_{xy}$ is left invariant under $K_1 \times K_2$. Let $M \ge 0$ and let $f$ be a nonzero family of continuous functions on $H$ such that:

1. each $f_i$ is left invariant under $\mathrm{GL}_a(E) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E)$;
2. $\int_{\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{wt}(x)^{-2M}|f_i(x)|^2\,d\mu(x) < \infty$ for each $i$, where $\mathrm{wt}$ is the weight function of the fundamental domain;
3. $\Phi * f = cf$ with $c \ne 0$;
4. $\Phi[k_{a,v} \to k'_{a,v,\mathcal{D}}] * f = c_a(v, \mathcal{D})f$ and $\Phi[k_{b,v} \to k'_{b,v,\mathcal{D}}] * f = c_b(v, \mathcal{D})f$ for all $v$ and $\mathcal{D}$.

Then there are parameters $\chi_a(v)$ of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{C})$ and $\chi_b(v)$ of $\mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{C})$ such that:

1. $\gamma_m(\chi_a(v)^\pm) = c_a(v, \mathcal{D})/c$ for $\mathcal{D} = \Gamma_m$ (sign $+$) and $\mathcal{D} = \bar\Gamma_m$ (sign $-$), and the same for $b$;
2. for every $i$, $y$ and $v$, the slices along $v$ of $f_i$ in the first and second variable are eigenfunctions with parameters $\chi_a(v)$ and $\chi_b(v)$;
3. every $f_i$ is right invariant under $K_1 \times K_2$, and its restriction to the archimedean maximal compact subgroup, after any left translation, lies in the span of the products $w_1 \otimes w_2$ with $w_i \in W_i$;
4. the slices of the $f_i$ are smooth functions, and they are automorphic forms;
5. for every embedding $\tau : E \to \mathbb{C}$, the span of the slices in the first variable has infinitesimal character $\chi_a(v)^+$ at $\tau$ if $\tau$ is the chosen embedding of its place $v$, and $\chi_a(v)^-$ otherwise; and the same for the second variable.

*Proof.* Take $W_1$ and $W_2$ spanned by the matrix coefficients of the $V_{a,v}$ and $V_{b,v}$; then every $f_i$ has type $(K_1, K_2, W_1, W_2)$, because the $\psi_{xy}$ are left invariant under $K_1 \times K_2$ and the archimedean kernels are equivariant, so that $f = c^{-1}\Phi * f$ inherits these invariances. The same identity, with a smooth compactly supported kernel, shows that the slices are smooth, and that $f$ has moderate growth: $|f_i(x)| \le Ce^{N(\sigma(x_1) + \sigma(x_2))}$ for the size function $\sigma$, by the square integrability in (2) and the basic properties of $\sigma$ (bounded on compact sets, nonnegative, invariant under the involution, subadditive up to a constant). Since $f \ne 0$, choose $i_0$ and $x^0$ with $f_{i_0}(x^0) \ne 0$. By [the Gelfand eigenvalues of slices](/entry/gel-of-of/), for each $v$ the slice $\varphi = f_{i_0}(\cdot, x^0_2)$ satisfies $\mathcal{D}\varphi_{y,v} = (c_a(v, \mathcal{D})/c)\varphi_{y,v}$ for every $y$ and every Gelfand operator $\mathcal{D}$. A nonzero joint eigenfunction of all the $\Gamma_m$ and $\bar\Gamma_m$ has a parameter: there is $\chi_a(v)$ whose values $\gamma_m(\chi_a(v)^\pm)$ are the eigenvalues. This gives (1), and then the theorem on Gelfand eigenvalues, applied to every $i$, gives (2). Finally, a family of functions that is left invariant under the rational points, of a fixed finite type under $K_1 \times K_2$ and the archimedean compact group, of moderate growth, and whose slices are eigenfunctions of all Gelfand operators, consists of automorphic forms; the span of the slices has the infinitesimal character given by the parameters, because a function on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$ is an eigenfunction with parameter $\chi$ along $v$ if and only if it has infinitesimal character $\chi^+$ at the chosen embedding of $v$ and $\chi^-$ at its conjugate. This gives (3) to (5). The existence of parameters, the automorphy criterion, the type, smoothness and growth statements, and the comparison of the two notions of infinitesimal character are taken from the companion development; the existence of waves is part 1 of [the theorem on waves and kernels](/entry/wave-existence-speed-multiplicity/). $\square$

**Remark 7.14.5 (Formalization).** The statements are `GelOf` (HeckeFubiniCompatibility/CompatFub.lean:109) and `Pack` (PackOf.lean:21). `gelOf` (GelOf.lean:15) passes its inputs to `Slice.gelA` and `Slice.gelB`, which we did not open; the proof above follows the inputs it receives (`WaveEx`, `CompatFub` at CompatFub.lean:23, and `TensTest` at line 34). `gelOf_of` (PackOfC.lean:9) supplies `compatFub` and `tensTest`. `pack_of` (PackOf.lean) combines seven inputs, `TensTest`, `TypeOf`, `SmoothOf`, `GrowthOf`, `GelOf`, `InfEx` and `AutOf` (CompatFub.lean:145); `pack_of_gel` (SmoothOfHolds.lean:27) supplies all but `GelOf` from `SizeBasic` (IsotropicMahlerBounds/OnlyIsotropic.lean:45) and `Bridge.BridgeSt` (InfinitesimalCharacterGL/BridgeSt.lean:9). `pack_holds` (GelOfHolds.lean:6) is `pack_of_C` with part 1 of the wave theorem and `Bridge.bridge`. In `Pack` the spaces $W_1$, $W_2$ are chosen before the data and depend only on the representations; we did not unfold `TypeOf`, so the description of $W_1$, $W_2$ in the proof is the natural one, not a checked one. The eigenvalue hypothesis of `GelOf` is stated for all kernels $k'$ for the word at once, and the slice functions $\varphi_{y,v}$ are `along` (EopW.lean:23), which is $0$ off the invertible matrices. In `BridgeSt` the field is assumed totally complex. The weight in (2) is `WL2` (EigenvalueSeparation/LeftInv.lean:61).

## Highest weight vectors and multiplicity one

This section studies a module for the complexified Lie algebra of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$, viewed as a real group, together with an involution that swaps the two copies of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$. Such a module is the archimedean component of a representation that is invariant under the Galois twist, and the twisted trace of the involution is the archimedean term of the [twisted trace formula](/learn/trace-formula/#the-twisted-trace-formula-and-base-change). We prove two facts. First, the twisted trace on the space of maps from a fixed $U(n)$-type is $2^n$ times a sum of traces on spaces of highest weight vectors. Second, at most one of these spaces is nonzero, and it has dimension at most $1$. Together they give [the twisted trace on a K-type](/entry/core-g/): the trace is $\pm 2^n$. That result is used in the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/). The background is in [representations of real groups](/learn/representations/#representations-of-real-groups) and [highest weights](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching).

Throughout, $n \ge 1$, the indices $a, b, c, d$ run over $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, and $\rho_a = \tfrac{n-1}{2} - a$.

**Definition 7.1.1 (Pair of commuting $\mathfrak{gl}_n$-actions).** Let $V$ be a complex inner product space. A pair on $V$ is two families $E^1_{ab}$ and $E^2_{ab}$ of linear operators on $V$ such that each family satisfies the commutation relations of the matrix units of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$, every $E^1_{ab}$ commutes with every $E^2_{cd}$, and the operators $K_{ab} = E^1_{ab} + E^2_{ab}$ and $P_{ab} = E^1_{ab} - E^2_{ab}$ satisfy $K_{ab}^* = K_{ba}$ and $P_{ab}^* = -P_{ba}$.

The complexified Lie algebra of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ is $\mathfrak{gl}_n \oplus \mathfrak{gl}_n$, and the two families are its two factors. The $K_{ab}$ span the complexified Lie algebra of $U(n)$, and the adjoint conditions say that the real form $\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{C})$ acts by skew-adjoint operators. We write $\mathcal{A}$ for the algebra generated by all $E^1_{ab}$ and $E^2_{ab}$.

**Definition 7.1.2 (Gelfand elements).** For a family $E = (E_{ab})$ of operators, let $E^m$ be the $m$-th power of the matrix $(E_{ab})$, with entries in the ring of operators, and put $\Gamma_m(E) = \sum_a (E^m)_{aa}$. Put $\bar E^2_{ab} = -E^2_{ba}$.

The $\Gamma_m(E^1)$ and $\Gamma_m(\bar E^2)$ generate the centre of the enveloping algebra of each factor. On an irreducible module they act by scalars, and these scalars are the infinitesimal character.

**Definition 7.1.3 (Core module).** Let $\chi = (\chi^+, \chi^-)$ be a pair of vectors in $\mathbb{C}^n$. A core module for $\chi$ is a complex inner product space $V$ with a pair $E^1, E^2$, a homomorphism $\sigma : U(n) \to \mathrm{End}(V)$ and an operator $A$ such that:

1. $\Gamma_m(E^1)$ acts by the scalar $\gamma_m(\chi^+)$ and $\Gamma_m(\bar E^2)$ acts by $\gamma_m(\chi^-)$, for each $m \ge 0$;
2. $V \neq 0$, and for $v \neq 0$ and every $w$ there is $x \in \mathcal{A}$ with $xv = w$;
3. $\sigma$ is unitary, each vector lies in a finite-dimensional subspace stable under $\sigma$ and the $K_{ab}$, and on such a subspace $\frac{d}{dt}\sigma(e^{tX})w|_{t=0} = \sum_{a,b} X_{ab} K_{ab} w$;
4. $A^2 = 1$, $A$ is an isometry, $A$ commutes with $\sigma$, and $A E^1_{ab} = E^2_{ab} A$.

Here $\gamma_m(\chi)$ is the scalar by which $\Gamma_m$ acts on the module of infinitesimal character $\chi$. Condition 2 is algebraic irreducibility. Condition 4 says that $A$ is the action of the Galois conjugation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$. For a non-increasing $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ we use $\chi_\lambda = (\lambda + \rho, -(\lambda + \rho))$.

**Definition 7.1.4 (Highest weight space).** For $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^n$ let $V_\mu$ be the space of $v \in V$ with $K_{aa}v = \mu_a v$ for every $a$ and $K_{ab}v = 0$ for $a < b$.

**Definition 7.1.5 (Levels and harmonic weights).** Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ be non-increasing. A level for $\lambda$ is a monotone map $\ell : \{0, \dots, n-1\} \to \{0, \dots, n-1\}$ with $\lambda_a = \lambda_b$ whenever $\ell(a) = \ell(b)$. Put
$$r_\ell(a) = \tfrac12\big(\#\{b : \ell(a) < \ell(b)\} - \#\{b : \ell(b) < \ell(a)\}\big), \qquad \mu_\ell = 2\lambda + 2r_\ell.$$
The set $H(\lambda)$ of harmonic weights is the set of all $\mu_\ell$.

A level cuts $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ into consecutive blocks on which $\lambda$ is constant. For the level with one block, $r_\ell = 0$ and $\mu_\ell = 2\lambda$. If $\lambda$ is strictly decreasing, every level is injective, $r_\ell = \rho$, and $H(\lambda) = \{2\lambda + 2\rho\}$.

Now let $E$ be a finite-dimensional unitary representation $\tau$ of $U(n)$ with an operator $T$ such that $T^2 = 1$, $T$ commutes with $\tau$, and for each diagonal $k = \mathrm{diag}(z)$ with the $z_a^2$ distinct,
$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}\tau(k) &= \prod_{a,b}(1 + z_a/z_b)\,\overline{s_\lambda(z)}^{\,2},\\
\operatorname{tr}\tau(k)T &= \prod_{a,b}(1 + z_a/z_b)\,\overline{s_\lambda(z^2)},
\end{aligned}$$
where $s_\lambda$ is the Weyl character of highest weight $\lambda$. These are the traces of a Lefschetz pair attached to $\lambda$. Let $\mathrm{Hom}_K(E^\vee, V)$ be the space of linear maps $t$ from the dual of $E$ to $V$ with $t(\xi \circ \tau(k)^{-1}) = \sigma(k)t(\xi)$. The operator $\Phi(t) = A \circ t \circ T^\vee$ acts on it. We write $\operatorname{tr}(B \mid S)$ for the trace of $B$ on a subspace $S$ that $B$ preserves.

**Theorem 7.1.6 (Counting).** Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ be non-increasing, let $(E, \tau, T)$ be as above, and let $V$ be a core module for $\chi_\lambda$ with $\mathrm{Hom}_K(E^\vee, V)$ finite-dimensional. Then:

1. for each $\mu \in H(\lambda)$ the space $V_\mu$ is finite-dimensional and stable under $A$;
2. if $\mathrm{Hom}_K(E^\vee, V) \neq 0$, then $V_\mu \neq 0$ for some $\mu \in H(\lambda)$;
3. $\operatorname{tr}(\Phi \mid \mathrm{Hom}_K(E^\vee, V)) = 2^n \sum_{\mu \in H(\lambda)} \operatorname{tr}(A \mid V_\mu)$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgBcC.RealD.countG_holds` (also `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.RealCountG.countG`, a second name).

*Proof.* The weights of $E^\vee$ are dominated by the harmonic weights, and its highest weight vectors live in the weights $\mu \in H(\lambda)$. Let $W_\mu \subset E^\vee$ be the space of highest weight vectors of weight $\mu$ for the dual action. A $K$-map $t$ intertwines the infinitesimal actions, so it sends $W_\mu$ into $V_\mu$. Restriction to the $W_\mu$ gives a map $\mathrm{Hom}_K(E^\vee, V) \to \bigoplus_{\mu \in H(\lambda)} \mathrm{Hom}(W_\mu, V_\mu)$. It is injective, and it is surjective because each map on highest weight vectors extends to a $K$-map. Since $A$ preserves the conditions defining $V_\mu$ (it swaps $E^1$ and $E^2$, so it fixes $K_{ab}$), and $T^\vee$ preserves each $W_\mu$, the operator $\Phi$ becomes $\bigoplus_\mu (y \mapsto A y T^\vee)$. The trace of $y \mapsto A y T^\vee$ on $\mathrm{Hom}(W_\mu, V_\mu)$ is $\operatorname{tr}(A \mid V_\mu)\operatorname{tr}(T^\vee \mid W_\mu)$. The two trace identities for $\tau(k)$ and $\tau(k)T$, expanded in Weyl characters, give $\operatorname{tr}(T^\vee \mid W_\mu) = 2^n$ for each $\mu \in H(\lambda)$. This gives 3. Part 1 follows from the injection and the finite dimension, and part 2 from it as well. $\square$

The second result needs a finer class of modules, where the group action and $A$ are dropped and a condition on weights is added.

**Definition 7.1.7 (Weight of second type).** Let $\ell$ be a level for $\lambda$. For a finite set $B$ of pairs $(p_1, p_2)$ let $w_B(c) = \#\{p \in B : p_1 = c\} - \#\{p \in B : p_2 = c\}$. A weight $\nu$ is of second type for $\ell$ if there is a nonempty $B$ with $\ell(p_1) < \ell(p_2)$ for each $p \in B$, such that $\nu + \rho$ is strictly decreasing and $\nu + \rho = (\mu_\ell + \rho - w_B) \circ w$ for a permutation $w$.

**Definition 7.1.8 (Admissible module for a level).** Let $\ell$ be a level for $\lambda$. A module $V$ is admissible for $\ell$ if it is a complex inner product space with a pair $E^1, E^2$ whose Gelfand elements act by the scalars of $\chi_\lambda$, such that each vector lies in a finite-dimensional subspace stable under the $K_{ab}$, and $V_\nu = 0$ for every weight $\nu$ of second type for $\ell$.

**Theorem 7.1.9 (Multiplicity formula).** Let $\lambda$ be non-increasing, let $\ell$ be a level for $\lambda$, and let $V$ be admissible for $\ell$. Let $v, w \in V_{\mu_\ell}$ with $v \neq 0$ and $w = xv$ for some $x \in \mathcal{A}$. Then $w = cv$ for some $c \in \mathbb{C}$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgBcC.RealD.multiplicity_formula_holds`.

The proof is homological. Call $K_{ab}$ and $P_{ab}$ with $a < b$ letters, and say that a letter lies in $\mathfrak{u}_\ell$ if $\ell(a) < \ell(b)$. So $\mathfrak{u}_\ell$ is the nilradical of the parabolic subalgebra cut out by the blocks of $\ell$. Let $C(V)$ be the space of $V$-valued functions on finite sets of letters, with a boundary map $\partial$ of Chevalley-Eilenberg type. Let $\omega_U$ be the set of $K$-letters in $\mathfrak{u}_\ell$, and let $B \subset C(V)$ be the image under $\partial$ of the chains of degree $|\omega_U| + 1$ on letters in $\mathfrak{u}_\ell$. For $u \in V$ write $u \otimes \omega_U$ for the chain with value $u$ at $\omega_U$.

**Lemma 7.1.10 (Injectivity and action).** Let $V$ be admissible for $\ell$. Then:

1. if $u \in V_{\mu_\ell}$ and $u \otimes \omega_U \in B$, then $u = 0$;
2. each word in the $E^i_{ab}$ that commutes with all $K_{cd}$ is congruent, modulo $\mathfrak{u}_\ell$, to a word in the Levi letters that commutes with the Levi $K_{cd}$;
3. if $a$ is a word that commutes with all $K_{cd}$, $b$ is a word in the Levi letters congruent to $a$ modulo $\mathfrak{u}_\ell$, and $v \in V_{\mu_\ell}$, then $(av) \otimes \omega_U - b \cdot (v \otimes \omega_U) \in B$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgBcC.RealD.injective_decomposition_action_holds`.

*Proof.* Part 3 holds because the $K$-letters above the diagonal kill $v$, so the letters of $\mathfrak{u}_\ell$ in $a - b$ act on $v \otimes \omega_U$ through boundaries. Part 2 is the Harish-Chandra decomposition of the commutant relative to the Levi factor. Part 1 comes from $\partial^2 = 0$ and the exactness of the $K$-part of $\partial$ in the weight $\mu_\ell$: a boundary that is concentrated at $\omega_U$ with a highest weight value must vanish. These three inputs are proved in the companion development and we take them as given. $\square$

On the quotient $C(V)/B$ the two Levi families act, and their sum acts on the class $\bar c$ of $v \otimes \omega_U$ by known scalars. The Capelli classification is the statement that the Capelli products of the Levi families act on $\bar c$ by explicit polynomials in $\lambda$. For the Capelli identity and its role in multiplicity-free actions, see R. Howe and T. Umeda, *The Capelli identity, the double commutant theorem, and multiplicity-free actions*, Math. Ann. 290 (1991).

**Proposition 7.1.11.** If the Capelli classification holds, then the conclusion of the multiplicity formula holds for every admissible module.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgBcC.RealD.multFor_of_capCls`.

*Proof.* Let $v, w$ be as in the multiplicity formula. Since $v$ and $w$ are both of weight $\mu_\ell$ and are highest weight vectors, $w = av$ for a word $a$ that commutes with all $K_{cd}$. By part 2 of the Lemma, $a$ is congruent modulo $\mathfrak{u}_\ell$ to a Levi word $b$. By part 3, $w \otimes \omega_U \equiv b \cdot (v \otimes \omega_U)$ modulo $B$. On $C(V)/B$ the Levi words that commute with the Levi $K_{cd}$ are words in the Capelli elements, on vectors of this kind. By the Capelli classification each Capelli element acts on $\bar c$ by a scalar, so $b\bar c = z\bar c$ for some $z \in \mathbb{C}$. Hence $(w - zv) \otimes \omega_U \in B$, and part 1 of the Lemma gives $w = zv$. $\square$

*Proof of the multiplicity formula.* The Capelli classification is deduced from part 3 of the Lemma, which describes the action of words on the class $\bar c$. So this is the Proposition applied to that classification. $\square$

**Theorem 7.1.12 (Multiplicity one).** Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ be non-increasing and let $V$ be a core module for $\chi_\lambda$. Then there is $\mu_0 \in H(\lambda)$ such that $V_\mu = 0$ for every $\mu \in H(\lambda)$ with $\mu \neq \mu_0$, and $\dim V_{\mu_0} \le 1$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgBcC.RealD.multG_of_multFor`.

*Proof.* If $V_\mu = 0$ for all $\mu \in H(\lambda)$, any $\mu_0$ works. Otherwise choose a level $\ell_0$ with $V_{\mu_{\ell_0}} \neq 0$ and with $|\mu_{\ell_0} + \rho|^2$ least among all such levels, and put $\mu_0 = \mu_{\ell_0}$. A compression argument shows that the minimum over $H(\lambda)$ is also the minimum over all levels. Then the minimality shows that $V$ has no highest weight vectors of second type for $\ell_0$. So $V$ is admissible for $\ell_0$.

Other weights vanish. Let $\mu \in H(\lambda)$, $\mu \neq \mu_0$, and suppose $0 \neq x \in V_\mu$. Take $0 \neq v \in V_{\mu_0}$. By irreducibility $x = yv$ for some $y \in \mathcal{A}$. A model module for $\ell_0$ exists and has no weights of second type. In it, the $K$-types reached from the highest weight vector have weights in $\mu_0$ plus the cone spanned by the $e_a - e_b$ with $\ell_0(a) < \ell_0(b)$. So $\mu - \mu_0$ lies in this cone. A further statement about this cone, for harmonic weights, then forces $\mu = \mu_0$, a contradiction.

A line. Let $v, w \in V_{\mu_0}$ with $v \neq 0$. Irreducibility gives $x \in \mathcal{A}$ with $xv = w$, and the [multiplicity formula](/entry/multiplicity-formula-holds/) for $\ell_0$ gives $w \in \mathbb{C}v$. So $\dim V_{\mu_0} \le 1$. $\square$

**Corollary 7.1.13.** In the setting of the Counting theorem, if $\mathrm{Hom}_K(E^\vee, V) \neq 0$, then $\operatorname{tr}(\Phi \mid \mathrm{Hom}_K(E^\vee, V)) = \pm 2^n$.

*Proof.* By part 2 of the Counting theorem some $V_\mu$ is nonzero, and by Multiplicity one it is a line and all others vanish. The sum in part 3 has one term, $\operatorname{tr}(A \mid V_{\mu_0})$. Since $A^2 = 1$, this trace on a line is $\pm 1$. This is [the twisted trace on a K-type](/entry/core-g/). $\square$

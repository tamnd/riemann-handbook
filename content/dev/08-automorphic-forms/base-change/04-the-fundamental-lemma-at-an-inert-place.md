## The fundamental lemma at an inert place

The comparison of the trace formula of a unitary group $U(n)$ with the twisted trace formula of $\mathrm{GL}_a \times \mathrm{GL}_b$ needs, at each finite place, pairs of test functions with the same orbital integrals. This section defines this matching at a place $w$ that is inert in the CM extension and proves two cases. The first is the fundamental lemma for the unit elements of the Hecke algebras. The second matches the characteristic function of one Iwahori double coset on $U(n)$ with an explicit combination of Iwahori double cosets on $\mathrm{GL}_a \times \mathrm{GL}_b$. These are the local inputs at inert places for [the matching at the places of a frame](/entry/positivity-theory-rg-bc-b-f-match-all-match-lmb2/), which serves the step [BaseChangeN](/step/base-change-n/). The background is in [the twisted trace formula and base change](/learn/trace-formula/#the-twisted-trace-formula-and-base-change) and [unitary groups](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups).

Throughout, $E$ is a CM field with maximal totally real subfield $F$, and every finite place of $E$ is unramified over $F$. Let $w$ be a finite place of $E$ that is not split over $F$, and let $x \mapsto \bar x$ be the conjugation of $E_w$ over $F_v$. Let $q$ be the order of the residue field of $F_v$, so that the residue field of $E_w$ has $q^2$ elements, and let $|\cdot|_w$ and $v$ be the normalized absolute value and valuation of $E_w$. For $m \ge 1$ let $K_m = \mathrm{GL}_m(\mathcal{O}_w)$, let $\theta$ be the involution of $\mathrm{GL}_m(E_w)$ whose fixed points form the unitary group, and let $N(y) = y\theta(y)$ be the norm. Let
$$U = \{g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E_w) : \bar g^{\mathsf T}g = 1\}, \qquad K_U = U \cap K_n.$$

**Definition 7.4.1 (Twisted orbital integral).** Let $H$ be a locally compact group with an automorphism $\theta$ and a compact open subgroup $K$. For $\delta \in H$ let $H_\delta = \{g : g^{-1}\delta\theta(g) = \delta\}$ be its twisted centralizer and $C_\delta$ the subgroup of $H_\delta$ generated by its compact elements. For $f$ on $H$ put
$$O^\theta_\delta(f) = \sum_{g \in H_\delta\backslash H/K}[C_\delta : C_\delta \cap gKg^{-1}]\;\mathrm{avg}_{k \in K}\,f\big((gk)^{-1}\delta\,\theta(gk)\big).$$

This is the integral of $f(h^{-1}\delta\theta(h))$ over $H_\delta\backslash H$ for the Haar measures that give $K$ and $C_\delta$ volume $1$, written as a sum over double cosets. For $\theta = 1$ it is the ordinary orbital integral $O_\delta(f)$.

**Definition 7.4.2 (Stable and $\kappa$-orbital integrals).** Let $a + b = n$ and let $Q_1, Q_2 \in E_w[X]$ be monic of degrees $a$ and $b$. For $\varphi$ on $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_w) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E_w)$, with $\theta$ acting on both factors and $K = K_a \times K_b$, put
$$SO_{Q_1,Q_2}(\varphi) = \sum_{\delta} O^\theta_\delta(\varphi),$$
the sum over the $\theta$-twisted conjugacy classes of $\delta = (\delta_1, \delta_2)$ such that $N(\delta_1)$ has characteristic polynomial $Q_1$ and $N(\delta_2)$ has characteristic polynomial $Q_2$. For $f$ on $U$ put
$$\kappa O_{Q_1,Q_2}(f) = \sum_\gamma \kappa(\gamma)\,O_\gamma(f),$$
the sum over the conjugacy classes of $\gamma \in U$ with characteristic polynomial $Q_1Q_2$, with $K = K_U$. Here $\kappa(\gamma) = (-1)^{v(d)}$, where $d$ is the determinant of the Gram matrix of the form $\bar x^{\mathsf T}y$ on a basis of $\ker Q_1(\gamma)$.

The sign $\kappa$ is the endoscopic character for the endoscopic group $U(a) \times U(b)$ of $U(n)$: it records whether the restriction of the Hermitian form to the $Q_1$-part has even or odd discriminant valuation.

**Definition 7.4.3 (Matching).** A monic $Q$ is conjugate self-reciprocal if $\overline{Q(0)}\,Q = \widetilde{\bar Q}$, where $\bar Q$ has conjugated coefficients and $\widetilde{P}$ is the reverse polynomial. Let $R = \mathrm{Res}(Q_1, Q_2)$ and put
$$\Delta(Q_1, Q_2) = (-1)^{v(R)}\,|R|_w^{1/2}.$$
Functions $f$ on $U$ and $\varphi$ on $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_w) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E_w)$ match if
$$SO_{Q_1,Q_2}(\varphi) = \Delta(Q_1, Q_2)\,\kappa O_{Q_1,Q_2}(f)$$
for all monic conjugate self-reciprocal $Q_1, Q_2$ of degrees $a, b$ with $Q_1Q_2$ separable.

The characteristic polynomial of an element of $U$ is conjugate self-reciprocal, since the eigenvalues of a unitary matrix are stable under $z \mapsto \bar z^{-1}$. So the condition runs over the regular semisimple stable classes that come from $U(a) \times U(b)$. The factor $\Delta$ is the transfer factor for the composite of endoscopic transfer from $U(n)$ to $U(a) \times U(b)$ and base change to $\mathrm{GL}_a \times \mathrm{GL}_b$.

**Theorem 7.4.4 (Fundamental lemma for the unit).** Let $a + b = n$. Then the characteristic function of $K_U$ on $U$ and the characteristic function of $K_a \times K_b$ match.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Bc.ArchbcB.B.m1` (also `PositivityTheory.Rg.Bc.ArchbcB.B.archimedean_base_change_first`, a second name).

*Proof.* Fix $Q_1, Q_2$ as in the definition. Since $w$ is inert, $E_w/F_v$ is an unramified quadratic extension with residue order $q$, and $|x|_w = q^{-2v(x)}$. The characteristic function of $K_a \times K_b$ is a product, and its stable twisted orbital integral at $(Q_1, Q_2)$ is the product of the stable twisted orbital integrals of $\mathbf{1}_{K_a}$ at $Q_1$ and of $\mathbf{1}_{K_b}$ at $Q_2$. Each of these is a weighted count of self-dual lattices in a Hermitian space over the étale algebra $E_w[X]/(Q_i)$. On the unitary side, $\kappa O_{Q_1,Q_2}(\mathbf{1}_{K_U})$ is a signed count of self-dual lattices over $E_w[X]/(Q_1Q_2)$. The [unit lemma](/entry/unit-lemma-lie-and-group/) for the product algebra gives
$$SO_{Q_1,Q_2}(\mathbf{1}_{K_a \times K_b}) = (-q)^{-v(R)}\,\kappa O_{Q_1,Q_2}(\mathbf{1}_{K_U}).$$
Finally $\Delta(Q_1, Q_2) = (-1)^{v(R)}(q^{-2v(R)})^{1/2} = (-q)^{-v(R)}$. $\square$

The fundamental lemma for unitary groups, for the endoscopic transfer, is a theorem of G. Laumon and B. C. Ngô, *Le lemme fondamental pour les groupes unitaires*, Ann. of Math. 168 (2008). The base change fundamental lemma for the unit is due to R. Kottwitz, *Base change for unit elements of Hecke algebras*, Compositio Math. 60 (1986). The theorem here is the composite, proved directly by counting lattices.

We now turn to Iwahori double cosets. Let $m = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ and let $I_0 \subset K_n$ be the standard Iwahori subgroup. Fix $b \in K_n$ with $\bar b^{\mathsf T}b = J$, the antidiagonal matrix of ones, and a uniformizer $\varpi$ of $E_w$ with $\bar\varpi = \varpi$. Put $I_U = U \cap bI_0b^{-1}$, and for $c \ge 1$ let $I_c \subset K_c$ be the standard Iwahori subgroup. For $z \in \mathbb{Z}^c$ write $t(z) = \mathrm{diag}(\varpi^{z_1}, \dots, \varpi^{z_c})$.

Let $x \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ be strictly decreasing and put $l_i = x_i - x_{n+1-i}$ for $1 \le i \le m$. Assume that $l$ is a cone point: $l_i \ge 2$ and $l_i - l_{i+1} \ge 2$ for all $i$, with $l_{m+1} = 0$. Let $f_U$ be the characteristic function of
$$I_U\,b\,t(l_1, \dots, l_m, [0], -l_m, \dots, -l_1)\,b^{-1}\,I_U$$
in $U$, with the middle $0$ present only for $n$ odd.

**Definition 7.4.5 (The function $\varphi_x$).** For $s \subset \{1, \dots, m\}$ and a condition $\epsilon \in \{0, 1\}$ let $S_\epsilon(s)$ be the set of $i$ and $n+1-i$ for $i \in s$, together with the middle index $(n+1)/2$ if $n$ is odd and $\epsilon = 1$. Let $a + b = n$. Call $s$ admissible if $|s| = \lfloor a/2 \rfloor$, $|s^c| = \lfloor b/2 \rfloor$, $(a \bmod 2) + (b \bmod 2) = n \bmod 2$, and the sets $S = S_{a \bmod 2}(s)$ and $S' = S_{b \bmod 2}(s^c)$ have $a$ and $b$ elements. Put
$$v(s) = -2\sum_{i \in s}\sum_{j \notin s}\max(l_i, l_j) - \epsilon_n(s),$$
where $\epsilon_n(s) = 0$ for $n$ even, and for $n$ odd it is $\sum_{j \notin s} l_j$ if $a$ is odd and $\sum_{j \in s} l_j$ if $a$ is even. Then
$$\begin{aligned}
\varphi_x(y_1, y_2) = \sum_{s \text{ admissible}} &(-q)^{-v(s)}\,\frac{[K_a : I_a][K_b : I_b]}{[K_U : I_U]}\\
&\cdot \mathbf{1}_{I_a t(x|_S) I_a}(y_1)\,\mathbf{1}_{I_b t(x|_{S'}) I_b}(y_2),
\end{aligned}$$
where $x|_S$ is the restriction of $x$ to $S$, listed in increasing order of the index.

Each admissible $s$ splits the eigenvalue valuations $\pm l_i$ of $f_U$ between the two factors. The index factors normalize the Iwahori double cosets so that each has twisted orbital integral $1$ on its orbit, and $(-q)^{-v(s)}$ is the transfer factor at those valuations.

**Theorem 7.4.6 (Iwahori matching at an inert place).** Let $a \le n$ and $b = n - a$, and let $x$, $l$, $f_U$ and $\varphi_x$ be as above. Then:

1. $f_U$ and $\varphi_x$ match;
2. if $\varphi_x(y_1, y_2) \neq 0$, then the product of the characteristic polynomials of $N(y_1)$ and $N(y_2)$ is separable.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Bc.ArchbcB.B.M4.m4` (also `PositivityTheory.Rg.Bc.ArchbcB.B.archimedean_base_change_fourth` and `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.MatchAll.hM4`, second names).

*Proof.* We first describe the twisted orbits that meet an Iwahori double coset. Let $z \in \mathbb{Z}^c$ be strictly decreasing and let $\delta \in \mathrm{GL}_c(E_w)$ be twisted conjugate to an element of $I_ct(z)I_c$. Then the set of $p$ with $p^{-1}\delta\theta(p) \in I_ct(z)I_c$ is one double coset $H_\delta p_0 I_c$, the compact part $C_\delta$ lies in $p_0I_cp_0^{-1}$, and the roots of the characteristic polynomial of $N(\delta)$ have valuations $z_i - z_{c+1-i}$. A computation with the sum defining $O^\theta_\delta$ then gives $O^\theta_\delta(\mathbf{1}_{I_ct(z)I_c}) = [K_c : I_c]^{-1}$. The same analysis on $U$, using the Witt basis $b$, shows that two elements of $U$ whose conjugates meet the support of $f_U$ and that have the same characteristic polynomial are conjugate in $U$, and that $O_\gamma(f_U) = [K_U : I_U]^{-1}$ for such $\gamma$.

Now fix $Q_1, Q_2$. Case A: for some admissible $s$, the roots of $Q_1$ have valuations $x_{S(i)} - x_{S(a+1-i)}$ and the roots of $Q_2$ have valuations $x_{S'(i)} - x_{S'(b+1-i)}$, where $S(i)$ is the $i$-th element of $S$. Since the valuations determine $s$, only the term $s$ of $\varphi_x$ contributes, and it contributes one twisted class with integral $[K_a : I_a]^{-1}[K_b : I_b]^{-1}$. So $SO_{Q_1,Q_2}(\varphi_x) = (-q)^{-v(s)}[K_U : I_U]^{-1}$. On the other side, there is one conjugacy class in the support of $f_U$ with characteristic polynomial $Q_1Q_2$, its sign $\kappa$ is $1$, and its orbital integral is $[K_U : I_U]^{-1}$. A computation of $v(R)$ from the valuations of the roots gives $\Delta(Q_1, Q_2) = (-q)^{-v(s)}$. So both sides agree. If Case A fails for every $s$, then no twisted class with these norms meets the support of $\varphi_x$, and no conjugacy class with characteristic polynomial $Q_1Q_2$ meets the support of $f_U$, so both sides are $0$.

For part 2, take $s$ with a nonzero term at $(y_1, y_2)$. By the description of orbits, the roots of the two norms have valuations $x_i - x_{n+1-i}$ for $i \in S$ and $i \in S'$. Since $S$ and $S'$ are disjoint and $x$ is strictly decreasing, these valuations are distinct, so the product has distinct roots. $\square$

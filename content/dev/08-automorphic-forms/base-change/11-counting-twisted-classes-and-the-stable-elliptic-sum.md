## Counting twisted classes and the stable elliptic sum

[The theorem on elliptic terms](/entry/global-character-of-finite-inputs/) writes the elliptic part of the twisted trace formula for $\mathrm{GL}_a \times \mathrm{GL}_b$ as a sum over pairs of global twisted classes, each term a mass times character values times local twisted orbital integrals. To compare it with the unitary side we must regroup this sum by characteristic polynomials: the local factors then become the [stable twisted orbital integrals](/entry/m1/) $SO_{Q_1,Q_2}$, which are the ones that match. This section proves that the regrouped sum is the rational sum up to the factor $2^{(a+b)r_E}$, where $r_E$ is the number of archimedean places of $E$. The key is a count: the global twisted classes with a given elliptic norm and given local classes at a section number $2^{nr_E - r(P)}$, where $r(P)$ is the number of irreducible factors of the polynomial. The count is the Hasse norm theorem for the quadratic extensions $K_i(\sqrt d)/K_i$ attached to the factors, with the product formula. The result feeds [the chains of endoscopic terms](/entry/chain-existence-of-global-character/) of the next section.

We keep the notation of Sections 04 and 07. The field $E$ is CM with maximal real subfield $F$, every finite place of $E$ is unramified over $F$, $\theta_E$ is the involution of $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ that defines the unitary group, and $\theta$ is the involution used at the finite places. A twisted class is a $\theta_E$-twisted conjugacy class in $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$; its norm is $\delta\theta_E(\delta)$ for a representative $\delta$. For a finite place $w$ and $\delta \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ let $\delta_w$ be as in Section 07: the image of $\delta\theta_E(\delta)$ if $w$ is split and of $\delta$ if $w$ is inert. Let $r_E$ be the number of archimedean places of $E$.

**Definition 7.11.1 (Elliptic polynomials and stable masses).** A polynomial $P \in E[X]$ is elliptic of degree $n$ if it is monic of degree $n$, separable, and every root of every image $\tau(P)$, $\tau : E \to \mathbb{C}$, has absolute value 1. Let $r(P)$ be the number of monic irreducible factors of $P$. The stable mass of $P$ is
$$\mathfrak{m}(P) = \frac{m(\delta)}{2^{r(P)}}$$
if some $\delta \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ has a norm with characteristic polynomial $P$, where $m(\delta)$ is the mass of Section 07 for a fixed choice of such $\delta$; otherwise $\mathfrak{m}(P) = 0$.

The mass depends only on $P$: the companion development proves $m(\delta) = \mathfrak{m}(P)\,2^{r(P)}$ for every $\delta$ whose norm has characteristic polynomial $P$. So the choice in the definition does not matter.

**Definition 7.11.2 (Rational and stable elliptic sums).** Let $S$ be a section, $l_1$ and $l_2$ families of dominant weights for $\mathrm{GL}_a$ and $\mathrm{GL}_b$ at the archimedean places, and $\varphi = (\varphi_w)$ a family of functions on $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_w) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E_w)$. The rational elliptic sum is
$$\begin{aligned}\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{rat}}(S, l_1, l_2, \varphi) = \sum_{(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)}\ &m(\gamma_1)\,m(\gamma_2)\,\mathrm{ch}_{l_1}(P_{\gamma_1})\,\mathrm{ch}_{l_2}(P_{\gamma_2})\\&\cdot\prod_{w \in S}O^\theta_{(\gamma_{1,w}, \gamma_{2,w})}(\varphi_w),\end{aligned}$$
over the pairs of twisted classes whose norms have elliptic characteristic polynomials $P_{\gamma_1}$ and $P_{\gamma_2}$. The stable elliptic sum is
$$\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{st}}(S, l_1, l_2, \varphi) = \sum_{(P_1, P_2)}\mathfrak{m}(P_1)\,\mathfrak{m}(P_2)\,\mathrm{ch}_{l_1}(P_1)\,\mathrm{ch}_{l_2}(P_2)\prod_{w \in S}SO_{P_1,P_2}(\varphi_w),$$
over the pairs of coprime elliptic polynomials of degrees $a$ and $b$, where $SO_{P_1,P_2}$ at $w$ is taken for the images of $P_1$, $P_2$ in $E_w[X]$.

The rational sum is the right side of the theorem on elliptic terms, with $\varphi_w = s_w$. The stable sum is the shape in which the local factors can be compared with the unitary group.

**Theorem 7.11.3 (Parametrization of twisted classes).** Let $S$ be a section, $P$ an elliptic polynomial of degree $n$, and $y = (y_w)$ a family with $y_w \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$, such that the norm of $y_w$ (that is, $y_w$ if $w$ is split and $y_w\theta(y_w)$ if $w$ is inert, so that the norm of $\delta_w$ is the image of the norm of $\delta$) has characteristic polynomial $P$ at every $w$, and $y_w \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_w)$ for almost every $w$. Then there are $r(P)$ quadratic extensions $L_i/K_i$ of number fields over $F$, with $K_i$ totally real and $L_i$ totally complex, and for each $i$ a family $x_i = (x_i(u))$ indexed by the finite places $u$ of $K_i$, with $x_i(u) \in K_{i,u}^\times$ a local norm from $L_i$ for almost every $u$, such that $\sum_i r_{K_i} = nr_E$, where $r_{K_i}$ is the number of archimedean places of $K_i$, and there is a bijection between:

1. the twisted classes $c$ whose norm has characteristic polynomial $P$ and whose image $c_w$ is $\theta$-twisted conjugate to $y_w$ for every $w \in S$;
2. the families $(q_i)$ with $q_i \in K_i^\times/N(L_i^\times)$, such that some $t \in K_i^\times$ represents $q_i$ and $x_i(u)/t$ is a local norm from $L_{i,u}$ at every finite $u$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.G3.param`.

*Proof.* Choose $s \in E$ with $s \ne 0$, $\bar s = -s$ and $s^2 = d \in F$. Let $P = \prod_iP_i$ be the factorization. For each $i$ the algebra $E[X]/(P_i)$ is $K_i \oplus sK_i$, where $K_i$ is a field of degree $\deg P_i$ over $F$, and $K_i$ is totally real because the roots of $P_i$ have absolute value 1 and the class of $X$ satisfies $\bar x x = 1$. Put $L_i = K_i(\sqrt d)$; since $d$ is totally negative, $L_i$ is totally complex, and the archimedean places of $K_i$ number $[K_i : F]\,r_E$. Summing over $i$ gives $\sum_ir_{K_i} = nr_E$. Next fix $\delta$ with norm characteristic polynomial $P$. It determines a frame, an injective map $\rho : \prod_iK_i \to M_n(E)$ with $\rho(d) = s^2$ that commutes with the norm of $\delta$, whose image together with $s$ spans the centralizer of the norm. Put $\delta_t = \rho(t)\delta$ for $t \in \prod_iK_i^\times$. Then $\delta_t$ has the same norm polynomial; every element with this separable norm polynomial is twisted conjugate to some $\delta_t$; and $\delta_t$ is twisted conjugate to $\delta_{t'}$ if and only if $t = t'(u^2 - dv^2)$, that is, $t/t'$ is a norm from $\prod_iL_i$. So the twisted classes with norm polynomial $P$ are in bijection with $\prod_iK_i^\times/N(L_i^\times)$. The same algebra at each place, with the local norm groups of $K_i \otimes F_v$ and $L_i \otimes F_v$, which describe the local twisted classes at split and inert places, turns the condition at $w \in S$ into the condition that $x_i(u)/t$ is a local norm, where $x_i(u)$ is the local parameter of $y_w$ at the places $u$ of $K_i$ over $w$. Since $y_w$ is integral at almost every $w$, its parameter is a local unit norm there, so $x_i$ is a norm at almost all $u$. These inputs (the existence of $s$, the frame, the description of the classes, the quadratic fields and the local dictionary) are taken from the companion development. $\square$

**Theorem 7.11.4 (Count of twisted classes).** Under the hypotheses of the previous theorem, let $N$ be the number of twisted classes $c$ whose norm has characteristic polynomial $P$ and with $c_w$ twisted conjugate to $y_w$ for all $w \in S$. Then
$$N \cdot 2^{r(P)} = 2^{nr_E}.$$

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.G3.count1`.

*Proof.* By the [parametrization](/entry/param/), $N$ is the product over $i$ of the sizes of the sets in part 2. Fix $i$ and write $K = K_i$, $L = L_i$, $r = r_{K_i}$. By the Hasse norm theorem for the cyclic extension $L/K$, an element of $K^\times$ is a global norm if and only if it is a local norm everywhere, and the image of $K^\times/N(L^\times)$ in $\bigoplus_uK_u^\times/N(L_u^\times) \cong \bigoplus_u\{\pm1\}$ is the kernel of the product of the local symbols. Every real place of $K$ is ramified in $L$, because $L$ is totally complex, so each archimedean symbol takes both values. The finite components of a class in part 2 are fixed by $x$, and the product of the local symbols of $x$ is defined because $x$ is a norm almost everywhere. The archimedean components can be chosen freely subject to the product formula. This gives $2^{r - 1}$ classes, so the set has $2^{r-1}$ elements, and its size times 2 is $2^r$; this count is proved in the companion development. Taking the product over the $r(P)$ factors gives $N \cdot 2^{r(P)} = 2^{\sum_ir_{K_i}} = 2^{nr_E}$. $\square$

**Theorem 7.11.5 (Stable elliptic sum).** Let $S$ be a section, $l_1$, $l_2$ dominant weights, and $\varphi$ a family that is bi-invariant under $\theta$-stable levels at every place and equal to the unit almost everywhere. Assume that at some $w \in S$ the function $\varphi_w$ vanishes at every $(y_1, y_2)$ for which the product of the characteristic polynomials of the norms of $y_1$ and $y_2$ is not separable. Then
$$\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{rat}}(S, l_1, l_2, \varphi) = 2^{(a+b)r_E}\,\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{st}}(S, l_1, l_2, \varphi).$$

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.G3.g3`.

*Proof.* Group the terms of the rational sum by the pair $(P_1, P_2)$ of norm polynomials. If a term is not zero, then $\varphi_w$ does not vanish on the twisted orbit of $(\gamma_{1,w}, \gamma_{2,w})$ at the place $w$ of the hypothesis, so $P_1P_2$ is separable at $w$ and $P_1$, $P_2$ are coprime. The masses are $m(\gamma_i) = \mathfrak{m}(P_i)\,2^{r(P_i)}$, and the character values depend only on $P_i$. It remains to show, for fixed coprime $(P_1, P_2)$, that
$$\sum_{(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)}\prod_{w \in S}O^\theta_{(\gamma_{1,w}, \gamma_{2,w})}(\varphi_w) = 2^{(a+b)r_E - r(P_1) - r(P_2)}\prod_{w \in S}SO_{P_1,P_2}(\varphi_w).$$
Expand the right side: it is a sum over families $(y_w)_{w \in S}$ of local twisted classes with the norm polynomials $P_1$, $P_2$ of the products of their orbital integrals. Only families with $y_w$ integral at almost every $w$ contribute, since $\varphi_w$ is the unit almost everywhere, and there the unit has one contributing local class with orbital integral 1. Sort the global pairs on the left by their local classes. By the [count of twisted classes](/entry/count1/), applied to $\mathrm{GL}_a$ and $\mathrm{GL}_b$, each family of local classes is hit by exactly $2^{ar_E - r(P_1)}\cdot2^{br_E - r(P_2)}$ global pairs. This gives the identity. The sums are finite or absolutely convergent and may be rearranged, by finiteness and summability statements taken from the companion development: finitely many polynomial pairs and finitely many local classes contribute, the local integrals are invariants of the classes, and the support of $\varphi$ is controlled. $\square$

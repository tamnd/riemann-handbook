## Satake parameters at split places

The limit argument of Section 08 produces a pair of trees and a nonzero function $G$ in the span of products of their spaces, on which certain Hecke operators act by scalars. To identify the base change of a representation $\pi$ of the unitary group we must read, at each split place, the local components of the isobaric sum attached to the trees from these scalars. This section proves two results. The first is local: an irreducible subquotient of a representation induced from several pieces, with an exponent of decreasing size, is spherical, all its pieces are spherical, and their [Satake parameters](/learn/representations/#unramified-representations) add up to the exponent. The second is global: if the twisted constant terms of the unitary Hecke operators act on $G$ by the Satake eigenvalues of $\pi$ at a split place $w$, then the isobaric datum of the trees has these Satake parameters at $w$ and at the conjugate place, and it describes the component of $\pi$ there. Both are used in the frame reading of [Section 7.16.2](/entry/frame-ex0/).

**Local notation.** Let $k$ be a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic 0 with residue order $q$, $\mathcal{O}$ its ring of integers and $K = \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O})$. For $\xi \in (\mathbb{C}^\times)^n$ let $I(\xi)$ be the normalized principal series induced from the unramified character $\mathrm{diag}(t_1, \dots, t_n) \mapsto \prod_i\xi_i^{v(t_i)}$ of the diagonal torus, as in [Section 7.3.4](/entry/extreme-u/). We say that $\xi$ is decreasing if $|\xi_1| \ge |\xi_2| \ge \dots \ge |\xi_n|$. A smooth representation $\rho$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ has exponent $\xi$ if its Jacquet module for the upper Borel subgroup has a nonzero linear form on which the torus acts by this character. For $N_1 + \dots + N_c = n$ and irreducible smooth representations $\sigma_i$ of $\mathrm{GL}_{N_i}(k)$, let $\sigma_1 \times \dots \times \sigma_c$ be the representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ [parabolically induced](/learn/representations/#parabolic-induction-and-the-jacquet-module) from $\sigma_1 \otimes \dots \otimes \sigma_c$ on the standard block diagonal Levi subgroup. A spherical irreducible $\sigma$ of $\mathrm{GL}_N(k)$ has Satake parameter $b$, a multiset of $N$ elements of $\mathbb{C}^\times$, if the Hecke operator $T_j$ of the double coset of $\mathrm{diag}(\varpi, \dots, \varpi, 1, \dots, 1)$, with $j$ entries $\varpi$, acts on $\sigma^K$ by
$$\lambda_j(b) = q^{j(N-j)/2}e_j(b), \qquad 0 \le j \le N,$$
where $e_j$ is the $j$-th elementary symmetric function. Since $q \ne 0$, the numbers $\lambda_j(b)$, $j \le N$, determine the polynomial $\prod_{x \in b}(X + x)$ and hence $b$.

**Theorem 7.15.1 (Spherical pieces of a subquotient).** Let $E$ be a number field and $w$ a finite place of $E$, and put $k = E_w$. Let $N_1 + \dots + N_c = n$, let $\sigma_i$ be irreducible smooth representations of $\mathrm{GL}_{N_i}(k)$, and let $P$ be an irreducible smooth subquotient of $\sigma_1 \times \dots \times \sigma_c$. If $P$ has a decreasing exponent $\xi$, then every $\sigma_i$ is spherical, and there are Satake parameters $b_i$ of the $\sigma_i$ with
$$b_1 + \dots + b_c = \{\xi_1, \dots, \xi_n\}$$
as multisets.

*Proof.* We use five local facts, each proved separately in the development.

1. If a smooth $\rho$ has exponent $\xi$, there is a nonzero intertwining map $\rho \to I(\xi)$. This is Frobenius reciprocity for the Jacquet module: the linear form on the Jacquet module is torus equivariant, so it defines a map into the induced representation.
2. $I(\xi)$ has an irreducible spherical subquotient, since the space of $K$-fixed vectors of $I(\xi)$ is a line by the Iwasawa decomposition.
3. If $\xi$ is decreasing, every irreducible spherical subquotient of $I(\xi)$ embeds into $I(\xi)$. This is reduced to $\mathrm{GL}_2$, where the two orderings of a pair of characters give principal series with the same semisimplification and an explicit intertwining map exchanges them; a sequence of such exchanges, one adjacent pair at a time, moves the spherical vector into the required position.
4. If $\xi$ is decreasing, any two irreducible representations with nonzero maps into $I(\xi)$ are isomorphic: $I(\xi)$ has a unique irreducible subrepresentation for this ordering.
5. If $P$ is a spherical irreducible subquotient of $\sigma_1 \times \dots \times \sigma_c$ with a nonzero map into $I(\xi)$, then every $\sigma_i$ is spherical and their Satake parameters add up to $\xi$.

From 2, 3 and 4 we get: if $\xi$ is decreasing and an irreducible $P$ has a nonzero map $f$ into $I(\xi)$, then $P$ is spherical. Indeed, let $\sigma$ be a spherical subquotient of $I(\xi)$ given by 2, and $g : \sigma \to I(\xi)$ the injective map of 3. Since $\sigma$ has a nonzero $K$-fixed vector and $g$ is injective, $g \ne 0$. By 4, $P \cong \sigma$, and an isomorphism carries the nonzero $K$-fixed vector of $\sigma$ to a nonzero $K$-fixed vector of $P$.

Now let $P$ be as in the theorem. By 1 there is a nonzero $f : P \to I(\xi)$. Since $E$ is a number field, $E_w$ has characteristic 0, so the previous paragraph applies and $P$ is spherical. Then 5, applied to $P$ and $f$, gives the claim. $\square$

The proof of fact 3 compares the spherical vector of $I(\xi)$ with the images of the $\mathrm{GL}_2$ intertwining maps; when two adjacent characters differ by the modulus character $|\cdot|$, the pair is linked and the $\mathrm{GL}_2$ step uses the one-dimensional subquotient instead.

**Global notation.** Let $E$ be a CM field with maximal real subfield $F$, in which every finite place is unramified over $F$. We keep the trees of [Section 7.8.9](/entry/e-final-of/) and the admissible characters $(\mu, e)$ of [Section 7.12.5](/entry/chain-ex-holds/). An isobaric datum $D$ of rank $n$ consists of integers $N_1, \dots, N_c > 0$ with sum $n$, discrete spaces $V_i \subseteq \mathcal{S}_{N_i}$, irreducible smooth representations $\sigma_i(w)$ of $\mathrm{GL}_{N_i}(E_w)$ for every finite place $w$ that are the local components of $V_i$, and multisets $m_i(\tau)$ for every embedding $\tau : E \to \mathbb{C}$ that are the infinitesimal characters of $V_i$ at $\tau$. For a finite place $w$ and a multiset $B$ of $n$ elements of $\mathbb{C}^\times$, $D$ has Satake parameter $B$ at $w$ if every $\sigma_i(w)$ is spherical, with Satake parameter $b_i$, and $b_1 + \dots + b_c = B$.

Let $\pi$ be an automorphic representation of $U(n)$, and let $w$ be a place of $E$ that is split over $F$, so that $U(n)(F_v) \cong \mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$ for the place $v$ of $F$ below $w$. We say that $\pi$ has Satake parameter $B$ at $w$ if some irreducible smooth representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$ with Satake parameter $B$ occurs in $\pi$, under this identification. We say that $D$ describes $\pi$ at $w$ if some irreducible subquotient of $\sigma_1(w) \times \dots \times \sigma_c(w)$ occurs in $\pi$ in the same sense.

For Hecke characters $\chi_1$, $\chi_2$ and isobaric data $D_a$, $D_b$ of ranks $a$ and $b$, a datum $D$ of rank $a + b$ is a join of $(D_a, \chi_1)$ and $(D_b, \chi_2)$ if its list of spaces is the list of the spaces of $D_a$, each twisted by $\chi_1 \circ \det$, followed by the list of the spaces of $D_b$, each twisted by $\chi_2 \circ \det$. A tree $s$ of rank $a$ has parts $D_a$ if the spaces of $D_a$, in order, are the leaf spaces of $s$ of positive rank.

Let $a + b = n$ and let $w$ be finite. For a character $\nu$ of $E_w^\times$ and the Hecke operator $T_j$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$, let $T_j^\nu$ be the function on $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_w) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E_w)$ given by
$$\begin{aligned}T_j^\nu(m_1, m_2) &= \nu\big((-1)^{ab}\det(m_1)^b\det(m_2)^a\big)\,\big|\det(m_1)^b/\det(m_2)^a\big|_w^{1/2} \\ &\quad \times \int_{\mathfrak{n}}\int_KT_j\Big(k^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}m_1 & X\\ 0 & m_2\end{pmatrix}k\Big)\,dk\,dX,\end{aligned}$$
the $\nu$-twisted constant term of $T_j$ along the block upper triangular parabolic subgroup. It acts on functions on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{A}_E)$ that are invariant under $K_w = \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathcal{O}_w) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(\mathcal{O}_w)$ by right convolution at $w$. We write $T_j^\nu[w]$ for this action and $T_j^\nu[w, \theta]$ for the same action composed with the involution $\theta$ of Section 07.

**Theorem 7.15.2 (Reading Satake parameters at a split place).** Let $E$ be a CM field in which every finite place is unramified over $F$. Let $a + b = n$, $(\mu, e)$ admissible, and $\pi$ an automorphic representation of $U(n)$. Let $s_1$, $s_2$ be good untwisted trees of ranks $a$ and $b$ with parts $D_a$ and $D_b$, and $D$ a join of $(D_a, \mu^b)$ and $(D_b, \mu^a)$. Let $G \ne 0$ be in the span of the products $\varphi_1(x_1)\varphi_2(x_2)$ with $\varphi_1 \in \mathrm{space}(s_1)$ and $\varphi_2 \in \mathrm{space}(s_2)$. Let $w$ be a split place at which $\mu$ is unramified, $\varpi$ a uniformizer of $E_w$, and $u$ the conjugate place. Assume that $\pi$ has Satake parameter $B$ at $w$. Then:

1. if $G$ is invariant under $K_w$ and $T_j^{\mu_w}[w]G = \lambda_j(B)G$ for all $j \le n$, then $D$ has Satake parameter $B$ at $w$ and describes $\pi$ at $w$;
2. if $G$ is invariant under $K_u$ and $T_j^{\mu_w}[w, \theta]G = \lambda_j(B)G$ for all $j \le n$, then $D$ has Satake parameter $B^{-1} = \{x^{-1} : x \in B\}$ at $u$ and describes $\pi$ at $u$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.RS.reading_split_holds` (also `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.RS.readSplit_of_join`, the same result with the module statement for joins as a hypothesis).

*Proof.* We use the following facts.

1. Trees. If $s$ is a good untwisted tree with parts $D_a$ and $\mathrm{space}(s)$ contains a nonzero $K_w$-invariant function, then $D_a$ has a Satake parameter at $w$ and every such function is a Satake vector for it. This follows from a module statement: $\mathrm{space}(s)$, as a representation of $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_w)$, is separated by intertwining maps into $\sigma_1(w) \times \dots \times \sigma_c(w)$. The module statement is proved by induction on the tree, from leaves (a discrete space maps to its local component) and nodes (induction in stages), and from the module statement for joins, which says that the product attached to a join is the representation induced from the twisted products attached to $D_a$ and $D_b$.
2. Pairs. If $G \ne 0$ is in the span of products as above and invariant under $K_w$, there are Satake parameters $B_a$ of $D_a$ and $B_b$ of $D_b$ at $w$ such that $T_i \otimes T_j$ acts on $G$ by $\lambda_i(B_a)\lambda_j(B_b)$.
3. Constant terms. If $\nu$ is unramified with $\nu(\varpi) = z$ and $G$ is as in 2, then $T_j^\nu[w]G = \lambda_j(z^bB_a + z^aB_b)G$, where $z^bB_a$ is the multiset of the $z^bx$ with $x \in B_a$. This is the Satake transform of the constant term.
4. Conjugation. If $w$ is split, $u$ its conjugate, and $G$ is a Satake vector at $u$ for $(B_a, B_b)$, then $G$ is a Satake vector at $w$ for $(B_a^{-1}, B_b^{-1})$ for the action twisted by $\theta$.
5. The character. Since $\mu$ is admissible and unramified at $w$, $\mu_w$ is the unramified character with value $z = \mu_w(\varpi)$, $\mu^k_w$ is unramified with value $z^k$, and $\mu$ is unramified at $u$ with value $z^{-1}$.
6. Joins. If $\chi_1$, $\chi_2$ are unramified at $w$ with values $z_1$, $z_2$ and $D_a$, $D_b$ have Satake parameters $B_a$, $B_b$ at $w$, then the join $D$ has Satake parameter $z_1B_a + z_2B_b$ at $w$.
7. If $\pi$ has Satake parameter $B$ at $w$ and $D$ has Satake parameter $B$ at $w$, then $D$ describes $\pi$ at $w$. If $\pi$ has Satake parameter $B$ at $w$, it has Satake parameter $B^{-1}$ at $u$.

For part 1, take $B_a$, $B_b$ from 2. By 5 and 3, $T_j^{\mu_w}[w]G = \lambda_j(z^bB_a + z^aB_b)G$. By hypothesis this is $\lambda_j(B)G$, and $G \ne 0$, so $\lambda_j(z^bB_a + z^aB_b) = \lambda_j(B)$ for all $j \le n$. Both multisets have $n$ elements, since $B$ is the Satake parameter of a representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$, so they are equal. By 6, applied to $\mu^b$ and $\mu^a$ with values $z^b$ and $z^a$, $D$ has Satake parameter $z^bB_a + z^aB_b = B$ at $w$, and by 7 it describes $\pi$ at $w$.

For part 2, take $B_a$, $B_b$ from 2 at the place $u$. By 4, $G$ is a Satake vector at $w$ for $(B_a^{-1}, B_b^{-1})$ for the twisted action, and the argument of part 1 gives $z^bB_a^{-1} + z^aB_b^{-1} = B$. Inverting, $z^{-b}B_a + z^{-a}B_b = B^{-1}$. By 5, $\mu^b$ and $\mu^a$ have values $z^{-b}$ and $z^{-a}$ at $u$, so 6 gives that $D$ has Satake parameter $B^{-1}$ at $u$. By 7, $\pi$ has Satake parameter $B^{-1}$ at $u$, and $D$ describes $\pi$ at $u$.

The facts 2 to 7 and the leaf and node cases of 1 are taken from the companion development, as is the module statement for joins in the unconditional form. $\square$

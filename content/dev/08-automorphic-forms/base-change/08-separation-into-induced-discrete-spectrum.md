## Separation into the induced discrete spectrum

This section works on the spectral side. A limit argument gives automorphic forms whose infinitesimal characters converge, and we must identify what they generate. A finitely generated space of automorphic forms with nonpositive exponents, a unitary central character and a regular infinitesimal character maps, with a nonzero image, into a space induced in stages from pieces of the [discrete spectrum](/learn/automorphic-forms/#the-discrete-spectrum). A binary tree records the stages. This is the shape of Langlands' description of the spectrum by [Eisenstein series and residues](/learn/trace-formula/#eisenstein-series-and-the-residual-spectrum). We prove this by induction on the rank and pass it to the limit statement that the [base change step](/step/base-change-n/) uses.

Let $E$ be a CM field and $d = [E : \mathbb{Q}]$. For $N \ge 0$ let $\mathcal{S}_N$ be the space of functions on $\mathrm{GL}_N(\mathbb{A}_E)$ that the companion development uses for automorphic forms. A family of operators fixed there acts on $\mathcal{S}_N$, and a subspace is stable if these operators preserve it. The notions of automorphic form, of the infinitesimal character $m(\tau)$ of a space at an embedding $\tau : E \to \mathbb{C}$ (a multiset of $N$ numbers) and of a discrete space are also taken from there. Let $K_\infty$ be the standard maximal compact subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_N(E_\infty)$. For $z \in \mathbb{C}$ and a subspace $V$, the twist $V(z)$ is the image of $V$ under multiplication by $|\det|^z$.

**Definition 7.8.1 (Exponents).** Let $0 < r < N$ and let $P_r$ be the standard parabolic subgroup with Levi factor $M_r = \mathrm{GL}_r \times \mathrm{GL}_{N-r}$ and unipotent radical $U_r$. Let $a_r(t) = \operatorname{diag}(e^t I_r, I_{N-r})$ at every archimedean place. For $\varphi \in \mathcal{S}_N$ let $\varphi_{P_r}$ be its [constant term](/learn/automorphic-forms/#constant-terms-and-cusp-forms) along $P_r$. A number $c \in \mathbb{C}$ is an exponent of $\varphi$ along $P_r$ if there is a nonzero function $F$ in the span of the translates $g \mapsto \varphi_{P_r}(a_r(t)g)$ such that $F(a_r(t)g) = e^{ct}F(g)$ for all $t$ and $g$. The normalized real part of $c$ is
$$p_{N,r}(c) = \frac{\operatorname{Re} c}{d} - \frac{r(N-r)}{2}.$$

**Definition 7.8.2 (Admissible spaces).** Let $U$ be a subspace of $\mathcal{S}_N$. Then:

1. $U$ has nonpositive exponents if $p_{N,r}(c) \le 0$ for every $\varphi \in U$, every $0 < r < N$ and every exponent $c$ of $\varphi$ along $P_r$.
2. $U$ has an imaginary central exponent if there is $z$ with $\operatorname{Re} z = 0$ such that $\varphi(z_N(t)g) = e^{zt}\varphi(g)$ for all $\varphi \in U$, where $t \mapsto z_N(t)$ is a one-parameter subgroup of the archimedean center fixed in the companion development. It is split invariant if this holds with $z = 0$.
3. $U$ is regular if, for every $\tau$, $U$ has an infinitesimal character $m(\tau)$ without repeated entries.
4. $U$ is finitely generated if it is the smallest stable space that contains some finite set of its elements.

We call $U$ admissible if it is stable, consists of automorphic forms, is finitely generated and regular, and has nonpositive exponents and an imaginary central exponent.

**Definition 7.8.3 (Induced pieces).** Let $r + s = N$, $V_1 \subseteq \mathcal{S}_r$ and $V_2 \subseteq \mathcal{S}_s$. A function $\varphi \in \mathcal{S}_N$ lies in the induced piece of $(V_1, V_2)$ if it is left invariant under $U_r(\mathbb{A}_E)$ and, for every $k \in K_\infty$ and every finite adelic $g$, the function $(m_1, m_2) \mapsto \varphi(\operatorname{diag}(m_1, m_2)kg)$ lies in the span of the products $\psi_1(m_1)\psi_2(m_2)$ with $\psi_i \in V_i$. It models [parabolic induction](/learn/representations/#parabolic-induction-and-the-jacquet-module) through restrictions to the Levi factor.

**Definition 7.8.4 (Trees).** A tree of rank $N$ is either a leaf $(V, z)$ with $V \subseteq \mathcal{S}_N$ and $z \in \mathbb{C}$, or a node $(t_1, t_2)$ with trees $t_1$ of rank $r$ and $t_2$ of rank $s$, and $N = r + s$. Its space is $V(z)$ for a leaf. For a node it is the span of the $K_\infty$-finite functions in the induced piece of $(\mathrm{space}(t_1)(s/2), \mathrm{space}(t_2)(-r/2))$. A leaf is good if either $N = 0$, $V = \mathcal{S}_0$ and $z = 0$, or $N > 0$, $V$ is discrete and $\operatorname{Re} z = 0$. A node is good if both subtrees are good, and a tree is untwisted if every leaf has $z = 0$.

For a space $U$ and $\varphi \in U$, we say that $\varphi$ separates in $U$ if there are a good tree $t$ with all leaves of positive rank and a linear map $T$ on $\mathcal{S}_N$ such that $T$ commutes with the action on $U$, $T(U) \subseteq \mathrm{space}(t)$ and $T\varphi \neq 0$.

**Definition 7.8.5 (Slice map).** Let $N = r + s$ with $r, s > 0$, let $U \subseteq \mathcal{S}_N$ and let $c \in \mathbb{C}$. A slice map for $(U, c)$ is a linear map $T$ on $\mathcal{S}_N$ that commutes with the action on $U$, is not zero on $U$, and makes each $T\varphi$ left invariant under $U_r(\mathbb{A}_E)$. For fixed $k$, $g$ and $m_2$, the left slice $m_1 \mapsto T\varphi(\operatorname{diag}(m_1, m_2)kg)$ is an automorphic form on $\mathrm{GL}_r$ that transforms by $e^{ct}$ under $z_r(t)$, and its exponents along $P_{r_1}$ are exponents of $U$ along $P_{r_1}$. In the same way the right slices are automorphic forms on $\mathrm{GL}_s$ that transform by $e^{-ct}$, and $c + c_2$ is an exponent of $U$ along $P_{r + r_2}$ for each exponent $c_2$ of a right slice along $P_{r_2}$. The left and right slice spaces of $T$ are the spans of these slices.

**Theorem 7.8.6 (Block decomposition).** Assume that, for every slice map $T$ for $(U, c)$ with $U$ stable, finitely generated and consisting of automorphic forms, the left and right slice spaces of $T$ are finitely generated. Let $r, s > 0$, let $U \subseteq \mathcal{S}_{r+s}$ be admissible and split invariant, and let $T$ be a slice map for $(U, c)$ with $p_{r+s,r}(c) = 0$. Then there are a finite set $J$, linear maps $P_j$ and admissible spaces $W_{1,j} \subseteq \mathcal{S}_r$ and $W_{2,j} \subseteq \mathcal{S}_s$ for $j \in J$, such that:

1. each $P_j$ commutes with the action on $T(U)$;
2. $\sum_{j \in J} P_j(T\psi) = T\psi$ for every $\psi \in U$;
3. $P_j(T\psi)$ lies in the induced piece of $(W_{1,j}(s/2), W_{2,j}(-r/2))$ for every $j$ and $\psi \in U$.

*Proof.* Fix the regular infinitesimal character $m$ of $U$. By the hypothesis, the slice spaces $L$ and $R$ of $T$ are finitely generated, and they are stable. By an input from the companion development, $L$ is contained in the sum of its pieces whose infinitesimal characters are admissible sub-multisets $\sigma$ of $m$ of size $r$, shifted by $s/2$, and $R$ is contained in the sum of the pieces with sub-multisets $\rho$ of size $s$, shifted by $-r/2$. There are finitely many such $\sigma$ and $\rho$, and a Lagrange interpolation input gives projections $z_1(\sigma)$ on $L$ and $z_2(\rho)$ on $R$. These commute with the action, take values in the piece of $\sigma$ or $\rho$, and sum to the identity. For each pair $p = (\sigma, \rho)$ an input on induced maps gives a map $T'_p$ on $U$ whose values are left invariant under $U_r(\mathbb{A}_E)$ and whose Levi restrictions are obtained from those of $T\psi$ by applying $z_1(\sigma) \otimes z_2(\rho)$. A function left invariant under $U_r(\mathbb{A}_E)$ is determined by its Levi restrictions. So $T'_p\psi = 0$ whenever $T\psi = 0$, and $T'_p$ factors as $P'_p \circ T$. The same principle and the fact that the projections sum to the identity give $\sum_p T'_p\psi = T\psi$. Let $J$ index the pairs, $P_p = P'_p$, $W_{1,p} = z_1(\sigma)(L)(-s/2)$ and $W_{2,p} = z_2(\rho)(R)(r/2)$. Then $W_{1,p}(s/2) = z_1(\sigma)(L)$ and $W_{2,p}(-r/2) = z_2(\rho)(R)$, so the Levi restrictions of $P_p(T\psi)$ lie in the products of these two spaces. That $W_{1,p}$ and $W_{2,p}$ are admissible is taken as an input from the companion development. $\square$

**Theorem 7.8.7.** The conclusion of the Block decomposition theorem holds without its hypothesis.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.SpectralAccumulation.Cz.blocks_holds`.

*Proof.* This is the Block decomposition theorem applied to the finite generation of the left and right slice spaces, which is taken as an input from the companion development. $\square$

We use five statements that the companion development proves. Let $U$ be admissible of rank $N > 0$.

1. Slices. If $N = r + s$ with $r, s > 0$, $U$ is split invariant, $\varphi \in U$ and $c$ is an exponent of $\varphi$ along $P_r$, then there is a slice map $T$ for $(U, c)$ with $T\varphi \neq 0$.
2. Leaves. If $\varphi \in U$ is nonzero and $p_{N,r}(c) < 0$ for every exponent $c$ of $\varphi$ along every $P_r$, then $\varphi$ separates in $U$.
3. Nodes. Let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be stable spaces of automorphic forms in which every nonzero element separates, and let $T$ commute with the action on $U$ and map $U$ into the induced piece of $(W_1(s/2), W_2(-r/2))$. Then every $\varphi \in U$ with $T\varphi \neq 0$ separates in $U$.
4. Types. A function has type $(K, W)$, for an open subgroup $K$ of $\mathrm{GL}_N(\mathbb{A}_{E,f})$ and a finite-dimensional space $W$ of functions on $K_\infty$, if it is right invariant under $K$ and its right $K_\infty$-translates lie in $W$. For each $W$ there are finite sets $\Omega_M$ of such spaces and, for each $K$, finite sets $\Theta_M$ of open subgroups, such that if $t$ is good and $\mathrm{space}(t)$ contains a nonzero function of type $(K, W)$, then every leaf of $t$ contains a nonzero function of a type in $(\Theta_M, \Omega_M)$.
5. Twists. If $\operatorname{Re} z = 0$ and $\varphi$ separates in $U$, then $|\det|^z\varphi$ separates in $U(z)$.

**Theorem 7.8.8 (Separation).** Let $N > 0$ and let $W$ be a finite-dimensional space of functions on $K_\infty$. Then there are finite sets $\Omega_M$ of type spaces and, for every open $K$, finite sets $\Theta_M$ of open levels with this property. Let $U \neq 0$ be a stable space of automorphic forms generated by a finite set of forms of type $(K, W)$, with nonpositive exponents, an imaginary central exponent and a regular infinitesimal character, so that $U$ is admissible. Then there are a good tree $t$, whose leaves have positive rank and contain nonzero functions of types in $(\Theta_M, \Omega_M)$, and a linear map $T$ that commutes with the action on $U$, with $T(U) \subseteq \mathrm{space}(t)$ and $T\psi \neq 0$ for some $\psi \in U$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.SpectralAccumulation.Cz.separation_holds`.

*Proof.* We first prove, by strong induction on $N > 0$, that every nonzero $\varphi$ in an admissible $U$ separates in $U$. If $p_{N,r}(c) < 0$ for all exponents of $\varphi$, the Leaves statement applies. Otherwise some exponent $c$ along some $P_r$ has $p_{N,r}(c) \ge 0$, so $p_{N,r}(c) = 0$ because $U$ has nonpositive exponents. Put $s = N - r > 0$. Let $z$ be the central exponent of $U$ and $w = -z/(Nd)$, so $\operatorname{Re} w = 0$. The space $U' = U(w)$ is admissible, since a twist by an imaginary power keeps each condition, and it is split invariant, since $z + wNd = 0$. The function $\varphi' = |\det|^w\varphi$ is a nonzero element of $U'$, and $c' = c + wrd$ is an exponent of $\varphi'$ along $P_r$. Since $\operatorname{Re}(wrd) = 0$, $p_{N,r}(c') = 0$. The Slices statement gives a slice map $T$ for $(U', c')$ with $T\varphi' \neq 0$. The Block decomposition theorem gives $J$, $P_j$, $W_{1,j}$ and $W_{2,j}$. Because $\sum_j P_j(T\varphi') = T\varphi' \neq 0$, there is $j$ with $P_j(T\varphi') \neq 0$. The admissible spaces $W_{1,j}$ and $W_{2,j}$ have ranks $r, s < N$, so by induction their nonzero elements separate. The Nodes statement for the map $P_j \circ T$ shows that $\varphi'$ separates in $U'$. The Twists statement with $-w$ shows that $\varphi = |\det|^{-w}\varphi'$ separates in $U'(-w) = U$.

Now take $\Omega_M$ and $\Theta_M$ from the Types statement. Let $U$ be generated by a finite set $S$. Since $U \neq 0$, some $\varphi \in S$ is nonzero, and the first part gives $t$ and $T$ with $T\varphi \neq 0$. Since $T$ commutes with the action and $\varphi$ has type $(K, W)$, the function $T\varphi$ also has type $(K, W)$. It is a nonzero element of $\mathrm{space}(t)$, so the Types statement shows that each leaf contains a nonzero function of a type in $(\Theta_M, \Omega_M)$. $\square$

Two further inputs enter. The finiteness statement says that, for a fixed type, level, bound $R$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, the discrete spaces of that type with infinitesimal character bounded by $R$ and with $p_{N,r}(c) \le -\varepsilon r(N-r)$ for all exponents fall into finitely many isomorphism classes. The limit vector statement is about limits of vectors in the spaces of trees whose twists converge, and we do not state it here.

**Theorem 7.8.9 (Limit vectors).** Assume the Separation theorem, the finiteness statement and the limit vector statement. Let $a, b \ge 0$ and let $W_1$, $W_2$ be finite-dimensional type spaces. There are finite sets of types $\Omega^1$, $\Omega^2$ and, for all open levels $K_1$, $K_2$, finite sets of levels $\Theta^1$, $\Theta^2$ with this property. Let $f_k$ be nonzero continuous functions on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{A}_E)$ with values in $\mathbb{C}^\iota$ for a finite set $\iota$. Assume that each $f_k$ is left invariant under the rational points, weighted square integrable and of type $(K_1 \times K_2, W_1 \otimes W_2)$, that its slices are automorphic forms with infinitesimal characters $m_{1,k}$ and $m_{2,k}$, and that $hf_k = \lambda_k(h)f_k$ for the Hecke matrices $h$ in a countable set $C$, with $\lambda_k(h) \to \lambda_0(h)$. Assume that $m_{i,k}(\tau)$ converges, as a multiset, to $n_i(\tau)$, where $n_i(\tau)$ is real, $n_i(\bar\tau) = -n_i(\tau)$ and distinct entries of $n_i(\tau)$ differ by at least $\tfrac12$. Assume a reproducing kernel condition in all ranks. Then there are good untwisted trees $s_1$, $s_2$ of ranks $a$, $b$, whose leaves have rank $0$ or contain nonzero functions of types in $(\Theta^i, \Omega^i)$, with infinitesimal characters $n_1$ and $n_2$, and a nonzero $F$ of the same type with coordinates in the span of the products of $\mathrm{space}(s_1)$ and $\mathrm{space}(s_2)$ and with $hF = \lambda_0(h)F$ for $h \in C$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.SpectralAccumulation.eFinal_of`.

*Proof.* This is the gluing theorem of the companion development applied to the three assumptions and to companion lemmas on trees, on typed vectors in discrete spaces, on untwisted trees and on the slice hulls of the $f_k$. $\square$

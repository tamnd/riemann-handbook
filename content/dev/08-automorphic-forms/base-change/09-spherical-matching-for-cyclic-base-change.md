## Spherical matching for cyclic base change

The comparison of the twisted trace formula for a cyclic extension with the trace formula of the base field needs, at almost every place, pairs of spherical functions with the same orbital integrals. This section proves such a matching for spherical functions whose Satake transforms are multiples of an auxiliary polynomial that does not vanish at given Satake parameters. We first prove it for an unramified extension of local fields, then at an inert place of a number field, and then at all unramified places at once. The last result feeds [the matched test functions for one Hecke monomial](/entry/term-ex/) and so the [global matching](/entry/gm-of-unit/) of the next section. The background is in [the twisted trace formula and base change](/learn/trace-formula/#the-twisted-trace-formula-and-base-change) and [unramified representations](/learn/representations/#unramified-representations).

In this section and the next one, $E/F$ is a cyclic extension of number fields, or of local fields, with a generator $\sigma$ of its Galois group, and $l = [E:F]$. This is a different setting from the CM extension of the earlier sections. For a finite place $v$ of $F$ we write $E_v = E \otimes_F F_v$, the product of the completions $E_w$ for $w \mid v$, and $\mathcal{O}_{E,v}$ for its ring of integers.

**Definition 7.9.1 (Local matching).** Let $v$ be a finite place of $F$. Normalize the Haar measures on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_v)$ and $\mathrm{GL}_n(F_v)$ so that $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_{E,v})$ and $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_v)$ have volume 1. For $\delta \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E_v)$ let $N\delta = \delta\sigma(\delta)\cdots\sigma^{l-1}(\delta)$ be its norm. Let $\varphi$ be a function on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_v)$ and $f$ a function on $\mathrm{GL}_n(F_v)$, both locally constant with compact support, and let $c \in \mathbb{C}$. We say that $\varphi$ matches $f$ with constant $c$ if:

1. for every $\delta$ such that $N\delta$ is regular semisimple and conjugate to $\gamma \in \mathrm{GL}_n(F_v)$ by some $h$, the twisted orbital integral of $\varphi$ at $\delta$ is $c$ times the orbital integral of $f$ at $\gamma$, when the Haar measures on the two centralizers correspond under conjugation by $h$;
2. the orbital integral of $f$ is 0 at every regular semisimple $\gamma$ that is not a norm.

The twisted orbital integral at $\delta$ is the integral of $g \mapsto \varphi(g^{-1}\delta\sigma(g))$ over the quotient of $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_v)$ by the twisted centralizer of $\delta$. Condition 1 is the identity of [orbital integrals](/learn/trace-formula/#the-twisted-trace-formula-and-base-change) that base change asks for, and condition 2 says that $f$ has nothing to match where no $\delta$ exists.

**Definition 7.9.2 (Satake transform and base change of polynomials).** Let $k$ be a nonarchimedean local field with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}$. The spherical Hecke algebra of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ is the algebra of bi-$\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O})$-invariant locally constant functions of compact support, under convolution. The Satake transform $\mathcal{S}$ maps it isomorphically onto the symmetric Laurent polynomials in $x_1, \dots, x_n$. For $l \ge 1$ let $b_l$ be the algebra map with $b_l(x^a) = x^{la}$. A spherical function $h$ on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$ has transform $p$ in the elementary symmetric polynomials if $\mathcal{S}(h) = p(e_1(x), \dots, e_n(x))$.

If $E/k$ is unramified of degree $l$, then the unramified character $x_i$ of $E^\times$ restricts to the $l$-th power on the Frobenius, and $b_l$ is the map on Satake transforms that base change induces.

**Theorem 7.9.3 (Matching with an auxiliary factor).** Let $E/k$ be a finite Galois extension of nonarchimedean local fields of characteristic 0 with cyclic Galois group generated by $\sigma$. Assume that a uniformizer of $k$ is a uniformizer of $E$, and let $l = [E:k]$. Let $P$ be a finite set of symmetric Laurent polynomials in $n$ variables and let $z \in (\mathbb{C}^\times)^n$. Then there is a symmetric polynomial $g$, with no negative exponents, such that $g(z) \ne 0$ and the following holds for every $p$ in the span of $P$: if $h$ on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ and $f$ on $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ are spherical with $\mathcal{S}(h) = gp$ and $\mathcal{S}(f) = b_l(gp)$, then $h$ matches $f$ with constant 1.

Lean: `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.H.deepHecke`.

*Proof.* Since $E/k$ is unramified, the residue field of $E$ has $q^l$ elements, where $q$ is the order of the residue field of $k$. For $a \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ let $\theta(a)$ be the normalized characteristic function of the Iwahori double coset $I\varpi^aI$. We take the following criterion as given, from the companion development. There is a symmetric $g$ with $g(z) \ne 0$ such that, for any two linear functionals $\Lambda_E$ and $\Lambda_k$ on the locally constant functions of compact support that vanish on every commutator $a * b - b * a$ with $a$ the unit or an Iwahori double coset indicator, and that satisfy $\Lambda_E(\theta_E(a)) = \Lambda_k(\theta_k(la))$ for every strictly decreasing $a$, we have $\Lambda_E(h) = \Lambda_k(f)$ whenever $\mathcal{S}(h) = gp$ and $\mathcal{S}(f) = b_l(gp)$ with $p$ in the span of $P$. For condition 1, fix $\delta$ and $\gamma$ and take $\Lambda_E$ the twisted orbital integral at $\delta$ and $\Lambda_k$ the orbital integral at $\gamma$. Then $\gamma$ is regular semisimple, since $N\delta$ is conjugate to $\gamma$ and so has the same separable characteristic polynomial. Both functionals are linear. They vanish on commutators: the orbital integral is invariant under conjugation, and the twisted one is invariant under twisted conjugation, which handles $a * b - b * a$ because the Iwahori double coset indicators of $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ for $\varpi \in k$ are $\sigma$-invariant. The identity on $\theta(a)$ is the matching of Iwahori indicators for strictly decreasing $a$, which we also take from the companion development. For condition 2 take $\Lambda_E = 0$ and $\Lambda_k$ the orbital integral at a $\gamma$ that is not a norm; the same matching of Iwahori indicators says that $\Lambda_k(\theta_k(la)) = 0$. $\square$

The theorem is weaker than matching for the whole spherical Hecke algebra: it covers only the multiples of $g$, and $g$ depends on $P$ and $z$. For the use below this is enough, since we only need test functions whose Satake transforms do not vanish at one given parameter.

**Theorem 7.9.4 (Matching at an inert place).** Let $E/F$ be a cyclic extension of number fields of prime degree $l$ and let $n > 0$. Let $v$ be a finite place of $F$ that is unramified in $E$ and not split, and let $w$ be the place of $E$ over $v$. Let $t$ be a multiset of $n$ nonzero complex numbers and let $D \in \mathbb{N}$. Then there is a polynomial $\gamma$ in $n$ variables with $\gamma(e_1(t), \dots, e_n(t)) \ne 0$ such that, for every polynomial $p$ of degree at most $D$: if $h$ is spherical on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$ with transform $\gamma p$, and $f$ is spherical on $\mathrm{GL}_n(F_v)$ with $\mathcal{S}(f) = (\gamma p)(e_1(x^l), \dots, e_n(x^l))$, then $h$, as a function on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_v)$, matches $f$ with constant 1.

Lean: `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.H.locHeckeInert`.

*Proof.* Since $l$ is prime and $v$ does not split, $w$ is the only place over $v$, so $E_v = E_w$, and $E_w/F_v$ is a cyclic unramified extension of degree $l$ with a generator compatible with $\sigma$. Write $t$ as the entries of $z \in (\mathbb{C}^\times)^n$. The symmetric Laurent polynomials $p(e_1, \dots, e_n)$ with $\deg p \le D$ lie in the span of a finite set $P$, namely the images of the monomials of degree at most $D$, and $b_l(p(e(x))) = p(e(x^l))$. The [matching with an auxiliary factor](/entry/deep-hecke/) gives a symmetric $g$ with $g(z) \ne 0$. A symmetric polynomial is a polynomial $\gamma(e_1, \dots, e_n)$ in the elementary symmetric polynomials, and $\gamma(e(t)) = g(z) \ne 0$. For $h$ and $f$ as in the statement, the theorem gives the matching of $h$ and $f$ on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$, with the measures normalized on the maximal compact subgroups, and this is the matching on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_v)$. $\square$

**Definition 7.9.5 (Local unit matching).** Let $E/F$ be a cyclic extension of number fields of prime degree and $n > 0$. We say that the units match if, for every finite place $v$ of $F$ unramified in $E$, the characteristic function of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_{E,v})$ matches the characteristic function of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_v)$ with constant 1.

This is the fundamental lemma for the unit elements. We keep it as a hypothesis here; the next section discharges it.

**Theorem 7.9.6 (Matching at all unramified places).** Assume that the units match for every cyclic extension of totally complex number fields of prime degree. Let $E/F$ be such an extension, $n > 0$, and for a finite place $w$ of $E$ let $f(w)$ be its residue degree over $F$. Let $\kappa$ be a finite set of places of $E$ that lie over places of $F$ unramified in $E$, let $t_0(w)$ be a multiset of $n$ nonzero numbers for each $w \in \kappa$, and let $D \in \mathbb{N}$. Then there are polynomials $\gamma_w$, $w \in \kappa$, with $\gamma_w(e(t_0(w))) \ne 0$, with the following property. Let $p_w$ have degree at most $D$ for $w \in \kappa$, and let $v$ be a finite place of $F$ unramified in $E$. For $w \mid v$ let $h_w$ be spherical on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$ with transform $\gamma_wp_w$ if $w \in \kappa$, and the unit if $w \notin \kappa$. Let $f$ be spherical on $\mathrm{GL}_n(F_v)$ with
$$\mathcal{S}(f) = \prod_{w \mid v,\ w \in \kappa}(\gamma_wp_w)\big(e_1(x^{f(w)}), \dots, e_n(x^{f(w)})\big).$$
Then $\bigotimes_{w \mid v} h_w$ matches $f$ with constant 1.

Lean: `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.GM.locAll`.

*Proof.* For $w \in \kappa$ over a split place put $\gamma_w = 1$. For $w \in \kappa$ over a place that does not split, take $\gamma_w$ from the [matching at an inert place](/entry/loc-hecke-inert/) for $t_0(w)$ and $D$. Now fix $v$. If $v$ splits, then $f(w) = 1$ for all $w \mid v$, $E_v$ is a product of $l$ copies of $F_v$, and the matching of $\bigotimes h_w$ with the spherical function whose transform is the product of the transforms is the split case, which we take from the companion development. If $v$ does not split, there is one place $w$ over $v$ and $f(w) = l$. If $w \in \kappa$, the choice of $\gamma_w$ gives the matching. If $w \notin \kappa$, then $\mathcal{S}(f) = 1$; the Satake transform is injective, so $f$ is the unit, $h_w$ is the unit, and the matching of the units gives the claim. $\square$

**Remark 7.9.7 (Formalization).** The local matching is `Loc.Match` (Match.lean:13); Match.lean:23 proves, as an example, that $0$ matches $0$. The Iwahori criterion and the Iwahori matching are `deepAbs` and `thMatch` in the folder of DeepHecke.lean:81; we have not unfolded the proof of `deepAbs`. The split case is `locHeckeSplit`, and the statements `LocUnit`, `LocHeckeSplit` and `LocHeckeInert` are in GeneralizedMeasures/LocHeckeInert.lean (lines 140 to 164); `LocAll` is HeckeFinAlt.lean:142. In LocAll.lean:82 the unit matching is used at one point only (line 139), at a non-split place whose place above is not in $\kappa$. The name "deep" seems to refer to the lemma `depth` that `deepAbs` calls. Also listed here is `PositivityTheory.Rg.Bc.ArchbcB.B.archimedean_base_change_first` (ArchimedeanBaseChangeFirst.lean:29). It is the proof of the [fundamental lemma at an inert place](/entry/m1/) for the unitary comparison, and `m1` (line 63 of the same file) is defined as it. Despite its name, it concerns a finite inert place of a CM field, not an archimedean place; its inputs are statements of the companion development (the unramified quadratic datum at an inert place, the lattice sums for the stable and $\kappa$-orbital integrals of the units, and the unit lemma for products of unitary groups), and its last step is $\sqrt{(q^2)^{-v}} = q^{-v}$.

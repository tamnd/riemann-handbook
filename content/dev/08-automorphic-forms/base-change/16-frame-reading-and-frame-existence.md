## Frame reading and frame existence

The base change of an automorphic representation $\pi$ of $U(n)$ is found by a comparison of trace formulas run on a frame: a choice of weights, an admissible character, a section, a local datum, a chain and archimedean test functions, all adapted to $\pi$. A run of the frame adds powers of local operators, and the [growth bound for traces on a run frame](/entry/frame-word-identity-frame-bound/) controls the spectral sides. This section proves two things. First, every $\pi$ has a frame. Second, when a character of the algebra of operators of a run is read off from a vector, it produces candidate data: a chain index, two trees, an isobaric datum $D$ of rank $n$, and a nonzero function, such that $D$ has the infinitesimal character of $\pi$ and describes $\pi$ at almost every split place. This is the reading step of the [base change step](/step/base-change-n/).

We keep the notation of Sections 05, 08, 12, 14 and 15. Let $E$ be a CM field with maximal real subfield $F$ in which every finite place is unramified over $F$. A section $\mathrm{Sec}$ contains exactly one place above each finite place of $F$, so every split place is in $\mathrm{Sec}$ or conjugate to a place of $\mathrm{Sec}$. The limit argument of Section 08, in the form used here, provides for all ranks $a$, $b$ and module families finite-dimensional type spaces $W_1$, $W_2$, finite sets of types $\Omega^1$, $\Omega^2$ and, for every pair of open levels, finite sets of levels $\Theta^1$, $\Theta^2$. We fix such a choice. For trees $s_1$, $s_2$ of ranks $a$, $b$, isobaric data $D_a$, $D_b$, $D$ and an admissible $(\mu, e)$, we say that $(s_1, s_2, D_a, D_b, D)$ is a context for $\mu$ if $s_1$, $s_2$ are good and untwisted with parts $D_a$, $D_b$, and $D$ is a join of $(D_a, \mu^b)$ and $(D_b, \mu^a)$, as in [Section 7.15.2](/entry/reading-split-holds/).

**Definition 7.16.1 (Frame).** Let $n \ge 1$, $\pi$ an automorphic representation of $U(n)$ and $S$ a finite set of places. A frame for $(\pi, S)$ consists of:

1. dominant weights $\lambda_v$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n$, one for each archimedean place $v$, such that $\pi$ has weight $\lambda_v$ at the chosen embedding of $v$;
2. an admissible pair $(\mu, e)$, as in [Section 7.12.5](/entry/chain-ex-holds/);
3. a section $\mathrm{Sec}$ and a [local datum](/entry/positivity-theory-rg-bc-b-f-match-all-match-lmb2/) $d_0$ compatible with the places, with $T = \emptyset$, characters $\mu_w$ the local components of $\mu$, and uniformizers $\varpi_w$ with $\bar\varpi_w = \varpi_w$;
4. conditions on the places: every $w \in S_0$ is a split place of $\mathrm{Sec}$, every split place of $S$ lies in $S_0$, every $w \in \mathrm{Bad}$ lies in $\mathrm{Sec}$ (and if $w$ is not split, $\pi$ is ramified at $w$), and at every other place of $\mathrm{Sec}$ both $\pi$ and $\mu$ are unramified;
5. at the places of $\mathrm{Bad}$: at a split $w$ a level $K^B_w$ of $U_w$ and $\mathrm{fix}_{a,w}$ the twisted constant term along $\mu_w$ of the normalized indicator of $K^B_w$; at an inert $w$ an integer $k_w \ge 1$, $K^B_w$ the principal congruence subgroup of level $\varpi^{k_w}$, and $\mathrm{fix}_{a,w}$ the index $[K_{U,w} : K^B_w]$ times the function of part 1 of the matching theorem of Section 05; and at every $w \in \mathrm{Bad}$, $\mathbf{1}_{K^B_w}$ normalized matches $\mathrm{fix}_{a,w}$ with the character $\mu_w$;
6. a level family $K_U$ of $U(n)$ such that $\pi$ has level $K'$ on $\mathrm{Sec}$ for every $K' \le K_U$, and levels at $S_0$ as in the local datum;
7. translators $u_1(a)$ of $\mathrm{GL}_a$ and $u_2(a)$ of $\mathrm{GL}_{n-a}$ for every $a \le n$;
8. a chain $C$ for $(\mu, e, \lambda)$ and, for each chain index $i$, archimedean data $A_i$ for $(l_{1,i}, l_{2,i})$: module families, multipliers, Haar measures and the parameters $\chi_a$, $\chi_b$ of the multipliers;
9. a compact open level $K = (K_w)$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ with $K_w = \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_w)$ at the places not conjugate to a place of $S_0 \cup \mathrm{Bad}$, such that block upper triangular elements of $K_w$ at $w \in S_0 \cup \mathrm{Bad}$ have Levi part in $L_{a,w}$ and are killed by $\mu_w(\det{}^{n-a} \times \det{}^a)$, and similarly at the conjugate places;
10. finite-dimensional type spaces $W(N)$ for every $N$, such that for every chain index $i$ and every context $(s_1, s_2, D_a, D_b, D)$ whose leaves of positive rank contain functions of types in $\Omega^1$, $\Omega^2$, every space of $D$ has $K_\infty$-types in $W$.

**Theorem 7.16.2 (Existence of frames).** Let $E$ be a CM field in which every finite place is unramified over $F$, let $n \ge 1$, $\pi$ an automorphic representation of $U(n)$, and $S$ a set of at most one place. Then $(\pi, S)$ has a frame.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.Ex0.frameEx0` (also `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.Ex0.frameEx0_plug`, the same result with parts 1 and 2 of the matching theorem of Section 05 as hypotheses).

*Proof.* We choose the pieces in order.

1. Weights. For each archimedean $v$, $\pi$ has a weight at the chosen embedding of $v$, by the theory of [highest weights](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching).
2. Character. There is an admissible $(\mu, e)$ that is unramified at every place where the conditions of the local datum require it.
3. Section and local datum. There are a section and a local datum with the conditions 3 to 6; the bad places use parts 1 and 2 of the [matching theorem](/entry/positivity-theory-rg-bc-b-f-match-all-match-lmb2/).
4. Translators exist for every $\mathrm{GL}_a$.
5. Chain. [The existence of chains](/entry/chain-ex-holds/) gives $C$ for $(\mu, e, \lambda)$.
6. Archimedean data exist for every $(l_{1,i}, l_{2,i})$, built from the multipliers and kernels of Section 02.
7. Level. Given the finite set $S_0 \cup \mathrm{Bad}$ of places of $\mathrm{Sec}$, the levels $L_{a,w}$ and the characters $\mu_w$, there is a compact open level $K$ with condition 9.
8. Types. There are type spaces $W$ with condition 10.

The existence statements in 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 are taken from the companion development, with the matching statements of Section 05 supplied in the unconditional form. $\square$

A run of a frame on a finite set $T$ of places replaces $d_0$ by a local datum $d$ with $T$ nonempty and the same data elsewhere, chooses a power $m \ge 1$, a family $\sigma$ of local representations of the unitary group that are components of $\pi$ on $\mathrm{Sec}$, and a nonzero vector $v$ in a finite-dimensional Hecke module. For each chain index $i$ it gives a block: a Hilbert space $H_i$ of functions on $\mathrm{GL}_{a_i}(\mathbb{A}_E) \times \mathrm{GL}_{n-a_i}(\mathbb{A}_E)$, commuting normal operators $g_x$ on $H_i$ (tensor kernels as in Section 14, and Hecke operators at the places of $d$), and an operator $\beta_i$ built from the matrices of the local datum. The same generators act on $\mathbb{C}^d$, and $v$ is a joint eigenvector there.

**Definition 7.16.3 (Reading condition).** Let $Q$ be a statement. A run reads $Q$ if the following holds for every chain index $i$ and every $*$-character $\chi$ of the $C^*$-algebra generated by the $g_x$ on $H_i$ and $X_i = \beta_i^{*m}\beta_i^m$: if $|\chi(y)| \le \|y\|$ for all $y$, if $\chi(g) = c$ whenever $g$ acts on $v$ by a nonzero scalar $c$, and if $\chi(X_i) \ne 0$, then $Q$.

**Definition 7.16.4 (Candidate).** Let $d$ be a local datum and $i$ a chain index, with $a = a_i$. A candidate for $(d, i)$ is a context $(s_1, s_2, D_a, D_b, D)$ for $\mu$ such that the leaves of positive rank of $s_1$ and $s_2$ contain nonzero functions of types in $(\Theta^1, \Omega^1)$ and $(\Theta^2, \Omega^2)$, $\mathrm{space}(s_1)$ and $\mathrm{space}(s_2)$ have the infinitesimal characters of the parameters $\chi_a$, $\chi_b$ of $A_i$ at every embedding, and the span of the products of $\mathrm{space}(s_1)$ and $\mathrm{space}(s_2)$ contains a nonzero function of type $(K_1(d), K_2(d), W_1, W_2)$, where $K_1(d)$ and $K_2(d)$ are the levels generated by the local levels of $d$ on $\mathrm{Sec}$.

**Theorem 7.16.5 (Good places).** Let $a \le n$, $(\mu, e)$ admissible and $\pi$ an automorphic representation of $U(n)$. Let $\mathrm{Sec}$ be a section, $d$ a local datum compatible with the places with characters $\mu_w$ and uniformizers $\varpi_w$, with $\mu$ unramified at the places of $\mathrm{Sec}$ outside $S_0 \cup \mathrm{Bad}$. Let $\sigma_w$ be components of $\pi$ at the places $w \in \mathrm{Sec}$ with nonzero $K_{U,w}$-fixed vectors. Let $(s_1, s_2, D_a, D_b, D)$ be a context for $\mu$, and let $G \ne 0$ be a family of functions in the span of the products of $\mathrm{space}(s_1)$ and $\mathrm{space}(s_2)$, of the level of $d$ on $\mathrm{Sec}$. Call $w \in \mathrm{Sec}$ good if it is split, not in $T \cup S_0 \cup \mathrm{Bad}$, and $\mu_w$ is unramified. Assume that for every good $w$, every $k$ and $\varepsilon \in \{0, 1\}$, the frame matrix of the local datum with the entry at $w$ replaced by the twisted constant term of $T_k$ (in the plain component if $\varepsilon = 0$ and in the $\theta$-twisted component if $\varepsilon = 1$) acts on $G$ by the trace of $\sigma_w$ on $T_k$. Then $D$ describes $\pi$ at all but finitely many split places.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.FG.good_level_holds`.

*Proof.* Let $w$ be a good place. Since $\sigma_w$ is a component of $\pi$ with a nonzero spherical vector, it has a Satake parameter $B$, so $\pi$ has Satake parameter $B$ at $w$ and the trace of $\sigma_w$ on $T_k$ is $\lambda_k(B)$. At a good place the frame matrix with the identity permutation reduces to its entry at $w$, and the action of that entry on $G$ is the action $T_k^{\mu_w}[w]$ (for $\varepsilon = 0$) or $T_k^{\mu_w}[w, \theta]$ (for $\varepsilon = 1$) of Section 15; Since $G$ has the level of $d$, it is invariant under $K_w$, and under $K_u$ at the conjugate place $u$, because invariance under the $\theta$-twisted action at $w$ is invariance at $u$. Part 1 of [the reading of Satake parameters](/entry/reading-split-holds/) shows that $D$ describes $\pi$ at $w$, and part 2 that it describes $\pi$ at $u$. Every split place is in $\mathrm{Sec}$ or conjugate to a place of $\mathrm{Sec}$, and only finitely many places of $\mathrm{Sec}$ are not good, since $T$, $S_0$, $\mathrm{Bad}$ are finite and $\mu$ is unramified almost everywhere. Hence $D$ describes $\pi$ at all but finitely many split places. $\square$

**Theorem 7.16.6 (Reading good places on a run).** Assume that for every section and local datum, the levels of $d$ are levels and every function of type $(K_1(d), K_2(d), W_1, W_2)$ has the level of $d$ on $\mathrm{Sec}$. Let $F$ be a run of a frame, $i$ a chain index, $(s_1, s_2, D_a, D_b, D)$ a context, and $G \ne 0$ a family in the span of the products of $\mathrm{space}(s_1)$ and $\mathrm{space}(s_2)$, of the level of $d$, on which the operators of the run attached to the good places act by the unitary eigenvalues. Then $D$ describes $\pi$ at all but finitely many split places.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.FG.frGood_of`.

*Proof.* The frame and the run supply the hypotheses of the theorem on good places (section, character, uniformizers, components $\sigma_w$ with fixed vectors, archimedean data), the assumption supplies the levels, and the eigenvalue hypothesis is the same. $\square$

**Theorem 7.16.7 (Block candidates).** Assume the following three statements.

1. Characters. For every run, $Q$ is read as soon as $Q$ follows from every character $\chi$ of a block algebra that is bounded by the norm, takes the value $c(x)$ on each generator $g_x$, where $c(x)$ is the eigenvalue of $g_x$ on the distinguished coordinate of $v$, and does not vanish on $X_i$.
2. Limits. For every run with its structural conditions, every chain index $i$ and every such character $\chi$, there is a limit output: good untwisted trees $s_1$, $s_2$ with leaves of types in $(\Theta^j, \Omega^j)$, Hermitian parameters $\chi'_a$, $\chi'_b$ giving the infinitesimal characters of the trees, and a nonzero family $F$ in the span of the products, of type $(K_1(d), K_2(d), W_1, W_2)$, on which every finite generator acts by its value under $\chi$, divided by the value of the reference generator.
3. Identification. For every limit output, the infinitesimal characters $\chi'_a$, $\chi'_b$ agree with those of the archimedean data $A_i$ at every embedding.

Then for every run $F$ of a frame for $\pi$ satisfying the structural conditions, $F$ reads the following statement: there are a chain index $i$, a candidate $(s_1, s_2, D_a, D_b, D)$ for $(d, i)$ and a family $G$ in the span of the products of $\mathrm{space}(s_1)$ and $\mathrm{space}(s_2)$, of the level of $d$, such that the $m$-th power of the convolution by the test matrix of $\beta_i$ does not kill $G$, $D$ has infinitesimal character $\chi(\lambda')$ at every embedding at which $\pi$ has weight $\lambda'$, and $D$ describes $\pi$ at all but finitely many split places.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.BC.block_candidates_of_inputs`.

*Proof.* By statement 1 it suffices to treat a character $\chi$ of the block algebra of $i$ with the prescribed values on the generators and $\chi(X_i) \ne 0$. Statement 2 gives a limit output $(s_1, s_2, \chi'_a, \chi'_b, F)$. By statement 3 its parameters are those of $A_i$, so the trees have the infinitesimal characters required of a candidate. Good untwisted trees with infinitesimal characters have parts $D_a$ and $D_b$, and a join $D$ exists. This gives a context, and with the types of the leaves and of $F$, a candidate. Take $G = F$, indexed by the block. It has the level of $d$ by the level reading statement. Since $\chi(X_i) \ne 0$, the eigenvalue of $(\beta_i^{m-1})^2$ on $F$ is nonzero, and a statement on powers of convolutions, derived from the multiplicativity of convolution of finite test matrices, gives that the $m$-th power of the convolution by $\beta_i$ does not kill $F$. The generators attached to good places act on $F$ by their values under $\chi$, which are the unitary eigenvalues, so the [theorem on good places on a run](/entry/fr-good-of/), applied with the level reading statement, shows that $D$ describes $\pi$ at almost every split place. Finally, the parameters of the trees are those of the chain, which add up, after the shifts by $e_v$, to $\chi(\lambda_v)$; a statement on weights of joins then gives the infinitesimal character of $D$. $\square$

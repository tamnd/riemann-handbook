## Elliptic terms as products of local integrals

By [the elliptic expansion](/entry/ell-formula2-j/), the truncated twisted trace for $\mathrm{GL}_a \times \mathrm{GL}_b$ is a sum of adelic twisted orbital integrals over the pairs of regular elliptic classes. This section writes each of these orbital integrals as a product: a global mass, an archimedean character value, and a product of local twisted orbital integrals over a set of finite places with one place above each place of the real subfield. The archimedean test functions are Lefschetz functions, whose twisted orbital integrals at elliptic elements are character values. At the finite places the computation is an Euler product for twisted orbital integrals on the restricted product, and a count of double cosets. The result is the geometric expansion that the global comparison uses on the general linear side, on the way to the step [BaseChangeN](/step/base-change-n/). For background see [the twisted trace formula and base change](/learn/trace-formula/#the-twisted-trace-formula-and-base-change).

We keep the notation of [the previous section](/entry/class-vanish/). The field $E$ is CM with maximal real subfield $F$, and every finite place of $E$ is unramified over $F$. Let $\mathbb{A}_f$ be the finite adeles of $E$, $K_f = \prod_w \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_w)$, and $G_f = \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_f) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{A}_f)$ with the involution $\theta$ of the previous section in each factor. We also use a second involution $\theta_E$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$, the one that defines the unitary group. A translator is an element $u \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ with $\theta_E(g) = u\,\theta(g)\,u^{-1}$ for all $g$ and $u\,\theta(u) = 1$. Then $\delta \mapsto \delta u$ carries $\theta_E$-twisted classes to $\theta$-twisted classes.

**Definition 7.7.1 (Sections and local data).** A section is a set $S$ of finite places of $E$ that contains exactly one place above each finite place of $F$. A local test family is a family $\varphi = (\varphi_w)$ of functions on $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_w) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E_w)$ that is bi-invariant under a $\theta$-stable level at every $w$ and equal to the unit of $K_a \times K_b$ at almost every $w$. For two families $\varphi^1$ and $\varphi^2$ put
$$\varphi^\flat(y) = \prod_{w \in S}\varphi^1_w(y_w)\cdot\begin{cases}\varphi^2_w(\theta(y)_w) & w \text{ split},\\ 1 & w \text{ inert},\end{cases}$$
for $y \in G_f$, and let $s_w = \varphi^1_w * \varphi^2_w$ (convolution over $K_a \times K_b$) if $w$ is split and $s_w = \varphi^1_w$ if $w$ is inert. For $\delta \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ let $\delta_w$ be the image of $\delta\theta_E(\delta)$ in $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$ if $w$ is split, and the image of $\delta$ if $w$ is inert.

At a split place $w$ of $E$ above $v$, the two places above $v$ are exchanged by $c$, and a function of the pair of components is the same as the product of $\varphi^1_w$ and $\varphi^2_w \circ \theta$. This is why the split places carry a convolution in $s_w$.

**Definition 7.7.2 (Mass and twisted orbital integral).** For a group $H$ with involution $\theta$, a subgroup $\Gamma$ of $H_\delta$ and the compact part $C_\delta$ of $H_\delta$, the mass is
$$m(\Gamma, \delta) = \frac{\#(\Gamma\backslash H_\delta/C_\delta)}{\#(\Gamma \cap C_\delta)}.$$
The twisted orbital integral $O^\theta_\delta(f)$ with respect to a compact open $K$ is the double coset sum of [the fundamental lemma at an inert place](/entry/m1/). For a twisted class of $\delta \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ we take $\Gamma$ to be the image of $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ and write $m(\delta)$.

**Theorem 7.7.3 (Euler product).** Let $S$ be a section, $\varphi^1$ and $\varphi^2$ local test families, and $\delta_1 \in \mathrm{GL}_a(E)$, $\delta_2 \in \mathrm{GL}_b(E)$ with $\theta_E$-norms whose characteristic polynomials are elliptic, that is monic, separable and with all roots of absolute value $1$ under every embedding into $\mathbb{C}$. Then
$$O^\theta_{(\delta_1, \delta_2)}(\varphi^\flat) = \prod_{w \in S}O^\theta_{(\delta_{1,w}, \delta_{2,w})}(s_w),$$
where the left side is the twisted orbital integral on $G_f$ for $K_a \times K_b$, and the factor at $w$ is the local one on $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_w) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E_w)$.

*Proof.* Group the places of $E$ by the places $v$ of $F$ below them. Then $G_f$ is the restricted product of the groups $Y_v = \prod_{w \mid v}\mathrm{GL}_a(E_w) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E_w)$, with $\theta$ acting on each $Y_v$ and preserving its maximal compact subgroup, and $\varphi^\flat$ is the product of functions $f_v$ on $Y_v$. We use the following inputs, proved in the companion development: the existence of a continuous conjugation on $\prod_{w\mid v}E_w$; the restricted product structure; the regrouping $\varphi^\flat = \prod_v f_v$; the local comparison, which says that the twisted orbital integral of $f_v$ on $Y_v$ equals the local factor at the place $w \in S$ above $v$; the local hypotheses (commutative twisted centralizers, finiteness of the relevant double cosets, and, at almost every $v$, $f_v$ is the unit, $\delta \in K_v$ and the twisted orbit of $\delta$ meets $K_v$ in one twisted centralizer coset); and the reindexing of products over $S$ as products over the places of $F$. With these, the twisted orbital integral on a restricted product is the product of the local ones, because almost all local factors are $1$. The local comparison at an inert $v$ is a direct identification, since there is one place above $v$. At a split $v$, the twisted orbital integral of $\varphi^1_w \otimes \varphi^2_{\bar w}\circ\theta$ on the pair of places is the ordinary orbital integral of the convolution at the norm $\delta_w$. $\square$

**Theorem 7.7.4 (Finite half).** Under the hypotheses of the Euler product theorem, let $\Gamma_{\delta}$ be the intersection of the image of $\mathrm{GL}_a(E) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(E)$ with the twisted centralizer of $\delta = (\delta_1, \delta_2)$ in $G_f$. Then only finitely many double cosets $q \in \Gamma_\delta\backslash G_f/K_f$ meet the support of $x \mapsto \varphi^\flat(x^{-1}\delta\theta(x))$, the groups $\Gamma_\delta \cap xK_fx^{-1}$ are finite, and
$$\sum_{q \in \Gamma_\delta\backslash G_f/K_f}\frac{\mathrm{avg}_{k \in K_f}\,\varphi^\flat\big((qk)^{-1}\delta\theta(qk)\big)}{\#(\Gamma_\delta \cap qK_fq^{-1})} = m(\delta_1)\,m(\delta_2)\prod_{w \in S}O^\theta_{(\delta_{1,w}, \delta_{2,w})}(s_w).$$

*Proof.* We use a double coset count: for a group with an involution, an open compact $K$, an element $\delta$ whose twisted centralizer is commutative and meets each conjugate of $K$ in a subgroup of finite index of the compact part, and a subgroup $\Gamma$ of the twisted centralizer with finite intersections with the conjugates of $K$, the left side equals $m(\Gamma, \delta)\,O^\theta_\delta(f)$. This follows by sorting the double cosets $\Gamma\backslash G_f/K_f$ into the fibres of the map to $H_\delta\backslash G_f/K_f$: each fibre is in bijection with $\Gamma\backslash H_\delta/(H_\delta \cap qKq^{-1})$, and the weights add up to the mass times the index $[C_\delta : C_\delta \cap qKq^{-1}]$. The hypotheses hold for the rational $\delta$ by the finiteness statements for elliptic elements, and for the support by the finiteness of the relevant double cosets. The mass of a product is the product of the masses. Finally the twisted orbital integral of $\varphi^\flat$ is the Euler product of the previous theorem. $\square$

Now we state the global result. For $v$ an archimedean place and $l$ a dominant weight, let $\mathrm{ch}_l(Q)$ be the trace of the representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ attached to $l$ at any element with characteristic polynomial $Q$. For an elliptic polynomial $P$ over $E$ and a family $l = (l_v)$ put $\mathrm{ch}_l(P) = \prod_v\overline{\mathrm{ch}_{l_v}(P^v)}$, where $P^v$ is the image of $P$ under the embedding at $v$.

**Definition 7.7.5 (Lefschetz function).** A function $\psi$ on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{C}) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{C})$ is a Lefschetz function for $(l_1, l_2)$ with value $c \in \mathbb{C}$ if it is continuous with compact support and, for all $d_1$ and $d_2$ whose norms have separable characteristic polynomials $Q_i$ with roots of absolute value $1$,
$$\int\psi\big(x_1^{-1}d_1\theta(x_1), x_2^{-1}d_2\theta(x_2)\big)\,dx = c\;\overline{\mathrm{ch}_{l_1}(Q_1)}\;\overline{\mathrm{ch}_{l_2}(Q_2)}.$$

**Theorem 7.7.6 (Elliptic terms).** Fix Haar measures. There is $c \ne 0$ with the following property. Let $S$ be a section, $u_1$ and $u_2$ translators, and for each archimedean $v$ let $\psi_v$ be a Lefschetz function for $(l_{1,v}, l_{2,v})$ with value $c_v$. Let $\varphi^1$ and $\varphi^2$ be local test families, and let $\Phi$ be the function on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}) \times \mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb{A})$ whose value at $x$ is $\prod_v\psi_v(x_v)$ times $\varphi^\flat$ at the finite component of $x\,(u_1^{-1}, u_2^{-1})$. Then
$$\sum_{\text{ell}}O(\Phi, \delta_1, \delta_2) = c\prod_v c_v\sum_{(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)}m(\gamma_1)\,m(\gamma_2)\,\mathrm{ch}_{l_1}(P_{\gamma_1})\,\mathrm{ch}_{l_2}(P_{\gamma_2})\prod_{w \in S}O^\theta_{(\gamma_{1,w}, \gamma_{2,w})}(s_w),$$
where the left sum runs over the pairs of regular elliptic $\theta$-twisted classes, as in [the elliptic expansion](/entry/ell-formula2-j/), and the right sum runs over the pairs of $\theta_E$-twisted classes whose norms $P_{\gamma_i}$ have elliptic characteristic polynomials.

The first name is the form of the theorem with three inputs: $K_f$ is open and compact, $\varphi^\flat$ is invariant under twisted conjugation by an open subgroup, and the Finite half theorem. The second name is the theorem with these inputs supplied.

*Proof.* The class map $\gamma \mapsto \gamma u$ is a bijection from $\theta_E$-twisted classes to $\theta$-twisted classes, and $\gamma u$ is regular elliptic exactly when the norm of $\gamma$ has an elliptic characteristic polynomial; at each archimedean place the characteristic polynomial of the norm of $\gamma u$ is the image of $P_\gamma$. The orbital term is an invariant of the twisted class, so the left sum can be indexed by the $\gamma$. It remains to compute one term. Write $\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{A}) = \mathrm{GL}(E_\infty) \times G_f$. The function $\Phi$ at $x^{-1}\delta\theta(x)$ splits as the product of the archimedean factors at the components $x_v$ and of $\varphi^\flat$ at the finite component, evaluated at $\gamma = \delta u^{-1}$. The rational twisted centralizer of $\delta$ maps injectively to $G_f$ with image $\Gamma_\gamma$, and into the archimedean twisted centralizers. Elliptic elements at the archimedean places have closed twisted orbits with proper orbit maps, because an elliptic element is twisted conjugate to a diagonal element with entries of absolute value $1$ and distinct squares. So the quotient integral splits into an archimedean integral and a sum over $\Gamma_\gamma\backslash G_f/K_f$. The archimedean integral is $\prod_v c_v\,\mathrm{ch}_{l_1}(P_{\gamma_1})\,\mathrm{ch}_{l_2}(P_{\gamma_2})$ up to the constant $c$ from the Haar measures, by the Lefschetz property. The finite sum is given by the Finite half theorem. The continuity of $\Phi$ along twisted orbits, which the class invariance needs, comes from the continuity of the $\psi_v$ and the level of $\varphi^\flat$. $\square$

**Remark 7.7.7 (Formalization).** The statements are `EulerFin` (EulerFinOf.lean:98), `FinHalf` (GlobalCharacterOfInputs.lean:62) and `G4` (IsTransl.lean:82). The proof of `eulerFin_holds` (FinLevelHolds.lean:30) is `finite_euler_product_of` applied to six inputs whose shape is `EulerFinOf'` (LocCompareSplit.lean:100); the input for the restricted product structure is called `finite_restriction_vanishing` and the regrouping input is called `aux_statement_21`, names that do not describe them. The proposition `EulerFinOf` (EulerFinOf.lean:106) states another route to `EulerFin` and is not used by it. `finHalf_holds` (FinLevelHolds.lean:33) applies `finite_half_of_twist` to `DcCount_pf` (DcCount.lean:44), the mass product formula and the finiteness inputs. `global_character_of_finite_inputs` (GlobalCharacterOfFiniteInputs.lean:31) chains `termOf_holds`, `orbInvOf`, `adelContOf`, `classBij` and `polEq`; its proof above follows these steps, which we did not unfold. `global_character_holds` (GlobalCharacterHolds.lean:6) is `global_character_of_finite_inputs kfOC_holds finLevel_holds finHalf_holds`, and `kfOC_holds` and `finLevel_holds` are wrappers for `GlobalTheory.T` statements. A different theorem with the same short name, `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.JSBound.global_character_holds` (MemUnipBOfMemUnipotent.lean:104), proves a different statement that is also called `G4`; several unrelated statements named `G4` exist in the library. The character `chAt` uses the representation `GlobalTheory.Branching.sqRep`, which we did not unfold. The masses are complex numbers defined as a ratio of cardinalities, so they are $0$ when one of the sets is infinite.

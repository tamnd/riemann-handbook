## Good vectors on the definite space and scalars at conjugate places

This section proves two facts about the theta data of a unitary dual pair with one definite member. First, on the definite space the level groups act freely, so the space of algebraic modular forms is a free module over the group algebra of each level group, and the good vectors fill all of it except a share $\delta$ that is exponentially small in the size parameter. This is an input of [the bundles with large unramified exponents](/entry/member-bundles-large-unramified-exponents-alt/). Second, at each archimedean place other than the base place, the theta lift restricted to a part is a scalar multiple of an isometry for the majorant. This is an input of [the twisted scalar conjugation](/entry/twisted-scalar-conjugation/).

### Algebraic modular forms on a definite space

Let $E/F$ be a CM extension, and let $W$ be a positive definite Hermitian space over $E$ of dimension $r$, with unitary group $G_W$ and a unimodular lattice $\Lambda$. Then $G_W(F \otimes \mathbb{R})$ is compact. For an open compact subgroup $K \le G_W(\mathbb{A}_f)$, the *class set* is $\mathrm{Cl}(K) = G_W(F) \backslash G_W(\mathbb{A}_f) / K$. For a class $c = G_W(F) x K$, let $\Gamma_c = G_W(F) \cap xKx^{-1}$ be its stabilizer. It is a discrete subgroup of a compact group, hence finite. Let $\rho$ be the archimedean representation fixed by the discrete series model (on the definite space it is a finite-dimensional irreducible representation). The space of *algebraic modular forms* of level $K$ and weight $\rho$ is the space of functions $f : G_W(\mathbb{A}_f) \to \rho$ with $f(\gamma x k) = \rho(\gamma) f(x)$ ([definite quaternion algebras](/learn/automorphic-forms/#definite-quaternion-algebras) treat the case of $D^\times$). When every $\Gamma_c$ is trivial, a form is determined by its values at representatives of the classes, and these values are arbitrary. So the dimension is $d_K = \#\mathrm{Cl}(K) \cdot \dim\rho$.

In the theta datum, $W$ is the last weight space of the chain, and $K = K_1$ is the level of the self-dual frame with the flag condition at the level places and the trivial character. For each of the two levels $b$ of the datum there is a finite group $P_b$, a product of groups $\mathrm{GL}_r(k_{\mathfrak{P}})$ over the type places, with a reduction map from a larger level group onto $P_b$. The group $P_b$ acts on the space of forms of level $K_1$ by right translation. Let $d_0(b)$ be the dimension of the forms of level $b$, that is, of the $\chi_b$-isotypic vectors of $P_b$, and $d_{\mathrm{good}}(b)$ the dimension of the good part of level $b$.

**Lemma 6.8.1 (No fixed points).** Let $\mathfrak{P}$ be a prime of $E$ of residue characteristic $p$ with $p > [E:\mathbb{Q}]\, r + 1$. Let $u \in G_W(F)$ preserve $\Lambda$, and assume that the reduction of $u$ at $\mathfrak{P}$ is unipotent. Then $u = 1$.

*Proof.* The group of elements of $G_W(F)$ that preserve $\Lambda$ is finite, so $u$ has finite order $m$. Suppose $u \ne 1$, and replace $u$ by a power of prime order $\ell$. The eigenvalues of $u$ are $\ell$-th roots of unity, and one of them is primitive. It lies in an extension of $E$ of degree at most $r$, so $\ell - 1 \le [E:\mathbb{Q}]\, r$ and $\ell < p$. A unipotent matrix over $k_{\mathfrak{P}}$ of order prime to $p$ is $1$. So $u$ is in the kernel of reduction modulo $\mathfrak{P}$, which has no elements of order $\ell$ for $\ell \ne p$. This contradicts $u \ne 1$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.8.2 (Free action on the definite space).** Let the theta datum satisfy the hypotheses of a Hida setting. The type primes $g_k$ of the datum satisfy $g_k > [E:\mathbb{Q}]\, r + 1$. On the last weight space:

- the class set is finite and nonempty, every stabilizer $\Gamma_c$ is trivial, and $d_K = \#\mathrm{Cl}(K_1) \cdot \dim\rho \ge 1$;
- for each level $b$, the reduction map is onto $P_b$, and an element of the level group that fixes some class has trivial reduction;
- for each level $b$, the space of forms is a free $\mathbb{C}[P_b]$-module of rank $N$ with $\#P_b \cdot N = d_K$, and $\#P_b \cdot d_0(b) = d_K$ with $d_0(b) \ge 1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.DefFree.goodVectorsDefinite` (the free part).

*Proof.* An element of $\Gamma_c$ preserves the lattice $x\Lambda$ and has unipotent reduction at a type place, by the flag condition in $K_1$. By the lemma it is $1$, so $\Gamma_c$ is trivial and $d_K = \#\mathrm{Cl}(K_1) \cdot \dim\rho$, as computed above. The same argument shows that an element $\kappa$ of the level group that fixes a class $c$ has trivial reduction: if $x\kappa = \gamma x k$ with $k \in K_1$, then $\gamma = x\kappa k^{-1} x^{-1}$ is in $G_W(F)$, preserves $x\Lambda$, and has the reduction of $\kappa$ at the type places, which is unipotent; so $\gamma = 1$ and $\kappa = k$. Now let $g \in P_b$ be different from $1$, and let $\kappa$ be a lift. Then right translation by $\kappa$ moves every class, so it permutes the class representatives without fixed points, and its trace on the forms is $0$. A representation of a finite group whose character vanishes off the identity is a multiple of the regular representation. So the forms are free over $\mathbb{C}[P_b]$, of rank $N = d_K / \#P_b$. The forms of level $b$ are the image of the idempotent of the character $\chi_b$ of $P_b$, which has rank $1$ in each copy of $\mathbb{C}[P_b]$. So $d_0(b) = N \ge 1$. $\square$

### The good share

The good part is defined through the irreducible representations of $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{F}_g)$. For a monic squarefree polynomial $f \in \mathbb{F}_g[X]$ of degree $r$ with $f(0) \ne 0$, Green's theory gives an irreducible generic representation $\tau_f$ of $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{F}_g)$, of dimension $\prod_{i=1}^{r}(g^i - 1)/\#T_f(\mathbb{F}_g)$, where $T_f$ is the torus of $f$. The *good idempotent* at a type prime is the sum of the central idempotents of the $\tau_f$ for $f$ in a good set of labels. The good part of level $b$ is the image of the tensor product $c$ of the good idempotents at the two type primes, applied to the forms of level $b$.

**Theorem 6.8.3 (Good share on the definite space).** Assume Green's classification of the generic cuspidal series of $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{F}_g)$. For each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $x_0$ with the following property. Let the size data have exponent $\varepsilon$ and size $x \ge x_0$, and let them satisfy the size conditions and the tie between the series and the coefficients. Then, under the hypotheses of a Hida setting, for both levels $b$,
$$(1 - \delta)\, d_0(b) \le d_{\mathrm{good}}(b), \qquad (1 - \delta)\, d_0(b) > 0, \qquad \delta = \exp\bigl(-x^{e_z/7}\bigr),$$
where $e_z$ is the zero exponent of the size data.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.DefFree.definiteGoodShare` (also `GlobalTheory.DefFree.goodVectorsDefinite`, which joins the two theorems).

*Proof.* If $\varepsilon > 1$ there are no size data of exponent $\varepsilon$, and there is nothing to prove. Let $\varepsilon \le 1$. By Green's classification, for each type prime $g_k$ there is a series $f \mapsto \tau_f$ on the squarefree labels with the properties above, and the idempotent of the datum at $g_k$ is the good idempotent of this series. The tensor product $c$ is a central idempotent of $\mathbb{C}[P_b]$, and $c\, e_b$ (with $e_b$ the idempotent of $\chi_b$) has image the good part of level $b$. By the free action theorem, for every idempotent $e$ of $\mathbb{C}[P_b]$ the rank of $e$ on the forms is $N \cdot \#P_b \cdot e(1)$, where $e(1)$ is the coefficient of the identity. So the share of the good part is the ratio $(c\,e_b)(1)/e_b(1)$, a quantity computed in the group algebra. Since the trace of every nonidentity element vanishes, the multiplicities of the representations of $P_b$ on the forms are those of a multiple of the regular representation. The bad-share bound, applied with the size $x$, the zero exponent $e_z$, the cardinalities of the type primes and the rank $r \ge x^{\varepsilon}$ (which follows from the size conditions), then gives the ratio at least $1 - \delta$ when $x \ge x_0$. The threshold $x_0$ is the maximum of the threshold of the bad-share bound, $e^{209}$ and $16$. Positivity of $(1 - \delta)d_0(b)$ follows from $d_0(b) \ge 1$ and $\delta < 1$. $\square$

### Scalars at conjugate places

Let $v_0$ be the base archimedean place of $F$, and $v \ne v_0$ another archimedean place. The theta datum gives, for each index $j$ of the chain, a decomposition of the space of the lift into parts $W_p$, a lift matrix that carries coordinates of the definite side to coordinates of the row, a majorant $\mathcal{M}_v$ of the Hermitian form at $v$, and positive scalars $\lambda_{j,p,v}$. The scalars at $v_0$ come from the Rallis inner product formula ([the global theta lift](/learn/theta/#the-global-theta-lift)). The question is whether the same scalar identity holds at the conjugate places $v$, with the same parts. The local input is Howe's theorem for the unramified correspondence ([the local theta correspondence](/learn/theta/#the-local-theta-correspondence)).

**Definition 6.8.4 (Unramified Howe property).** Let $L$ be a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic $0$, let $(\mathrm{U}(J_S), \mathrm{U}(J_T))$ be an unramified dual pair over $L$, and let $\omega$ be a Weil representation of the pair on $M$, realized in a model where a vector $s$ is the vacuum of the standard lattice. The pair has the *unramified Howe property* if: every vector of $M$ fixed by the integral points of $\mathrm{U}(J_S)$ is in the span of the translates $\omega(1, h)s$, and every vector fixed by the integral points of $\mathrm{U}(J_T)$ is in the span of the translates $\omega(g, 1)s$.

This is the statement that the spherical vectors on one side are generated by the vacuum under the other group. It is Howe's theorem for unramified dual pairs ([reductive dual pairs](/learn/theta/#reductive-dual-pairs)).

**Theorem 6.8.5 (Scalar identity at the conjugate places).** Assume the unramified Howe property. Let the theta datum satisfy the weak characterization of the lift, the source level law, the inner product formula, the Fock coefficient law and the finite zeta law, together with the hypotheses on the archimedean model, the phases, the Hecke operators, the rational tests and the positivity of the scalars, and assume that the parts $W_p$ are orthogonal and span the image of the base lift. Then for every index $j$, every archimedean place $v \ne v_0$, every part $p$, every coordinate vector $x$ whose lift lies in $W_p$, and every $y$,
$$\bigl\langle w_v(Ax),\ w_v(Ay) \bigr\rangle = \lambda_{j,p,v}\, \mathcal{M}_v(x, y),$$
where $A$ is the lift matrix at the base level and $w_v$ is the realization at $v$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.ScalConjRow.galD_of_inputs` (also `GlobalTheory.T.conjugate_baseLevel_of_howeUnramifiedCorrespondence`, a second name).

*Proof.* The weak characterization gives the core of the datum. The inner product formula, with the square integrability of the coefficients of the lowest $K$-type, shows that the lift for the conjugate datum is a scalar multiple of an isometry on each part. The conjugates of a rational theta lift are again theta lifts, by the characterization of the lift through its Hecke eigenvalues and its level. The unramified Howe property identifies the spherical vectors at the unramified places, so the conjugate lift at $v$ and the lift of the conjugate datum agree at the base level, up to the scalars. Combined with the base facts on the parts (orthogonality, the Hecke and rational tests, positivity), this gives the identity with the scalar $\lambda_{j,p,v}$ at each $v \ne v_0$. $\square$

**Remark 6.8.6 (Formalization).** In the good share theorem, the case $\varepsilon > 1$ is vacuous: the proof takes $x_0 = 0$ and derives a contradiction from the bound on the exponent ([IsNilpotentSubOneOfFullBlockUnipotent.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/IsNilpotentSubOneOfFullBlockUnipotent.lean#L368)). The count of labels is an input whose proof we did not unfold, and the module is named after a helper lemma at line 74, not after the theorem. The free part needs no hypothesis beyond the Hida setting; only the good share uses Green's classification. The scalar identity at the conjugate places takes about thirty hypotheses ([ConjugateBaseLevelOfHoweUnramifiedCorrespondence.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/ConjugateBaseLevelOfHoweUnramifiedCorrespondence.lean#L20)). One of them, that all phases equal $1$, is introduced and never used. The orthogonality and spanning of the parts is a hypothesis, not a conclusion. The lift for the conjugate datum, the conjugation of rational theta lifts and the base facts are inputs we did not unfold.

## Lowest types and globalisation for U(p, q)

This section collects three results on the representations of the real unitary group $\mathrm{U}(p,q)$. The first says that an irreducible unitary $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module is fixed by one highest weight vector of a given type and its Casimir eigenvalue, when the parameter is far from the walls. It is used in [the embedding of a module of lowest type](/entry/embeds-of-lowest-type/). The second is the globalisation theorem: an admissible $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-submodule of a unitary representation has a closure that is a subrepresentation, and an isomorphism of the $K$-finite vectors of two irreducible unitary representations comes from a unitary isomorphism up to a scalar. The third is the analyticity of $K$-finite matrix coefficients with a density criterion on Levi subgroups. The last two are proved here and given to the proof through [the literature form of globalisation](/entry/gk-globalisation-of-literature/) and [the literature form of analyticity](/entry/harish-chandra-analyticity-of-literature/).

### Modules over the Lie algebra

Let $p, q \ge 0$, $n = p + q$, and index the rows and columns of $n \times n$ matrices by the disjoint union $I = \{1, \dots, p\} \sqcup \{1, \dots, q\}$. Let $J = \operatorname{diag}(1_p, -1_q)$. The Lie algebra of $\mathrm{U}(p,q)$ is $\mathfrak{u}(p,q) = \{X : X^* J + J X = 0\}$, and its complexification is $\mathfrak{g} = M_n(\mathbb{C})$. For $Z \in \mathfrak{g}$ put $Z^\dagger = J Z^* J$. Let $E_{ab}$ be the matrix units. Two indices are in the *same block* if both lie in the first part or both in the second. A matrix is in $\mathfrak{k}$ if its entries outside the two diagonal blocks are $0$; so $\mathfrak{k} = \mathfrak{gl}_p \times \mathfrak{gl}_q$ is the complexified Lie algebra of $K = \mathrm{U}(p) \times \mathrm{U}(q)$. Write $a \prec b$ if $a$ and $b$ are in the same block and $a < b$ there.

**Definition 1.1.1 (Unitary $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module).** A $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module is a complex vector space $V$ with a linear map $Z \mapsto Z_V$ from $\mathfrak{g}$ to $\operatorname{End}(V)$ such that $[X, Y]_V = X_V Y_V - Y_V X_V$, each $v \in V$ lies in a finite-dimensional subspace stable under $\mathfrak{k}$, and $V$ is the sum of the *weight spaces* $V_\mu = \{v : (E_{aa})_V v = \mu_a v \text{ for all } a\}$ for $\mu \in \mathbb{Q}^I$. It is *unitary* if it has a positive definite Hermitian form with $\langle Z_V x, y \rangle = \langle x, Z^\dagger_V y \rangle$ for all $Z$, $x$, $y$.

The form makes $\mathfrak{u}(p,q)$ act by skew-Hermitian operators, as the derivative of a unitary representation does. A vector $v$ is a *highest weight vector of weight* $\mu$ if $v \ne 0$, $v \in V_\mu$ and $(E_{ab})_V v = 0$ for $a \prec b$. These are the highest weight vectors of the $K$-types, for the action of $\mathfrak{k}$. Let $V^{\mathrm{hw}}_\mu$ be the space spanned by $0$ and these vectors. The module is *admissible* if each $V^{\mathrm{hw}}_\mu$ is finite-dimensional, and *irreducible* if $V \ne 0$ and $0$ and $V$ are its only $\mathfrak{g}$-stable subspaces. For $u \in V$ let $\langle u \rangle$ be the smallest $\mathfrak{g}$-stable subspace that contains $u$. The *Casimir operator* is
$$\Omega_V = \sum_{a, b \in I} (E_{ab})_V (E_{ba})_V .$$
It commutes with the action of $\mathfrak{g}$.

**Definition 1.1.2 (Parameter and Blattner weight).** A *parameter* is an injective $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^I$ with $\lambda_b < \lambda_a$ for $a \prec b$, $2\lambda_a \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $\lambda_a - \lambda_b \in \mathbb{Z}$ for all $a, b$. It has *gap* greater than $h$ if $|\lambda_a - \lambda_b| > h$ whenever $a$ and $b$ are in different blocks. Its *Blattner weight* is $\Lambda = \lambda + \delta(\lambda)$, where
$$\delta(\lambda)_a = \tfrac{1}{2}\big(n^{\mathrm{nc}}_{<}(a) - n^{\mathrm{nc}}_{>}(a)\big) - \tfrac{1}{2}\big(n^{\mathrm{c}}_{<}(a) - n^{\mathrm{c}}_{>}(a)\big),$$
with $n^{\mathrm{nc}}_{<}(a)$ the number of $b$ in the other block with $\lambda_b < \lambda_a$, $n^{\mathrm{nc}}_{>}(a)$ the number with $\lambda_b > \lambda_a$, and $n^{\mathrm{c}}_{\lessgtr}(a)$ the same counts in the block of $a$. Put $|\lambda|^2 = \sum_a \lambda_a^2$ and $\rho^2(n) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \big(\tfrac{n-1}{2} - i\big)^2$.

So $\delta(\lambda)$ is $\rho_{\mathrm{n}} - \rho_{\mathrm{c}}$ for the positive system that $\lambda$ defines. By the theorem of Schmid cited in the Notes, $\Lambda$ is the lowest $K$-type of the discrete series with Harish-Chandra parameter $\lambda$, and $\rho^2(n) = |\rho|^2$ for $\mathfrak{gl}_n$, so $|\lambda|^2 - \rho^2(n)$ is the Casimir eigenvalue of that discrete series. For example, let $p = q = 1$ and $\lambda = (k - \tfrac12, -\tfrac12)$ with an integer $k \ge 1$. The second index has the smaller value, so $\delta(\lambda) = (\tfrac12, -\tfrac12)$ and $\Lambda = (k, -1)$.

### One highest weight vector fixes the module

The next theorem rests on a result on the module generated by one vector, which we take from the theory of filtrations of [the embedding of lowest type](/entry/embeds-of-lowest-type/): let $V$ be a unitary $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module, $\lambda$ a parameter with gap greater than $n^3/24 + 1$ and greater than $n - 1$, and $u$ a highest weight vector of weight $\Lambda$ with $\Omega_V u = (|\lambda|^2 - \rho^2(n)) u$. Then (a) every $\mathfrak{g}$-stable subspace of $\langle u \rangle$ that does not contain $u$ is $0$, and (b) for each $\mu$ the highest weight vectors of weight $\mu$ in $\langle u \rangle$ lie in a finite-dimensional space.

**Theorem 1.1.3 (Uniqueness of the module of lowest type).** Let $\lambda$ be a parameter with gap greater than $n^3/24 + 1$ and greater than $n - 1$, and let $\Lambda$ be its Blattner weight. Let $V$ and $V'$ be unitary $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-modules, and $u \in V$, $u' \in V'$ highest weight vectors of weight $\Lambda$ with
$$\Omega_V u = (|\lambda|^2 - \rho^2(n))\, u, \qquad \Omega_{V'} u' = (|\lambda|^2 - \rho^2(n))\, u'.$$
Suppose that $V'$ is irreducible. Then there is an injective $\mathfrak{g}$-linear map $e : V' \to V$ with $e(u') = u$ and image $\langle u \rangle$, and $V'$ is admissible.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.LowGen.gen_iso_of_lowest_type` (also `GlobalTheory.T.highest_weight_iso_of_equal_casimir`, a second name).

*Proof.* Let $N = \langle (u, u') \rangle$ in the unitary module $V \oplus V'$, with the sum of the two forms. The vector $(u, u')$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\Lambda$ with the same Casimir eigenvalue, and it lies in a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{k}$-stable space, the product of such spaces for $u$ and $u'$. Let $\pi : N \to V'$ be the second projection. The space $N \cap (V \oplus 0)$ is $\mathfrak{g}$-stable and does not contain $(u, u')$, since $u' \ne 0$. By (a) it is $0$, so $\pi$ is injective. Its image is $\mathfrak{g}$-stable and contains $u'$, so it is $V'$ by irreducibility. Put $e = \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ \pi^{-1}$. It is $\mathfrak{g}$-linear and $e(u') = u$. Its kernel is $\mathfrak{g}$-stable and does not contain $u'$, since $u \neq 0$, so it is $0$. The image of $e$ is the first projection of $N$, which is $\mathfrak{g}$-stable, contains $u$, and lies in $\langle u \rangle$; so it equals $\langle u \rangle$. For admissibility, $\langle u' \rangle = V'$ by irreducibility, and (b) applied to $u'$ in $V'$ puts each $V'^{\mathrm{hw}}_\mu$ in a finite-dimensional space. $\square$

### Globalisation

Now let $G = \mathrm{U}(J_\varepsilon) = \{g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C}) : g^* J_\varepsilon g = J_\varepsilon\}$, where $J_\varepsilon$ is the diagonal matrix with signs $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}^n$, and let $K \subset G$ be the unitary matrices that commute with $J_\varepsilon$. Let $\sigma$ be a unitary representation of $G$ on a Hilbert space $H$, strongly continuous. A vector is *$K$-finite* if its $K$-translates span a finite-dimensional space. For $X$ in the Lie algebra, $\sigma(X) v$ is the derivative of $t \mapsto \sigma(\exp tX) v$ at $0$ when it exists.

**Definition 1.1.4 ($(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-submodule).** A subspace $V \subseteq H$ is a *$(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-submodule* if its vectors are smooth and $K$-finite, and $V$ is stable under $\sigma(k)$ for $k \in K$ and under $\sigma(X)$ for $X$ in the Lie algebra. It is *admissible* if for each highest weight $\Lambda$ of $K$ the sum of the subspaces of $V$ of $K$-type $\Lambda$ is finite-dimensional. A *$(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-map* between the $K$-finite vectors of two representations is a linear map that commutes with $\sigma(k)$ and with $\sigma(X)$.

**Theorem 1.1.5 (Harish-Chandra globalisation for $\mathrm{U}(J_\varepsilon)$).** The following hold.

1. Let $V$ be an admissible $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-submodule of a unitary representation $(\sigma, H)$. Then the closure $\bar V$ is stable under $G$, and every $K$-finite vector of $\bar V$ lies in $V$.
2. Let $(\sigma_1, H_1)$ and $(\sigma_2, H_2)$ be irreducible unitary representations on nonzero Hilbert spaces, and $e$ a linear isomorphism of their spaces of $K$-finite vectors that is a $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-map. Then there are a unitary isomorphism $U : H_1 \to H_2$ that intertwines $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ and a real $c > 0$ with $U v = c\, e(v)$ for each $K$-finite $v$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.gkGlobalisation_holds`.

*Proof.* For part 1, write $V$ as the sum of its $K$-isotypic parts $V_\Lambda$. Each $V_\Lambda$ is finite-dimensional by admissibility, and it is stable under the Casimir operator, since the Casimir operator commutes with $K$. For a finite-dimensional subspace of $V$ that is stable under $K$ and under the Casimir operator, the closure of the $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-submodule that it generates is stable under $G$. So $\sigma(g) v \in \bar V$ for each $v \in V$, and $\sigma(g) \bar V \subseteq \bar V$ by continuity of $\sigma(g)$. A $K$-finite vector $w \in \bar V$ is a finite sum of its isotypic components, and the component of type $\Lambda$ lies in the closure of $V_\Lambda$, which is $V_\Lambda$ since $V_\Lambda$ is finite-dimensional. So $w \in V$. For part 2, the $K$-finite vectors of an irreducible unitary representation form an irreducible $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module with finite-dimensional isotypic parts. The forms $(v, w) \mapsto \langle e v, e w \rangle$ and $\langle v, w \rangle$ on it are both invariant and positive, so $\langle e v, e w \rangle = c' \langle v, w \rangle$ for one real $c' > 0$. Then $c'^{-1/2} e$ is isometric on the dense subspace of $K$-finite vectors. It extends by continuity to an isometry $U : H_1 \to H_2$ that intertwines the two representations. The image of $U$ is closed and contains the $K$-finite vectors of $H_2$, which are dense, so $U$ is onto. Put $c = c'^{-1/2}$. $\square$

**Theorem 1.1.6 (Analyticity and density).** Let $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}^n$ and $G = \mathrm{U}(J_\varepsilon)$.

1. Let $\pi$ be an admissible representation of $G$ on a Hilbert space. If $v$ and $w$ are $K$-finite, then $g \mapsto \langle w, \pi(g) v \rangle$ is real analytic on $G$.
2. Let $P$ be the stabilizer of an isotropic flag whose steps all have positive dimension, $M$ its Levi subgroup, and $\sigma$ an irreducible unitary representation of $M$. Let $S \ne 0$ be a subspace of vectors that are finite under the maximal compact subgroup of $M$, and stable under the derivatives $\sigma(X)$ for all $X$ with $\exp(tX) \in M$ for all $t$. Then $S$ is dense.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Hypotheses₂.harishChandra_analyticity_density`.

*Proof.* Both parts go through a bound on iterated derivatives. Let $v$ be $K$-finite. Then $v$ lies in a finite-dimensional $K$-stable space $Y$ of smooth vectors that each operator commuting with $K$ preserves. Choose a basis $b_1, \dots, b_d$ of the Lie algebra on which $K$ acts by orthogonal matrices. Then $\Delta = \sum_i \sigma(b_i)^2$ commutes with $K$, so $\Delta$ preserves $Y$. For a finite-dimensional space of smooth vectors that $\Delta$ preserves, there is $C$ with
$$\| \sigma(X_1) \cdots \sigma(X_k)\, v \| \le C^{k+1}\, k!\, \|X_1\| \cdots \|X_k\|$$
for all $k$ and $X_i$, if $\pi$ grows at most exponentially along one-parameter subgroups. This is the form of Nelson's theorem on analytic vectors. For part 1 the growth bound comes from the polynomial growth of $\|\pi(g)\|$, and the derivative bound gives analyticity of the coefficient. For part 2, $\sigma$ is unitary, and it is admissible by the Iwasawa decomposition of $M$. For $v \in S$ and $X$ as in the statement, the function $t \mapsto \langle w, \sigma(\exp tX) v\rangle$ is analytic, and its derivatives at $0$ vanish for $w \perp S$ by the stability of $S$. So $\sigma(\exp X) v \in \bar S$. Each element of $M$ is a product of two such exponentials, so $\bar S$ is $M$-stable. It is not $0$, so it is $H$ by irreducibility. $\square$

**Remark 1.1.7 (Formalization).** The uniqueness theorem takes two further propositions as hypotheses: a statement on highest weights in tensor products and a statement of Poincaré, Birkhoff and Witt type for induced modules. Neither is used. The second name is proved by `first | exact @...` with the first. Its proof applies `GlobalTheory.FiltrationEquivalenceRelation.main` for (a) and (b) ([HighestWeightIsoOfEqualCasimir.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/HighestWeightIsoOfEqualCasimir.lean#L37)). A $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module in this form carries no action of $K$, only of $\mathfrak{k}$. The globalisation theorem is the pair of two lemmas, `admissibleClosure_gKSubmodule` and `infinitesimalEquivalence_unitaryGroupPQ` ([InfinitesimalEquivalenceUnitaryGroupPQ.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/InfinitesimalEquivalenceUnitaryGroupPQ.lean#L737)). We read the outline of their proofs and the statements of the lemmas they use (`rep_mem_closure_of_vIso`, `mem_of_mem_closure`, `form_prop`, `kfinDense_UJC`), not the proofs of those lemmas. The analyticity theorem is the pair of `admissibleCoefficientsAnalytic` and `leviSubmoduleDense`; the lemmas below them are second names of lemmas in other files, which we did not open. We have not unfolded the predicate of $K$-type $\Lambda$.

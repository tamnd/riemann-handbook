This theorem bounds the kernel in the induction of [the finiteness of forms on a standard parabolic](/entry/finite-forms-on/). It cuts the Levi subgroup $M_T$ into commuting pieces and treats one piece at a time. The analytic work is in the two slice lemmas, [the general linear blocks](/entry/finite-slices-gl-part/) and [the unitary part](/entry/finite-slices-u-part/).

## Statement

Use the notation of [the finiteness of forms on a standard parabolic](/entry/finite-forms-on/). For nonempty $T$, let $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cusp}}_T$ (`cuspOn`) be the set of $\varphi \in \mathcal{A}_T$ whose constant term along $N_{T'}$ is $0$ for each $T' \supsetneq T$.

**Theorem.** The span of $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cusp}}_T$ is finite dimensional.

## Proof

1. **The pieces of the Levi.** $M_T(\mathbb{A})$ is generated by pairwise commuting subgroups (`levi_parts_commute`, `levi_eq_iSup_parts`). One is the unitary part $H_0$, the pointwise stabilizer of the span of the columns of $X$ and $Y$ up to $\max T$. For each $k \in T$ there is a block $H_k$, which fixes the complement of the block of columns that ends at $k$.
2. **A finite set that separates.** There is a finite $B \subset G(\mathbb{A})$ such that a $\varphi \in \mathcal{A}_T$ that vanishes on $M_T(\mathbb{A})\, b$ for each $b \in B$ is $0$ (`archimedean_frame_K_type_decomposition`). Its proof, `exists_finset_sep_levi`, writes $G(\mathbb{A}) = N_T(\mathbb{A}) M_T(\mathbb{A}) C K_\infty U$ with $C$ finite and uses that $\varphi$ has a fixed $K_\infty$-type.
3. **The abstract lemma.** `finiteDimensional_of_parts` takes a group with pairwise commuting subgroups $H_i$ that generate $L$, a set $\mathcal{C}$ of functions, a finite separating set $B$ as in step 2, and the hypothesis that for each $i$ and $b$ the *slices* $t \mapsto \varphi(t\, y\, b)$ on $H_i$, for $\varphi \in \mathcal{C}$ and $y \in L$ commuting with $H_i$, span a finite dimensional space. It concludes that $\mathcal{C}$ spans a finite dimensional space. The proof multiplies the $H_i$ into $L$, so that $x \mapsto \varphi(x\, b)$ becomes a function on $\prod_i H_i$ whose one variable slices lie in finite dimensional spaces. Such functions span a finite dimensional space (`finiteDimensional_of_slices`). Evaluation on $L B$ is then injective.
4. **The slices.** `finite_slices_uPart` gives the hypothesis for $H_0$, and `finite_slices_glPart` gives it for each $H_k$.

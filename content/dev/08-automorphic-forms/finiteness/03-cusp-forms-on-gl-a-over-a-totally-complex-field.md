## Cusp forms on GL(a) over a totally complex field

This section defines automorphic forms on $\mathrm{GL}_a$ over a totally complex number field $E$ and proves the finiteness theorem of Harish-Chandra for cusp forms: the cusp forms of a fixed level, with fixed $K_\infty$-types and a fixed generalized eigencharacter of the center, span a finite-dimensional space. The proof is the classical one. A space of functions whose unit ball is totally bounded for a sup norm is finite-dimensional. The reproducing function of [Harish-Chandra's convolution theorem](/entry/exists-alpha-convolution/) makes the unit ball equicontinuous, and the decay of cusp forms in the cusp controls the part near infinity. The result is the cuspidal half of [the finiteness of forms of one type](/entry/finite-dimensional-single/); the other half, for the constant terms, is the subject of the next section.

### Automorphic forms on GL(a)

Let $E$ be a totally complex number field, $\mathbb{A}$ its adele ring, $\mathbb{A}_f$ the finite adeles and $E_\infty = E \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R}$. Since $E$ is totally complex, $E_\infty = \mathbb{C}^{S_\infty}$, with $S_\infty$ the set of infinite places. Put $G_\infty = \mathrm{GL}_a(E_\infty)$, $\mathfrak{g}_\infty = M_a(E_\infty)$, and let $K_\infty = \mathrm{U}(a)^{S_\infty}$ be the unitary matrices in $G_\infty$. We regard $\mathrm{GL}_a(E)$, $G_\infty$ and $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_f)$ as subgroups of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A})$.

A function $\varphi : \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}) \to \mathbb{C}$ is *smooth* if $y \mapsto \varphi(g y)$ is smooth on $G_\infty$ for each $g$. The group $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A})$ acts on smooth functions by right translation, $(\rho(h)\varphi)(g) = \varphi(g h)$, and $\mathfrak{g}_\infty$ acts by the derivatives of right translation. For a complex embedding $\tau$ of $E$, let $E^\tau_{ij}$ be the operators of the complexified action of $\mathfrak{gl}_a$ at the place of $\tau$, and let $C_\tau(u)$ be the Capelli determinant of the matrix $(E^\tau_{ij})$, evaluated at $u + \tfrac{a-1}{2}$. It commutes with each $E^\tau_{ij}$, and on a highest weight vector of weight $\nu$ it acts by $\prod_{k=0}^{a-1} \big(u + \nu_k + \tfrac{a-1}{2} - k\big)$. Let $\mathcal{Z}$ be the algebra generated by all $C_\tau(u)$, $\tau$ an embedding and $u \in \mathbb{C}$. By Capelli's theorem the coefficients of $C_\tau(u)$ generate the center of the enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak{gl}_a$, so $\mathcal{Z}$ is the center of the enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak{g}_\infty \otimes \mathbb{C}$, acting on functions.

For $y \in G_\infty$ put $\|y\|_{\mathrm{ht}} = 1 + \|y\| + \|y^{-1}\|$.

**Definition 1.3.1 (Automorphic form).** A smooth function $\varphi$ on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A})$ is an *automorphic form* if

1. $\varphi(\gamma g) = \varphi(g)$ for $\gamma \in \mathrm{GL}_a(E)$;
2. the stabilizer of $\varphi$ in $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_f)$ is open;
3. the translates $\rho(k)\varphi$, $k \in K_\infty$, span a finite-dimensional space;
4. the functions $z\varphi$, $z \in \mathcal{Z}$, span a finite-dimensional space;
5. for each $g$ there are $C$ and $N$ with $|\varphi(g y)| \le C \|y\|_{\mathrm{ht}}^N$ for all $y \in G_\infty$.

This is the definition of Borel and Jacquet, in the form of [automorphic forms on adelic groups](/learn/automorphic-forms/#automorphic-forms-on-adelic-groups), with the growth condition along $G_\infty$ at each point.

**Definition 1.3.2 (Cusp form).** For $0 < r < a$, let $N_r$ be the unipotent radical of the standard parabolic subgroup with blocks of sizes $r$ and $a - r$: the matrices $1 + x$ with $x$ supported in the rows $i < r$ and the columns $j \ge r$ (rows and columns numbered from $0$). A function $\varphi$ is *cuspidal* if for each such $r$, each Haar measure $\mu$ on $N_r(\mathbb{A})$, each fundamental domain $\mathcal{F}$ of $N_r(E)$ in $N_r(\mathbb{A})$ and each $g$,
$$\int_{\mathcal{F}} \varphi(n g)\, d\mu(n) = 0.$$

So the constant term along each maximal standard parabolic vanishes, as in [constant terms and cusp forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/#constant-terms-and-cusp-forms).

**Definition 1.3.3 (Forms of given level, type and eigencharacter).** Let $L \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_f)$ be an open subgroup, $W$ a finite-dimensional space of functions on $K_\infty$, $X$ a set of maps $\chi$ from the operators on smooth functions to $\mathbb{C}$, and $N \ge 0$. Let $\mathcal{A}(L, W, X, N)$ be the set of automorphic forms $\varphi$ with

1. $\rho(k)\varphi = \varphi$ for $k \in L$;
2. for each $g$, the function $k \mapsto \varphi(g k)$ on $K_\infty$ lies in $W$;
3. there is $\chi \in X$ with $(z_1 - \chi(z_1)) \cdots (z_N - \chi(z_N))\, \varphi = 0$ for all $z_1, \dots, z_N \in \mathcal{Z}$.

Condition 3 says that $\varphi$ is a generalized eigenvector of $\mathcal{Z}$ of order $N$ for $\chi$. If $W$ is spanned by the matrix coefficients of a finite set of $K_\infty$-types, condition 2 bounds the $K_\infty$-types of $\varphi$.

For $g \in \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A})$ let $|\det g|$ be the adelic absolute value of its determinant. The *band* is $B = \{g : 1 \le |\det g| \le 2\}$.

### A criterion for finite dimension

**Lemma 1.3.4 (Totally bounded unit ball).** Let $T$ be a set, $B \subseteq T$, and $V$ a space of functions on $T$. Suppose that

1. each $\varphi \in V$ with $\varphi = 0$ on $B$ is $0$;
2. each $\varphi \in V$ is bounded on $B$;
3. for each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a finite set $F$ of functions with this property: for each $\varphi \in V$ with $|\varphi| \le 1$ on $B$ there is $\psi \in F$ with $|\varphi - \psi| \le \varepsilon$ on $B$.

Then $V$ is finite-dimensional.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.finiteDimensional_of_totallyBounded_supNormBall`.

*Proof.* By 1 and 2, $\|\varphi\|_B = \sup_B |\varphi|$ is a norm on $V$. By 3 with $\varepsilon = 1/4$ the closed unit ball is covered by finitely many sets of diameter at most $1/2$, so it is totally bounded. A normed space whose unit ball is totally bounded is finite-dimensional, by the theorem of Riesz. $\square$

**Lemma 1.3.5 (Equicontinuity and tails give total boundedness).** Let $H$ be a topological group, $S$ and $B$ subsets of $H$, and $\Phi$ a set of functions on $H$ with $\varphi(s g) = \varphi(g)$ for $s \in S$ and $|\varphi| \le 1$ on $B$. Suppose that

1. for each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $1$ with $|\varphi(g u) - \varphi(g)| \le \varepsilon$ for all $\varphi \in \Phi$, $g \in B$, $u \in U$ with $g u \in B$;
2. for each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a compact $C \subseteq B$ such that each $g \in B$ has $s g \in C$ for some $s \in S$, or has $|\varphi(g)| \le \varepsilon$ for all $\varphi \in \Phi$.

Then for each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a finite set $F$ with: each $\varphi \in \Phi$ is within $\varepsilon$ of some $\psi \in F$ on $B$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.totallyBounded_sup_of_equicontinuous_invariant_tails`.

*Proof.* This is the theorem of Arzelà and Ascoli on the compact set $C$ from 2: by 1 the restrictions of $\Phi$ to $C$ are equicontinuous and bounded, so they form a totally bounded set for the sup norm on $C$. A point of $B$ is either moved into $C$ by $S$, where $\varphi$ takes the same value, or is a point where all $\varphi$ are small. $\square$

### The finiteness of cusp forms

We use three estimates for the span of $\mathcal{A}(L, W, X, N)$, with $L$ open, $W$ finite-dimensional and $X$ finite.

1. (Wider bands.) For each $c \ge 1$ there is $C$ such that $|\varphi(g)| \le C \sup_B |\varphi|$ whenever $\varphi$ is in the span and $c^{-1} \le |\det g| \le 2c$.
2. (Boundedness.) Each cuspidal $\varphi$ in the span is bounded on $B$.
3. (Tails.) For each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a compact $C \subseteq B$ such that each $g \in B$ either has $\gamma g \in C$ for some $\gamma \in \mathrm{GL}_a(E)$, or has $|\varphi(g)| \le \varepsilon \sup_B |\varphi|$ for every cuspidal $\varphi$ in the span.

Estimates 2 and 3 are the classical decay of cusp forms on Siegel sets.

**Proposition 1.3.6 (Equicontinuity on the band).** For each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $1$ in $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A})$ such that $|\varphi(g u) - \varphi(g)| \le \varepsilon M$ whenever $\varphi$ is in the span of $\mathcal{A}(L, W, X, N)$, $|\varphi| \le M$ on $B$, $g \in B$, $u \in U$ and $g u \in B$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.harishChandraForms_equicontinuous_on_band`.

*Proof.* The forms of $\mathcal{A}(L, W, X, N)$ are all killed by $p(\Delta_\infty)$ for one nonzero polynomial $p$ and a Laplace operator $\Delta_\infty$ of $G_\infty$, since $W$ is finite-dimensional and $X$ is finite. The restriction of such a form to each coset $g G_\infty$ then lies in the space $S_P$ of [the convolution theorem](/entry/exists-alpha-convolution/) for a monic $P$, and that theorem with the empty word gives a continuous $\alpha$ with compact support on $G_\infty$ with
$$\varphi(g) = \int_{G_\infty} \varphi(g y)\, \alpha(y)\, d\mu(y)$$
for all $\varphi$ in the span and all $g$. Write $u = u_\infty u_f$. For $u_f \in L$ the factor $u_f$ does not change $\varphi$. For the archimedean part, the left invariance of $\mu$ gives
$$\varphi(g u_\infty) - \varphi(g) = \int \varphi(g y) \big(\alpha(u_\infty^{-1} y) - \alpha(y)\big)\, d\mu(y).$$
Let $V$ be a compact neighbourhood of $1$ in $G_\infty$ and $K' = V \cdot \operatorname{supp} \alpha$. For $u_\infty \in V$ the integrand vanishes outside $K'$. Since $g \in B$ and $K'$ is compact, $|\det(g y)|$ lies in $[c^{-1}, 2c]$ for $y \in K'$ and some $c \ge 1$ that does not depend on $g$, so estimate 1 gives $|\varphi(g y)| \le C M$ there. Since $\alpha$ is uniformly continuous, $|\alpha(u_\infty^{-1} y) - \alpha(y)| \le \delta$ for all $y$ when $u_\infty$ is close to $1$. So the difference is at most $C M \delta\, \mu(K')$, and we choose $\delta$ with $C \delta (\mu(K') + 1) = \varepsilon$. $\square$

**Theorem 1.3.7 (Finiteness of cusp forms).** Let $E$ be a totally complex number field, $L \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_f)$ an open subgroup, $W$ a finite-dimensional space of functions on $K_\infty$, $X$ a finite set of maps from operators to $\mathbb{C}$, and $N \ge 0$. Let $V$ be a subspace of the span of $\mathcal{A}(L, W, X, N)$ whose elements are all cuspidal. Then $V$ is finite-dimensional.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.HCF.finiteDimensional_cuspGL` (also `GlobalTheory.T.finiteDimensional_cuspidalForms_GL_totallyComplex`, a second name).

*Proof.* We apply the lemma on a totally bounded unit ball with $T = \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A})$ and the band $B$. If $\varphi \in V$ vanishes on $B$, estimate 1 with $c = \max(1, |\det g|, |\det g|^{-1})$ gives $\varphi(g) = 0$ for each $g$. Estimate 2 gives condition 2. For condition 3, let $\Phi$ be the set of $\varphi \in V$ with $|\varphi| \le 1$ on $B$, and apply the lemma on equicontinuity and tails with $H = \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A})$ and $S = \mathrm{GL}_a(E)$. Each $\varphi$ is left invariant under $S$. The proposition with $M = 1$ gives the equicontinuity, and estimate 3 with $M = 1$ gives the tails. $\square$

**Corollary 1.3.8 (Cusp forms of one eigencharacter).** Let $E$, $L$, $W$ and $N$ be as in the theorem, and $\chi$ one map from operators to $\mathbb{C}$. Let $\nu$ be a Haar measure on $\mathfrak{g}_\infty$ and $\alpha$ a smooth function with compact support in the invertible matrices, and suppose that $\int \alpha(y)\, \varphi(g y)\, d\nu(y) = \varphi(g)$ for all $\varphi \in \mathcal{A}(L, W, \{\chi\}, N)$ and all $g$. Then the span of the cuspidal elements of $\mathcal{A}(L, W, \{\chi\}, N)$ is finite-dimensional.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.HCFCusp.finiteDimensional_cuspSpan` (also `GlobalTheory.T.finiteDimensional_cuspForms_fixedLevel_Ktypes`, a second name).

*Proof.* A linear combination of cuspidal forms that are invariant under $L$ and $\mathrm{GL}_a(E)$ is cuspidal: such a form is continuous, so the integrals over $\mathcal{F}$ converge and are additive. So the span lies in the span of $\mathcal{A}(L, W, \{\chi\}, N)$ and consists of cuspidal functions. This is [the theorem](/entry/finite-dimensional-cusp-gl/) applied with $X = \{\chi\}$. $\square$

**Remark 1.3.9 (Formalization).** In the corollary, the Haar measure $\nu$, the function $\alpha$ and the reproducing identity are hypotheses that the proof does not use ([FiniteDimensionalCuspFormsFixedLevelKtypes.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/FiniteDimensionalCuspFormsFixedLevelKtypes.lean#L9)). The integral is the smoothing of $\varphi$ in the chart $x \mapsto g x$ of $M_a(E_\infty)$ near the invertible matrices. The maps in $X$ are arbitrary functions on operators, not characters of $\mathcal{Z}$. The three estimates are the statements of `GlobalTheory.T.norm_le_sup_wider_band_genCentralEigenforms`, `GlobalTheory.T.cuspidal_harishChandraFiniteForm_bounded_detNormBand` and `GlobalTheory.T.cuspForms_uniformly_small_outside_compact_band`; we read these statements, not their proofs, and we did not read the proof of the lemma on equicontinuity and tails. The polynomial $p$ comes from `GlobalTheory.T.exists_laplacianPolynomial_kills_harishChandraFinite` and the passage to $S_P$ from `GlobalTheory.T.laplacianPolynomialKernel_of_polynomial_laplacian`, statements that we read; we did not unfold the Laplace operator. The reproducing function on $G_\infty$ is `GlobalTheory.T.exists_harishChandra_reproducing_function_GLinfinity`. The identification of $\mathcal{Z}$ with the center rests on Capelli's theorem; the definition only takes the algebra generated by the Capelli operators.

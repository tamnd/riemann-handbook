## Notes and references

The double coset invariance of Section 1 gives [the nonvanishing theorem for twisted Eisenstein coefficients](/entry/twisted-eisenstein-nonvanishing/) of Volume 10. Through Sections 2 and 3 it gives [the good character index](/entry/tw-idx-ok-of-ok/) of Volume 13. The commutation of Hecke operators feeds [the large coefficient field](/entry/large-coefficient-field-contains-twist-values/) of Volume 14. The nonvanishing in a double coset and the unimodular genus feed [members with large unramified exponents](/entry/member-bundles-large-unramified-exponents-alt/) and [nonempty hypotheses](/entry/hypotheses-nonempty-of-unramified-exponents-alt/) of Volume 15. The reference frames and the length bound give [the existence of a reference frame](/entry/sc-reference-exists/) and [the length bound for the poles](/entry/length-pole-t0/) of Volume 10. All of these lead to the step [nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/).

The method of Section 1 is the unfolding of an Eisenstein series against a period, as in the Rankin-Selberg method. The first unfolding replaces the period over the group by an integral over a unipotent group $R_1$ against a character, and the local integral depends only on a double coset of $R_1$ and the level group. The classical form of this principle, for $\mathrm{GL}_n \times \mathrm{GL}_m$, is in Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika. Here the local integral is replaced by a finite Riemann sum over $q^{-A}\mathcal{O}/q^{B}\mathcal{O}$, and the statement is that this sum is nonzero for all large $A$ and $B$. The sum is formed with values in an abstract field $K$, and $q^{-B}$ plays the role of the Haar measure.

The commutation of Hecke operators is classical for modular forms and for definite forms; the argument by symmetry of path counts follows Shimura. The uniqueness of the unimodular genus over an unramified CM extension follows from Jacobowitz's classification of hermitian lattices over local fields. The local lengths of Section 5 use only the structure of discrete valuation rings, as in Serre.

- D. Bump, *Automorphic Forms and Representations*, Chapter 1, Sections 1.4 and 1.6.
- G. Shimura, *Introduction to the Arithmetic Theory of Automorphic Functions*, Chapter 3.
- G. Shimura, *Arithmetic of unitary groups*, Ann. of Math. 79 (1964), 369 to 409.
- R. Jacobowitz, *Hermitian forms over local fields*, Amer. J. Math. 84 (1962), 441 to 456.

- J.-P. Serre, *Local Fields*, Chapter I.

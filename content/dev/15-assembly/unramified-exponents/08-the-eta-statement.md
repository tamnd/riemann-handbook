## The eta statement

This section proves the eta statement from the point statement and the published inputs $\mathcal{P}$: at every stage with a strict henselian acyclic level datum, if the automorphic side exists, then for all large enough coefficient rings the localized datum is large and its eta values agree with those of the column datum. Item 6 of [the stage statements](/entry/all-stages-of-specializations-alt/) uses it, through [the finiteness theorem](/entry/s-fin-and-s-finl-of-theta-data-exists/) of Section 7, where it is hypothesis 1 of the level three object at a stage. There are two parts. Largeness has three conditions, each from a separate lemma; the last, that every live index is taken, is proved here. The equality of eta values comes from the local compatibility of the eta values with the localization data, through a congruence on the lengths at twisted places.

Fix a stage $x$ of a datum $P$ and a strict henselian acyclic level datum $l$ over $x$, with data $A$, $M$, $\chi$ and a tuple. Let $D$ be the datum of $x$. For localization data $L$ write $D_L$ for $D$ with the localization of $L$.

**Definition 6.8.1 (Eventually).** A property $Q$ of data holds *eventually* at $(A, M, \chi)$ if there is $\mathrm{big}$ such that $Q(D_L)$ holds for all localization data $L$ with a Hecke specification for $\mathrm{big}$, an automorphic specialization and semisimple residual representations. Since localization data for a larger $\mathrm{big}$ are also localization data for a smaller one, this says that $Q$ holds for all large enough coefficient rings. Eventual properties are closed under conjunction and implication.

**Definition 6.8.2 (Large data).** Let $D$ be a datum with localization indices $i$, rings $O_i$ and Galois groups $G$. An index $i$ is *live* if there are an automorphic form $\pi$ at $i$ and representations $r : G \to \mathrm{GL}_{n_V}(O_i)$, $\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}_r(O_i)$ such that $r$ is Hecke-Galois for $\pi$, $r$ decomposes through $\rho$ and the character of $i$, and the reduction of $\rho$ has an absolutely irreducible subrepresentation. The index is *split* if every Hecke-Galois representation $r$ at $i$ splits over the fraction field of $O_i$. The datum *takes all indices* if at every live index $i$ there are good representations $r$ and $\rho$ that are taken over a discrete valuation ring finite over $\mathbb{Z}_{\ell_i}$. The datum is *large* if it satisfies the rationality condition, every index is split, and it takes all indices.

**Definition 6.8.3 (Point and eta statements).** Let $h_L$ be a recursion datum. The *point statement* for $h_L$ says that at every $(x, l)$, eventually $D_L$ has spanning eigenvectors and internal isotypic parts, and, under the rationality condition, the provision for the forty-first step, good irreducible residual representations, and the conclusions of steps 42 and Z. The *eta statement* for $h_L$ says that at every $(x, l)$, if the automorphic side exists, then eventually $D_L$ is large and $\eta_j(v)$ for $D_L$ equals $\eta_j(v)$ for $P$, for $1 \le j \le m$ and every $v$.

**Theorem 6.8.4 (Local compatibility of eta values).** Assume $\mathcal{P}$. Let $h_L$ be a recursion datum, $x$ a stage of a datum $P$, $l$ a strict henselian acyclic level datum over $x$, and $L$ localization data for some $\mathrm{big}$. Let $M$ be a normalized local compatibility datum, and assume the standard Hecke facts with twist and their equation, integrality of the lattices, the Hecke tie, Hecke rationality, the tie of the abelian varieties, the class sums, spanning eigenvectors, internal isotypic parts, the conclusions of steps 42 and Z, and the good fixed twists at all places. Then for $1 \le j \le m$ and all $v, v'$ with $v' = v$, the value $\eta_j(v')$ for $D_L$ equals $\eta_j(v)$ for $P$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.etaJ_with_local_data_eq_alt` (also `GlobalTheory.T.etaJ_local_compatibility_alt`, a second name).

*Proof.* The theta series of the localized datum have no eta defect: the rational reading has no congruence, and the complement of the theta series is spanned by the eigenvectors. By [the congruence on lengths at twisted places](/entry/congruence-length-twisted-places-of-hecke-integrality/), from Hecke integrality, the lengths at the twisted places of $D_L$ agree with those of the column datum of $x$. The eta value of $D_L$ is computed from these lengths, and the eta value of $x$ is that of $P$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.8.5 (Eta values at a level).** Assume $\mathcal{P}$, and let $h_L$ be a recursion datum with the point statement. Then at all stages $x$ of a datum $P$, for every strict henselian acyclic level datum $l$ over $x$, eventually the following holds for $D_L$: under the rationality condition, for $1 \le j \le m$ and all $v, v'$ with $v' = v$, $\eta_j(v')$ for $D_L$ equals $\eta_j(v)$ for $P$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.EtaMem.exists_etaJ_alt` (also `GlobalTheory.T.eventually_etaJ_eq_eta_of_levelC`, a second name).

*Proof.* At all stages the good fixed twists hold at all places. The inner product of the class sums at twisted places, the density of split Frobenius elements, the integrality of the lattices, the series statement and the openness of the Hecke action for localized data give the hypotheses of the local compatibility theorem; the normalized local compatibility datum is built from the Hecke specification of $L$. The point statement gives spanning eigenvectors, internal isotypic parts and the two conclusions under the rationality condition. The local compatibility theorem then gives the equality. $\square$

**Theorem 6.8.6 (All indices taken).** Assume Chebotarev density and the standard Hecke facts. Let $D$ be a datum with the data of the theorem on Hecke specifications (Section 7), and assume a choice side, a matrix input, stability of $L_0$ under conjugation, integral exponents, the splitting of the places over two primes and the density of split Frobenius elements. Assume that the automorphic side exists, and that eventually the provision for the forty-first step holds, every index is split, and every index has the decomposition over the coefficient ring. Then eventually $D_L$ takes all indices.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CoefDuty.TakenLarge.evO_takenAll_alt` (also `GlobalTheory.T.side_eigen_hecke_match_alt`, a second name).

*Proof.* Eventually the lattices are integral and contain the localized lattices after localization. Enlarge $\mathrm{big}$ by finite sets $S_0$ chosen from Chebotarev and the splitting, so that for every live index there is a finite set of eigenvalues that generates the coefficients. At a live index, the irreducible residual point descends to the coefficient extension; the chosen finite sets give a coefficient ring that is taken; and the localization of the coefficient extension gives the representations $r$ and $\rho$ over it. $\square$

**Theorem 6.8.7 (Large localized data).** Assume Chebotarev density. Let $D$ be a datum with the data of the theorem on Hecke specifications, a choice side, a matrix input, stability of $L_0$ under conjugation, integral exponents, the splitting of the places over two primes and the density of split Frobenius elements. Assume that the automorphic side exists, and that eventually, under the rationality condition, the provision for the forty-first step and good irreducible residual representations hold, and the Galois representations of the cusp forms exist. Then eventually $D_L$ is large.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CoefDuty.large_of_large_rings_alt`.

*Proof.* The standard Hecke facts are a proved theorem. Eventually $D_L$ satisfies the rationality condition, by the rationality of the side eigenvalues. Eventually every index is split, by the multiplicity of the cusp side. Eventually every index has the decomposition over the coefficient ring, by Chebotarev and the decomposition of the theta locus. Combining these with the hypothesis, eventually the provision for the forty-first step holds. [The theorem on all indices taken](/entry/ev-o-taken-all-alt/) then gives that eventually $D_L$ takes all indices. The conjunction of the three is largeness. $\square$

**Theorem 6.8.8 (Eta statement).** Assume $\mathcal{P}$. Let $h_L$ be a recursion datum. If the point statement holds for $h_L$, then the eta statement holds for $h_L$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.StEta.sEta_alt_alt_alt`.

*Proof.* At all stages we have: the eta values at the level (the theorem above); stability of $L_0$ under conjugation; integral exponents; splitting of the places over the two primes; density of split Frobenius elements; and eventually the Galois representations of the cusp forms. These are joined with the point statement along the steps. At one stage with the automorphic side, the laws of eventual properties give the hypothesis of the theorem on large localized data from the point statement and the cusp Galois representations. With the Chebotarev field of $\mathcal{P}$ that theorem gives that eventually $D_L$ is large. Since largeness contains the rationality condition, the eta values at the level give the equality of the eta values. $\square$

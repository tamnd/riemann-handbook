## Notes and references

The chapter proves [the theorem on membership and bundles at the stronger parameters](/entry/large-unramified-exponent-bundles-exist-alt/): the Siegel mass ratio for CM fields, the membership statement and the bundle statement for the origin extension. This theorem feeds [the theorem that the hypotheses are nonempty](/entry/hypotheses-nonempty-of-unramified-exponents-alt/), which is step 1 of branch 1 of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/). Sections 1 and 2 reduce the main theorem to the pair theorem. Sections 3 to 9 prove the step from level three to level four, the export of built cores and the stage statements up to the second level. Sections 10 to 16 prove the inputs that Sections 1 and 2 quote: the bundle statement, the moving statement, the strict henselian acyclic levels, the rationality and second nonvanishing statements, the group side, the weight case condition and the saturated states. None of the main results has a classical counterpart in the form given here. Each is an eventual statement over a family of data, and the classical theorems enter only as hypotheses, gathered in the records $\mathcal{H}_A$, $\mathcal{H}_E$, $\mathcal{H}_M$ and $\mathcal{H}_L(h_L)$ of Section 1. The notes below name the sources of those hypotheses and say how the form used here differs.

Section 1. The Siegel mass ratio of part 1 is a quotient of two Siegel masses of standard hermitian lattices. The mass formula for hermitian lattices goes back to Siegel's analytic theory of quadratic forms; Weil's second Acta paper puts it in adelic form. In the Lean form the ratio is a proved theorem for unramified CM fields, so part 1 needs no hypothesis. The factorization theorem of Dixmier and Malliavin and the tensor product theorem of Flath are among the member hypotheses: every smooth vector of a Fréchet representation is a finite sum of convolutions, and an irreducible admissible representation of an adelic group is a restricted tensor product of local representations ([representations](/learn/representations/#generic-representations-and-local-factors)).

Sections 2 to 5. The export of built cores rests on the Galois inputs of $\mathcal{H}_E$: the Chebotarev density theorem, Poitou-Tate duality, local Tate duality, the local and global Euler characteristic formulas and the Greenberg-Wiles formula. All but the first are in Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, Chapters VII and VIII. The realization of theta series at a datum uses the [Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation) in the form of Weil's first Acta paper and the theta correspondence of Howe. The large gaps of Harish-Chandra parameters in Section 3 are gaps of the parameters of [discrete series](/learn/real-groups/#discrete-series-and-formal-degrees).

Sections 6 and 7. The weight data use the Harish-Chandra isomorphism between the center of the enveloping algebra and invariant polynomials, which attaches an infinitesimal character to each highest weight. The row cusp condition asks for the Galois representations attached to conjugate self-dual regular algebraic cuspidal representations of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over a CM field ([the Galois representation of an automorphic representation](/learn/galois-representations/#the-galois-representation-of-an-automorphic-representation)). For representations with a discrete series component at a finite place these were built by Harris and Taylor, who also proved the [local Langlands correspondence](/learn/galois-representations/#the-local-langlands-correspondence) for $\mathrm{GL}_n$; Henniart gave a second proof of the local correspondence. Here the existence and the local-global compatibility are hypotheses at the stages, not theorems. The Hecke specifications of Section 7 use global Artin reciprocity for the characters of the datum ([global class field theory](/learn/class-field-theory/#global-class-field-theory)).

Sections 8 and 9. The eta statement uses the Chebotarev density theorem to find primes of degree one in given Frobenius classes; see Neukirch, Chapter VII, §13 ([the splitting of primes](/learn/number-fields/#the-splitting-of-primes)). The series data of Section 9 use only global Artin reciprocity.

Section 10. The bundle witnesses rest on Harish-Chandra's discrete series. The existence and classification of discrete series by Harish-Chandra parameters are Harish-Chandra's theorems in his two Acta papers on discrete series; we cite the second. The finiteness theorem for automorphic forms with given $K$-type and infinitesimal character is in Harish-Chandra's lecture notes on automorphic forms, Chapter I. Rapid decay of cusp forms is in the same notes ([cusp forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/#constant-terms-and-cusp-forms)). The Casselman-Wallach theorem gives a unique smooth Fréchet globalization of moderate growth for each Harish-Chandra module ([representations of real groups](/learn/representations/#representations-of-real-groups)); see Casselman, and Wallach, Chapter 11. In the Lean form the square integrable columns of the zeta compatibility are records of equalities, and the square integrability itself is not stated.

Section 11. The moving statement is a transport of level data along a twisted copy. Its classical content is the action of Hecke operators on eigenforms and their twists by characters, with the twist given by class field theory. It has no single source.

Section 12. The local comparison at a twisted place uses the local theta correspondence in the Fock model of the [Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-fock-model); see Howe ([the local theta correspondence](/learn/theta/#the-local-theta-correspondence)). The local tuple of a discrete series read at a place uses Harish-Chandra's classification, the Casselman-Wallach theorem and the theory of Harish-Chandra modules. The admissibility of a $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module of an irreducible unitary representation is Harish-Chandra's theorem in his Banach space paper.

Section 13. The rationality statement is a form of the rationality of Hecke eigenvalues on spaces of automorphic forms with an integral structure. Here it is derived from the inputs $\mathcal{P}$ and $\mathcal{S}$ and the isotypic decomposition at the stages, which is a hypothesis.

Section 14. The group side uses Green's classification of the characters of $\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb{F}_q)$, in which the cuspidal characters are labelled by regular characters of $\mathbb{F}_{q^d}^\times$ ([depth zero representations and types](/learn/representations/#depth-zero-representations-and-types)). Ray class fields and Artin reciprocity are in Neukirch, Chapter VI. The Chebotarev density theorem is in Neukirch, Chapter VII, §13; the Lean form asks for a prime of degree one, but this hypothesis is not used. Potential semistability of de Rham representations is Berger's theorem, which proves Fontaine's conjecture with the $p$-adic monodromy theorem; see Fontaine for the period rings and the definitions ([period rings](/learn/galois-representations/#period-rings), [potentially semistable representations](/learn/galois-representations/#crystalline-de-rham-and-potentially-semistable-representations)). For $\ell \neq p$ the analogue is Grothendieck's $\ell$-adic monodromy theorem, in the appendix of Serre and Tate. The finiteness of automorphic forms on $\mathrm{GL}_a$ is again Harish-Chandra's finiteness theorem; it is not used.

Section 15. The theta condition theorems collect the main results of the theory of theta lifts for unitary dual pairs. The Siegel-Weil formula is due to Weil, in the convergent range, and to Kudla and Rallis in its regularized form ([the Siegel-Weil formula](/learn/theta/#the-siegel-weil-formula)). The doubling method and its local zeta integrals are due to Piatetski-Shapiro and Rallis, in Part A of the book of Gelbart, Piatetski-Shapiro and Rallis ([the doubling method](/learn/theta/#the-doubling-method)). The inner product formula for theta lifts is the [Rallis inner product formula](/learn/theta/#the-rallis-inner-product-formula). The finiteness theorem for induced representations is due to Bernstein and Zelevinsky. Schur orthogonality for square integrable representations, with the formal degree, is due to Harish-Chandra ([discrete series and formal degrees](/learn/real-groups/#discrete-series-and-formal-degrees)). The continuation of partial Hecke $L$-functions goes back to Hecke, and Tate's thesis gives the adelic proof ([Tate's thesis and Hecke $L$-functions](/learn/class-field-theory/#tates-thesis-and-hecke-l-functions)). The Lean form of the conjugation invariance takes ten published statements and uses six; the multiplicity equation at the weights is a hypothesis.

Section 16. The saturated states use the same inputs as Section 15, Green's classification and the discrete series. The multiplicity equations enter as the two multiplicity statements of $\mathcal{H}_M$; they are not derived from a multiplicity one theorem.

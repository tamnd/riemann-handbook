## Membership and bundles at the stronger parameters

This section states the main theorem of the chapter. Under the literature hypotheses of the unitary groups, three facts hold: the Siegel mass ratio for CM fields, the membership statement for the stronger parameters, and the bundle statement for the same parameters. The theorem is step 1 of [the theorem that the hypotheses are nonempty](/entry/hypotheses-nonempty-of-unramified-exponents-alt/), which is branch 1 of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/) and lies below the step [nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/). The section also states the theorem that gives the membership statement for the parameter set of the origin extension. Its proof uses the pair theorem of Section 2 and the stage statements of Section 6. We first fix the language of eventual statements and of parameter sets.

A *point* is a context $K$, a base $B$, a frame $f$ of the pre-base of $B$ and a twist datum $T$ of $B$. A twist datum has a finite set of primes $T_{\mathrm{pl}}$, and $T$ is *untwisted* if it is the untwisted datum on $T_{\mathrm{pl}}$. A *core* at a point is a choice of stages $C, W, V, S, N, G$ over $B$ (columns, rows, a division stage, an $S$-stage, an $N$-stage and a stage of $G$-data) together with a core $c$ over the assembled datum $X = (B, C, W, V, S, N, G)$. A core $c$ carries a norm choice $c_{\mathrm{nch}}$ and a datum $c_{\mathrm{xd}}$. A *slot* is a predicate $Q(K, X, f, T, c)$ on cores. For two slots $Q, R$ we write $Q \wedge R$ for the slot of cores that satisfy both.

**Definition 6.1.1 (Eventual statements).** Let $p$ be a property of points. We say that $p$ *holds at large bases eventually* if, for all contexts $K$ whose scale constants are large in the fixed order of quantifiers of the volume, the property $p(K, B, f, T)$ holds at every base $B$ that is large with respect to $K$, every frame $f$ and every twist datum $T$. We write $\mathrm{Ev}_{\mathrm{lg}}(p)$.

The order of quantifiers is the one of the large contexts of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/): first a bound $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ for the exponent, then the constants of the field data, then a lower bound for the scale $x$ of $K$. The point of the definition is that each step of the construction below may enlarge the constants, and the final statement still holds for all large contexts.

**Definition 6.1.2 (Field condition and forced primes).** A base $B$ with frame $f$ satisfies the *field condition* $\mathrm{KP}_x(B, f)$ if there is a $\delta$ for which the field $K_s$ of $B$ is exact at $f$ with parameter $\delta$, and the pre-base of $B$ is pinned. A twist datum $T$ of $B$ is *forced* at $K$ if $T_{\mathrm{pl}}$ contains every prime $p$ that is one of the primes $g_i$ of the host of $B$, or the prime $d$ of the host, or satisfies $p \le X''_K(n)$, where $n$ is the rank of $B$.

**Definition 6.1.3 (Step statements).** Let $\mathrm{KP}$ be a condition on bases with frames, and let $p, q$ be properties of points. The *step* $\mathrm{Step}(\mathrm{KP}, p, q)$ says: at large bases eventually, if $\mathrm{KP}(B, f)$ holds, $T$ is forced and $p$ holds, then $q$ holds. For properties $p, q$ of points with a column datum $w$, the *column step* $\mathrm{Step}_w(p, q)$ is $\mathrm{Step}(\mathrm{KP}_x, \text{true}, q')$, where $q'$ says that every column datum $w$ of the point with $p(w)$ has $q(w)$.

Two properties of column data occur often. A column datum $w$ satisfies the *start condition* if it is pinned, if it is untwisted or its data are good, and if it is coherent: places with the same valuations on the field $L_0$ carry the same set $Q$, the same exponent $k$ and the same character $\psi$, and the roots of unity at these places are primitive of order $\mathrm{resChar}^k$. For a scale $s$, the column datum $w$ *has level three* (or *level four*) if there is a stage $Z$ of $w$ with a level three (or level four) object of $s$ at $Z$. We write $\mathrm{Has}_3(s)$ and $\mathrm{Has}_4(s)$.

**Definition 6.1.4 (Membership statement).** Let $s$ be a scale, and let $Q_s$ and $Q'_s$ be its slots of *built* and of *good* cores. A core is built if it has a built object whose kernel satisfies the entry condition, and it is good if it is built and the good orientation condition holds at its norm choice and datum. The *membership statement* for $s$ is the conjunction of four conditions.

1. (Base condition.) Eventually in $K$, there are a pre-base $P$ and a frame $f_0$ with $K$ over $P$ such that, for any two finite sets $S_1, S_2$ of algebraic numbers, there is a base $B$ over $P$ with frame $f_0$ that satisfies $\mathrm{KP}_x$ and contains $S_1$ and $S_2$ in its fields. And $\mathrm{KP}_x$ passes from a base to each enlargement of it.
2. (First condition.) $\mathrm{Step}(\mathrm{KP}_x, \text{untwisted}, \text{there is a core in } Q_s)$.
3. (Export condition.) At large bases eventually, if $\mathrm{KP}_x(B, f)$ holds and $T$ is untwisted, then every core $c$ in $Q_s$ exports at its norm choice and datum.
4. (Second condition.) The pass condition for $Q_s$ into bases that have a core in $Q_s \wedge \mathrm{TwAll} \wedge Q'_s$, where $\mathrm{TwAll}$ says that all twists are admissible at the core.

The *pass condition* $\mathrm{Pass}(\mathrm{KP}, Q, r)$ says: at large bases eventually, if $\mathrm{KP}(B, f)$ holds, $T$ is untwisted and $c$ is a core in $Q$ that exports, then for each family $A$ of finite sets of primes and each finite set $S_2$ of algebraic numbers there are an enlargement $B'$ of $B$, the same frame $f$ and a twist datum $T'$ with the same primes as $T$, which avoids $A$, such that $B'$ contains $S_2$ and $r(K, B', f, T')$ holds. An *enlargement* $B'$ of $B$ has the same pre-base, the same images of the fields $K_s$ and $L_0$, and a field $L$ whose image contains the image of the field $L$ of $B$.

**Definition 6.1.5 (Bundle statement).** Let $\mathrm{KP}$ be a condition on bases, $Q, Q'$ slots and $\Lambda$ a bundle linkage. The *large base condition* $\mathrm{LB}(\mathrm{KP}, Q, R)$ says that at large bases eventually, if $\mathrm{KP}(B, f)$ holds, every core in $Q$ lies in $R$. The *bundle statement* $\mathrm{ExB}(\mathrm{KP}, Q, Q', \Lambda)$ says that there is a slot $R$ with $\mathrm{LB}(\mathrm{KP}, Q, R)$ such that eventually, for every built point $\beta$ of $Q' \wedge R$ and every $c \in \mathbb{R}$, there is a bundle $b$ of $\beta$ at $c$ with $\Lambda(K, \beta, c, b)$.

Now the parameters. Let $h_L$ be a recursion datum for the [local Langlands correspondence](/learn/galois-representations/#the-local-langlands-correspondence). A *norm choice extension* $N$ for $h_L$ assigns a type of extra data to each point with a column datum $w$, a stage $Z$, a weight, a base point, a row, a norm choice and a core datum. A *parameter set* is a quadruple of a position, a norm choice extension, an $S$-extension and a geometric predicate. Its *scale* has four slots: the position slot, the norm choice slot of $N$, the $S$-slot and the geometric slot. For a norm choice extension $N$, let $p(N)$ be the parameter set with the position $\mathrm{RX}$, the extension $N$, the trivial $S$-extension and the closure of the trivial geometry. Let $s(N)$ be its scale, and let $\Lambda_N$ be the bundle linkage of $p(N)$.

**Definition 6.1.6 (The origin extension).** Let $h_L$ be a recursion datum and $c_{\mathrm{Dep}} \in \mathbb{R}$. The *extension with law* gives at each point the default data and a proof of the bundle law at the datum of $w$ at $Z$. The *origin extension* $N_0 = N_0(h_L, c_{\mathrm{Dep}})$ gives the data of the extension with list at $c_{\mathrm{Dep}}$ and an *origin*: a datum $P$ over $w$, a state $x$ over $P$ and a strict henselian acyclic level $l$ over $x$, such that $x$ is listed at $c_{\mathrm{Dep}}$, $P$ is unlocalized saturated, the weight of the point is the weight of $P$, and the norm choice of the point is kept in the choice of $l$. The *geometric extension* $N_g$ adds to $N_0$ the good orientation condition. For a property $A$ of points, the extension $N \times A$ adds to $N$ a proof of $A$ at the point.

**Definition 6.1.7 (Multiplicity condition and saturated states).** The *multiplicity condition* for $p(N)$ says that eventually, at every witness point $q$ of the scale $s(N)$, the multiplicity equation holds for the real datum of $q$. The *unlocalized saturated state statement* is the column step from the start condition to the following property of $w$: there are a datum $P$ over $w$ that is unlocalized saturated and a state $x$ over $P$ whose set of localized places is empty and whose values $\eta_j(v)$ are all $0$.

We take as given the literature hypotheses of the unitary groups of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/) and the rational row statement of that chapter. These are statements of published results on [discrete series](/learn/real-groups/#discrete-series-and-formal-degrees), Harish-Chandra modules, the [Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation), Siegel-Weil formulas, [cusp forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/#constant-terms-and-cusp-forms), the local Langlands correspondence and Galois cohomology. The proofs below group them into four records: the first record $\mathcal{H}_A$ (twenty-one statements on ray class fields, principal ideals, Harish-Chandra modules, rapid decay of cusp forms, the Casselman-Wallach theorem, the Weil representation of a unitary pair, discrete series of $U(p,q)$, Siegel-Weil convergence, a Siegel mass ratio and global Artin reciprocity), the elliptic record $\mathcal{H}_E$ (twenty-five statements, among them the Chebotarev density theorem, Poitou-Tate duality, local and global Euler characteristics, local Tate duality, the Greenberg-Wiles formula, finiteness of automorphic forms on $\mathrm{GL}_n$ and potential semistability of de Rham representations), the multiplicity cover record $\mathcal{H}_M$ (a discrete series cover, theorems on theta conditions, a continuation of partial Hecke $L$-functions and two multiplicity statements) and the local Langlands record $\mathcal{H}_L(h_L)$ (six statements on the local Langlands correspondence).

**Theorem 6.1.8 (Membership and bundles at the stronger parameters).** Assume the literature hypotheses of the unitary groups and the rational row statement. Let $h_L$ be a recursion datum, let $c_{\mathrm{Dep}} \in \mathbb{R}$, and let $A$ be a property of points with $\mathrm{Ev}_{\mathrm{lg}}(A)$. Put $N = N_0(h_L, c_{\mathrm{Dep}}) \times A$. Then:

1. the Siegel mass ratio for CM fields holds: for each CM number field $E$ that is unramified in the sense of the volume and each $M \ge 3$, the ratio of the masses of the standard hermitian lattices of ranks $M$ and $M - 1$ over $E$ is the explicit right side of the volume;
2. the membership statement holds for $s(N)$;
3. the bundle statement $\mathrm{ExB}(\mathrm{KP}_x, Q_{s(N)}, Q'_{s(N)}, \Lambda_N)$ holds.

*Proof.* We first form the hypothesis records. Eleven of the literature hypotheses give the first record $\mathcal{H}_A$; three of its statements are proved theorems (the Weil representation of a unitary pair, the finite dimension of the rows and the mass ratio for unramified CM fields). Ten statements on Harish-Chandra analyticity, discrete series, cusp forms, the factorizations of Dixmier-Malliavin and Flath, the polynomial ring of Zelevinsky, the geometric lemma and green cuspidal characters form a record of member hypotheses. With ten more literature hypotheses and the rational row statement, they give the multiplicity cover record $\mathcal{H}_M$. The records $\mathcal{H}_A$ and $\mathcal{H}_M$ give the unlocalized saturated state statement. The same ten member hypotheses give the multiplicity condition for $p(N_0)$. The literature hypotheses on Galois representations give, by [the theorem on the third layer](/entry/hyps-e-third-layer-ser-n-of-geometric-galois/), the elliptic record $\mathcal{H}_E$ and the local Langlands record $\mathcal{H}_L(h_L)$.

Part 1 is a statement of the record $\mathcal{H}_A$, and it holds with no hypothesis. For part 2, [the Theorem on the origin extension](/entry/exists-base-lattice-of-prime-alt/) below gives the membership statement for $s(N_0)$. At large bases eventually the property $A$ holds, so the extra datum of $N_0 \times A$ exists at every point that matters, and the membership statement passes from $s(N_0)$ to $s(N_0 \times A)$. For part 3, a theorem on Weil blocks gives the bundle statement for $N_0 \times A$ directly from $\mathcal{H}_A$, $\mathcal{H}_M$ and the member hypotheses, from the map that sends a datum of $N_0$ to its datum with law, and from the moving condition for the slots of $p(N_0)$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.1.9 (Membership at the origin extension).** Assume the records $\mathcal{H}_A$, $\mathcal{H}_E$ and $\mathcal{H}_M$ and the unlocalized saturated state statement. Let $h_L$ be a recursion datum with the record $\mathcal{H}_L(h_L)$, let $c_{\mathrm{Dep}} \in \mathbb{R}$, and assume the multiplicity condition for $p(N_0)$, where $N_0 = N_0(h_L, c_{\mathrm{Dep}})$. Then the membership statement holds for $s(N_0)$, and the bundle statement $\mathrm{ExB}(\mathrm{KP}_x, Q_{s(N_0)}, Q'_{s(N_0)}, \Lambda_{N_0})$ holds.

*Proof.* We build the inputs of [the pair theorem](/entry/exists-base-lattice-of-pair-alt/) of Section 2 for the pair $N_0$ and $N_g$.

1. Three statements of $\mathcal{H}_M$ give the weight case condition. The discrete series cover and six statements of $\mathcal{H}_A$ give the extension of fine adelic data to larger fields.
2. [The Theorem on all stages](/entry/all-stages-at-member-of-specializations-alt/) of Section 6 gives the stage statements. We use six of them: the same Galois level statement, the multiplicity input statement, the rationality statement, the cusp statement, the nonempty strict henselian acyclic level, and, for each constant $c$, four list statements on settings and forms, series, moduli points and finiteness.
3. [The finiteness and nonvanishing theorem](/entry/s-fino-and-second-no-of-s-finl-prime/) gives at $c_{\mathrm{Dep}}$ a spectral finiteness statement and a second nonvanishing statement.
4. The unlocalized saturated state statement, the stage statements of step 2 and the spectral finiteness of step 3 give the column step from the start condition to $\mathrm{Has}_3(s(N_0))$.
5. The level three condition holds for $s(N_0)$ and for $s(N_g)$: each level three object at a stage moves to each new stage, with its row package. The moving condition holds for the slots of $p(N_0)$.
6. A datum of $N_0$ gives a datum with law by the first projection, and a datum of $N_g$ gives a datum of $N_0$ at which the good orientation condition holds.

The pair theorem applied to these inputs, with the second nonvanishing statement as the pass hypothesis, gives the two conclusions. $\square$

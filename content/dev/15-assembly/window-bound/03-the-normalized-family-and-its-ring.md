## The normalized family and its ring

This section and the next two prove the closed form of the Selmer bound at a point, [Theorem 2.5.7](/entry/theorem-n5-from-inputs/). That theorem is the main input of [the Selmer bound at a point](/entry/selmer-bound-at-point-main/) in Volume 4. The proof changes the family $r$ at the one point $\pi$, so that $r_\pi$ becomes block diagonal, and then builds a finite extension of the Hecke algebra on which the eigenvalues of one matrix of the family become functions. This section gives the change at $\pi$ and the ring. Its main result is the theorem on the ring of a normalized family at the end.

We use the data of [the Selmer bound at a point](/entry/selmer-bound-at-point-main/): the number field $E$ with $G_E = \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/E)$, the ring $\mathcal{O}$ with fraction field $L$ and residue field $k$, the finite set $X$, the Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T} \subseteq \mathcal{O}^X$, the subset $Z$, the families $r_x$ of rank $n_V = r + t$ and $\rho_x$ of rank $r$, the point $\pi$, and the characters $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_t$. The datum also fixes a character $\varepsilon : G_E \to \mathcal{O}^\times$ with $\chi_c = \chi_1 \varepsilon^{1-c}$ for $1 \leq c \leq t$, and it makes $\mathbb{T}$ a local ring whose functions separate the points of $X$. For an $\mathcal{O}$-module $M$ of finite length write $\ell(M)$ for its length, and put $e_{\mathcal{O}} = \ell(\mathcal{O}/\ell)$. The congruence ideal $\eta_\pi$ is as in Volume 4. Since $\mathcal{O}$ is a discrete valuation ring, $\eta_\pi$ is generated by $f(\pi)$ for one $f$ that vanishes off $Z$, and $\ell(\mathcal{O}/\eta_\pi)$ is the valuation of $f(\pi)$.

We split the index set $\{1, \dots, n_V\}$ into blocks: block $0$ is $\{1, \dots, r\}$ and block $c$ is the single index $r + c$, for $1 \leq c \leq t$.

**Definition 2.3.1 (Normal form at $\pi$).** The family $r$ is *normal at* $\pi$ if each $r_\pi(g)$ is block diagonal, its block $0$ is $\rho_\pi(g)$, and its entry in block $c$ is $\chi_c(g)$, for $c = 1, \dots, t$.

**Definition 2.3.2 (Normalized family).** The *normalized family* $r^{\mathrm{N}}$ is equal to $r_x$ at each $x \neq \pi$, and at $\pi$ it is
$$r^{\mathrm{N}}_\pi = \rho_\pi \oplus \chi_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \chi_t .$$

The family $r^{\mathrm{N}}$ is normal at $\pi$. Condition 6 of the Selmer bound says $r_\pi \otimes L \cong r^{\mathrm{N}}_\pi \otimes L$. So the traces and the characteristic polynomials of $r$ and $r^{\mathrm{N}}$ agree at each point, and the conditions on characteristic polynomials pass from $r$ to $r^{\mathrm{N}}$. The bound to prove names $\rho_\pi$ and not $r$, so we may prove it for $r^{\mathrm{N}}$.

Let $\mathbb{T}[r]$ be the $\mathbb{T}$-module of functions $X \to M_{n_V}(\mathcal{O})$ spanned by the functions $x \mapsto r_x(g)$, $g \in G_E$. For matrices $e$ and $a$ of size $n_V$ over a commutative ring, the *block polynomial* is $P_{e,a}(X) = \det(X e - a + (1 - e))$. If $e$ is the projection onto a set of $t$ coordinates, then $P_{e,a}$ is the characteristic polynomial of the corner of $a$ on these coordinates.

**Definition 2.3.3 (Idempotent pair).** An *idempotent pair* for $r$ is a pair $e_B, e_1 \in \mathbb{T}[r]$ with $e_B^2 = e_B$, $e_1^2 = e_1$, $e_B e_1 = e_1 e_B = 0$, such that over $L$ the rank of $e_B(x)$ is $t$ and the rank of $e_1(x)$ is $1$ at each $x$, $e_B(\pi)$ is the projection onto the blocks $1$ to $t$, and $e_1(\pi)$ has all its nonzero entries in block $0$.

**Definition 2.3.4 (Corner perturbation).** Let $g_0 \in G_E$ and let $e_B$ be as above. A *corner perturbation* for $g_0$ is an $s \in \mathbb{T}[r]$ with $e_B s = s e_B = s$ and $s(\pi) = 0$, such that at each $x$ the block polynomial of $e_B(x)$ and $X_0(x) = e_B(x) r_x(g_0) e_B(x) + s(x)$ has $t$ distinct roots in $L$.

At $\pi$ the matrix $X_0(\pi)$ is the diagonal of $\chi_1(g_0), \dots, \chi_t(g_0)$ on the blocks $1$ to $t$. With $\chi_c = \chi_1 \varepsilon^{1-c}$ its eigenvalues are $\chi_1(g_0) \varepsilon(g_0)^{-i}$ for $0 \leq i < t$. The perturbation $s$ makes the eigenvalues distinct at every point, not only at $\pi$.

**Definition 2.3.5 (Ring datum).** Let $(e_B, e_1)$ be an idempotent pair and $s$ a corner perturbation for $g_0$. A *ring datum* consists of:

1. a finite set $X'$ with a map $p : X' \to X$, and functions $\varrho_0, \dots, \varrho_{t-1} : X' \to \mathcal{O}$, such that at each $x'$ the block polynomial at $p(x')$ is $\prod_{i<t} (X - \varrho_i(x'))$, and each ordered list of the roots at a point $x$ comes from exactly one $x'$ over $x$;
2. the $\mathcal{O}$-algebra $\mathbb{T}'$ generated by the functions $f \circ p$, $f \in \mathbb{T}$, and the $\varrho_i$; it is free over $\mathbb{T}$ with a basis of $t!$ elements;
3. a point $\pi'$ over $\pi$ with $\varrho_i(\pi') = \chi_1(g_0) \varepsilon(g_0)^{-i}$;
4. the $\mathbb{T}'$-algebra $R'$ of functions $X' \to M_{n_V}(L)$ spanned by $x' \mapsto r_{p(x')}(g)$, with the action of $\mathbb{T}'$ by values;
5. the elements $X_0$ and $\mathcal{E} = e_B \prod_{0 < i < t} (X_0 - \varrho_i e_B)$ of $R'$, and the evaluation $R' \to M_{n_V}(\mathcal{O})$ at $\pi'$ with its block parts.

Put $\delta = \prod_{0 < i < t} (\varrho_0 - \varrho_i) \in \mathbb{T}'$ and $\delta_{\mathcal{O}} = \delta(\pi')$.

The element $\mathcal{E}$ is the projection onto the eigenline of $X_0$ for the root $\varrho_0$, times the factor $\delta$: on that eigenline each factor $X_0 - \varrho_i$ acts by $\varrho_0 - \varrho_i$, and on each other eigenline one factor is zero. So $\mathcal{E}^2 = \delta \mathcal{E}$, and $\mathcal{E}$ has rank one at each point. At $\pi'$ we get
$$\delta_{\mathcal{O}} = \prod_{0 < i < t} \big(u_1 - u_1 \varepsilon(g_0)^{-i}\big), \qquad u_1 = \chi_1(g_0).$$
If $\varepsilon(g_0) = 1 + \ell^{s_0} u_0$ with $u_0$ a unit, then $v_\ell(1 - \varepsilon(g_0)^{-i}) = s_0 + v_\ell(i)$. This is the source of the term $\delta = \sum_{0<k<t} (s_0 + v_\ell(k))$ in the Selmer bound.

The *display bound* for a number $\lambda$ is the following statement, which we do not write out in full. For each choice of column places, local lattices, local conditions $F(c, w)$ on the blocks, cocycles and constants that satisfy a list of local hypotheses, $\lambda$ is at most the sum on the right side of the Selmer bound at a point, written with lengths:

$$\begin{aligned} \lambda \leq {}& \sum_{w \in \mathrm{row}} \mathrm{len}_w + \sum_{w \in \mathrm{col}} \big(e_{\mathcal{O}} e'_w + j_w (e_{\mathcal{O}} e'_w + v_\delta) + 2 v_\delta + h_{0,w}\big) + s \\ &+ \sum_{0<e<t} \text{Ш}_e + e_{\mathcal{O}} v_\ell(h_E) + t e_{\mathcal{O}} c_B \sum_{w \in \mathrm{row}} (d_w + 2) \\ &+ 4 n_V (|\mathrm{col}| + 1) v_\delta + t |\mathrm{col}| H_0 + t \sum_{v \in Q} v(\zeta_v - 1), \end{aligned}$$

where $v_\delta$ is the valuation of $\delta_{\mathcal{O}}$. It also says $v_\delta = e_{\mathcal{O}} \sum_{0<i<t} (s_0 + v_\ell(i))$ and $v_\delta \leq e_{\mathcal{O}} (t s_0 + v_\ell(t!))$. Among the hypotheses are: $\varepsilon$ is a cyclotomic character, $\varepsilon(g_0) - 1 = \ell^{s_0} y$ with $y$ a unit, $s_0 \geq 1$, and the local clauses on the blocks that the next section proves.

**Theorem 2.3.6 (Ring of a normalized family).** Let $r$ be normal at $\pi$. Assume that for each $g \in G_E$ the coefficients of the reverse characteristic polynomial of $r_x(g)$ lie in $\mathbb{T}$ as functions of $x$, and that the reductions $\bar\rho_\pi(g)$ span $M_r(k)$ over $k$. Let $(e_B, e_1)$ be an idempotent pair and $s$ a corner perturbation for $g_0$. Then there is a ring datum such that:

1. $R'$ is Noetherian, and the compatibility conditions of the scalar actions of $\mathbb{T}'$, $R'$ and $\mathcal{O}$ on the fibres and on the blocks hold;
2. the congruence length of $p(\pi') = \pi$ is $\ell(\mathcal{O}/\eta_\pi)$;
3. the ring datum has the properties (T) of Lemma (Ring objects) below;
4. for every choice of topologies on $L$ and $R'$ that make them topological groups with continuous operations, the display bound holds for $\lambda = \ell(\mathcal{O}/\eta_\pi)$.

The proof uses three results that are proved elsewhere in the development. We state them.

**Lemma 2.3.7 (Ring objects).** Let $r$ be a family with these properties: $\mathbb{T}$ is local and separates points, the characteristic polynomial coefficients lie in $\mathbb{T}$, $r_\pi$ is block diagonal for the blocks above, and the entry of block $c \geq 1$ is $\chi_1 \varepsilon^{1-c}$. Let the reduction of block $0$ of $r_\pi$ be absolutely irreducible. Then for each idempotent pair and corner perturbation there is a ring datum with property 1 of the theorem and the following properties (T): $\mathbb{T}'$ is spanned over $\mathbb{T}$ by $t!$ elements that are independent on each fibre of $p$; each determinant of a matrix over $R'$ is given by a function in $\mathbb{T}'$; $\mathcal{E}^2 = \delta \mathcal{E}$ and $e_1^2 = e_1$; at each $x'$ the element $\delta$ acts by a nonzero scalar and $\mathcal{E}$ and $e_1$ have rank one; the evaluation at $\pi'$ is block triangular with surjective block parts; and on the blocks at $\pi'$, the elements $\mathcal{E}$, $\delta$ and $e_1$ act by the scalars that the block form forces.

**Lemma 2.3.8 (Freeness).** For a ring datum as in the last lemma and each subset $Z$, a freeness property of the fibres of $R'$ over $\mathbb{T}'$ relative to $Z$ holds. We use it only as an input of the next lemma.

**Lemma 2.3.9 (Display from freeness).** For a ring datum with the properties (T), the freeness property, and $\delta_{\mathcal{O}} = \prod_{0<i<t} (u_1 - u_1 \varepsilon(g_0)^{-i})$ for a unit $u_1$, the display bound holds for $\lambda$ equal to the congruence length of $p(\pi')$.

*Proof of the theorem.* The normal form gives the block diagonal shape of $r_\pi$ and its entries $\chi_c = \chi_1 \varepsilon^{1-c}$. With the condition on characteristic polynomials and the two properties of $\mathbb{T}$, this gives the hypotheses of Lemma (Ring objects) on $r$. The span condition gives absolute irreducibility of $\bar\rho_\pi$. Indeed, a subspace of $k^r$ that is stable under all of $M_r(k)$ is $0$ or $k^r$, and a matrix that commutes with all of $M_r(k)$ is a scalar. So Lemma (Ring objects) gives a ring datum with property 1 and the properties (T); this is property 3. Lemma (Freeness) gives the freeness property. The ring datum has $p(\pi') = \pi$. Since $\eta_\pi$ is principal, generated by $f(\pi)$ with $f$ as above, the congruence length of $\pi$ is $\ell(\mathcal{O}/\eta_\pi)$; this is property 2. The product formula for $\delta_{\mathcal{O}}$, with $u_1 = \chi_1(g_0)$, follows from item 3 of the ring datum. Lemma (Display from freeness) with $\lambda = \ell(\mathcal{O}/\eta_\pi)$ gives property 4. $\square$

**Remark 2.3.10 (Formalization).** The family $r^{\mathrm{N}}$ is `Norm.rN`, and the normal form is `NormAt`. The three lemmas are `ring_objects_exist`, `displayFree` and `realDisplayAllK_of`; the absolute irreducibility step is `absIrr_of_res`, and the step from the normal form to the hypotheses of the first lemma is `primHyp_of`. We read their statements, not their proofs. The properties (T) are `RingData.Top`; the freeness property is `LocalToDisplayFree`, which we did not unfold. The display bound `RealDisplayAllK` has about thirty hypotheses on local lattices and local conditions; we name only those that this chapter uses. The relation $\chi_c = \chi_1 \varepsilon^{1-c}$ and the two properties of $\mathbb{T}$ are fields of the datum. In [NormalizedRingData.lean](lean:Formalism/AdmissibleDesigns/NormalizedRingData.lean#L43) the proof discards with `-` the `SMulCommClass` instance that `ring_objects_exist` gives in its bundle, and uses a second copy of it that the same result gives; this does not change the statement.

## The example record

Section 24 built the [example host](/entry/h0/) $H_0$ and stated [that a datum exists once local period data exist](/entry/record-nonempty-of-local-gap/). This section gives the four objects behind that proof: the host axioms of $H_0$, the record built from a host with explicit data, its specialization to $H_0$, and the theorem that packs the record into a datum. We keep the notation of Section 24: the ranks $R_0$, the primes $q = 571$ and $d = 599$, the layer field $L_0$ with its generators $\zeta, a, b$, the type primes $g_0, g_1$, the level prime $T_0$, the element $\pi_0 = 1 + e_0 \sqrt{-d}$, and the set $\mathrm{LG}(H)$ of local period data for a host $H$.

### The host axioms

The ranks $R_0$ come with a cyclic frame of order $4 = 2^e$, so its exponent is $e = 2$. The *host conditions* on a pair $(q, d)$ of integers are that $q$ and $d$ are prime and

$$\begin{aligned} q &\equiv 3 \pmod 8, \\ d &\equiv 7 \pmod{16}, \\ \Bigl(\frac{-d}{q}\Bigr) &= 1. \end{aligned}$$

A *layer* of level $e + 2$ for $(q, d)$ is a number field $L$ with elements $\zeta, a, b$ such that $\zeta$ is a primitive root of unity of order $2^{e+2}$, $a^2 = -q$ and $b^2 = -d$. For $e = 2$ the order of $\zeta$ is $16$, which is the field $L_0$ of Section 24.

**Definition 7.25.1 (Host axioms from a layer).** Let $\varphi$ be a frame with injective rank map and exponent $e \geq 2$, let $(q, d)$ satisfy the host conditions, and let $(L, \zeta, a, b)$ be a layer of level $e + 2$. The *host axioms* of these data consist of two parts.

1. The raw part: the CM field $E$ generated by $\zeta$, $a$ and $b$ inside $L$, its totally real subfield $F$, the quadratic field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, the conjugation, the identification of the Galois group of $E$ with the group of the frame, a CM type, and an ideal $\mathfrak{b}$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$.
2. The derived part: the facts about these objects that the axioms ask for. These include the order of the Galois group, the reality of the squares of the CM type, the coprimality of $2^{e+2}$ and $15$, and the factorization of the different of $E$ over $K$ as $\mathfrak{b}\,\bar{\mathfrak{b}}$ with $\mathfrak{b} + \bar{\mathfrak{b}} = \mathcal{O}_E$.

The *example host axioms* are the host axioms of the frame of $R_0$, of the pair $(571, 599)$, and of the layer $L_0$.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.Examples₂.hostAx0`.

The host conditions for $(571, 599)$ are three congruences and one Jacobi symbol: $571 = 8 \cdot 71 + 3$, $599 = 16 \cdot 37 + 7$, and $-599$ is a square modulo $571$. These are checked by a finite computation. The bound $e \geq 2$ holds with equality.

### The record from explicit data

**Definition 7.25.2 (The record from explicit data).** Let $H$ be a host over $R_0$, and assume:

1. each type prime $g_k$ has exactly $[E : \mathbb{Q}]$ places of $E$ above it, so it splits completely;
2. the type hypothesis of $H$ holds;
3. $T$ is a level prime of $H$, different from the two split primes of $H$;
4. $\pi \in \mathcal{O}_E$ lies in $K$, has $\pi\,\bar{\pi} = T$, and satisfies
$$\pi - 1 \in M_H\, \mathcal{O}_E, \qquad M_H = Q_H\, q\, d\, (g_0 g_1)^2,$$
where $Q_H$ is the square part of the modulus of $H$.

For $G \in \mathrm{LG}(H)$, the *record* of these data is the datum with the following fields:

1. the ranks $R_0$ and the host $H$;
2. the hermitian spaces built from $H$, $T$ and $\pi$, and the rows built from $H$ and $T$;
3. one word, with one fixed Harish-Chandra parameter;
4. the members built from $T$, the zero lattice datum over $E$ as divisor data, and the row field $E$ with the identity embedding;
5. the local period data of $G$ at every prime.

The remaining families are indexed by empty types, so they are given by the empty function.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.Examples₂.exampleRecordOfData`.

For $H_0$ the four conditions are the four facts of Section 24: $g_0$ and $g_1$ split completely in $E$, the type hypothesis holds, $T_0$ is a level prime different from $751$ and $991$, and $\pi_0$ lies in $K$ with norm $T_0$ and $\pi_0 \equiv 1$ modulo $M = 16\, q\, d\, (g_0 g_1)^2$. Here the square part is $Q_{H_0} = 16$.

**Definition 7.25.3 (The example record).** For $G \in \mathrm{LG}(H_0)$, the *example record* of $G$ is the record of $H_0$, $T_0$ and $\pi_0$ with the local period data $G$.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.Examples₂.exampleRecord`.

**Theorem 7.25.4 (The example record is a datum).** For each $G \in \mathrm{LG}(H_0)$ there is a datum, namely the example record of $G$.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.Examples₂.nonempty_D7_of_localGap`.

*Proof.* The example record of $G$ is a datum by its definition. $\square$

All checks are made when the record is built: each field of a datum carries its own conditions, and the record supplies them from the four conditions on $(H, T, \pi)$ and from the conditions of the host. So the theorem has no content of its own beyond the construction. This is the reason why [the theorem of Section 24](/entry/record-nonempty-of-local-gap/) is one line: it takes an element of $\mathrm{LG}(H_0)$ and applies the present theorem.

### What the record is used for

The record is built only to show that the hypothesis "for every datum $D$" is not empty. The [theorem that the record is nonempty](/entry/record-nonempty/), Section 34, gives an element of $\mathrm{LG}(H_0)$ from the local theory at each prime, and so a datum. The record is degenerate in many fields. Its divisor data are the zero lattice datum, its row field is $E$ itself, it has a single word, and several of its families are indexed by empty types. A datum with these fields satisfies the axioms, but no bound of the volume is computed at it.

The user of the nonemptiness, the [choice of global positions](/entry/exists-positions-global/), reads only the local period data of the datum it gets. Those fields come directly from $G$. So in the present use the global part of the record is not needed. It is still built in full, because the type of data asks for all fields.

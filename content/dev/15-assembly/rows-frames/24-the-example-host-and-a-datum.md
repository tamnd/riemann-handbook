## The example host and a datum

This section builds an explicit host and uses it to show that data exist, as [the theorem that the record is nonempty](/entry/record-nonempty/) needs. Many statements of this volume begin with "for every datum $D$". The theorem shows that such statements are not empty. The only input that is not explicit is the local period data at each prime: the period rings, the data for $A_{\inf}$ and the ties to the concrete rings. All the global data come from one host $H_0$ with explicit primes, and all its checks are finite computations with integers.

### The setting

A *datum* $D$ consists of ranks, a host, a family of hermitian spaces, row data with a row field, Harish-Chandra parameters, embeddings and members, and local period data at every prime $\ell$. The *local period data* at $\ell$ are rings $B$ and $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ with the action of the Galois group, the Frobenius, the filtration and the element $t$, data for $A_{\inf}$, and ring isomorphisms that tie $B$ and $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ to the period rings of Fontaine. For a host $H$ let $\mathrm{LG}(H)$ be the set of families of local period data at all primes. The host does not enter these fields.

The *example ranks* $R_0$ have $k = 8$, $m = 2$, $t = 15$, $f = 8$ and $n = 4$, over the cyclic frame of order $4$. They satisfy the rank conditions: $k$ and $m$ are even, $t$ is odd, $m + 3 \leq k$, $3m < t$, $m \leq f \leq k$ and $m^2 \leq n$.

Let $q = 571$ and $d = 599$. These satisfy the *host conditions*: $q$ and $d$ are prime,

$$\begin{aligned} q &\equiv 3 \pmod 8, \\ d &\equiv 7 \pmod{16}, \\ \Bigl(\frac{-d}{q}\Bigr) &= 1. \end{aligned}$$

Let $L_0$ be a number field generated by a primitive $16$th root of unity $\zeta$ and elements $a, b$ with $a^2 = -q$ and $b^2 = -d$. Such a field exists by the decomposition of the $2$-adic cyclotomic tower, and we fix one with its three generators. The host axioms built from $L_0$ give a CM field $E \subseteq L_0$ over a totally real field $F$, with a nonempty CM type and an ideal $\mathfrak{b}$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$ such that the different of $E$ over its quadratic subfield $K$ is $\mathfrak{b}\, \bar{\mathfrak{b}}$ with $\mathfrak{b} + \bar{\mathfrak{b}} = \mathcal{O}_E$. Here the bar is the conjugation of $E$ over $F$.

Put

$$\begin{aligned} g_0 &= 413718278401, \\ g_1 &= 603339156001, \\ M &= 16\, q\, d\, (g_0 g_1)^2. \end{aligned}$$

### The host

**Definition 7.24.1 (The example host).** The *example host* $H_0$ is the host over $R_0$ with the host axioms of $L_0$, the square roots $\sqrt{-q}$ and $\sqrt{-d}$ in $E$, the chosen element of the CM type and the ideal $\mathfrak{b}$, and the following values:

1. the type primes $g_0$ and $g_1$;
2. the split primes $751$ and $991$;
3. the level primes $751$, $991$, $g_0$, $g_1$ and the prime $T_0$ below;
4. the square part $16$ of the modulus, and the split bound $2$;
5. the conductor ideal $\mathcal{O}_K$, the empty set of twist places, the set $\{0\}$ of second places, the base index $0$, the width $50$ and the constant $0$.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.Examples₂.H0`.

The host conditions are checks on the numbers. The square part $16$ is at most $n^2 = 16$. The bound $4 t r + 1 = 541$ is below $q$ and $d$. Each odd prime that divides $16$ is at most the split bound, and each odd prime up to $2$ is split; both conditions hold since there are no such primes. The level primes are prime to $16$. The type primes and the split primes are distinct, and the conductor ideal is the whole ring, so its conditions hold at once.

The checks on the primes are the following. For $k = 0, 1$,

$$g_k \equiv 1 \pmod{16\, q\, d}, \qquad \Bigl(\frac{-d}{g_k}\Bigr) = 1.$$

Here $16\, q\, d = 5472464$. With the disjointness of the bad sets of the two type primes, this gives the type hypothesis, and the type primes split completely in $E$. For $p = 751$ and $p = 991$,

$$p \nmid 16\, q\, d, \qquad \Bigl(\frac{-q}{p}\Bigr) = \Bigl(\frac{-d}{p}\Bigr) = 1, \qquad p \equiv -1 \pmod{16}.$$

So the split primes also split completely in $E$.

Now the level prime. Put

$$e_0 = 972\, M, \qquad T_0 = 1 + d\, e_0^2.$$

The number $T_0$ has $116$ digits, and it is prime. It is a level prime of $H_0$ by definition, it is not $751$ or $991$, and $T_0 \geq 55$. The element

$$\pi_0 = 1 + e_0 \sqrt{-d}$$

is an integer of $E$, it lies in $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, and

$$\pi_0\, \bar{\pi}_0 = 1 + d\, e_0^2 = T_0, \qquad \pi_0 - 1 = 972 \sqrt{-d} \cdot M \in M \mathcal{O}_E.$$

So $T_0$ is a norm from $K$ of an element that is $1$ modulo $M$.

### A datum

**Theorem 7.24.2 (A datum from local period data).** If $\mathrm{LG}(H_0)$ is nonempty, then there is a datum.

Lean: `Formalism.BasicNotions.Examples₂.record_nonempty_of_localGap`.

*Proof.* Let $G \in \mathrm{LG}(H_0)$. We take the steps in order.

1. The example record takes the ranks $R_0$ and the host $H_0$.
2. Its hermitian spaces and its rows are built from $H_0$ and the prime $T_0$ with the element $\pi_0$. This uses the four facts above: the type primes split in $E$, the type hypothesis holds, $T_0$ is a level prime different from the split primes, and $\pi_0 \in K$ has norm $T_0$ and is $1$ modulo $M$.
3. The members are built from $T_0$, the divisor data are the zero lattice datum over $E$, there is one word with one fixed Harish-Chandra parameter, and the row field is $E$ with the identity embedding.
4. The local period data of the record are the three fields of $G$.

This gives a datum. $\square$

The converse also holds: each datum carries local period data, and these form an element of $\mathrm{LG}(H_0)$, since the host does not enter. So data exist if and only if local period data exist. The theorem that the record is nonempty proves that local period data exist at every prime, and so there is a datum.

The example is small in its ranks and large in its primes. The type primes must be $1$ modulo $16 q d$, and $T_0$ must be $1$ modulo $M$, so the norm $T_0$ of $\pi_0 = 1 + e_0 \sqrt{-d}$ has more than a hundred digits. The example is built only to show that data exist, and no bound of the volume is evaluated at it.

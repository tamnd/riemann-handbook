## Highest weights and surviving subquotients

This section proves the weight inputs with lattice links for the Hida setting of a frame at a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of its Hecke algebra. The inputs ask that each simple subquotient of the reduction of a dual Weyl module that survives localization at $\mathfrak{m}$ carries a lattice link. The result is used by [the model statement for a small frame](/entry/model-st-of-small-frame-gal/), in the surjectivity branch of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/). There are two steps. First, a simple subquotient of the reduction of a dual Weyl module embeds into the reduction of another dual Weyl module, with a highest weight $\mu$. Second, if the subquotient survives localization at $\mathfrak{m}$, then $\mu$ is linked to the original weight and has a crystalline block, so [the lattice link of a block](/entry/crystalline-lattice-link-of-block-data/) of the last section applies.

We use [highest weights](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching) and [general linear groups](/learn/groups/#general-linear-and-symplectic-groups) of Volume 1.

Let $\mathcal{O}$ be a discrete valuation ring of characteristic $0$ with an irreducible element $\varpi$, and let $\iota$ be a finite index set. For a family $\lambda = (\lambda_\tau)_{\tau \in \iota}$ of weights $\lambda_\tau : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$, each antitone on $\{0, \dots, N-1\}$, let
$$\nabla(\lambda) = \bigotimes_{\tau \in \iota} \nabla_{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda_\tau)$$
be the tensor product of the dual Weyl modules of $\mathrm{GL}_N$ over $\mathcal{O}$, realized as polynomials in the matrix entries. The group $\prod_\tau \mathrm{GL}_N(\mathcal{O})$ acts on it. For a factor $\tau$ and a row weight $w$, the *weight projector* $e_{\tau, w}$ is the projection onto the weighted homogeneous component of row weight $w$ in factor $\tau$. Let $A$ be the $\mathcal{O}$-algebra of endomorphisms generated by the group and all weight projectors.

A *subquotient* $G$ of $\nabla(\lambda)/\varpi$ is an $A$-module with a surjection $\pi : U \to G$ from an $A$-submodule $U \subseteq \nabla(\lambda)$ such that $\pi(\varpi y) = 0$ for all $y$ with $\varpi y \in U$. It is *simple* if it is simple as an $A$-module. For weights $\lambda$ and $\mu$, $G$ *embeds with weight* $\mu$ if there is an injective additive map $\psi : G \to \nabla(\mu)/\varpi$ that is compatible with the scalars, the group action and the weight projectors.

A *joint weight* of $G$ is a family $w = (w_\tau)$ of row weights such that some nonzero $z \in G$ is fixed by all $e_{\tau, w_\tau}$. Its *potential* is
$$\mathrm{pot}(w) = \sum_{\tau} \sum_{a} (N - a)\, w_\tau(a).$$
Two facts on dual Weyl modules enter. *Base change*: the dual Weyl module over $\mathcal{O}/\varpi$ is the reduction of the one over $\mathcal{O}$, and the kernel of the reduction is $\varpi \nabla(\mu)$. *Coefficients*: generic row coefficients of a domain lie in the algebra generated by the coefficients of $A$.

**Lemma 7.7.1 (Embedding at a weight of maximal potential).** Let $G$ be a simple subquotient of $\nabla(\lambda)/\varpi$, and let $w$ be a joint weight of $G$ of maximal potential, with $\mu$ the weight given by $w$. Assume base change and the coefficient fact. Then $G$ embeds with weight $\mu$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.simple_subquotient_embeds_dualWeyl_reduction_maxWeight` (also `GlobalTheory.HighWtEmb.embeds_of_maxWeight`).

*Proof.* Let $z_0 \neq 0$ be fixed by the projectors of $w$. There is a weight form $\phi$ on $G$ that is nonzero on $z_0$. With $\phi$ we form matrix coefficients: the map $P : G \to \nabla_{\mathcal{O}/\varpi}(w)$ that sends $z$ to the polynomial function $g \mapsto \phi(g z)$. It has $P(z_0) \neq 0$, and it is compatible with the group and the weight projectors, since $w$ has maximal potential. By base change, the reduction map
$$\nabla(\mu)/\varpi \to \nabla_{\mathcal{O}/\varpi}(\mu)$$
is injective, and each value of $P$ lifts to $\nabla(\mu)$. So $P$ factors as $L \circ \psi$ with $\psi : G \to \nabla(\mu)/\varpi$ and $L$ the injective reduction map. The kernel of $P$ is an $A$-submodule of $G$. It does not contain $z_0$, so it is $0$ since $G$ is simple. So $\psi$ is injective. It is compatible with scalars, the group and the projectors because $P$ is and $L$ is injective. $\square$

**Theorem 7.7.2 (Simple subquotients have a highest weight).** Let $\lambda$ be antitone and let $G$ be a simple subquotient of $\nabla(\lambda)/\varpi$. Then there is $\mu$ with each $\mu_\tau$ antitone,
$$\sum_a \mu_\tau(a) = \sum_a \lambda_\tau(a), \qquad \lambda_\tau(N-1) \leq \mu_\tau(a) \leq \lambda_\tau(0),$$
a weight projector $e$ and $z \in G$ with $e z \neq 0$, and $G$ embeds with weight $\mu$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.simpleSubquotient_dualWeyl_embeds_highestWeight` (also `GlobalTheory.HighWt.simpleSubquot_embeds`).

*Proof.* $G$ is nonzero, so it has a joint weight. The row weights of $\nabla(\lambda)$ have entries between $\lambda_\tau(N-1)$ and $\lambda_\tau(0)$ and the same sum as $\lambda_\tau$, so there are finitely many joint weights. Take one, $w$, of maximal potential. If some $w_\tau$ were not antitone, a raising operator in factor $\tau$ applied to a vector of weight $w$ would give a vector of larger potential, so $w$ would not be maximal. So $w$ is antitone, and $\mu = w$ is a weight with the bounds and the sum above. The lemma gives the embedding; base change holds for dual Weyl modules over a discrete valuation ring of characteristic zero, and the coefficient fact holds over a domain. $\square$

Now let $F$ be a good frame with Hecke algebra $T$ over $\mathcal{O}$, with $\varpi \mid \ell$ irreducible. Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a maximal ideal of $T$, and let $\theta_0$ be an eigensystem of $F$ over $\mathfrak{m}$ with the Galois side condition. A Hecke module $G$ *survives* at $\mathfrak{m}$ if its localization $G_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is nonzero.

**Theorem 7.7.3 (Lattice link of a surviving subquotient).** Assume the Chebotarev theorem in degree one and the lattice link for all $\mu$ that are linked and have a block. Let $G$ be a simple subquotient of $\nabla(\lambda)/\varpi$ with a Hecke action, with $\mu$ as in the last theorem, and let $G$ survive at $\mathfrak{m}$. Then a lattice link holds for $\mu$ at every $\tau$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.latticeLink_of_surviving_simple_subquotient` (also `GlobalTheory.ExpFrame.LinkQ.latticeLink_of_surviving`).

*Proof.* Since $G_{\mathfrak{m}} \neq 0$, some composition factor of the forms with values in $G$ lies over $\mathfrak{m}$. It lifts to an eigenform $\theta$ of characteristic zero with weight $\mu$. The eigensystem $\theta_0$ and $\theta$ both lie over $\mathfrak{m}$, so there is a Galois conjugate $\theta'$ of $\theta$ whose eigenvalues are congruent to those of $\theta_0$. The Galois representations attached to $\theta_0$ and $\theta'$ then have congruent characteristic polynomials of Frobenius at a set of primes of degree one. By the Chebotarev theorem the congruence holds on the whole Galois group, which gives a block for $\mu$. The embedding of $G$ into $\nabla(\mu)/\varpi$ links the simple module to the dual Weyl lattice, which gives the linkage of $\mu$ and $\lambda$: the products $\prod_a (X + \lambda_\tau(a) - a)$ and $\prod_a (X + \mu_\tau(a) - a)$ agree modulo $\ell$. The hypothesis on lattice links now applies. $\square$

The *weight inputs with lattice links* for a Hida setting $X$ at $\ell$ ask for a uniformizer $\varpi$ with $\varpi \mid \ell$ and $\ell \mid \varpi^{n_0}$, weight projectors, and a family of Hecke modules $L_\mu$ with the frame conditions, such that each $L_\mu$ that is a subquotient of $\nabla(\lambda)/\varpi$ and survives at $\mathfrak{m}$ has a lattice link at each $\tau$ in a given set $T_2$.

**Theorem 7.7.4 (Weight inputs of a frame).** Let $E$ be CM, $\mathrm{inc}$ a half, and $W$ weights with their hypotheses. Let $\mathrm{fr}$, $\mathrm{cj}$ be as in the last section and $W'$ the weights $W$ with these Frobenius data. Assume the Chebotarev theorem in degree one, the lattice link for all linked $\mu$ with a block, a good frame $F$ with a Galois side condition and a Hecke action, that $\mathcal{O}/\ell$ is finite and $\ell$ is not a unit in $\mathcal{O}$, and an eigensystem $\theta_0$ over a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$. Then the weight inputs with lattice links hold for $W'$ and the Hida setting $X_{\mathfrak{m}}$ of $F$ at $\mathfrak{m}$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.ExpModel.winputs_frame`.

*Proof.* Since $\ell$ is not a unit and $\mathcal{O}$ is a discrete valuation ring, there is an irreducible $\varpi$ with $\varpi \mid \ell$ and $\ell \mid \varpi^{n_0}$. Weight projectors exist and are unique. Take for the family $L_\mu$ the simple subquotients of $\nabla(\lambda)/\varpi$ themselves. For such a $G$, the forms with values in $G$ carry a Hecke action, and the theorem on highest weights gives $\mu$. The Hecke maps are natural, since the Hecke frame acts by polynomials. If $G$ survives at $\mathfrak{m}$, the last theorem gives the lattice link for $\mu$. The frame conditions hold since the algebra $A$ preserves the chain of the frame. $\square$

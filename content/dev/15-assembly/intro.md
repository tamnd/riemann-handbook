This volume joins the branches of the proof. Its last result is that no Dirichlet $L$-function vanishes in the half-plane $\operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12$. This is the generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet $L$-functions, in the form [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character-def/). The volume proves it from three inputs: the statement Green on the cuspidal characters of $\mathrm{GL}_d$ over a finite field, the window bound VWin, and a record of hypotheses on families of theta data. It then proves VWin from counts, and it proves that the record of hypotheses has an element. For this it takes a list of published results as given and derives short consequences from them. Volume 1 gives the background, and this volume uses it without proof. The analytic part uses [the explicit formula](/learn/analytic-number-theory/#the-explicit-formula), [Dirichlet $L$-functions](/learn/l-functions/#dirichlet-l-functions) and [primes in progressions](/learn/analytic-number-theory/#primes-in-progressions-and-in-short-intervals). The arithmetic part uses [unitary groups](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups), [discrete series](/learn/real-groups/#discrete-series-and-formal-degrees), [the Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation) and [the Siegel-Weil formula](/learn/theta/#the-siegel-weil-formula). The path on [the literature](/learn/literature/) explains which statements the proof takes from published sources.

The proof uses the volume in four places. The first is [the literature chapter](/proof/literature/). Chapter 1 groups the published results into bundles for its step [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/). The second is [the proof chapter on VWin](/proof/vwin/). Chapter 2 proves VWin from counts, and [the theorem with four counts](/entry/of-four-alt/) uses this bound. The third is the step [EveryCharacter from theta integrals and local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/). It applies the steps [ZeroFreeAll from Green, VWin and HypsAlt](/step/zero-free-all-of-green-v-win/) and [EveryCharacter from Green, VWin and HypsAlt](/step/green-vwin/), which Chapter 3 proves. The fourth is [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/). Its step [nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/) needs an element of [the record of hypotheses](/step/hyps-alt-def/) under [the unitary group hypotheses at the third place](/step/unitary-group-third-place-hypotheses-def/). Chapter 8 gives this element as branch 1 of that node. Chapter 6 proves step 1 of branch 1, Chapter 5 proves branch 2, and Chapter 4 proves branch 3. Chapter 7 collects inputs for Chapters 5 and 8.

Chapter 1 states the results that the proof takes from the literature, each in the form that the development uses. It covers real unitary groups, such as the Casselman-Wallach theorem, [Theorem 1.1.4](/entry/casselman-wallach-of-literature/), and archimedean Howe duality, [Theorem 1.2.2](/entry/howe-duality-real-of-literature/). It covers the theta lift for $\mathrm{GL}$ of Mínguez, [Theorem 1.2.5](/entry/theta-lift-general-linear/), strong approximation for $SU$, [Theorem 1.3.1](/entry/strong-approx-su-of-literature/), and the strong form of Flath's theorem, [Theorem 1.3.4](/entry/flath-factorisation-of-literature/). On the arithmetic side it gives the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel theorem, [Theorem 1.4.2](/entry/arith-hilbert-samuel-of-literature/), the toroidal compactifications of Faltings and Chai, [Theorem 1.5.5](/entry/siegel-toroidal-integral-of-literature/), and the theorem of Bhatt and Scholze on crystalline lattices, [Theorem 1.6.3](/entry/bhatt-scholze-integral-of-literature/). The last section groups the results into four bundles under one record of open literature statements: the non-endoscopic inputs, [Theorem 1.7.2](/entry/literature-nonexistence/), the 53 local theta inputs, [Theorem 1.7.3](/entry/published-results-first/), the 60 unitary group hypotheses, [Theorem 1.7.4](/entry/published-results-eigen/), and the 28 Hodge bundle inputs, [Theorem 1.7.5](/entry/published-results-arithmetic/).

Chapter 2 has three groups of results. The first bounds the plus part of the Tate-Shafarevich lengths of a CM field at one point from counts, [Theorem 2.1.10](/entry/point-hv-of-counts/). It moves this bound to a window of points and joins it with the minus bound, which gives VWin, [Theorem 2.2.5](/entry/vwin-of-counts/). The second group changes the family at a point so that it becomes block diagonal, [Theorem 2.3.6](/entry/normalized-ring-data/), and bounds the congruence length by Selmer, Tate-Shafarevich, class number and local terms, [Theorem 2.5.7](/entry/theorem-n5-from-inputs/). This is the closed form of [the Selmer bound at a point](/entry/selmer-bound-at-point-main/). The third group gives local facts on theta lifts and smooth vectors: unramified Howe duality at the inert places, [Theorem 2.6.4](/entry/howe-unram-holds/), the approximation of smooth vectors by $K$-finite vectors, [Theorem 2.7.3](/entry/sob-approx-holds/), and a generating $K$-type of the real theta quotient, [Theorem 2.8.5](/entry/side-of/).

Chapter 3 proves the zero-free half-plane. Its analytic part starts with a Chebyshev bound on a coset, [Theorem 3.1.5](/entry/ch-core/), and turns it into a window inequality on $[Y, Y^\lambda]$, [Theorem 3.2.4](/entry/window-inequality-of-artin-inputs/). A criterion of Landau type turns these bounds into zero-free half-planes, [Theorem 3.2.8](/entry/zero-free-all-of-top-g/). Its arithmetic part builds hosts on windows of scale $\tfrac32$ from VWin, [Theorem 3.3.7](/entry/supply-chi/). It proves the local bound $\mathrm{Link}_G$, [Theorem 3.4.12](/entry/link-g-concrete5/), and the remaining division bounds, [Theorem 3.5.7](/entry/b-rest-all-alt/), and joins them in the shape theorem, [Theorem 3.6.8](/entry/shape-closed-link-n-of-green/). The last section gives ZeroFreeAll, [Theorem 3.7.2](/entry/zero-free-all-of-green-v-win/), and EveryCharacter, [Theorem 3.7.3](/entry/every-character-of-green-v-win/). It also proves the standard Siegel-Weil formula, [Theorem 3.7.5](/entry/siegel-weil-std-of-literature/), and the theta inputs that follow from it, [Theorem 3.7.7](/entry/theta-integrals-agree-and-local-matching-of-literature/).

Chapter 4 proves that the parameter set of dependence satisfies twelve open parameter statements, [Theorem 4.1.4](/entry/open0-of-unitary-group-hypotheses/). Eleven of them come from results of other chapters. The twelfth, the multiplicity row, asks for a period identity at every good linked bundle: each period of the package equals, up to a nonzero scalar and a unitary change of coordinates, an inner product of a theta vector with a vector of the row side, [Theorem 4.2.7](/entry/global-theory-mi-star-row-alt-alt/). The chapter reduces this identity to family conditions and proves them, [Theorem 4.4.4](/entry/fam-all-q-of-all-true-alt/). Its tools are one central pin, [Theorem 4.5.7](/entry/exists-bot-dat-pin-of-placed-of-framed/), a rescaling by roots of unity, [Theorem 4.7.5](/entry/dat-exists/), the constancy of volumes, [Theorem 4.8.4](/entry/vol-level-wt-const-alt/), and the column side, [Theorem 4.9.4](/entry/col-side-alt/). It ends with the defect row, [Theorem 4.18.6](/entry/linked-at-prm-r-df-of-cusp-rapid-decay/), and the label row, [Theorem 4.19.7](/entry/row-lab-of-layer-alt-alt/).

Chapter 5 proves the surjectivity statement and the lower bound for $\lambda$ on the indefinite side of a point, [Theorem 5.1.9](/entry/onto-x-and-lam-low-of-unitary-group-hypotheses-prime/). It derives the geometric statement from the page statement, [Theorem 5.2.5](/entry/geo-st-of-page-st-alt/), compares the trace lattice with the theta base lattice away from the own prime, [Theorem 5.3.5](/entry/compare-off-own-at-runs-alt/), and proves the export statement, [Theorem 5.4.7](/entry/export-st-of-hypotheses-alt/). At the small twisted places it builds a Hecke frame, an automorphic representation of a definite unitary group and a crystalline Galois representation. These give the model statement, [Theorem 5.8.4](/entry/model-st-of-small-frame-gal/), and with a cut of Weyl lattices the exponent statement, [Theorem 5.9.8](/entry/exponent-bound-of-unitary-theta-standard-theorems-alt/). The surjectivity statement follows, [Theorem 5.10.7](/entry/onto-x-of-hypotheses-alt-alt/). The lower bound for $\lambda$ comes from the Siegel-Weil formula and the doubling method, [Theorem 5.11.10](/entry/lam-low-of-hypotheses-alt-alt/).

Chapter 6 proves the first step of branch 1. Under the literature hypotheses of the unitary groups, it gives the Siegel mass ratio for CM fields, the membership statement for the stronger parameters and the bundle statement, [Theorem 6.1.8](/entry/large-unramified-exponent-bundles-exist-alt/). The proof goes through the pair theorem, [Theorem 6.2.3](/entry/exists-base-lattice-of-pair-alt/), and the step from level three to level four, [Theorem 6.3.6](/entry/has4-of-has3-of-s-ext-none/). It proves that built cores export, [Theorem 6.4.6](/entry/export-f-is-built-e-alt-alt/), and the eta statement, [Theorem 6.8.8](/entry/s-eta-alt-alt-alt/). The later sections prove the bundle statement, [Theorem 6.10.5](/entry/ex-bl-is-linked-prm-r-alt/), finiteness and nonvanishing, [Theorem 6.13.6](/entry/s-fino-and-second-no-of-rat-cusp-finl/), and the unlocalized saturated state statement, [Theorem 6.16.4](/entry/weight-one-saturated-of-hypotheses/).

Chapter 7 collects inputs proved by rows of explicit data and frames of lattices. In finite group theory it proves that Harish-Chandra induction of cuspidal characters with distinct labels is irreducible for $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{F}_q)$, [Theorem 7.1.2](/entry/is-irr-rep-harish-chandra-distinct-cuspidal-labels/). It bounds the volumes of height shells in a frame quotient of an adelic unitary group, [Theorem 7.4.1](/entry/measure-height-shell-le-isotropic-frame-quotient/). For the surjectivity chapter it builds lattice links of crystalline blocks through Wach lattices, [Theorem 7.6.6](/entry/crystalline-lattice-link-of-block-data/), puts trace lattices in the span of theta series away from the own prime, [Theorem 7.10.6](/entry/trace-lattice-le-span-theta-series-away-from-own-prime/), and finds cusp forms with independent translates, [Theorem 7.10.7](/entry/exists-cusp-form-independent-translates/). For the unitary hypotheses it compares Satake parameters at the inert places, [Theorem 7.23.1](/entry/sat-agree/), proves generation at the finite places, [Theorem 7.28.2](/entry/gen-fin/), and proves irreducibility for the compact pair, [Theorem 7.31.1](/entry/howe-duality-compact-pair-real-places-irreducible/). It also shows that the type of data is nonempty, [Theorem 7.34.1](/entry/record-nonempty/).

Chapter 8 proves that the record of hypotheses has an element, [Theorem 8.1.6](/entry/hypotheses-nonempty-of-unramified-exponents-alt/). The record is read off a record of twenty six rows. Most rows come from the open parameter statements, [Theorem 8.2.6](/entry/all-rows-of-open0-alt/). The others come from a short list of published results: the convergent Siegel-Weil formula gives three bounds at every selection, [Theorem 8.3.3](/entry/at-choice-alt/), and global Artin reciprocity gives the service row, [Theorem 8.4.4](/entry/row-h-serv-of-link-alt/). The chapter also proves the closed link bound for the congruence valuation of a family, [Theorem 8.6.8](/entry/final-alt-alt/), from local Galois facts at the type primes, the inert places and the places over two and three. The record then follows from all rows, [Theorem 8.11.4](/entry/nonempty-hyps-of-all-rows-alt/). The last sections prove base change compatible at all places, [Theorem 8.16.10](/entry/base-change-local-compatible-all-places-alt/).

Chapter 1 comes first, because the later chapters take its statements as hypotheses. Theorem 1.7.4 gives the unitary group hypotheses that Theorems 4.1.4, 5.1.9 and 8.1.6 assume. The volume then has two lines. In the first line, Chapter 3 uses VWin of Chapter 2 to build its hosts. In the second line, Chapter 8 uses Theorem 6.1.8 in its first step, and Theorems 5.1.9 and 4.1.4 as branches 2 and 3. Chapter 4 also uses the unramified Howe duality of Theorem 2.6.4. Chapter 7 supplies inputs to Chapters 5 and 8, so a reader can consult it when a proof cites it. The two lines meet in Chapter 3: its last theorems assume the record HypsAlt, and Chapter 8 gives an element of it. So a reader can take Chapter 1, then Chapters 2 and 3, then Chapters 4, 5, 6 and 8, with Chapter 7 at hand.

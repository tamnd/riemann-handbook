This chapter proves two statements about the indefinite side of a point of a family: the surjectivity statement and the lower bound for $\lambda$. Section 1 defines families, the closed properties of families, the onto condition and the lower bound for $\lambda$, and states the main theorem. Sections 2 and 3 prove the geometric statement from the page statement, give two runs at each point, and compare the trace lattice with the theta base lattice away from the own prime. Section 4 proves the export statement. Sections 5 to 9 prove the exponent bound: at the small twisted places they build a Hecke frame, an automorphic representation of a definite [[unitary group|0R9R0Y1HDK]] and a crystalline Galois representation, and from these a model of the Hecke algebra; with a cut of Weyl lattices this gives the bound on the exponents. Section 10 joins the estimates into the surjectivity statement, and Section 11 proves the lower bound for $\lambda$ from the Siegel-Weil formula and the doubling method.

The chapter needs from Volume 1 the [unitary groups](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups), the [Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation), the [unramified representations](/learn/representations/#unramified-representations), the [discrete series](/learn/real-groups/#discrete-series-and-formal-degrees), the [Galois representation of an automorphic representation](/learn/galois-representations/#the-galois-representation-of-an-automorphic-representation), the [crystalline representations](/learn/galois-representations/#crystalline-de-rham-and-potentially-semistable-representations) and their [lattices](/learn/padic-hodge/#lattices-wach-breuil-kisin-and-prismatic-f-crystals), and the [length of a module](/learn/algebra/#rings-ideals-and-modules). The lower bound for $\lambda$ is the third item of the step [the hypotheses are not empty](/step/nonempty-hyps/) of [the chapter on the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/).

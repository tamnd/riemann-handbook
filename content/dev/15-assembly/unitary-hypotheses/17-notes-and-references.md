## Notes and references

This chapter feeds one node. [The main theorem](/entry/hypotheses-nonempty-of-unramified-exponents-alt/) of Section 1 gives an element of the record of hypotheses of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/), and [the record of hypotheses](/step/hyps-alt-def/) is the form in which the later chapters read it. The chapter does almost no new mathematics. It assembles rows from the open parameter statements and from a short list of classical results, and each classical result enters at one place. The notes below name these results section by section.

Section 1 fixes the language of rows. The row facts are formal. The two other branches and the bundles of large unramified exponents come from earlier chapters.

Sections 2 and 3 rest on the Siegel-Weil formula in the convergent range. Weil (1965) proved it for dual pairs in the range of absolute convergence, and Kudla and Rallis (1994) extended it with the regularized form. The [Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation) and the [global theta lift](/learn/theta/#the-global-theta-lift) are as in Howe (1979) and in Moeglin, Vignéras and Waldspurger (1987).

Section 4 uses global Artin reciprocity. See Neukirch, Chapter VI, Cassels and Fröhlich, Chapter VII, and Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, Chapter VIII.

Section 5 uses the classification of the discrete series of Harish-Chandra (1966) and the Blattner formula of Hecht and Schmid (1975) for the placement of the real realization. The structure of unitary groups of hermitian forms over a CM extension is in Scharlau, Chapter 10, and in Shimura (1997).

Sections 6 and 8 are a closing bound for a congruence valuation. The local inputs are local class field theory, as in Serre, *Local Fields*, Chapters IV and XIII, and the crystalline period bounds of Fontaine and Laffaille (1982) and of Bloch and Kato (1990). The congruence modules come from Ribet's method of congruences, in the form of Wiles (1995) and Taylor and Wiles (1995).

Section 7 ties the local characters at the type primes to de Rham data and to labels. The de Rham data are those of Fontaine; the local characters come from local class field theory and the local Langlands correspondence for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ of Harris and Taylor (2001), Henniart (2000) and Scholze (2013).

Section 9 uses the bounds for the lengths of Selmer groups at twisted places. These rest on local Tate duality and the local Euler characteristic formula of Tate. See Milne, Chapter I, Serre, *Galois Cohomology*, Chapter II, and Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, Chapter VII.

Section 10 uses a central element of a group algebra and Artin reciprocity. The finite group facts are as in Serre, *Linear Representations of Finite Groups*, Chapters 2 and 6.

Section 11 is formal. It reads the record of hypotheses off the record of all rows.

Section 12 uses [depth zero types](/learn/representations/#depth-zero-representations-and-types). The Moy-Prasad filtration and unrefined minimal types are in Moy and Prasad (1994, 1996). The characters of the finite general linear groups are in Green (1955). Types for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ are in Bushnell and Kutzko (1993), and the Bernstein decomposition is in Bernstein (1984) and in Bernstein and Zelevinsky (1977).

Section 13 uses [local Galois cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/#local-fields): finiteness, local Tate duality and the local Euler characteristic formula, with the Chebotarev density theorem of Neukirch, Chapter VII. The comparison of a Hecke character with its $\ell$-adic realization is in Serre, *Abelian $\ell$-adic Representations*, Chapter II.

Section 14 uses the doubling method of Gelbart, Piatetski-Shapiro and Rallis (1987) and the Rallis inner product formula of Rallis (1982) and Kudla and Rallis (1994) for the nonvanishing of theta lifts. The local theta correspondence is as in Howe (1979) and Kudla (1986).

Section 15 uses the local theta inputs, the classification of generic representations of Jacquet and Shalika (1981), the segments of Zelevinsky (1980) and the finiteness theorem of Harish-Chandra (1968). The Galois representations attached to conjugate self-dual automorphic representations are those of Shin (2011) and Chenevier and Harris (2013).

Section 16 uses the base change from unitary groups to $\mathrm{GL}_n$ and the [local Langlands correspondence](/learn/galois-representations/#the-local-langlands-correspondence) for unitary groups. These are in Mok (2015) for quasi-split groups and in Kaletha, Minguez, Shin and White (2014) for inner forms, after Arthur and Clozel (1989) and Labesse (2011). The unitary dual of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ is in Tadić (1986), and the Speh representations are in Moeglin and Waldspurger (1989). Residual irreducibility follows the method of Clozel, Harris and Taylor (2008), with the Brauer-Nesbitt theorem of Curtis and Reiner.

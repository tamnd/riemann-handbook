## Reciprocity on arithmetic varieties

This section removes the hypothesis that the bundles are generated from [the reciprocity law for generated bundles](/entry/reciprocity-sec/). Then it passes from a projective variety over a field to an arithmetic variety, one complex embedding at a time, and sums over the embeddings. The result is the archimedean reciprocity law. It is one of the two analytic inputs of [the construction of mixed heights](/entry/is-mix-succ/), and through it of [the arithmetic intersection calculus](/entry/ar-calc-of-principalisation/).

We keep the notation of the previous section: $S(\bar N, \bar{\mathbf L}; f, g; \Delta)$ is the reciprocity side, and the reciprocity defect of $(\bar M, s)$ and $(\bar N, t)$ is
$$\operatorname{Def}(\bar M, s; \bar N, t) = S\big(\bar N, \bar{\mathbf L}; \log\|s\|, \log\|t\|; \operatorname{div} s\big) - S\big(\bar M, \bar{\mathbf L}; \log\|t\|, \log\|s\|; \operatorname{div} t\big).$$

### Removing the generation hypothesis

We use two facts. The first is algebraic: on a noetherian scheme projective over an infinite field $F$, for every line bundle $M$ and every finite set $\Sigma$ of points there are a line bundle $A$ and a section $a$ of $A$ such that $A$ and $M \otimes A$ are generated by global sections and $a$ vanishes at no point of $\Sigma$. Any field with an embedding into $\mathbb{C}$ is infinite. A generated bundle carries a smooth semipositive weight, so we may view $A$ as a bundle $\bar A$ with a smooth weight. The second fact is the additivity of the defect: for sections $s$ of $M$ and $a$ of $A$, nonzero at the generic point, the product $s \otimes a$ is a section of $M \otimes A$ whose nonzero locus is the intersection of the two nonzero loci, and if both $s$ and $a$ have no common divisor with $t$, then
$$\operatorname{Def}(\bar M \otimes \bar A, s \otimes a; \bar N, t) = \operatorname{Def}(\bar M, s; \bar N, t) + \operatorname{Def}(\bar A, a; \bar N, t).$$
This holds because $\log \|s \otimes a\| = \log\|s\| + \log\|a\|$, $c_1(\bar M \otimes \bar A) = c_1(\bar M) + c_1(\bar A)$ and $\operatorname{div}(s \otimes a) = \operatorname{div} s + \operatorname{div} a$.

Let $F$, $\sigma$ and $X$ be as in [the Poincaré-Lelong formula](/entry/poincare-lelong-sec/), with $X$ of dimension $e + 1$.

**Lemma 6.3.1 (One generated bundle).** Assume principalisation of ideals. Let $\bar{\mathbf L}$ be any family of $e$ bundles, let $s$ and $t$ be sections of $M$ and $N$, nonzero at the generic point, with no common divisor, and assume that $N$ is generated by global sections. Then reciprocity holds for $(\bar M, s)$, $(\bar N, t)$ and $\bar{\mathbf L}$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Mix.reciprocity_of_generated_right`.

*Proof.* The set $\Sigma$ of points of codimension one where $t$ vanishes is finite. Add the generic point to $\Sigma$, and choose $\bar A$ and $a$ as above for $M$ and $\Sigma$. Then $a$ and $t$ have no common divisor, since $a$ does not vanish where $t$ vanishes. The same holds for $s \otimes a$ and $t$: at a point of codimension one where $t$ vanishes, $s$ does not vanish by hypothesis and $a$ does not vanish by the choice of $a$. The bundles $A$, $M \otimes A$ and $N$ are generated. So the defects of $(\bar A, a; \bar N, t)$ and of $(\bar M \otimes \bar A, s \otimes a; \bar N, t)$ vanish by [the reciprocity law for generated bundles](/entry/reciprocity-sec/). By additivity, $\operatorname{Def}(\bar M, s; \bar N, t) = 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.3.2 (Reciprocity without common divisor).** Assume principalisation of ideals. Let $\bar{\mathbf L}$ be any family of $e$ bundles with smooth weights at $\sigma$, and let $s$ and $t$ be sections of any bundles $M$ and $N$ with smooth weights, nonzero at the generic point, with no common divisor. Then reciprocity holds for $(\bar M, s)$, $(\bar N, t)$ and $\bar{\mathbf L}$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Mix.reciprocity_of_noCommonDivisor` (also `GlobalTheory.T.arithReciprocity_noCommonDivisor_projective`, a second name).

*Proof.* Exchange the roles of the two bundles in the proof of the lemma. Let $\Sigma$ consist of the generic point and the points of codimension one where $s$ vanishes. Choose $\bar A$ and a section $a$, nonzero on $\Sigma$, with $A$ and $N \otimes A$ generated. The defect is additive in the second pair as well, by the same three identities. So
$$\operatorname{Def}(\bar M, s; \bar N \otimes \bar A, t \otimes a) = \operatorname{Def}(\bar M, s; \bar N, t) + \operatorname{Def}(\bar M, s; \bar A, a).$$
Both terms other than $\operatorname{Def}(\bar M, s; \bar N, t)$ vanish by [the lemma](/entry/reciprocity-of-generated-right/), since $A$ and $N \otimes A$ are generated and the sections have no common divisor with $s$. $\square$

### Arithmetic varieties

**Definition 6.3.3 (Arithmetic variety).** Let $k$ be a domain that is finite as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module, such as the ring of integers of a number field. An arithmetic variety of relative dimension $D$ over $k$ is a scheme $X$ with a morphism $p : X \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ such that $X$ is integral, projective over $k$ and flat over $\mathbb{Z}$, of dimension $D + 1$.

See the [arithmetic varieties and intersection numbers](/learn/arakelov/#arithmetic-varieties-and-intersection-numbers) of Volume 1. For an embedding $\sigma : k \to \mathbb{C}$, the complex points $X_\sigma(\mathbb{C})$ form a projective variety of dimension $D$. There are finitely many embeddings.

**Theorem 6.3.4 (Reciprocity at one embedding).** Assume principalisation of ideals. Let $X$ be an arithmetic variety of relative dimension $e + 1$ over $k$, and let $\sigma : k \to \mathbb{C}$ be an embedding. Let $\bar{\mathbf L} = (\bar L_1, \dots, \bar L_e)$, $\bar M$ and $\bar N$ be line bundles on $X$ with smooth weights at $\sigma$. Let $s$ and $t$ be sections of $M$ and $N$, nonzero at the generic point of $X$, such that every point of codimension one of $X$ lies outside the zero locus of $s$ or of $t$. Then reciprocity holds on $X_\sigma(\mathbb{C})$:
$$S\big(\bar N, \bar{\mathbf L}; \log\|s\|, \log\|t\|; \operatorname{div} s\big) = S\big(\bar M, \bar{\mathbf L}; \log\|t\|, \log\|s\|; \operatorname{div} t\big).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.MixRec.side_symm` (also `GlobalTheory.T.arith_variety_reciprocity_at_complex_embedding`, a second name).

*Proof.* Let $K$ be the field of fractions of $k$. The kernel of $\sigma$ is a prime of $k$ that meets $\mathbb{Z}$ in $0$; since $k$ is finite over $\mathbb{Z}$, it is $0$. So $\sigma$ is injective and extends to an embedding $\tau : K \to \mathbb{C}$. Let $G = X \times_k K$ be the generic fibre, with $j : G \to X$. Then $G$ is integral and projective over $K$ of dimension $e + 1$; $j$ maps the generic point to the generic point, induces isomorphisms of local rings, and maps points of codimension one to points of codimension one. The complex points of $X$ over $\sigma$ are the complex points of $G$ over $\tau$, and the bundles with their weights pull back to $G$.

The sections $s$ and $t$ pull back to sections $s'$ and $t'$ on $G$ with the same nonzero loci and the same norms. By the last property of $j$, $s'$ and $t'$ have no common divisor on $G$. So reciprocity holds on $G$ by [the theorem without common divisor](/entry/reciprocity-of-no-common-divisor/). Each side computed on $X$ equals the side computed on $G$; this is a comparison result of the development for the generic fibre. Hence reciprocity holds on $X$. $\square$

### The archimedean reciprocity law

**Definition 6.3.5 (Hermitian line bundle).** A hermitian line bundle $\bar L$ on an arithmetic variety $X$ over $k$ is a line bundle $L$ with a smooth weight $\varphi_\sigma$ at every embedding $\sigma : k \to \mathbb{C}$, compatible with complex conjugation: $\varphi_{\bar\sigma}(\bar y) = \varphi_\sigma(y)$.

For hermitian line bundles we sum over the embeddings. For sections $s$ of $M$ and $t$ of $N$ as in the theorem at one embedding, put
$$S_\infty(\bar N, \bar{\mathbf L}; s, t) = \sum_{\sigma : k \to \mathbb{C}} S_\sigma\big(\bar N, \bar{\mathbf L}; \log\|s\|_\sigma, \log\|t\|_\sigma; \operatorname{div} s\big).$$

**Theorem 6.3.6 (Archimedean reciprocity).** Assume principalisation of ideals. Let $X$ be an arithmetic variety of relative dimension $e + 1$, locally noetherian, and let $\bar M$, $\bar N$ and $\bar L_1, \dots, \bar L_e$ be hermitian line bundles on $X$. Let $s$ and $t$ be sections of $M$ and $N$, nonzero at the generic point, with no common divisor. Then
$$\begin{aligned} &\textstyle\sum_\sigma \int \log\|s\|_\sigma \, c_1(\bar N) \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}) + \sum_z \operatorname{mult}_z(\operatorname{div} s) \sum_\sigma \int_{\overline{\{z\}}} \log \|t\|_\sigma \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}) \\ &\quad = \textstyle\sum_\sigma \int \log\|t\|_\sigma \, c_1(\bar M) \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}) + \sum_z \operatorname{mult}_z(\operatorname{div} t) \sum_\sigma \int_{\overline{\{z\}}} \log \|s\|_\sigma \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}). \end{aligned}$$
That is, $S_\infty(\bar N, \bar{\mathbf L}; s, t) = S_\infty(\bar M, \bar{\mathbf L}; t, s)$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.MixHt.archRecip_of_principalisation` (also `GlobalTheory.T.archLogNormReciprocity_of_principalisation`, a second name).

*Proof.* The set of embeddings is finite, since $k$ is finite over $\mathbb{Z}$. The archimedean term of the left side is the polarised integral of $\log\|s\|$ against $c_1(\bar N) c_1(\bar{\mathbf L})$, and it is the sum over $\sigma$ of the mixed integrals. The term at a point $z$ of $\operatorname{div}(s)$ is the same polarised integral on the closure of $z$, of the section $t$ restricted there; since $t$ does not vanish identically on the closure of $z$, it is the sum over $\sigma$ of the integrals of $\log\|t\|_\sigma$. Both facts are results of the development. So each side is a sum over $\sigma$ of reciprocity sides. Apply [reciprocity at one embedding](/entry/side-symm/) to each $\sigma$. $\square$

**Remark 6.3.7 (Formalization).** The archimedean law is stated through the polarisation of the map $n \mapsto \sum_\sigma \int \log\|s\|_\sigma \, c_1(\bar L_n)^{e+1}$ divided by $(e+1)!$, and the divisor term through a restricted version of the same polarisation, set to $0$ when the section vanishes on the closure. The polarisation operator is not unfolded here; the two identifications with sums of mixed integrals are cited results. The variety is assumed locally noetherian, which follows from the other hypotheses.

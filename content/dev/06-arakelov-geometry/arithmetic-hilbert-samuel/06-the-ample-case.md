## The ample case

This section proves the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel formula for an ample line bundle with a semipositive metric, after a large shift of the metric. The proof compares the given metric with a Fubini-Study metric built from sections without common zero. For the Fubini-Study metric the count of small sections is close to an explicit value, and a change of metric changes the logarithm of the count by the change of the self intersection. The result is the input of [the nef case](/entry/arith-hilbert-samuel-of-ample-hs/).

### Pairs and balls

Let $X$ be an arithmetic variety of relative dimension $D$ over $k$, with function field $K$. See the [arithmetic volume and bigness](/learn/arakelov/#arithmetic-volume-and-bigness) of Volume 1 for the counts of small sections.

**Definition 6.6.1 (Pair).** A pair $P$ on $X$ consists of two hermitian line bundles $\bar L$ and $\bar A$ with rational sections $r$ and $r'$. For integers $a$, $b$ let
$$H(a, b) = \{ f \in K : f \, r^a r'^b \text{ is regular on } X \},$$
which is the group of global sections of $L^a \otimes A^b$ written as functions. For $f \in H(a, b)$, an embedding $\sigma$ and a point $y \in X_\sigma(\mathbb{C})$, let $\|f\|_{a,b}(y)$ be the norm of $f r^a r'^b$ in $\bar L^a \otimes \bar A^b$. For $R > 0$ and a subgroup $\Lambda \subseteq H(a, b)$ the ball is
$$\Lambda_{\le R} = \{ f \in \Lambda : \|f\|_{a,b}(y) \le R \text{ for all } \sigma \text{ and } y\},$$
and we write $\operatorname{lc}(a, b, R) = \log \# H(a, b)_{\le R}$.

So $H(m, 1)_{\le e^{tm}}$ is the set of small sections of $\bar L(t)^m \otimes \bar A$, where $\bar L(t)$ is $\bar L$ with its norm multiplied by $e^{-t}$. By the shift rule of [the calculus](/entry/ar-calc-of-principalisation/), if $a$ is a self intersection of $\bar L$, then $a + (D + 1) t \deg L_{\mathbb{C}}$ is a self intersection of $\bar L(t)$, where $\deg L_{\mathbb{C}} = \sum_\sigma \int c_1(\bar L)^D$.

**Definition 6.6.2 (Ample Hilbert-Samuel).** The ample Hilbert-Samuel formula is the statement: for every arithmetic variety $X$ of relative dimension $D$, every pair $P$ on $X$ whose bundle $L$ is ample with $\bar L$ semipositive, and every self intersection $a$ of $\bar L$, there is $t_0$ such that for all $t \ge t_0$
$$\lim_{m \to \infty} \frac{\operatorname{lc}(m, 1, e^{tm})}{m^{D+1}} = \frac{a + (D + 1) \, t \deg L_{\mathbb{C}}}{(D+1)!}.$$

We change metrics by smooth functions. A smooth function $u$ is a family of smooth real functions $u_\sigma$ on $X_\sigma(\mathbb{C})$ with $u_{\bar\sigma}(\bar y) = u_\sigma(y)$. We write $\bar L(u)$ for $\bar L$ with the weight $\varphi_\sigma + u_\sigma$, and $P(u)$ for the pair with $\bar L$ replaced by $\bar L(u)$.

**Definition 6.6.3 (Good family).** Let $g \in \mathbb{R}$. A good family of degree $g$ for $P$ is a family of subgroups $\Lambda_m \subseteq H(m, 1)$ such that each $\Lambda_m$ is finitely generated; there is $C \ge 1$ such that for $m \ge 1$ the group $\Lambda_m$ is generated by its ball of radius $C^m$; and $\operatorname{rank} \Lambda_m / m^D \to g$.

For an ample $L$ the full groups $\Lambda_m = H(m, 1)$ form a good family of degree $\deg L_{\mathbb{C}} / D!$; this is a result of the development that uses the calculus. The rank condition is the geometric Hilbert-Samuel formula on the generic fibre.

### Change of metric

We say that $X$ is generically smooth if each $X_\sigma(\mathbb{C})$ is covered by algebraic charts of dimension $D$.

**Theorem 6.6.4 (Change of metric).** Assume the arithmetic intersection calculus. Let $X$ be generically smooth. Let $P$ be a pair with $\bar L$ semipositive and with self intersection $a$, and let $(\Lambda_m)$ be a good family of degree $\deg L_{\mathbb{C}}/D!$. Then there is $t_0$ with the following property. Let $B \ge 0$ and let $u$ be a smooth function with $|u| \le B$ such that $\bar L(u)$ is semipositive with self intersection $a'$. Then for every $t \ge t_0 + B$
$$\lim_{m \to \infty} \frac{\log \# \Lambda_{m, \le e^{tm}}^{P(u)} - \log \# \Lambda_{m, \le e^{tm}}^{P}}{m^{D+1}} = \frac{a' - a}{(D+1)!},$$
where the upper index says which pair measures the norm.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.HSAmp.tendsto_logCount_sub_up` (also `GlobalTheory.T.tendsto_logSectionCount_change_of_metric`, a second name).

*Proof.* Let $C$ be the constant of the good family and put $t_0 = \log C + 1$. Since $|u| \le B$, the norms for $P(u)$ and for $P$ differ by a factor at most $e^{Bm/2}$ on $\Lambda_m$, so $\Lambda_m$ is generated by its $P(u)$-ball of radius $(C e^{B})^m$. For $t \ge t_0 + B$ both balls of radius $e^{tm}$ contain a set of generators with room to spare.

Choose a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\Lambda_m$ (it is a finitely generated subgroup of a field of characteristic zero, so it is free) and a smooth positive volume density on the complex points, which exists since $X$ is generically smooth. A result of the development expresses the logarithm of the number of points of a sup-norm ball of radius $e^{tm}$, up to $o(m^{D+1})$, through the logarithm of the determinant of the $L^2$ Gram matrix of the basis; we apply it to $P$ with the constant $C$ and to $P(u)$ with the constant $C e^B$. A second result of the development computes the change of the logarithm of the Gram determinant under the change of a semipositive metric: divided by $m^{D+1}$ it tends to a limit given by $a' - a$, which is where the calculus enters. Subtracting the two expansions gives the limit $(a' - a)/(D+1)!$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.6.5 (Change of metric from principalisation).** Assume principalisation of ideals. Let $X$ be an arithmetic variety of relative dimension $D$, and let $P$ be a pair with $\bar L$ semipositive with self intersection $a$, such that $(H(m, 1))_m$ is a good family of degree $\deg L_{\mathbb{C}}/D!$. Then the conclusion of the change of metric holds for $\Lambda_m = H(m, 1)$, without the hypothesis that $X$ is generically smooth.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.HSAmp.ChangeSing.count_change_of_principalisation` (also `GlobalTheory.T.arithHilbertSamuel_smallSections_change_of_metric`, a second name).

*Proof.* By [the calculus from principalisation](/entry/ar-calc-of-principalisation/), the calculus holds. By principalisation there is an arithmetic variety $Y$ of the same relative dimension, generically smooth, with a morphism $\pi : Y \to X$ that maps the generic point to the generic point with residue degree $1$. So $\pi$ identifies the function fields. The map $\pi$ is surjective on complex points.

Pull back the pair $P$ to a pair $\pi^* P$ on $Y$. Semipositivity pulls back. By the projection rule with $\delta = 1$, self intersections are preserved, and the geometric degree $\deg L_{\mathbb{C}}$ is preserved. Let $\Lambda'_m \subseteq H_Y(m, 1)$ be the image of $H_X(m, 1)$ under the identification of function fields. The norms agree at corresponding complex points, and every complex point of $X$ is the image of one of $Y$, so $f \mapsto \pi^* f$ is a bijection from each ball on $X$ to the corresponding ball on $Y$, for the pair and for the pair raised by $u$, and from $u$ to $u \circ \pi$. Hence $(\Lambda'_m)$ is a good family of the same degree for $\pi^* P$, and all counts on $Y$ equal those on $X$. Apply [the change of metric](/entry/tendsto-log-count-sub-up/) on $Y$ to $\Lambda'$. $\square$

### Sections without common zero

**Proposition 6.6.6 (Sections without common zero).** Let $X$ be an arithmetic variety of relative dimension $D$ and let $P$ be a pair with $L$ ample. Then there are $e > 0$ and $D + 1$ elements $x_0, \dots, x_D$ of $H(e, 0)$, that is, sections of $L^e$, with no common zero on $X$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.HSAmp.NoethGens.exists_gens` (also `GlobalTheory.T.exists_basepointFreeSectionTuple_ample_arith`, a second name).

*Proof.* We prove by induction on $d$: every closed subset $Z$ of $X$ whose intersection with each fibre of $X \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ has dimension less than $d$ is covered by the nonzero loci of $d$ sections of a common power $L^e$. For $d = 0$, $Z$ is empty. For the step, a result of the development gives a section $f$ of some $L^{e'}$ that vanishes identically on no component of the fibres of $Z$ of dimension $d - 1$; this uses that $L$ is ample. The zero locus $Z \cap V(f)$ has fibres of dimension less than $d - 1$, so it is covered by $d - 1$ sections of a power $L^{e''}$, by induction. Raising $f$ and these sections to suitable powers puts them all in one power $L^{e}$ without changing their zero loci, and the $d$ sections cover $Z$. Every fibre of $X$ has dimension at most $D$, so the case $Z = X$, $d = D + 1$ gives the proposition. $\square$

### The ample formula

**Theorem 6.6.7 (Ample Hilbert-Samuel formula).** Principalisation of ideals implies the ample Hilbert-Samuel formula.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.HSNef.ampleHS_of_principalisation` (also `GlobalTheory.T.ampleArithHilbertSamuel_of_principalisation`, a second name).

*Proof.* Let $X$, $P$ and $a$ be as in the formula and put $g = \deg L_{\mathbb{C}}$. The calculus holds by [principalisation](/entry/ar-calc-of-principalisation/). Choose $x_0, \dots, x_D \in H(e, 0)$ without common zero by [the proposition](/entry/exists-gens/). For $n \ge 1$ let
$$u_n = \frac{1}{e n} \log \sum_{l} \|x_l\|^{2n}$$
be the Fubini-Study function of the sections. A result of the development gives $B \ge 0$ with $|u_n| \le B$ for all $n$, shows that $\bar L(u_n)$ is semipositive, and shows that the $x_l$ have norm at most $1$ for $\bar L(u_n)^e$, with the largest of them at least $e^{-T_n e}$, where $T_n = \log(D+1)/(2ne)$. The groups $H(m, 1)$ form a good family of degree $g/D!$. Let $t_0$ be the threshold of [the change of metric from principalisation](/entry/count-change-of-principalisation/), and take the threshold $\max(t_0 + B, 1)$.

Fix $t \ge \max(t_0 + B, 1)$, $n \ge 1$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. Let $a_n$ be a self intersection of $\bar L(u_n)$. Results of the development give a constant $C$, independent of $n$, with $|a_n| \le |C|/n$, and show that for large $m$ the count for $P(u_n)$ satisfies
$$\left| \frac{\operatorname{lc}^{P(u_n)}(m, 1, e^{tm})}{m^{D+1}} - \frac{g\,t}{D!} \right| \le \frac{|g|}{D!}\, T_n + \varepsilon,$$
where $t > 0$ is used. By the change of metric, for large $m$ the difference of the counts for $P(u_n)$ and for $P$, divided by $m^{D+1}$, is within $\varepsilon$ of $(a_n - a)/(D+1)!$. Together, for large $m$,
$$\left| \frac{\operatorname{lc}(m, 1, e^{tm})}{m^{D+1}} - \frac{g\,t}{D!} - \frac{a}{(D+1)!} \right| \le \frac{|g|}{D!} \, \frac{\log(D+1)}{2 n e} + \frac{|C|}{n \, (D+1)!} + 2\varepsilon.$$
Given $\delta > 0$, choose $n$ so large and $\varepsilon$ so small that the right side is less than $\delta$. Since $g t/D! + a/(D+1)! = (a + (D+1) t g)/(D+1)!$, the limit is as claimed. $\square$

**Remark 6.6.8 (Formalization).** The threshold $\max(t_0 + B, 1)$ has the bound $1$ only to ensure $t > 0$. The threshold $t_0 = \log C + 1$ of the change of metric does not depend on $u$ or on $B$. The good family for $H(m,1)$, the uniform bound $B$, the bound on $a_n$ and the count for the Fubini-Study metric are cited results whose proofs we did not open. The count $\operatorname{lc}$ uses the cardinality of a set, which is $0$ for an infinite set, so its logarithm is then $0$.

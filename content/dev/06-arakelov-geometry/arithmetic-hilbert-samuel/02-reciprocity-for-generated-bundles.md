## Reciprocity for generated bundles

The arithmetic intersection number of $\bar M$ and $\bar N$ against other bundles is computed from a section $s$ of $M$ or from a section $t$ of $N$. The two answers agree because of a symmetry of archimedean integrals, the reciprocity law. This section proves the law at one complex place when $M$ and $N$ are generated by global sections: first when the other bundles are semipositive, and then for all bundles. The general case follows in [the next section](/entry/reciprocity-of-no-common-divisor/).

### The reciprocity law

We keep the setting of [the Poincaré-Lelong formula](/entry/poincare-lelong-sec/): $F$ is a field, $\sigma : F \to \mathbb{C}$ is an embedding, and $X$ is integral, locally noetherian and projective over $F$ of dimension $e + 1$. All bundles are line bundles with smooth weights at $\sigma$, and all integrals are mixed integrals over $X_\sigma(\mathbb{C})$ or over closures of points.

**Definition 6.2.1 (Reciprocity side).** Let $\bar N$ be a bundle, let $\bar{\mathbf L} = (\bar L_1, \dots, \bar L_e)$ be a family of bundles, let $f$ and $g$ be functions on $X_\sigma(\mathbb{C})$ and let $\Delta$ be a Cartier divisor on $X$. Put
$$S(\bar N, \bar{\mathbf L}; f, g; \Delta) = \int f \, c_1(\bar N) \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}) + \sum_z \operatorname{mult}_z(\Delta) \int_{\overline{\{z\}}} g \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}),$$
where $c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}) = c_1(\bar L_1) \cdots c_1(\bar L_e)$ and the sum runs over the points of codimension one.

Let $s$ be a section of $M$ and $t$ a section of $N$, both nonzero at the generic point. We say that $s$ and $t$ have no common divisor if every point of codimension one lies outside the zero locus of $s$ or outside the zero locus of $t$.

**Definition 6.2.2 (Reciprocity law).** We say that reciprocity holds for $(\bar M, s)$, $(\bar N, t)$ and $\bar{\mathbf L}$ if
$$S\big(\bar N, \bar{\mathbf L}; \log\|s\|, \log\|t\|; \operatorname{div}(s)\big) = S\big(\bar M, \bar{\mathbf L}; \log\|t\|, \log\|s\|; \operatorname{div}(t)\big).$$
The difference of the two sides is the reciprocity defect.

Written out, the law says
$$\begin{aligned} &\int \log\|s\| \, c_1(\bar N) \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}) + \int_{\operatorname{div}(s)} \log \|t\| \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}) \\ &\quad = \int \log\|t\| \, c_1(\bar M) \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}) + \int_{\operatorname{div}(t)} \log \|s\| \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L}). \end{aligned}$$
This is the symmetry of the star product of the Green currents $-\log\|s\|^2$ and $-\log\|t\|^2$ in the theory of Gillet and Soulé, integrated against $c_1(\bar{\mathbf L})$. If $X$ is a curve ($e = 0$), it is an analytic form of Weil reciprocity: $\sum_{\operatorname{div}(s)} \log \|t\| - \sum_{\operatorname{div}(t)} \log\|s\| = \int \log\|t\| c_1(\bar M) - \int \log \|s\| c_1(\bar N)$.

A bundle is generated by global sections if it has finitely many global sections with no common zero. A weight is semipositive if its Levi form $\big(\partial^2 \varphi / \partial z_j \partial \bar z_l\big)$ is positive semidefinite in every algebraic chart of dimension $e + 1$. Semipositive weights are the [[plurisubharmonic|FF2P7ER3QT]] weights among the smooth ones.

### Semipositive bundles

We use two results of the analytic part of the development.

- Change of the first metric. The reciprocity defect does not change if the weight of $\bar M$ is replaced by $\varphi + u$ for a smooth function $u$. Its proof applies [the Poincaré-Lelong formula](/entry/poincare-lelong-sec/) to $s$ and to $t$ with the function $u$.
- Inequality. If $\bar N$ and all $\bar L_i$ are semipositive, then the left side of the law is at most the right side. Its proof approximates $\log\|t\|^2$ by the smooth functions $\log(\|t\|^2 + \delta_n)$, which are potentials of semipositive weights on $N$, applies the Poincaré-Lelong formula to each of them, uses the symmetry of $\int v \, dd^c u \, c_1(\bar{\mathbf L})$ in $u$ and $v$, and passes to the limit.

**Theorem 6.2.3 (Reciprocity for generated bundles, semipositive case).** Assume principalisation of ideals. Let $\bar L_1, \dots, \bar L_e$ be semipositive. Let $M$ and $N$ be generated by global sections, and let $s$, $t$ be sections of $M$, $N$, nonzero at the generic point, with no common divisor. Then reciprocity holds for $(\bar M, s)$, $(\bar N, t)$ and $\bar{\mathbf L}$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.Mix.side_eq_of_generated` (also `GlobalTheory.T.generatedBundles_reciprocity_semiPositive`, a second name).

*Proof.* Since $M$ is generated by sections $u_1, \dots, u_n$, it carries the Fubini-Study weight $\log \sum_i |u_i|^2$, which is smooth and semipositive. Two smooth weights on the same bundle differ by a smooth function. So there are smooth functions $u$ and $v$ such that $\bar M(u)$ and $\bar N(v)$ are semipositive. By the change of the first metric, the defect of $(\bar M, s), (\bar N, t)$ equals the defect of $(\bar M(u), s), (\bar N, t)$. Apply the same rule to the second bundle, with the roles exchanged: this defect equals the defect of $(\bar M(u), s), (\bar N(v), t)$. The divisors do not change, since they depend only on the sections. Now the inequality applied to $(\bar M(u), s), (\bar N(v), t)$ shows that the defect is at most $0$. The inequality applied with the two pairs exchanged shows that it is at least $0$. So the defect is $0$. $\square$

### All bundles

**Theorem 6.2.4 (Reciprocity for generated bundles).** Assume principalisation of ideals. Let $\bar L_1, \dots, \bar L_e$ be any bundles with smooth weights. Let $M$ and $N$ be generated by global sections, and let $s$, $t$ be sections of $M$, $N$, nonzero at the generic point, with no common divisor. Then reciprocity holds for $(\bar M, s)$, $(\bar N, t)$ and $\bar{\mathbf L}$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.RecSec.reciprocity_sec` (also `GlobalTheory.T.reciprocity_sections_generatedBundles`, a second name).

*Proof.* Let $\operatorname{Def}(\bar{\mathbf L})$ be the reciprocity defect as a function of the family $\bar{\mathbf L}$, with $(\bar M, s)$ and $(\bar N, t)$ fixed.

First, $\operatorname{Def}$ is additive in each $\bar L_i$: if $\bar L_i = \bar A \otimes \bar C$, then $\operatorname{Def}$ is the sum of the defects with $\bar A$ and with $\bar C$ in place $i$. Indeed $c_1(\bar A \otimes \bar C) = c_1(\bar A) + c_1(\bar C)$, so each mixed integral on $X$ and on the closures of the points of $\operatorname{div}(s)$ and $\operatorname{div}(t)$ is additive. The integrals exist: $\log\|s\|$ is integrable for every mixed measure on $X$, and on the closure of a point of $\operatorname{div}(t)$ the section $s$ does not vanish identically, because $s$ and $t$ have no common divisor.

Second, every bundle $\bar A$ on a projective variety has a semipositive $\bar Q$ such that $\bar A \otimes \bar Q$ is semipositive. We take this from the analytic part of the development.

We show by induction on the size of a set $S$ of indices that $\operatorname{Def}(\bar{\mathbf L}) = 0$ whenever $\bar L_i$ is semipositive for all $i \notin S$. If $S$ is empty, this is the semipositive case. For the step, let $a \notin S$ and let $\bar L_i$ be semipositive for $i \notin S \cup \{a\}$. Choose $\bar Q$ for $\bar A = \bar L_a$. Additivity in place $a$ gives
$$\operatorname{Def}(\bar{\mathbf L} \text{ with } \bar L_a \otimes \bar Q) = \operatorname{Def}(\bar{\mathbf L}) + \operatorname{Def}(\bar{\mathbf L} \text{ with } \bar Q).$$
The two families with $\bar L_a \otimes \bar Q$ and with $\bar Q$ in place $a$ are semipositive outside $S$, so their defects vanish by induction. Hence $\operatorname{Def}(\bar{\mathbf L}) = 0$. With $S$ the set of all indices we get the theorem. $\square$

**Remark 6.2.5 (Formalization).** The inequality for semipositive bundles is the only place where positivity is used, and it gives one inequality; equality comes from applying it twice, with the two pairs exchanged. Semipositivity is asked for in charts of dimension $e + 1$, the dimension of $X$. The tensor product used in the additivity step is the tensor product of bundles with weights, whose weight is the sum of the weights.

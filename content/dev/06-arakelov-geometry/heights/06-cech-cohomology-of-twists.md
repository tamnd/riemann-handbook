## Čech cohomology of twists by a generated line bundle

This section proves a splitting result for Čech cohomology. Let $\mathcal{G}$ be the sheaf of a finitely generated graded module on a projective variety and let $\mathcal{B}$ be a line bundle generated by sections $\eta_0, \dots, \eta_r$. We build one finitely generated graded module $\mathcal{M}$ over the polynomial ring $S[y_0, \dots, y_r][X_0, \dots, X_N]$ such that, for all $b \ge 0$ and all $c$, the Čech cohomology of $\mathcal{G} \otimes \mathcal{B}^{b}(c)$ is a direct summand of the degree $c$ Čech cohomology of $\mathcal{M}$. The module $\mathcal{M}$ does not depend on $b$. So a finiteness or vanishing theorem for the single module $\mathcal{M}$, in the style of Serre, gives a statement for all the twists $\mathcal{G} \otimes \mathcal{B}^b$ at once. The result belongs to the theory of ample line bundles that feeds [the step on the published results](/step/every-character-of-literature/).

The section is algebraic. All objects are concrete submodules of a field, and all cohomology is Čech cohomology of an explicit cover.

### Charts of a point of projective space

Let $S$ be a Noetherian ring, $\Omega$ a field with an $S$-algebra structure, and $x = (x_0, \dots, x_N) \in \Omega^{N+1}$. The support of $x$ is the set of $i$ with $x_i \ne 0$. A chart is a nonempty subset $I$ of the support. Think of $x$ as the generic point of a projective variety $V$ over $S$; the charts index the open sets $V \cap \{X_i \ne 0 \text{ for } i \in I\}$.

**Definition 4.6.1 (Chart ring, twisting module).** Let $I$ be a chart. The chart ring $A_I \subseteq \Omega$ is the set of quotients $f(x)/s(x)$, where $s$ is a monomial in the $X_i$ with $i \in I$, and $f \in S[X_0, \dots, X_N]$ is homogeneous of the same degree as $s$. For $c \in \mathbb{Z}$, the twisting module $\mathcal{O}(c)_I \subseteq \Omega$ is the $A_I$-span of the quotients $f(x)/s(x)$ with $s$ as before and $\deg f = \deg s + c$.

The chart ring is the ring of regular functions on the chart, written inside the function field. It grows with $I$. For $N = 1$ and $x = (1, t)$ with $t$ transcendental over a field $S$, we get $A_{\{0\}} = S[t]$, $A_{\{1\}} = S[t^{-1}]$ and $A_{\{0,1\}} = S[t, t^{-1}]$: the cover of the projective line by two affine lines.

**Definition 4.6.2 (System, sections, twist, Čech cohomology).** Let $E$ be an $\Omega$-vector space. A system $F$ in $E$ is a family of $A_I$-submodules $F_I \subseteq E$, one for each chart, with $F_I \subseteq F_J$ when $I \subseteq J$. Its sections are $\Gamma(F) = \bigcap_I F_I$. For a system $F$ in $E$ and a system $Q$ in $\Omega$, the twist $F \otimes Q$ is the system whose module on $I$ is the $A_I$-span of the products $q y$ with $q \in Q_I$ and $y \in F_I$. The powers are $Q^{0} = \mathcal{O}(0)$ and $Q^{b+1} = Q^{b} \otimes Q$. The Čech complex of $F$ has in degree $i$ the families $(z_K)$, indexed by the charts $K$ with $i + 1$ elements, with $z_K \in F_K$, and the usual alternating differential; $H^i(F)$ is its cohomology.

The twisting modules form a system $\mathcal{O}(c)$. The twist is the chartwise tensor product, computed inside $E$.

**Definition 4.6.3 (Finitely generated graded submodule, associated system).** A finitely generated graded submodule $M_0$ of $\Omega$ is a family of $S$-submodules $M_{0,a} \subseteq \Omega$, $a \in \mathbb{Z}$, with $f(x) M_{0,a} \subseteq M_{0,a+d}$ for $f$ homogeneous of degree $d$, and with finitely many elements $m_j \in M_{0,d_j}$ such that each $M_{0,a}$ lies in the $S$-span of the products $f(x) m_j$ with $f$ homogeneous of degree $a - d_j$. Its associated system $\widetilde{M_0}(c)$ has on the chart $I$ the $A_I$-span of the quotients $m/s(x)$, where $m \in M_{0,a}$, $s$ is a monomial in the $X_i$ with $i \in I$, and $a - \deg s = c$.

This is the sheaf associated to a graded module, in the form of Hartshorne II.5, written chart by chart.

### Graded modules over the second polynomial ring

Put $T = S[y_0, \dots, y_r]$ and $P = T[X_0, \dots, X_N]$, graded by the degree in the $X_i$. For a graded $P$-module $\mathcal{M} = \bigoplus_{c} \mathcal{M}_c$ and a nonempty $I \subseteq \{0, \dots, N\}$, let $\mathcal{M}_{(I)}$ be the localization at the monomials in the $X_i$, $i \in I$, and let $\mathcal{M}_{(I), c}$ be its part of degree $c$: the span of the fractions $m/s$ with $m \in \mathcal{M}_a$ and $a - \deg s = c$. The Čech cohomology $H^i(\mathcal{M})_c$ is the cohomology of the alternating Čech complex of the modules $\mathcal{M}_{(K), c}$ over the subsets $K$ of $\{0, \dots, N\}$ with $i + 1$ elements. It is the cohomology of the sheaf $\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(c)$ on $\mathbb{P}^N_T$ for the standard cover.

### The theorem

**Theorem 4.6.4 (Twist splitting).** Let $S$ be a Noetherian ring, $\Omega$ a field that is an $S$-algebra, $x \in \Omega^{N+1}$, and $M_0$ a finitely generated graded submodule of $\Omega$. Let $\mathcal{G}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ be systems in $\Omega$, and let $\eta_0, \dots, \eta_r \in \Omega$. Assume:

1. $\mathcal{G}_I = \widetilde{M_0}(0)_I$ for every chart $I$;
2. each $\eta_j$ is a section of $\mathcal{B}$;
3. $\mathcal{B}_I$ is the $A_I$-span of $\eta_0, \dots, \eta_r$ for every chart $I$.

Then there is a graded $P$-module $\mathcal{M}$, finitely generated over $P$, such that for all $b \in \mathbb{N}$, $c \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $i \in \mathbb{N}$, there are homomorphisms of abelian groups
$$\iota : H^i\bigl(\mathcal{G} \otimes \mathcal{B}^{b} \otimes \mathcal{O}(c)\bigr) \to H^i(\mathcal{M})_c, \qquad \pi : H^i(\mathcal{M})_c \to H^i\bigl(\mathcal{G} \otimes \mathcal{B}^{b} \otimes \mathcal{O}(c)\bigr)$$
with $\pi \circ \iota = \mathrm{id}$.

*Proof.* The module. Let $L = \Omega[u^{\pm 1}, v^{\pm 1}]$ be the Laurent polynomial ring in two variables. Let $\mathcal{M} \subseteq L$ be the set of Laurent polynomials $\sum_{b,a} z_{b,a} u^b v^a$ whose coefficient $z_{b,a}$ lies in the $S$-span of $M_{0,a} \cdot \eta^{b}$ (the products of an element of $M_{0,a}$ with $b$ of the $\eta_j$) when $b \ge 0$, and is $0$ when $b < 0$. Let $P$ act on $L$ through the ring map with $y_j \mapsto \eta_j u$ and $X_i \mapsto x_i v$, and give $\mathcal{M}$ the grading by the exponent of $v$. Then $\mathcal{M}$ is a graded $P$-submodule of $L$. It is generated by the finitely many elements $m_j v^{d_j}$: the coefficient $f(x) m_j \eta^\beta$ is the coefficient of the image of $f \cdot y^\beta$ applied to $m_j v^{d_j}$. So $\mathcal{M} = \bigoplus_{b \ge 0} \mathcal{M}^{(b)}$, where $\mathcal{M}^{(b)}$ is the part with $u$-exponent $b$, and $\mathcal{M}^{(b)}_a = \operatorname{span}_S(M_{0,a}\eta^b)$.

The comparison on a chart. Let $K \subseteq \{0, \dots, N\}$ be nonempty. If $K$ is not inside the support, some $X_i$ with $i \in K$ acts on $L$ as multiplication by $x_i v = 0$, so the localization $\mathcal{M}_{(K)}$ is zero. If $K$ is inside the support, each $x_i v$ with $i \in K$ is a unit of $L$, so $\mathcal{M}_{(K)}$ maps to $L$ by $w/s \mapsto w/s(xv)$. Let $\gamma_K$ be this map followed by taking the coefficient of $u^b v^c$. By conditions 1 and 3, the module of $\mathcal{G} \otimes \mathcal{B}^b \otimes \mathcal{O}(c)$ on $K$ is the $A_K$-span of the products $z/s(x)$, where $z \in \operatorname{span}_S(M_{0,a}\eta^b)$, $s$ is a monomial in the $X_i$ with $i \in K$, and $a - \deg s = c$. So $\gamma_K$ maps $\mathcal{M}_{(K),c}$ into this module, and for every $z$ in this module there is $w \in \mathcal{M}_{(K),c}$ whose image in $L$ is $z u^b v^c$; then $\gamma_K(w) = z$.

The cochain maps. Define $\iota$ on cochains as follows: for a cochain $(z_K)$ of the twist, its image is, on a subset $K$ inside the support, a chosen $w_K \in \mathcal{M}_{(K),c}$ with image $z_K u^b v^c$ in $L$, and $0$ on the other subsets. Define $\pi$ on cochains of degree $c$ by $(w_K) \mapsto (\gamma_K(w_K))$, on the charts $K$. The maps $\gamma_K$ are compatible with restriction, so $\pi$ commutes with the Čech differentials. For $\iota$: the cochain $\iota(dz) - d\iota(z)$ vanishes on the subsets outside the support (where the modules are zero), and on a subset inside the support its image in $L$ is $(dz)_K u^b v^c - (dz)_K u^b v^c = 0$; the map to $L$ is injective there, so $\iota$ commutes with $d$. Both maps send cocycles to cocycles and coboundaries to coboundaries, and $\pi \circ \iota$ is the identity on cochains, since $\gamma_K(w_K) = z_K$. Hence $\pi \circ \iota = \mathrm{id}$ on cohomology. $\square$

**Remark 4.6.5.** Condition 3 implies condition 2: if $\mathcal{B}_I$ is spanned by the $\eta_j$, then each $\eta_j$ lies in every $\mathcal{B}_I$, so in $\Gamma(\mathcal{B})$. The module $\mathcal{M}$ is the module $\bigoplus_{b \ge 0} M_0 \cdot \eta^b$, graded over $P$ by the degree in $X$, with the extra variables $y_j$ recording the power of $\mathcal{B}$. The proof uses only the part of $u$-exponent $b$ of its Čech complex.

**Remark 4.6.6 (Formalization).** The hypothesis that each $\eta_j$ is a section of $\mathcal{B}$ is not used in the proof ([PTwistSplit.lean](lean:GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/KleimanAmpleness/PTwistSplit.lean#L69)); it also follows from the spanning hypothesis. The retraction is a retraction of abelian groups, and the finiteness of $\mathcal{M}$ over $P$ is an instance proved from the generators of $M_0$ ([TwistSplitObj.lean](lean:GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/KleimanAmpleness/TwistSplitObj.lean#L428)). The Laurent ring is the monoid algebra of $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ over $\Omega$. The cohomology of $\mathcal{M}$ uses all nonempty subsets of $\{0, \dots, N\}$, while the cohomology of the twists uses only the charts inside the support of $x$.

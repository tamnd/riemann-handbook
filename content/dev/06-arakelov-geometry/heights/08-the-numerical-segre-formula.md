## The numerical Segre formula for a projective bundle

This section computes the intersection numbers on the projective bundle of a vector bundle with a flag, over a multiprojective variety. Let $E$ be a vector bundle of rank $N$ on a variety $V$ of dimension $e$, filtered by subbundles whose graded pieces are line bundles of classes $b_0, \dots, b_{N-1}$, and let $\zeta$ be the class of the dual tautological line bundle on the projective bundle of lines, twisted by a class $a$. Then $\zeta^n$ against classes from $V$ is the sum of all monomials of degree $n - (N - 1)$ in the roots $a - b_i$, against the same classes on $V$. This is the classical statement that the push forward of $\zeta^{N-1+k}$ is the complete symmetric function of degree $k$ in the Chern roots. Here intersection numbers are defined by Hilbert functions, so the result is purely algebraic. It is used in [the deep statement on volumes](/entry/deep-st-r-of-use/) of the chapter on arithmetic volumes.

### Multiprojective varieties and their numbers

Let $F$ be a field and $K$ a finite set. For each $k \in K$ let $\iota_k$ be a finite set, and let $F[X]$ be the polynomial ring in the variables $X_{k,j}$, $j \in \iota_k$, graded by $\mathbb{N}^K$: the variable $X_{k,j}$ has degree the $k$-th unit vector. A point $y$ with coordinates $y_{k,j}$ in a field $\Omega \supseteq F$ defines the ideal $\mathfrak{p}_y$ of polynomials $f$ with $f(y_{k,j} T_k) = 0$ in $\Omega[T_k : k \in K]$. When $\mathfrak{p}_y$ is a relevant multihomogeneous prime (for each $k$ some $X_{k,j}$ is not in it), $y$ is the generic point of an irreducible closed subvariety $V$ of $\prod_k \mathbb{P}^{\iota_k}$, and $\dim V = \dim F[X]/\mathfrak{p}_y - |K|$.

**Definition 4.8.1 (Hilbert function, intersection number).** Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a multihomogeneous ideal of $F[X]$. Its Hilbert function is $h_{\mathfrak{p}}(m) = \dim_F (F[X]/\mathfrak{p})_m$ for $m \in \mathbb{N}^K$, and $h_{\mathfrak{p}}(m) = 0$ for $m \in \mathbb{Z}^K$ with a negative coordinate. For $d \in \mathbb{Z}^K$ and a function $h$ on $\mathbb{Z}^K$ put $(\Delta_d h)(m) = h(m) - h(m - d)$. For a list $d_1, \dots, d_n \in \mathbb{Z}^K$, the intersection number $(d_1 \cdots d_n)_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is the eventual value of $\Delta_{d_1} \cdots \Delta_{d_n} h_{\mathfrak{p}}(m)$ as all coordinates of $m$ tend to infinity, and $0$ if this function is not eventually constant.

When $n = \dim V$, this number is the intersection number on $V$ of the divisor classes $d_1, \dots, d_n$, where $d \in \mathbb{Z}^K$ is the class of $\bigotimes_k \mathcal{O}(d_k)$. It does not depend on the order of the list, and it is additive in each entry. Both facts are used below.

### Bundles with a flag

The Segre charts of $V$ are the choices $c = (c_k)_k$ of one index $c_k \in \iota_k$ for each $k$ such that $s_c = \prod_k y_{k,c_k} \ne 0$. The chart ring $A_c \subseteq \Omega$ is the $F$-algebra generated by the quotients $s_{c'}/s_c$. For $b \in \mathbb{Z}^K$ let $A_c(b)$ be the set of $u \prod_k y_{k,c_k}^{b_k}$ with $u \in A_c$, the sections of $\mathcal{O}(b)$ on the chart, written in the function field.

**Definition 4.8.2 (Chart bundle).** Let $E$ be an $\Omega$-vector space of dimension $N$. A chart bundle of rank $N$ on $V$ with generic fibre $E$ is a family of additive subgroups $\mathcal{E}_c \subseteq E$ with these properties: $\mathcal{E}_c$ is an $A_c$-module; locally on the chart it is free of rank $N$ (there are $f_l \in A_c$ generating the unit ideal such that after inverting each $f_l$ the module has an $\Omega$-basis of $E$ as a basis); and an element of $\mathcal{E}_c$ times a power of $s_c/s_{c'}$ lies in $\mathcal{E}_{c'}$.

**Definition 4.8.3 (Flag frame, twisted dual generators).** A flag frame of a chart bundle $\mathcal{E}$ is a basis $w_0, \dots, w_{N-1}$ of $E$ with classes $b_0, \dots, b_{N-1} \in \mathbb{Z}^K$ such that on each chart $c$ and for each $i$, the $i$-th coordinates of the vectors $v \in \mathcal{E}_c$ whose coordinates $j > i$ vanish form exactly $A_c(b_i)$. Let $a \in \mathbb{Z}^K$. A family of linear forms $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_M$ on $E$ is a family of twisted dual generators of degree $a$ if on every chart $\varphi_l(\mathcal{E}_c) \subseteq A_c(a)$, and every linear form $\psi$ with $\psi(\mathcal{E}_c) \subseteq A_c$ is a combination $\sum_l u_l \prod_k y_{k,c_k}^{-a_k} \varphi_l$ with $u_l \in A_c$.

So $w_0, \dots, w_{i-1}$ span a subbundle $E_i$ of rank $i$, and $E_{i+1}/E_i \cong \mathcal{O}(b_i)$. The $\varphi_l$ are global sections of $E^{\vee}(a)$ that generate it on every chart.

**Definition 4.8.4 (The projective bundle).** Let $t_0, \dots, t_{N-1}$ be algebraically independent over $\Omega$ in a field $\Phi$, and put $v = \sum_i t_i w_i$, a generic vector of $E$. The projective bundle $\mathbb{P}(E)$ is the variety in $\mathbb{P}^{M-1} \times \prod_k \mathbb{P}^{\iota_k}$, over the index set $K \cup \{\ast\}$, with generic point $\bigl((\varphi_l(v))_l, y\bigr)$. Write $\zeta \in \mathbb{Z}^{K \cup \{\ast\}}$ for the unit vector at $\ast$, and $\pi^* d = (0, d)$ for $d \in \mathbb{Z}^K$.

So $\mathbb{P}(E)$ is the bundle of lines in $E$, embedded by the sections $\varphi_l$, and $\zeta$ is the class of the dual of its tautological line subbundle, twisted by $\pi^*a$.

### The formula

**Theorem 4.8.5 (Numerical Segre formula).** Let $V$ be as above, with $\mathfrak{p}_y$ a relevant multihomogeneous prime and $\dim F[X]/\mathfrak{p}_y = e + |K|$. Let $N \ge 1$, let $\mathcal{E}$ be a chart bundle of rank $N$ on $V$ with a flag frame of classes $b_0, \dots, b_{N-1}$, let $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_M$ be twisted dual generators of degree $a$, and form $\mathbb{P}(E)$ with algebraically independent $t_i$. Put $r_i = a - b_i$. Then for every $n \ge 0$ and every list $Y = (Y_1, \dots, Y_q)$ in $\mathbb{Z}^K$ with $n + q = N - 1 + e$,
$$\bigl(\zeta^n \cdot \pi^*Y_1 \cdots \pi^*Y_q\bigr)_{\mathbb{P}(E)} = \begin{cases} \displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha| = n - (N-1)} \bigl(r_0^{\alpha_0} \cdots r_{N-1}^{\alpha_{N-1}} \cdot Y_1 \cdots Y_q\bigr)_V, & n \ge N - 1, \\ 0, & n < N - 1. \end{cases}$$
The sum runs over $\alpha \in \mathbb{N}^N$ with $\alpha_0 + \cdots + \alpha_{N-1} = n - (N - 1)$, and $r^{\alpha}$ means the list in which $r_i$ appears $\alpha_i$ times.

In the classical language, the sum is the intersection on $V$ of $h_{n-N+1}(r_0, \dots, r_{N-1})$, the complete symmetric polynomial in the Chern roots of $E^{\vee}(a)$, with $Y$. This is the Segre class of $E^\vee(a)$ in the sign convention of Fulton (Ch. 3).

For $1 \le i \le N$, let $\mathbb{P}(E_i) \subseteq \mathbb{P}(E)$ be the variety with generic point $\bigl((\varphi_l(v_i))_l, y\bigr)$, where $v_i = \sum_{j < i} t_j w_j$. The proof uses four facts on these subbundles, proved elsewhere in the development, and the additivity of intersection numbers.

**Lemma 4.8.6 (Subbundles).** For $1 \le i \le N$, the ideal of $\mathbb{P}(E_i)$ is a relevant multihomogeneous prime, and $\dim \mathbb{P}(E_i) = e + i - 1$.

**Lemma 4.8.7 (The bottom bundle).** If $n + q = e$, then $(\zeta^n \cdot \pi^*Y)_{\mathbb{P}(E_1)} = (r_0^{n} \cdot Y)_V$.

**Lemma 4.8.8 (Cutting a subbundle).** For $1 \le i < N$ and every list $L$ in $\mathbb{Z}^{K \cup \{\ast\}}$ of length $e + i - 1$, $\bigl((\zeta - \pi^* r_i) \cdot L\bigr)_{\mathbb{P}(E_{i+1})} = (L)_{\mathbb{P}(E_i)}$.

**Lemma 4.8.9 (Pullbacks vanish).** For $2 \le i \le N$ and every list $Y$ in $\mathbb{Z}^K$ of length $e + i - 1$, $(\pi^*Y)_{\mathbb{P}(E_i)} = 0$.

On $\mathbb{P}(E_1) \cong V$ the class $\zeta$ is the class of $E_1^{\vee}(a) = \mathcal{O}(a - b_0)$, which gives the bottom bundle lemma. The coordinate $i$ of the generic vector is a section of $(\zeta - \pi^* r_i)$ on $\mathbb{P}(E_{i+1})$, and its divisor is $\mathbb{P}(E_i)$, which gives the cutting lemma. The last lemma holds because $\pi^*Y$ comes from $V$, whose dimension $e$ is less than $e + i - 1$.

*Proof.* For $1 \le i \le N$ and a list $Y$ in $\mathbb{Z}^K$ of length $q$ with $n + q + 1 = e + i$, let $S_i(n, Y)$ be the right side of the formula with $N$ replaced by $i$ and with only the roots $r_0, \dots, r_{i-1}$. We show
$$\bigl(\zeta^n \cdot \pi^*Y\bigr)_{\mathbb{P}(E_i)} = S_i(n, Y)$$
by induction on $i$, and for fixed $i$ by induction on $n$. The case $i = N$ is the theorem, since $v_N = v$.

For $i = 1$ this is the bottom bundle lemma, since the only $\alpha$ is $\alpha_0 = n$.

Let $1 \le i < N$ and assume the claim for $i$. For $n = 0$, the left side for $i + 1$ is $0$ by the vanishing lemma, and $S_{i+1}(0, Y) = 0$ since $0 < i$. For $n = m + 1$, write $L = (\zeta^m, \pi^*Y)$, of length $e + i - 1$ by the dimension lemma. Since $\zeta = (\zeta - \pi^*r_i) + \pi^*r_i$, additivity in the first entry gives
$$\begin{aligned}
\bigl(\zeta^{m+1} \cdot \pi^*Y\bigr)_{\mathbb{P}(E_{i+1})} &= \bigl((\zeta - \pi^* r_i) \cdot L\bigr)_{\mathbb{P}(E_{i+1})} + \bigl(\pi^* r_i \cdot L\bigr)_{\mathbb{P}(E_{i+1})} \\
&= \bigl(\zeta^m \cdot \pi^*Y\bigr)_{\mathbb{P}(E_i)} + \bigl(\zeta^m \cdot \pi^*(r_i, Y)\bigr)_{\mathbb{P}(E_{i+1})} \\
&= S_i(m, Y) + S_{i+1}(m, (r_i, Y)),
\end{aligned}$$
by the cutting lemma, a permutation of the list, the hypothesis for $i$ and the hypothesis for $m$. It remains to check $S_{i+1}(m+1, Y) = S_i(m, Y) + S_{i+1}(m, (r_i, Y))$. With $c = m + 1 - i$, split the $\alpha \in \mathbb{N}^{i+1}$ with $|\alpha| = c$ by the last entry. Those with $\alpha_i = 0$ give $S_i(m, Y)$. Those with $\alpha_i \ge 1$ are $\beta + e_i$ with $|\beta| = c - 1$, and give $S_{i+1}(m, (r_i, Y))$. This is the recursion $h_c(r_0, \dots, r_i) = h_c(r_0, \dots, r_{i-1}) + r_i\, h_{c-1}(r_0, \dots, r_i)$. When $c = 0$ the second group is empty and $S_{i+1}(m, (r_i, Y)) = 0$ because $m < i$. When $m + 1 < i$ all three terms are $0$. $\square$

**Example 4.8.10.** Take $N = 2$, $e = 1$, $n = 2$ and $Y$ empty. The formula gives $(\zeta^2)_{\mathbb{P}(E)} = (r_0)_V + (r_1)_V = \deg E^{\vee}(a)$ on the curve $V$, the classical relation $\zeta^2 = \pi^* c_1(E^\vee(a)) \cdot \zeta$ on a ruled surface. For $n = 1$ and $Y = (Y_1)$ it gives $(\zeta \cdot \pi^* Y_1)_{\mathbb{P}(E)} = (Y_1)_V$: a fibre meets $\zeta$ once.

**Remark 4.8.11 (Formalization).** The intersection number is the eventual value of the iterated differences of the Hilbert function, and it is set to $0$ when this value does not exist ([Objects.lean](lean:GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/DiscreteValuationNumbers/Objects.lean#L84)); the statement is only meaningful when the length of the list is the dimension. The subbundles $\mathbb{P}(E_i)$ are made by setting $t_j = 0$ for $j \ge i$. The proof is the double induction above ([PNumericalSegre.lean](lean:GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/DiscreteValuationNumbers/PNumericalSegre.lean#L182)); the four lemmas are assembled from about sixty results on Hilbert functions and local lengths in [NumericalSegre.lean](lean:GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/SemipositiveHeights/NumericalSegre.lean#L96). The hypothesis that $\mathfrak{p}_y$ is a prime is stated separately from the hypothesis on its dimension.

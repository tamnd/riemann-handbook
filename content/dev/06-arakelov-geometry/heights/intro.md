title: Introduction
---
This chapter proves the results on heights of points and cycles that the arithmetic part of the proof uses. Sections 1 to 3 bound the rest terms of the arithmetic volume formula: a flag integral identity, a bound at one prime and a sum over the primes. Sections 4 and 5 reduce positive definite symplectic matrices and bound theta functions. Sections 6 to 10 are algebraic and complex geometric inputs: Čech cohomology of twists, Grothendieck's existence theorem, the numerical Segre formula, Chow forms cut by a hyperplane, and the degree of a divisor as a Chern integral. Sections 11 to 13 build the arithmetic intersection numbers: the uniform integrability of Green functions, the Poincaré-Lelong formula and the product formula in dimension one. Section 14 proves an absolute Siegel lemma from the successive minima bounds, and Section 15 proves the seven rules of arithmetic intersection theory from the Poincaré-Lelong formula and resolution of singularities.

We use the [arithmetic varieties and intersection numbers](/learn/arakelov/#arithmetic-varieties-and-intersection-numbers), the [heights](/learn/arakelov/#heights) and the [hermitian lattices and slopes](/learn/arakelov/#hermitian-lattices-and-slopes) of Volume 1, together with the [rings of integers](/learn/number-fields/#number-fields-and-their-integers) of the chapter on number fields. The results feed [the finite bound](/entry/finite-bound/) of the chapter on arithmetic volumes, [the existence of the intersection family](/entry/thm-iexists/) of the chapter on sup norms of theta sections, and [the Siegel lemma from the successive minima bounds](/entry/line-of-large-degree-of-use/). The Poincaré-Lelong formula and the arithmetic intersection calculus serve the step [EveryCharacter of literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/).

## Separating orbits by cusp forms

This section proves that cusp forms of a large multiple of the Hodge weight separate the points of an orbit of a unipotent group of level automorphisms. From this it builds one cusp form whose group determinant is not a zero divisor in the ring of modular forms. The covolume bound uses this form to divide by the group determinant when it compares lattices at two levels. The argument is the method of point separation for adjoint bundles ([point separation](/learn/geometry/#point-separation)) applied on a compactification of a component of the Shimura variety. It needs one fact of linear algebra on the boundary: the Hodge line bundle has a linearization up to rational equivalence for every finite group of level automorphisms that is unipotent modulo a prime.

We fix a good cover $Y$ at a level $j$ of a tower over a CM field $E$, with a unitary datum of signature $(p, q)$ at level $j$. Let $X^\circ$ be its moduli space, with a coefficient field $F$, and let $Z$ run over the connected components of $X^\circ$. A *framed point* of $X^\circ$ is a point with a frame of the Hodge bundle ([the Hodge bundle](/learn/arakelov/#the-hodge-bundle-and-zarhins-trick)). A level automorphism $h$ moves framed points, and we write $y \mapsto h^{-1} y$. A *rule* is a function on framed points that is functorial in the coefficients. A rule $f$ is a modular form of integer weight $e$ if it lies in the space $\mathrm{sec}_e$ of sections of weight $e$; the *ring of modular forms* is $R = \sum_e \mathrm{sec}_e$. For a weight $\lambda$, we write $S_\lambda(Z)$ for the cusp forms of weight $\lambda$ on $Z$. The *Hodge weight* $\lambda_H$ is the weight of the determinant of the Hodge bundle of type $(p, q)$, and $L(\lambda)$ is the automorphic line bundle of weight $\lambda$ on the toroidal compactification.

**Definition 2.2.1 (Neat prime and unipotence).** Let $I$ be an ideal of $\mathcal{O}_E$, $g$ a natural number and $r \ge 1$. The pair $(I, g)$ is *neat for* $r$ if $g$ is a prime number, the norm of $I$ is $g$, $g \notin I^2$, and $r + 1 < g$. A level automorphism $\gamma$ is *unipotent modulo* $I$ if $(\gamma - 1)^n$ maps the lattice into $I$ times the lattice, where $n$ is its rank.

**Definition 2.2.2 (Rational linearization).** Let $X$ be an integral scheme with function field $K(X)$, let $\Gamma$ be a finite group acting on $X$ by $\rho$, and let $\mathcal{L}$ be a line bundle on $X$. A *rational linearization* of $\mathcal{L}$ is a Cartier divisor $D$ with $\mathcal{O}(D) \cong \mathcal{L}$, together with a map $c \colon \Gamma \to K(X)^\times$ such that:

- $c$ is a cocycle: $c(\gamma \delta) = \delta^*\bigl(c(\gamma)\bigr) \cdot c(\delta)$ for all $\gamma, \delta \in \Gamma$;
- the local equations of $D$ transform under $\gamma$ by the factor $c(\gamma)$;
- at each point fixed by $\delta$, the ratio that compares the $\delta$-twist of a local equation with the local equation is $1$ modulo the maximal ideal.

We say that $\mathcal{L}$ is *rationally linear* for $\rho$. The last condition says that the stabilizer of a fixed point acts trivially on the fibre. A rational linearization makes $\mathcal{L}$ descend to the quotient by $\Gamma$ up to rational equivalence.

Let $X_0$ be the part of the minimal compactification over one component: the complement of the closure of the images of the other components. Let $V_0$ be the preimage of $X_0$ in the interior, $j_0 \colon V_0 \to X_0$ the inclusion, $Y_0$ the toroidal flag space over $X_0$, with $\pi_0 \colon Y_0 \to X_0$, and $s_0$ the structure map of $X_0$. Let $L_0(\lambda)$ be the automorphic line bundle of weight $\lambda$ on $Y_0$. We say that the coefficient field is *algebraically closed in the function field* if every element of the ring of functions of $Z$ that is algebraic over $F$ lies in $F$.

**Theorem 2.2.3 (The Hodge line bundle is rationally linear).** Assume the published Hodge bundle theorems. Let the datum have $p + q \ge 1$, let the level have $A = 1$, and let $F$ be algebraically closed in the function field of $Z$. Then there is a line bundle $\delta$ on $X_0$ with $\pi_0^* \delta \cong L_0(\lambda_H)$ such that the following holds. Let $I$ be an ideal and $g$ a number such that $(I, g)$ is neat for $p + q$ and $I$ contains the level ideal. Let $\Gamma$ be a finite group with a map $\iota$ to the level automorphisms and an action $\rho$ on $V_0$, such that each $\iota(\gamma)$ is unipotent modulo $I$ and $\rho(\gamma)$ moves framed points by $\iota(\gamma)^{-1}$. Let $X'$ be an integral normal scheme with a morphism $\varphi \colon X' \to X_0$ such that $X'$ is projective over the base, with an open immersion $\ell \colon V_0 \to X'$ such that $\varphi \circ \ell = j_0$, and with an action $\rho'$ of $\Gamma$ on $X'$ that extends $\rho$. Then $\varphi^* \delta$ is rationally linear for $\rho'$.

*Proof.* The scheme $X_0$ is a variety. On the toroidal model there is a Cartier divisor $D$ on $X_0$ built from the determinant of the Lie eigenparts, for an auxiliary choice of embeddings, such that $\pi_0^* \mathcal{O}(D)$ is the automorphic line bundle of the exponent of $\lambda_H$. This exponent is $\lambda_H$, so we put $\delta = \mathcal{O}(D)$. Now fix $I$, $g$, $\Gamma$, $\iota$, $\rho$, $X'$, $\varphi$, $\ell$ and $\rho'$. [Theorem 2.10.3](/entry/equivariant-linearization-pullback/) gives a linearization on $X'$ of the pulled back Lie data that is compatible with $\rho'$. The interior $V_0$ has a point that maps to the generic point of $X_0$, so $\varphi$ is dominant, and $\ell$ is dominant because it is an open immersion with nonempty image. Each $\rho'(\gamma)$ commutes with the structure map $s_0 \circ \varphi$, because it does so on the dense open set $\ell(V_0)$, where $\rho$ commutes with the structure. A lemma on the Lie determinant then says: when the divisor $D$ is pulled back along a dominant morphism that is compatible with the actions and carries such a linearization, the pullback $\varphi^* \delta$ is rationally linear for $\rho'$. $\square$

The next theorem is the separation of orbits.

**Theorem 2.2.4 (Cusp forms separate unipotent orbits).** Assume the published Hodge bundle theorems, the Grothendieck-Messing deformation theory of abelian schemes (W. Messing, *The Crystals Associated to Barsotti-Tate Groups*, Lecture Notes in Math. 264), the quoted vanishing and positivity results, the toroidal compactification of the Siegel moduli space over the integers (G. Faltings and C.-L. Chai, *Degeneration of Abelian Varieties*, Ergeb. Math. 22, Chapter IV), the toroidal compactifications of PEL type with the log Kodaira-Spencer isomorphism (K.-W. Lan, *Arithmetic Compactifications of PEL-Type Shimura Varieties*), and the seminegativity of the Lie algebra of a semi-abelian scheme. Let $Y$ be a good cover at level $j$, with $[E : \mathbb{Q}]/2 > 2$ (integer division) and $p, q \ge 1$. Let $S$ be a subgroup of the level automorphisms, let $(I, g)$ be neat for $p + q$ with $I$ containing the level ideal, and let every element of $S$ be unipotent modulo $I$. Let $s$ be an integer with
$$s \ge p + q + \binom{n + 1}{2} + 1,$$
where $n$ is the dimension of the datum, and let $Z$ be a component. Then there is a complex framed point $y$ on $Z$ such that, for the finite set $S_Z = \{h \in S : h^{-1} y \in Z\}$ and every family $(a_h)_{h \in S_Z}$ of complex numbers,
$$\sum_{h \in S_Z} a_h\, u(h^{-1} y) = 0 \ \text{ for all } u \in S_{s \lambda_H}(Z) \implies a = 0.$$

So the evaluations at the points $h^{-1} y$ are linearly independent on cusp forms of weight $s \lambda_H$.

*Proof.* The cover has a toroidal flag space, and its part $\bar Z$ over $Z$ is integral, smooth, projective and geometrically connected. Take $y$ over the generic point of $\bar Z$. The stabilizer $\Gamma$ of $Z$ in $S$ acts by level automorphisms, and there is a normal projective $\Gamma$-equivariant compactification $X'$ with a finite quotient $X' \to W$. By the theorem above, the Hodge line bundle $\delta$ is rationally linear on $X'$, so it descends to $W$. The points $h^{-1} y$ with $h \in S_Z$ lie in one fibre of the quotient map. Write $s = m + t$ with $m = \binom{n+1}{2} + 1$. There is a line bundle $\kappa$ whose pullback is the automorphic line bundle of the canonical weight, with a section of $\kappa \otimes K^{-1}$ that is nonzero exactly on the interior, where $K$ is the canonical bundle. Put $N = t \delta - \kappa$ and $B = m \delta + N$. Then $N$ is nef, $\delta$ is nef and big on the Shimura variety, and $K \otimes B$ has weight $s \lambda_H$. For $\delta$ nef and big, $N$ nef and $m > \binom{n+1}{2}$, sections of $K \otimes B$ separate distinct points of one fibre of the quotient when one of them is generic. This step uses the Nadel vanishing theorem and principalization of ideals from the published list ([vanishing theorems](/learn/geometry/#vanishing-theorems-and-resolution)). The points $h^{-1} y$ are distinct: if $h^{-1} y = h'^{-1} y$, then $h' h^{-1}$ fixes a complex point and is unipotent modulo the neat ideal $I$, so it is $1$. Finally, a section of $K \otimes B$ times the section that vanishes on the boundary is a cusp form of weight $s \lambda_H$ on $Z$, with the same values up to nonzero units. So a relation among the values of cusp forms is a relation among the values of sections, and $a = 0$. $\square$

For a finite subgroup $S$ of level automorphisms and a rule $u$, the *group determinant* of $u$ is
$$\operatorname{det}_S(u) = \det\bigl( (h g)^* u \bigr)_{h, g \in S},$$
the determinant of the matrix whose entry at $(h, g)$ is the pullback of $u$ by $h g$. This is the group determinant of Dedekind and Frobenius, with the pullbacks of $u$ in place of the variables.

**Theorem 2.2.5 (A cusp form with regular group determinant).** Assume the six inputs of the previous theorem. Let $T$ be a tower with its laws, $j$ a level with $p, q \ge 1$, $[E : \mathbb{Q}]/2 > 2$, and $j$ in the range of the tower. Let $Y$ be a good cover at level $j$ with parameters $(e, o)$ and $e \ge 4$, and assume $r + 1 < g_k$ for both host primes $g_k$, where $r$ is the rank. Let $\mathrm{fr}$ be a type frame and let $S$ be one of the subgroups of level automorphisms attached to $\mathrm{fr}$ (the group $H_R$ of the frame, or one of the groups $G_k H_R$). Then there is a cusp form $u$ of weight $c_0 \lambda_H$ such that for every $f \in R$,
$$\operatorname{det}_S(u) \cdot f = 0 \implies f = 0.$$
Here $c_0$ is the weight constant of level $j$.

*Proof.* There is an ideal $I$ and a prime $g$ with $(I, g)$ neat for $p + q$, with $I$ containing the level ideal, such that each element of $S$ is unipotent modulo $I$; here we use $r + 1 < g_k$ and $p + q = r$. The weight constant satisfies $c_0 \ge p + q + \binom{n+1}{2} + 1$. So [Theorem 2.2.4](/entry/separate-orbits-by-cusp/) applies with $s = c_0$, and for each component $Z$ it gives a complex point $y_Z$. There are finitely many components. A lemma of linear algebra then gives one cusp form $u$ such that the matrix $\bigl(u(g^{-1} h^{-1} y_Z)\bigr)_{h, g}$ has nonzero determinant for every $Z$: for each $Z$ the set of $u$ with zero determinant is a proper Zariski closed subset of the space of cusp forms, because the evaluations at the points $h^{-1} y_Z$ are linearly independent, and a finite union of proper closed subsets is proper. Evaluation at $y_Z$ is a ring map, so this determinant is the value of $\operatorname{det}_S(u)$ at $y_Z$. The group determinant is a modular form of integer weight. A modular form of integer weight that is nonzero at a complex point of each component is not a zero divisor in $R$. $\square$

**Remark 2.2.6 (Formalization).** In [ExistsCuspRegularLevelDeterminant.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/HodgeBundleWeights/ExistsCuspRegularLevelDeterminant.lean#L27) the orbit separation quantifies over a `Fintype` instance on $S_Z$ inside its conclusion (line 36), so the finiteness of $S_Z$ is an argument and not a claim. The hypothesis $4 \le e$ of the second theorem is introduced as `_` and not used (line 146). The second name `GlobalTheory.T.orbits_separated_by_regular_cusp` (line 205) speaks of orbits and regular cusps, while the statement is about a group determinant that is not a zero divisor; it is proved by a `first` tactic. The separation step uses the fields `q15` and `q16` of the published list and the nefness field of the quoted list; we have not opened the separation lemma that it restates, and the constant $\binom{n+1}{2} + 1$ has the form of the bound of U. Angehrn and Y.-T. Siu, "Effective freeness and point separation for adjoint bundles", Invent. Math. 122 (1995), but no source is named. The definitions of rational linearity, neat primes and unipotence are in `GapGeSubKapBase.lean`. We have not unfolded the subgroups attached to a type frame, the range condition on $j$, or the weight constant $c_0$. In the first theorem, the Lie determinant step and the linear algebra step are lemmas that we did not open beyond their statements.

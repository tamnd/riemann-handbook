## Blow-ups, quotients and Zarhin's trick

This section collects four inputs of the arithmetic part of the argument. The first is local: it compares the components of a vertical divisor before and after a blow-up of an integral model over a discrete valuation ring. The second gives an arithmetic intersection theory on multiprojective arithmetic varieties. The third and fourth are on abelian schemes: quotients by finite flat subgroups, and Zarhin's trick. The first result is used in [the blow-up fibre theorem](/entry/blow-up-fibre-main/), and the second and third in [the deep slim theorem](/entry/deep-slim-use/).

### Blow-ups over a discrete valuation ring

Let $\mathcal{O}$ be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $\varpi$, and let $M = \mathcal{O}[X_{k,i}]$ be a polynomial ring graded by a finite set $K$ of factors, so that $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a quotient of $M$ is a closed subscheme of a product of projective spaces over $\mathcal{O}$.

**Definition 7.11.1 (Model and homogeneous local ring).** A *model* of dimension $n$ is a multihomogeneous prime $P \subset M$ with $P \cap \mathcal{O} = 0$ and $\dim M/P = n + 1 + |K|$. It is the ideal of an integral scheme, flat of relative dimension $n$ over $\mathcal{O}$. For a relevant multihomogeneous prime $W \supseteq P$, the *homogeneous local ring* $\mathcal{O}_W$ is the subring of the fraction field of $M/P$ generated by the quotients $f/g$ with $f, g$ of the same multidegree and $g \notin W$. It is the local ring of the model at the point $W$.

**Definition 7.11.2 (Blow-up of a model).** Let $u_1, \dots, u_m \in M$ be forms of one multidegree $N$, not all in $P$. Add a new factor with variables $Y_1, \dots, Y_m$. The *blow-up* of $P$ along the $u_j$ is the kernel of the map from the extended polynomial ring to $(M/P)[T]$ that fixes the $X_{k,i}$ and sends $Y_j$ to $u_j T$. It is the ideal of the closure of the graph of $(u_1 : \dots : u_m)$, that is, of the blow-up of the model along the ideal of the $u_j$. A form $x \in M$ lifts to a form $\tilde x$ of the extended ring by the inclusion.

**Theorem 7.11.3 (Blow-up fibres are semilocal).** Let $P$ be a model of dimension $n$, let $u_1, \dots, u_m$ be as above, let $x \in M$ be a form of multidegree $c$ with $x \notin P$, and let $W$ be a relevant minimal prime of $P + (x)$ with $\varpi \in W$. Let $g \notin W$ be a form of multidegree $c$, and put $z = x/g \in \mathcal{O}_W$. Then there is a domain $B$, finite over $\mathcal{O}_W$ and of generic rank $1$, with an injective structure map, and a bijection $\mathfrak{n} \mapsto W'_{\mathfrak{n}}$ from the maximal ideals of $B$ to the relevant minimal primes $W'$ of $\tilde P + (\tilde x)$ that lie over $W$, where $\tilde P$ is the blow-up. For each $\mathfrak{n}$:

1. the length of $B_{\mathfrak{n}} / z B_{\mathfrak{n}}$ is the multiplicity of $W'_{\mathfrak{n}}$ in the divisor of $\tilde x$;
2. the residue degree of $\mathfrak{n}$ over $\mathcal{O}_W$ is the degree of $W'_{\mathfrak{n}}$ over $W$, computed in the special fibre.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Xgn.t_s_blowupfibre`.

*Proof sketch.* The formal proof is a chain of lemmas, all proved.

1. The ring $\mathcal{O}_W$ is local and noetherian of dimension $1$ ([local rings](/learn/commutative-algebra/#local-rings-and-dimension)).
2. Let $B$ be the intersection, in the fraction field of $M/P$, of the homogeneous local rings of the blow-up at all relevant primes over $W$. This is the semilocal ring of the blow-up along the fibre over $W$, and it contains $\mathcal{O}_W$. On a chart of the blow-up, a bound on the number of generators of the powers of the ideal $(u_j)\mathcal{O}_W$ gives a principal reduction, and with it the finiteness of $B$ over $\mathcal{O}_W$. Since $B$ is birational over $\mathcal{O}_W$, its generic rank is $1$.
3. The maximal ideals of $B$ are the points of the blow-up over the closed point of $\mathcal{O}_W$. A localization lemma identifies $B_{\mathfrak{n}}$ with the homogeneous local ring of the blow-up at $W'_{\mathfrak{n}}$. Lengths of quotients by $z$ then compute multiplicities of the divisor of $\tilde x$, which gives 1.
4. The residue field of $B_{\mathfrak{n}}$ is the function field of $W'_{\mathfrak{n}}$ modulo $\varpi$. Its degree over the residue field of $\mathcal{O}_W$ is the relative degree of the special components, which gives 2. $\square$

With the length formula for a finite birational extension of a one-dimensional local domain,
$$\ell_{\mathcal{O}_W}(\mathcal{O}_W / z) = \sum_{\mathfrak{n}} \ell(B_{\mathfrak{n}} / z B_{\mathfrak{n}})\, [\kappa(\mathfrak{n}) : \kappa(W)],$$
the theorem says that the multiplicity of $W$ in $\operatorname{div}(x)$ is the sum, over the components $W'$ above $W$, of the multiplicity of $W'$ in $\operatorname{div}(\tilde x)$ times the degree of $W'$ over $W$. So the vertical part of a principal divisor is compatible with push-forward along the blow-up.

### Arithmetic intersection systems

**Definition 7.11.4 (Arithmetic intersection system).** An *intersection system* assigns a real number $\widehat{\deg}(\bar L_1 \cdots \bar L_n \mid P)$ to each multiprojective arithmetic variety $P$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ of dimension $n$ and each family of $n$ hermitian line bundles $\bar L_j = (\mathcal{O}(a_j), h_j)$ ([hermitian line bundles](/learn/arakelov/#hermitian-line-bundles)). It is *full* if:

1. it is symmetric, and additive in each $\bar L_j$ under tensor product;
2. for a form $s \notin P$ of the degree of $\bar L_n$ it satisfies the recursion
$$\widehat{\deg}(\bar L_1 \cdots \bar L_n \mid P) = \sum_{W} m_W(s)\, \widehat{\deg}(\bar L_1 \cdots \bar L_{n-1} \mid W) + \sum_{V} m_V(s)\, \deg_V(L_1 \cdots L_{n-1}) \log p_V - I(s),$$
with $W$ over the horizontal and $V$ over the vertical components of $\operatorname{div}(s)$, $p_V$ the residue characteristic of $V$, and $I(s)$ the integral of $\log \lVert s \rVert$ over $P(\mathbb{C})$ against the forms of $\bar L_1, \dots, \bar L_{n-1}$;
3. it satisfies the projection formula along a block map of coordinates, with the degree of the extension of function fields as factor;
4. it vanishes on a family of pulled-back bundles that is longer than the dimension of the image.

This is the recursive definition of the height of a variety with respect to hermitian line bundles ([arithmetic intersection numbers](/learn/arakelov/#arithmetic-varieties-and-intersection-numbers)).

**Theorem 7.11.5 (Existence of an arithmetic intersection system).** Assume resolution of singularities and principalization in characteristic $0$, and the Poincaré-Lelong formula for the integrals of the recursion. Then a full arithmetic intersection system exists.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.HtPt.Ah.ais`.

*Proof.* Resolution and principalization together give the statement `ResK` ([resolution and principalization](/learn/positivity-resolution/#resolution-of-singularities-and-principalization)). With `ResK`, the Poincaré-Lelong hypothesis gives the Poincaré-Lelong formula in the form that the construction needs. The construction then defines $\widehat{\deg}$ by the recursion and proves the four properties. $\square$

### Quotients and Zarhin's trick

**Theorem 7.11.6 (Quotient by a finite flat subgroup).** Let $R$ be a ring, $A$ an abelian scheme over $R$ with a relatively ample line bundle $L$, and $H \subset A$ a finite flat subgroup scheme. Then there are an abelian scheme $A'$ over $R$ with a relatively ample line bundle $L'$, and a homomorphism $q \colon A \to A'$ that is finite, flat and surjective, such that for each $R$-algebra $S$ a point $z \in A(S)$ satisfies $q(z) = 1$ if and only if $z \in H(S)$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.HtPt.Ah.quot`.

*Proof.* The group $H$ acts freely on $A$ by translation. The formal proof derives the theorem from the existence of quotients: if a finite locally free group scheme acts freely on a scheme, and each orbit lies in an affine open, then there is a quotient $q$ that is an fppf quotient and an fppf torsor under the group. This is proved in the formalization, from affine charts that are stable under the action. For the statement, compare [abelian schemes](/learn/abelian-varieties/#abelian-schemes) and Mumford, *Abelian Varieties*, §12, Theorem 1. $\square$

**Theorem 7.11.7 (Zarhin's trick).** Let $C$ be an algebraically closed field with a valuation, and $R$ its valuation ring. Assume that every abelian scheme has a dual abelian scheme with a Poincaré bundle. Then for each abelian scheme $A$ over $R$ with a relatively ample line bundle there are a principally polarized abelian scheme $P$ over $R$ and homomorphisms $i \colon A \to P$, $r \colon P \to A$ with $r \circ i = \mathrm{id}_A$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.HtPt.Narrow.zarhin`.

*Proof.* This is [Zarhin's trick over a valuation ring](/entry/zarhin-statement-of-dual-cube/), applied with the theorem of the cube, which is proved. The principally polarized scheme is $(A \times A^{\vee})^4$, with a polarization built from four integers whose squares sum to $-1$ modulo the degree ([polarizations and Zarhin's trick](/learn/abelian-varieties/#polarizations-and-zarhins-trick)). $\square$

The quotient theorem and Zarhin's trick are used together in the deep slim theorem. That theorem takes Zarhin's statement as a hypothesis, and the last theorem shows that this hypothesis follows from the existence of dual abelian schemes.

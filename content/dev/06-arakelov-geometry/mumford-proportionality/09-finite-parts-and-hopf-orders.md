## Finite parts and Hopf orders

This section studies the $p$-torsion of a degenerating abelian variety over a complete local ring. Over such a ring a semi-abelian scheme $G$ is an extension of an abelian scheme $B$ by a split torus $T_X$ on the formal completion, by Mumford's construction ([semi-abelian degenerations](/learn/abelian-varieties/#semi-abelian-degenerations-and-mumfords-construction)). We show that the finite part of the $N$-torsion of $G$ is an extension of $B[N]$ by $T_X[N]$, and that for $N = p$ the ring of functions of the $p$-torsion is a tensor product of three standard orders. Then we show that its base change to a test field is a factor of the $p$-torsion of an abelian scheme and is *tight*. The last two theorems apply this to the test groups of the Hodge-Tate chapter.

**Definition 7.9.1 (Quasi-finite groups and their orders).** Let $R$ be a domain with fraction field $K$ and let $T$ be a finite commutative group. A *quasi-finite group with points $T$* is a commutative group scheme $\Gamma$ over $R$ that is flat, locally quasi-finite, separated, quasi-compact and locally of finite presentation, with an injective homomorphism from $T$ to the $K$-points of $\Gamma$ that meets every point of the generic fibre, and with reduced generic fibre. Its *order* $\mathcal{O}(\Gamma)$ is the $R$-subalgebra of $R^T$ generated by the functions $f \colon T \to R$ that, with all their translates, are regular on $\Gamma$. We say that $\Gamma$ is the *$N$-torsion* of a commutative group scheme $G$ along $i \colon \Gamma \to G$ if $i$ is an injective homomorphism and a point $g$ of $G$ satisfies $g^N = 1$ exactly when it factors through $i$.

**Definition 7.9.2 (Hopf orders).** An $R$-subalgebra $A \subseteq R^T$ is a *Hopf order* if it is a finite projective $R$-module, contains $d\,R^T$ for some $d \neq 0$, is stable under $f \mapsto f(t^{-1})$, and its comultiplication $f \mapsto f(st)$ lands in the span of the products $g(s)\,h(t)$ with $g, h \in A$. The *multiplicative order* $\mu(R, T)$ is the $R$-subalgebra generated by the characters $T \to R^\times$. It is the ring of functions of the diagonalizable group scheme with character group $\operatorname{Hom}(T, R^\times)$, restricted to $T$.

**Theorem 7.9.3 (Finite part of the torsion, core form).** Let $R$ be a complete Noetherian local domain of characteristic $0$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, and let $R_e = R/\mathfrak{m}^{e+1}$. Let $G$ and $B$ be commutative group schemes over $R$ with $B$ an abelian scheme, and let $X$ be a finite free abelian group. Suppose that for each $e$ there is an exact sequence of group schemes over $R_e$
$$1 \to T_X \otimes R_e \xrightarrow{\ i_e\ } G \otimes R_e \xrightarrow{\ q_e\ } B \otimes R_e \to 1,$$
compatible with the reductions $R_{e+1} \to R_e$, with $q_0$ flat, surjective and quasi-compact and $i_0$ its kernel. Let $N \geq 1$, let $\Gamma$ be the $N$-torsion of $G$, and let $\Gamma^f \subseteq \Gamma$ be an open subgroup that is finite over $R$ and contains all points of $\Gamma$ over the closed point. Then there are the $N$-torsion $\Gamma_M$ of $T_X$, the $N$-torsion $\Gamma_B$ of $B$, a closed immersion $a \colon \Gamma_M \to \Gamma^f$ and a flat surjection $b \colon \Gamma^f \to \Gamma_B$ with $a$ the kernel of $b$, such that $b$ followed by the inclusion in $B$ agrees with $q_e$ on all thickenings.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.HtPt.Stight.D1.finitePart_extension_core`.

*Proof sketch.* Multiplication by $N$ is flat on the semi-abelian schemes $B$ and $T_X$. It is finite on $T_X$, and on $B$ it is proper and quasi-finite, hence finite. So their $N$-torsion groups $B[N]$ and $T_X[N]$ are finite flat. The subgroup $\Gamma^f$ is finite over $R$. Its reductions modulo $\mathfrak{m}^{e+1}$ map to $G \otimes R_e$, and composing with $q_e$ gives a compatible system of maps to $B[N] \otimes R_e$. Since $R$ is complete and $\Gamma^f$ and $B[N]$ are finite, these maps come from one homomorphism $b \colon \Gamma^f \to B[N]$ over $R$. Its kernel modulo each $\mathfrak{m}^{e+1}$ lies in $T_X[N] \otimes R_e$, so the kernel is $T_X[N]$. Flatness and surjectivity of $b$ follow from the fibres, since both groups are finite flat and the orders multiply in each fibre. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9.4 (Finite part of the torsion).** Let $R$ be as in the last theorem, let $G$ be a semi-abelian scheme over $R$ with degeneration data $D$ and uniformization data $U$, with torus $T_X$ and abelian part $B$. Let $N \geq 1$, let $\Gamma$ be a quasi-finite group that is the $N$-torsion of $G$, and let $\Gamma^f$ be an open subgroup that is finite over $R$ and contains all closed points of $\Gamma$. Then $\Gamma^f$ is an extension of $\Gamma_B = B[N]$ by $\Gamma_M = T_X[N]$ as in the last theorem.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.HtPt.Stight.D1.finitePart_extension`.

*Proof.* The uniformization data give the exact sequences on all thickenings, compatible with the reductions, and the kernel condition at $e = 0$. The map $q_0$ is affine, so it is quasi-compact. Apply the core form. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9.5 (Structure of the $p$-torsion).** Let $R$ be as above and also normal, let $G$, $D$ and $U$ be as in the last theorem, let $p$ be a prime and let $\Gamma$ be a quasi-finite group with points $T$ that is the $p$-torsion of $G$. Then there are finite groups $M$, $C$ and $Q$, the $p$-torsion $\Gamma_B$ of $B$ with points $C$, and an isomorphism $e \colon (M \times C) \times Q \to T$ such that $\mu(R, M)$ is a Hopf order and
$$\mathcal{O}(\Gamma) \circ e = \operatorname{span}_R \bigl\{ (m, c, x) \mapsto \alpha(m)\,\beta(c)\,\gamma(x) : \alpha \in \mu(R, M),\ \beta \in \mathcal{O}(\Gamma_B),\ \gamma \in R^Q \bigr\}.$$

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Xgn.TightHopfOrder.exists_struct`.

*Proof sketch.* Since $R$ is complete, it is Henselian. The group $\Gamma$ splits into its finite part $\Gamma^f$ and an étale part, and the étale part over a Henselian ring is a disjoint union of copies of the base. Its order is all of $R^Q$, with $Q$ the points that do not reduce into $\Gamma^f$. By the finite part theorem, $\Gamma^f$ is an extension of $B[p]$ by $T_X[p]$. The torus part is the dual of a constant group, with order $\mu(R, M)$. The extension splits on points, and the order of $\Gamma^f$ is the tensor product of the two orders. $\square$

**Definition 7.9.6 (Abelian factors and tightness).** Let $C$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ with a valuation $v$, with $v(p) < 1$. Let $L$ be a finite abelian group and $\mathcal{A} \subseteq C^L$ an $R$-submodule.

1. $\mathcal{A}$ is an *abelian factor* if there are an abelian scheme $A$ over $R$ with a relatively ample line bundle, its $p$-torsion points $L'$, and a split inclusion $L \to L'$, such that $\mathcal{A}$ is the restriction to $L$ of the functions on $A[p]$, and the functions on $A[p]$ are stable under the projection to $L$.
2. $\mathcal{A}$ is *tight* if there are constants $c_A, c_N, c_O$ such that the length of a quotient of $\mathcal{A}$ is $c_A$, the colength of $\mathcal{A}$ is $c_N$, the length of its module of invariant differentials is $c_O$, and $c_A^{|L|(p-1)} = c_N^2$ and $c_O^{|L|} = c_N^2$.

Here lengths are measured by $v$. Tightness is the numerical form of the relation between the discriminant of a finite flat group scheme and its module of differentials.

**Theorem 7.9.7 (Tight Hopf orders).** Assume the Hodge-Tate datum statement and the ordinary curve statement. Let $C$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ with a valuation $v$, $v(p) < 1$, whose ring of integers $V$ is $p$-adically complete. Let $R$ be a normal complete Noetherian local domain of characteristic $0$ with an injection $R \to V$, let $G$ be a semi-abelian scheme over $R$ with degeneration and uniformization data, and let $\Gamma$ be a quasi-finite group with points an $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space $L$, the $p$-torsion of $G$. Then the base change of $\mathcal{O}(\Gamma)$ to $C^L$ is an abelian factor and is tight.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Xgn.TightHopfOrder.core`.

*Proof sketch.* Take the decomposition $T \cong (M \times C) \times Q$ of the structure theorem. The $p$-torsion $\Gamma_B$ of $B$ is base changed along $R \to V$, where it is the $p$-torsion of a polarized abelian scheme over $V$, with a representation of its points. The order of the base change is the base change of the order. A lemma on bad reduction, which uses the two published statements, then gives that the tensor product of the order of $B[p]$, the multiplicative order on $M$ and the full function algebra on $Q$ is an abelian factor and is tight. Finally the base change of $\mathcal{O}(\Gamma)$ is identified with this tensor product, using that $\mathcal{O}(\Gamma)$ is a Hopf order of full rank. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9.8 (Test orders are tight).** Assume the Hodge-Tate datum statement and the ordinary curve statement. Let $\mathfrak{M}$ be a Hodge component of a model of a unitary Shimura variety, with a prime $p \neq 2$ not dividing the discriminant of $E$, at which $\mathfrak{M}$ is Hodge-Tate free. Let $P$ be a chart pack of generic $p$-torsion over a discrete valuation ring $O$ at a prime over $p$, with the hypotheses on the Frobenius orbit, the lattice sequences and the base of the chart. Let $C$ be a test field: an algebraically closed field with a valuation $v$, $v(p) < 1$, and $p$-adically complete ring of integers $R_C$ that contains the coordinate ring of the chart. Then the test order of $P$ over $R_C$ is an abelian factor and is tight.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Xgn.tight_hopf_order_test`.

*Proof sketch.* The chart pack gives a point of the model with values in $R_C$. By the uniformization at this point, there are a normal complete Noetherian local ring $R$ of characteristic $0$ with injections into $R_C$, a semi-abelian scheme with degeneration and uniformization data over $R$, and a base change of the generic $p$-torsion of $P$ to $R$ that is the $p$-torsion of this scheme. The order of the base change is the base change of the order. Apply the theorem on tight Hopf orders. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9.9 (Test group points).** Assume the Hodge-Tate statement of Fargues, the statement on quotients by finite flat subgroups, Zarhin's statement, the Hodge-Tate datum statement and the ordinary curve statement. In the setting of the last theorem, let $\tau$ be a label and $h_b \colon C^{r} \to C^{N}$ an injective linear map whose image is the kernel of the base change of the map $q$ of $P$, with the lattice conditions on its image. Then the Hodge lattice of the test point at the character $\chi_\tau$ maps onto the lattice $\omega_\tau$ under $q$, and its preimage under $h_b$ is $w^{-1}$ times the Lie lattice of $\tau$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Xgn.test_group_point_exists`.

*Proof sketch.* The chart pack gives integral models and the Hodge-Tate freeness at $p$. The test order is tight by the last theorem. Tightness fixes the lengths of the quotients of the Hodge lattice. The image under $q$ contains $\omega_\tau$ and the preimage under $h_b$ is contained in $w^{-1}\,\mathrm{Lie}_\tau$; the equality of lengths gives equality in both. $\square$

The decomposition into a multiplicative part, an abelian part and an étale part is the integral form of the connected-étale sequence for the $p$-torsion of a degenerating abelian variety ([$p$-divisible groups](/learn/formal-groups/#p-divisible-groups)). For Mumford's construction and the torsion of degenerating abelian varieties, see G. Faltings and C.-L. Chai, *Degeneration of Abelian Varieties*, Chapter III. The last theorem is used in [the ordinary curve relay](/entry/relay-ordinary-curve-model-relay/), and the tightness of test orders in [the Hodge relay for arithmetic varieties](/entry/relay-arith-variety-model-hodge-relay/).

## Column valuations

To bound the maximal slope of the level-zero lattice in Section 7 we need a model of it in coordinates: each element is a family, indexed by the level-zero classes, of polynomials in the entries of $N \times N$ matrices, one matrix for each embedding $\tau$ of the CM field. The lattice is cut out by local integrality conditions on these polynomials, and the metric is a sum of Fock norms. A *column valuation* is such a model, built from auxiliary hermitian lattices $Q_{c,\tau}$ of rank $N$, one for each class $c$ and each $\tau$. This section defines column valuations and proves that one exists whenever the column data are compatible and the stabilizers are trivial. The bound of Section 7 is proved for every column valuation, and the [maximal slope bound for families](/entry/slope-inequality-family-four/) applies it to the family of the proof.

### The data

We keep the setting of Section 4 at a word $u$, with $N = N_0$ the rank at level zero, and add the following.

- A CM field $E$ with complex conjugation $a \mapsto \bar a$, a finite set $\mathcal{T}$ of indices $\tau$, and a hermitian space $X_0$ over $E$ with hermitian form $h$. For each class $c \in \mathcal{C}$ there is a *class lattice* $\Lambda_c \subseteq X_0$, an $\mathcal{O}_E$-lattice.
- For a lattice $\Lambda \subseteq X_0$, its *dual* is $\Lambda^\vee = \{ x \in X_0 : h(y, x) \in \mathcal{O}_E \text{ for all } y \in \Lambda \}$. We say $\Lambda$ is *$q$-modular* (in the weak sense used here) if $\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda^\vee$ and the index $[\Lambda^\vee : \Lambda]$ is $q$.
- For each $\tau$ a dominant weight $\lambda_\tau$ of $\mathrm{GL}_N$, with its Weyl module $\Delta(\lambda_\tau)$, a space of polynomials in the entries of an $N \times N$ matrix $Z_\tau$. The space $\mathcal{P}$ of polynomials in all the entries of all the $Z_\tau$ contains the tensor products $\bigotimes_\tau p_\tau$ with $p_\tau \in \Delta(\lambda_\tau)$, and we write $\mathcal{P}_\lambda$ for their span over $K$.
- For a family $g = (g_\tau)$ of invertible matrices, the *twisted action* on $\mathcal{P}$ is
$$
(g \cdot p)(Z) = \prod_\tau \det(g_\tau)^{d_\tau}\; p\big( (g_\tau^{\mathsf{T}} Z_\tau)_\tau \big),
$$
where $d_\tau$ is the determinant shift of $\lambda_\tau$. The *normalized Fock form* on complex polynomials is $\langle p, q \rangle_\lambda = \langle p, q \rangle_{\mathrm{F}} / \prod_\tau \langle \xi_\tau, \xi_\tau \rangle_{\mathrm{F}}$, where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}$ is the Fock inner product and $\xi_\tau$ is the extremal vector of $\Delta(\lambda_\tau)$.
- For a finite place $w$ of $K$ with local ring $\mathcal{O}_w$, the *integral span* $\mathcal{P}_{\lambda, w}$ is the additive group generated by the elements $r \bigotimes_\tau p_\tau$ with $r \in \mathcal{O}_w$ and $p_\tau$ in the integral Weyl module over $\mathbb{Z}$.
- For a good hermitian lattice datum $Q = (V_Q, b_Q, X_Q, L_Q)$ of rank $N$ and a finite place $w$, a *local basis* at $w$ is a matrix $\gamma \in \mathrm{GL}_N(K)$ such that the vectors $\sum_i \gamma_{ia} b_{Q,i}$ for $a = 1, \dots, N$ span the localization $(L_Q)_w$ over $\mathcal{O}_w$.

**Definition 1.6.1 (Column valuation).** A *column valuation* at the word $u$ consists of:

1. a nonempty set of level-zero classes, and for each $\tau$ a ring homomorphism $\rho_\tau : E \to K$;
2. for each class $c$ and each $\tau$ a good hermitian lattice datum $Q_{c,\tau}$ of rank $N$ over $K$, and an additive map $\beta_{c,\tau} : X_0 \to V_{Q_{c,\tau}}$ with $\beta_{c,\tau}(a y) = \rho_\tau(a)\beta_{c,\tau}(y)$, such that $L_{Q_{c,\tau}}$ is the $\mathcal{O}_K$-span of $\beta_{c,\tau}(\Lambda_c)$ and
$$
\operatorname{Re} \langle \beta_{c,\tau} y, \beta_{c,\tau} y \rangle_v = \operatorname{Re} \sigma_v\big(\rho_\tau(h(y, y))\big) \qquad \text{for every infinite place } v;
$$
3. the class lattices $\Lambda_c$ are $T^{2f}$-modular;
4. an injective $K$-linear map $\kappa : V \to \mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{C}}$, the *coordinates*, whose image is the set of families $(p_c)$ with every $p_c \in \mathcal{P}_\lambda$;
5. the *finite condition*: for every finite place $w$ and every family $\gamma_{c,\tau}$ of local bases of $Q_{c,\tau}$ at $w$, an element $x \in V$ lies in $L_w$ if and only if $\gamma_c^{-1} \cdot \kappa(x)_c \in \mathcal{P}_{\lambda, w}$ for every class $c$;
6. the *infinite condition*: for every infinite place $v$ and every $x \in V$,
$$
\operatorname{Re} \langle x, x \rangle_v = \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \operatorname{Re} \big\langle X_v(Q_c)^{-1} \cdot \sigma_v \kappa(x)_c,\; X_v(Q_c)^{-1} \cdot \sigma_v \kappa(x)_c \big\rangle_\lambda ,
$$
where $X_v(Q_c)$ is the family of frames of the $Q_{c,\tau}$ at $v$ and $\sigma_v$ is applied to the coefficients.

So a column valuation writes the level-zero lattice as a lattice of polynomial functions, class by class: the finite condition says that integrality is integrality of coefficients after a change to local bases of the $Q_{c,\tau}$, and the infinite condition says that the metric is the Fock metric after a change to orthonormal frames of the $Q_{c,\tau}$. The $Q_{c,\tau}$ are the class lattices viewed over $K$ through $\rho_\tau$. This is the classical description of the integral structure of a space of vector-valued automorphic forms on a definite unitary group through its values on a set of representatives of the classes.

### Existence

The input is a datum of *compatible column data*: compatible forms at every word, in the sense of Section 4, together with a hermitian structure on the columns that agrees with them at the level prime and at level zero. We did not unfold this datum, and we use it only through the results cited in the proof.

**Theorem 1.6.2 (Existence of column valuations).** Let the column data be compatible, and assume that every level-zero class at every word has trivial stabilizer. Then at every word there is a column valuation.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.GlobalTheoryTheory.ColValS.colValOfTiedS_holds` (also `Formalism.L2.GlobalTheoryTheory.ColValS.colValOfTiedS_of_fin`, `Formalism.L2.GlobalTheoryTheory.ColValS.colRest_of_fin`, `Formalism.L2.GlobalTheoryTheory.ColValS.finCo_of`).

*Proof.* Fix a word $u$, and let $\rho_\tau$ be the homomorphisms attached to the column data. The hermitian space $X_0$ of the compatible data has an orthonormal basis, so for every $\sigma : E \to \mathbb{C}$ there is an invertible complex frame in which $\sigma(h(y, y))$ is a sum of squares of absolute values. From these frames one builds, for every class $c$ and every $\tau$, a good datum $Q_{c,\tau}$ whose space is $X_0$ viewed over $K$ through $\rho_\tau$ and whose lattice is spanned by $\beta_{c,\tau}(\Lambda_c)$; this gives item 2. The classes are nonempty, since the bijection between level-zero classes and double cosets of the compatible data hits the class of $1$. The class lattices are $T^{2f}$-modular. Each $\Lambda_c$ is the global lattice determined by an integral lattice of the compatible data and the local translate of the class, so it lies in its dual. The discriminant condition of the compatible data at the level prime identifies the quotient $\Lambda_c^\vee / \Lambda_c$ with a quotient of $\mathcal{O}_E$ of size $T^{2f}$.

The coordinates $\kappa$ come from the compatible forms at level zero: the value of a form on a class is a polynomial in the matrix entries. The map is injective. The description of its image uses the trivial stabilizers, since a class with a nontrivial stabilizer would impose invariance on the values. The infinite condition is the computation of the majorant in the orthonormal frames of the $Q_{c,\tau}$, and uses the trivial stabilizers and the nonempty classes.

For the finite condition, fix a finite place $w$ of residue characteristic $\ell$. Each class lattice $\Lambda_c$ is finitely generated and spans $X_0$, so it has a local basis $Y_c$ at $\ell$. Put $\gamma_{c,\tau} = M_{c,\tau}\, \rho_\tau(Y_c)$, where $M_{c,\tau}$ is the matrix of the basis of $Q_{c,\tau}$; this is a local basis of $Q_{c,\tau}$ at $w$, since $L_{Q_{c,\tau}}$ is spanned by $\beta_{c,\tau}(\Lambda_c)$. The local criterion of the compatible forms says that $x \in L_w$ if and only if, at each class, the value of $\varepsilon(x)$ written in the frame given by $Y_c$ has integral coefficients. A comparison of the two frames shows that this is the condition $\gamma_c^{-1} \cdot \kappa(x)_c \in \mathcal{P}_{\lambda, w}$ for this family $\gamma$. Finally, if the finite condition holds for one family of local bases it holds for all, because two local bases differ by an element of $\mathrm{GL}_N(\mathcal{O}_w)$, which preserves the integral span. $\square$

**Remark 1.6.3 (Formalization).** The theorem is a statement of existence about integral structures; no slope appears in it. The trivial stabilizers enter as a hypothesis for all words at once. The base datum exists for every choice of the homomorphisms $\rho_\tau$, and the proof uses it only for the choice made by the column data ([ColValOfTiedSOfRest.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GlobalTheory/ColValOfTiedSOfRest.lean#L45)). The formal proof first proves the finite condition for one family of local bases and then passes to all of them through a separate lemma ([ClassesLevelZeroNonempty.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GlobalTheory/ClassesLevelZeroNonempty.lean#L68)); we did not open that lemma, and the reason given in the last sentence of the proof is the classical one. The description of the coordinates and of the infinite condition follows the names and statements of the lemmas used ([ColValOfTiedSOfFin.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GlobalTheory/ColValOfTiedSOfFin.lean#L20)); we did not unfold the Fock norm or the Weyl modules.

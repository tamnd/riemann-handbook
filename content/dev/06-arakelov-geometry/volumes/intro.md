title: Introduction
---
This chapter proves the volume theorem for good towers. A root tower is a family of arithmetic varieties, one for each level $B$ and each component $c$, with hermitian lines indexed by the roots of a product of flag varieties. Its volume at $c$ is the geometric degree of the line of the half sum of positive roots, divided by the flag degree of that weight. The theorem says that, for a good tower over a global integral model of a unitary Shimura variety, the tower is proportional (the geometric degrees are the volume times the flag degree), all its volumes are equal, its twisted root lines are nef and semipositive near the boundary, its top intersection numbers have an asymptotic formula with error $C \log B / B$, and the mean of its slopes over the components is bounded below by $-C$ times a bracket in $\log(B\,\mathrm{rd}_E)$, $|L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)|$ and the spread of the weight. The proof joins about twenty tower estimates. The new arguments in the chapter are a bound for a sum over the primes of power sum remainders, a lower bound for the shift constant at the base level, the change of model formula for power sums along a Frobenius link, uniformization of the base by complex balls, the equality of ball volumes by the Landherr and Hasse norm theorems, and the tube mass bound for principal congruence subgroups.

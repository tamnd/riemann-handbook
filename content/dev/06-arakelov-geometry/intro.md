This volume proves the results of Arakelov geometry that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. It proves slope inequalities for hermitian lattices, the Chern-Weil theorems for smooth and good metrics, Mumford's proportionality theorem, the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel formula, Moriwaki bigness and results on heights of points and cycles. It then applies them to integral models of unitary Shimura varieties: a covolume bound for families, good towers, and the volume theorem for good towers. Volume 1 defines [hermitian lattices and slopes](/learn/arakelov/#hermitian-lattices-and-slopes), [hermitian line bundles](/learn/arakelov/#hermitian-line-bundles), [arithmetic intersection numbers](/learn/arakelov/#arithmetic-varieties-and-intersection-numbers), [heights](/learn/arakelov/#heights), [arithmetic volume](/learn/arakelov/#arithmetic-volume-and-bigness) and [Chern-Weil forms](/learn/positivity-resolution/#chern-weil-forms), and it states [Mumford's proportionality theorem](/learn/abelian-varieties/#mumfords-proportionality-theorem). The development took several of these theorems from the literature. This volume proves them.

The proof uses the volume in four places. The first is [the Weil bound](/proof/weil-bound/). The volume part of its Step 4 needs a lower bound for the mean slope with one constant along every tower, and Chapters 8 to 10 supply it. The second is the step [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/). It uses the covolume bound of Chapter 2, the intersection calculus of Chapter 4, the seminegativity of Lie algebras of Chapter 5, Moriwaki bigness of Chapter 6 and the positivity facts of Chapter 7. Chapters 2 and 3 prove [the covolume bound step](/step/covolume-bound-of-x-def/). The third is the step [published results in arithmetic geometry](/step/published-results-arithmetic/). There the theorem on good metrics of Chapter 5 and the Hilbert-Samuel formula and volume bound of Chapter 6 replace theorems from the literature. The fourth is [the theta matching](/proof/theta-matching/): Chapter 1 serves its step [ZeroFreeAll from Green, VWin and HypsAlt](/step/zero-free-all-of-green-v-win/).

Chapter 1 is about hermitian lattices over a totally complex field. Its slope is twice the slope of Volume 1. For the lattice of automorphic forms of level zero it proves the slope inequality, the ramified slope formula, [Theorem 1.5.4](/entry/slope-line-0/), and a bound for the maximal slope with the constant $4$ in front of a logarithm of the root discriminant, [Theorem 1.7.8](/entry/slope-inequality-constant-four/). For a level prime $T$ with $f$ places above it, the ramified sublattice has slope $\mu(A_0) - f \log(T-1)/(T-2)$. The last section proves the family statements, such as [Theorem 1.8.1](/entry/slope-lemma-division-side/).

Chapter 2 proves the covolume bound for a family of unitary Shimura data, from seven groups of hypotheses, [Theorem 2.9.1](/entry/top-covol-g-alt-alt/), and from fifteen results of the literature, [Theorem 2.9.2](/entry/top-covol-slim2-use-alt-alt/). The proof is a chain of estimates on the levels and good covers of a tower. Cusp forms of large weight separate unipotent orbits, [Theorem 2.2.4](/entry/separate-orbits-by-cusp/). At the last level the Lie degree is the negative of a flat degree, [Theorem 2.3.4](/entry/deg-lie-core-alt/). The chapter proves the low defect bound, [Theorem 2.4.4](/entry/covering-low-defect-alt/), and the height lower bound, [Theorem 2.8.6](/entry/height-lower-bound-general-alt/). Its inputs include a log canonical criterion, [Theorem 2.12.2](/entry/log-canonical-refl-of-boundary/), and birationality for large gaps, [Theorem 2.13.5](/entry/bir-on-of-weight-gaps-large/).

Chapter 3 proves the estimates that Chapter 2 quotes. The first are $p$-adic. At an integral point the formal group has Kodaira-Spencer coordinates, and at an unramified prime it splits into typed parts, [Theorem 3.1.5](/entry/formal-group-types/). This gives a bound for the oscillation at every prime, [Theorem 3.3.2](/entry/osc-le-e-of/), and for the reference exponent, [Theorem 3.4.7](/entry/ref-exp-le-of-osc-le/). The global estimates include a peak section, [Theorem 3.5.2](/entry/peak/), the Lie eigenlattice degree of a definite CM object, [Theorem 3.6.4](/entry/cm-diff-definite/), and the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel bound on a component, [Theorem 3.8.5](/entry/geo-bound-of-quoted-prime/). A Bertini theorem over number fields, [Corollary 3.9.6](/entry/hsshort-bertini-number-field-of-hilbert-samuel/), gives this bound in explicit form, [Theorem 3.12.5](/entry/geo-bound-of-abstract/).

Chapter 4 proves results on heights of points and cycles. It bounds the rest terms of the arithmetic volume formula over the primes, [Theorem 4.3.4](/entry/rest-bound-full/), and bounds theta functions, [Theorem 4.5.6](/entry/theta-bound-full/). Its algebraic inputs include Grothendieck's existence theorem, [Theorem 4.7.3](/entry/formal-thickening-formal-algebraisation/), and the numerical Segre formula, [Theorem 4.8.5](/entry/numerical-segre-full/). It proves the Poincaré-Lelong formula, [Theorem 4.12.4](/entry/poincare-lelong-of-leaves/), an absolute Siegel lemma, [Theorem 4.14.3](/entry/line-of-large-degree-from-z-of-open/), and the seven rules of the arithmetic intersection calculus, [Theorem 4.15.7](/entry/ar-calc-direct/).

Chapter 5 proves the Chern-Weil theorems. The integral of the top power of the first Chern form of a smooth weight is the self intersection number, [Theorem 5.1.8](/entry/chern-int-eq-top-self/). A fibre integral over the bundle of full flags gives the Chern numbers for smooth metrics, [Theorem 5.2.9](/entry/chern-weil-numbers-holds/), and a transgression extends them to good metrics, [Theorem 5.3.6](/entry/good-chern-weil/). Sub-bundles of the Lie algebra of a semi-abelian scheme over a curve have determinants of degree at most zero, [Theorem 5.4.9](/entry/semi-abelian-lie-seminegative/). On a model over a discrete valuation ring, lattice families satisfy the Chern number relations, [Theorem 5.5.8](/entry/ck-relations-of-model/), which reduce to [Theorem 5.6.6](/entry/stage5/).

Chapter 6 proves the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel formula for nef hermitian line bundles from principalisation of ideals alone. The route is the Poincaré-Lelong formula for a section, [Theorem 6.1.4](/entry/poincare-lelong-sec/), mixed heights, [Theorem 6.4.5](/entry/exists-is-mix-of-arch/), the intersection calculus, [Theorem 6.5.6](/entry/ar-calc-of-principalisation/), and the ample case, [Theorem 6.6.7](/entry/ample-hs-of-principalisation/). The nef case is [Theorem 6.7.5](/entry/arith-hilbert-samuel-of-principalisation/). The volume of a bundle nef on the fibres is at least its self intersection, [Theorem 6.8.6](/entry/vol-ge-of-hilbert-samuel/). A second proof in coordinates gives Moriwaki bigness and two bounds of Hilbert-Samuel type, [Theorem 6.10.3](/entry/hs-chain/).

Chapter 7 proves Mumford's proportionality theorem for a unitary Shimura variety. The Chern integrals of the Hodge metric are the covolume times the flag degrees, [Theorem 7.4.4](/entry/has-chern-integral-covol-flag-deg/), and the Hodge metric is good, [Theorem 7.6.4](/entry/lie-gram-is-good/). With Mumford's theorem on good metrics as a hypothesis, the top self-intersection of $L_\lambda$ on a toroidal flag space is the covolume times the flag degree of $\lambda$, [Theorem 7.7.9](/entry/toroidal-flag-proportionality-of-good-chern-weil/). The second part proves freeness of Hodge modules at every specialization, [Theorem 7.8.5](/entry/hodge-free-every-stalk/), Zarhin's trick, [Theorem 7.11.7](/entry/zarhin/), and Kleiman's positivity facts, [Theorem 7.12.10](/entry/k-twins/).

Chapter 8 builds good towers of integral models. It proves a slope bound for Hodge components, [Theorem 8.1.7](/entry/slope-unif/), the fibre input, [Theorem 8.3.2](/entry/fibre-input/), and the plurisubharmonicity of the Hodge weights at the cusps, [Theorem 8.5.2](/entry/relay-cusp-intersection-positive/). It computes the Chern numbers of the Hodge flag at a free prime, [Theorem 8.8.2](/entry/model-on-hodge-of-family-pos/). The final theorem says that, from a list of inputs, global integral models with split components have good towers and good towers satisfy the volume theorem, [Theorem 8.8.3](/entry/good-tower-volume-of-reduced/). The later sections give the deep statement, [Theorem 8.15.6](/entry/deep-st-r-of-volume/), and the flat degree at CM points, [Theorem 8.20.4](/entry/flat-degree-holds/).

Chapter 9 proves the volume theorem for good towers. A root tower has an arithmetic variety for each level $B$ and component, with hermitian lines indexed by roots. For a good tower the theorem gives proportionality, equal volumes, nef twisted root lines, top intersection numbers with error $C \log B / B$, and a lower bound for the mean slope. The proof joins about twenty tower estimates, [Theorem 9.5.1](/entry/chain-tower/). New inputs are the remainder over the primes, [Theorem 9.3.4](/entry/finite-bound/), uniformization by complex balls, [Theorem 9.11.7](/entry/base-unif-ex2/), equal ball volumes, [Theorem 9.12.3](/entry/ball-vol-eq/), and the mass of the components, [Theorem 9.14.7](/entry/plug-comp/). The theorem itself is [Theorem 9.13.4](/entry/good-tower-and-volume/).

Chapter 10 proves analytic and geometric inputs of the volume branch. Over all complex places, the mean of the logarithm of the sup norm of a theta section exceeds the mean $\Phi$ of the logarithms of its $L^2$ norms by at most $C g \log(2 + g + \Phi)$, [Theorem 10.2.2](/entry/mean-log-sup/). The chapter bounds the mean cusp weight on a fundamental set, [Theorem 10.3.2](/entry/fund-set-mean-cusp-w/), and proves convergence of mixed Monge-Ampère integrals off a divisor, [Theorem 10.5.4](/entry/mixed-integral-tendsto-of-c2-off-divisor/). From the fibre integral of the log-sine function of a square system, [Theorem 10.9.2](/entry/fibre-integral-normal/), it proves the swap relation, [Theorem 10.10.2](/entry/thm-a-swap/), and the antisymmetry of the height polynomial, [Theorem 10.10.4](/entry/antisymmetry/).

Chapter 1 uses no other chapter, and no chapter of the volume links to it. Chapter 3 feeds Chapter 2, Chapter 5 and the mass bound of Chapter 9. Chapter 2 uses Mumford proportionality from Chapter 7. Chapters 4 and 6 give two routes to the intersection calculus, and Chapter 6 takes its statements from Chapter 4. Chapter 5 feeds Chapter 7 through good metrics and Chapter 8 through the Chern number relations. Chapter 7 feeds Chapters 2, 8 and 9. Chapters 8 to 10 form one block. Chapter 8 quotes the volume theorem of Chapter 9, and Chapter 9 takes good towers from Chapter 8. Chapter 10 supplies its bounds to Chapters 8 and 9, and Chapter 4 supplies the remainder bound and the Siegel lemma to Chapter 9. So a reader can take Chapter 1 alone, then Chapters 3, 4, 6, 5, 7 and 2 in this order, and last Chapters 10, 8 and 9.

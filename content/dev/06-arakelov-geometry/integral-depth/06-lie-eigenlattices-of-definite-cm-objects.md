## Lie eigenlattices of definite CM objects

This section computes the degree of the Lie eigenlattices of an abelian scheme with CM by $E$ of definite signature $(s, 0)$, and from it the degree of a flat line on a component of the moduli space. The degree is $\tfrac{s}{2}$ times the real part of a logarithmic period $\log \Omega_E$, given by the Chowla-Selberg constant of $E$ ([the Chowla-Selberg formula](/learn/elliptic-curves/#the-chowla-selberg-formula)). The proof writes the abelian scheme as an image of $s$ copies of a CM abelian scheme of signature $(1, 0)$ and compares the eigenlattices through a self-dual hermitian lattice ([lattices over Dedekind domains](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#lattices-over-dedekind-domains)). The degree of the flat line is the Lie degree at the last level of [the previous chapter](/entry/deg-lie-core-alt/).

Let $E$ be a CM field with a host as in [the first section](/entry/formal-group-types/). The host is a *host field* if $E$ is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$, and for each prime $w$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$ that divides the different, $\bar w \ne w$, and each rational prime $\ell \in w$ satisfies $\ell \ne d$ with $-d$ a square modulo $\ell$, or $\ell = d$ with $w^3 \nmid \ell$. Let $\chi$ be the Kronecker character of $K_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, a Dirichlet character modulo $d$. Put
$$\log \Omega_E = \gamma + \log \frac{2\pi}{\mathrm{rd}(E)} - \frac{L'(1, \chi)}{L(1, \chi)},$$
where $\gamma$ is Euler's constant and $\mathrm{rd}(E)$ the root discriminant of $E$.

**Definition 3.6.1 (CM object).** Let $K$ be a number field in which $E$ splits, $\rho \in \mathcal{O}_K$ with $\rho^2 = -d$, $\sigma_1$ a complex embedding of $K$, and $s, t \in \mathbb{N}$. A *CM object* of signature $(s, t)$ over $K$ consists of polarized abelian schemes $A_+$, $A_-$ and $B$ over $\mathcal{O}_K$ with $\mathcal{O}_E$-action, of types $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$ and $(s, t)$ with respect to $\rho$, elements $h_j \in \mathcal{O}_E$ and $\mathcal{O}_E$-linear homomorphisms $\varphi_j \colon A_\pm \to B$ for $j$ in $\{1, \dots, s\} \sqcup \{1, \dots, t\}$ (from $A_+$ for the first $s$ indices and from $A_-$ for the last $t$), such that every point of $B$ over $\sigma_1$ is a sum of images of points under the $\varphi_j$, and the polarization of $B$ pulls back under $\varphi_j$ to the polarization of the source twisted by $h_j$.

For a generic fibre $\mathcal{F}$ of an abelian scheme with $\mathcal{O}_E$-action and a ring map $\tau \colon \mathcal{O}_E \to \mathcal{O}_K$, the *eigenlattice* $L_\tau(\mathcal{F})$ is the $\mathcal{O}_K$-lattice of vectors in the Lie algebra on which $\mathcal{O}_E$ acts through $\tau$. A hermitian family $h$ on the Lie algebra gives each eigenlattice a degree $\deg_h$ ([hermitian lattices and slopes](/learn/arakelov/#hermitian-lattices-and-slopes)). For tangent maps $M_a$ of $\varphi_a$ from a generic fibre $\mathcal{F}_+$ of $A_+$ to a generic fibre $\mathcal{F}$ of $B$, let $N_\tau$ be the sublattice of $L_\tau(\mathcal{F})$ spanned by the images $M_a(L_\tau(\mathcal{F}_+))$. *Gram matrices* $g_+$ and $g_-$ of sizes $s$ and $t$ with entries in $\mathcal{O}_E$ describe the polarization of $B$ in terms of the $\varphi_j$; we do not unfold this condition, nor the condition that $h$ is the hermitian family of $\mathcal{F}$.

**Lemma 3.6.2 (A self-dual lattice of CM homomorphisms).** Let the host be a host field, and let $X$ be a CM object of signature $(s, 0)$ over $K$ with Gram matrices $g_+$, $g_-$. There is a finitely generated $\mathcal{O}_E$-submodule $\Lambda \subseteq E^s$ with:

1. $\mathcal{O}_E^s \subseteq \Lambda$;
2. $\Lambda$ is self-dual for the form $\langle c, d \rangle = \sum_{a,b} c_a\, g_{+,ab}\, \bar d_b$: a vector $d$ lies in $\Lambda$ if and only if $\langle c, d \rangle \in \mathcal{O}_E$ for all $c \in \Lambda$;
3. for generic fibres $\mathcal{F}_+$ of $A_+$ and $\mathcal{F}$ of $B$, tangent maps $M_a$ of $\varphi_a$, $\tau$ with $\tau(\mathrm{rt}) = \rho$ and its extension $\tau_E \colon E \to K$,
$$L_\tau(\mathcal{F}) = \operatorname{span}_{\mathcal{O}_K}\Bigl\{\sum_a \tau_E(c_a)\, M_a(e) : c \in \Lambda,\ e \in L_\tau(\mathcal{F}_+)\Bigr\}.$$

*Proof.* Let $\Lambda$ be the coordinate lattice of the $\mathcal{O}_E$-linear homomorphisms $A_+^s \to B$: the vectors $c \in E^s$ for which $\sum_a c_a \varphi_a$ is a homomorphism. Inputs give that $\Lambda$ is finitely generated, that it contains $\mathcal{O}_E^s$, that the Gram pairing is integral on $\Lambda$, and that a vector with integral pairing against $\Lambda$ lies in $\Lambda$. For part 3, the inclusion $\subseteq$ is an input on the eigenlattice of the image. For $\supseteq$, induct on the span: a generator lies in $L_\tau(\mathcal{F})$ because $\sum_a c_a \varphi_a$ is a homomorphism and preserves the $\tau$-part, sums are clear, and for $x \in \mathcal{O}_K$ one has $x \cdot e \in L_\tau(\mathcal{F}_+)$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.6.3 (The index ideal).** In the setting of the previous lemma there is an ideal $\mathfrak{n}$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$ with $\mathfrak{n}\, \bar{\mathfrak{n}} = (\det g_+)$, such that for all $\mathcal{F}_+$, $\mathcal{F}$, tangent maps $M_a$ and $\tau$ with $\tau(\mathrm{rt}) = \rho$, the index ideal of $N_\tau$ in $L_\tau(\mathcal{F})$ is $\tau(\mathfrak{n})\, \mathcal{O}_K$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.FlatCM.IdealZero.idealSt_zero` (also `GlobalTheory.T.cmDefinite_lieIndex_ideal_eq_norm_ideal`, a second name).

*Proof.* Take $\Lambda$ from the previous lemma and let $\mathfrak{n}$ be the index ideal of $\mathcal{O}_E^s$ in $\Lambda$. Self-duality of $\Lambda$ for the form of $g_+$ gives $\mathfrak{n}\bar{\mathfrak{n}} = (\det g_+)$. For the index of $N_\tau$, a lemma on independence, an input, says that the maps $M_a$ are injective on $L_\tau(\mathcal{F}_+)$ with independent images. Since $A_+$ has type $(1, 0)$, the eigenlattice $L_\tau(\mathcal{F}_+)$ has rank $1$, by the eigen decomposition. So $N_\tau$ is the image of the rank one tensor $\mathcal{O}_E^s \otimes L_\tau(\mathcal{F}_+)$ and $L_\tau(\mathcal{F})$ is the image of $\Lambda \otimes L_\tau(\mathcal{F}_+)$, by the previous lemma. The index of such images is the image of $\mathfrak{n}$ under $\tau$. The fibre of $A_-$ that the inputs need is chosen arbitrarily, and the maps from it are empty, since $t = 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 3.6.4 (Degree of the Lie eigenlattices in the definite case).** Let the host be a host field, $\chi$ the Kronecker character, $X$ a CM object of signature $(s, t)$ over $K$ with $t = 0$, $\mathcal{F}$ a generic fibre of $B$ with hermitian family $h$, and $\tau \colon \mathcal{O}_E \to \mathcal{O}_K$ with $\tau(\mathrm{rt}) = \rho$. Then
$$\deg_h L_\tau(\mathcal{F}) - \deg_h L_{\tau \circ \bar{\ }}(\mathcal{F}) = \frac{s - t}{2}\, \operatorname{Re} \log \Omega_E.$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.FlatCore.CMDiff0.cmDiff_definite` (also `GlobalTheory.T.cmObject_definite_lieEigenLattice_degree_difference`, a second name).

*Proof.* Take Gram matrices $g_+$, $g_-$ of $X$ and $\mathfrak{n}$ from the lemma on index ideals. Choose generic fibres $\mathcal{F}_\pm$ of $A_\pm$ and tangent maps $M_j$ of the $\varphi_j$. Write $\bar\tau = \tau \circ \bar{\ }$. Then $\bar\tau(\mathrm{rt}) = -\rho \ne \rho$, since $\rho \ne 0$. The index of $N_\tau$ in $L_\tau(\mathcal{F})$ is $\tau(\mathfrak{n})$. The index of the image of the zero maps in $L_{\bar\tau}(\mathcal{F})$ is the unit ideal, since an index in rank $0$ is the unit ideal and $\det g_- = 1$. A lemma on the degrees of the images, an input, gives the degrees of $N_\tau$ and of the image at $\bar\tau$ in terms of $\operatorname{Re} \log \Omega_E$ and the norms of $\det g_\pm$. The lemma on independence and the eigen decomposition give the ranks: $s$ at $\tau$ and $0$ at $\bar\tau$. The degree of a lattice is the degree of a sublattice of the same rank plus the logarithm of the norm of the index divided by $[K : \mathbb{Q}]$. Taking norms in $\mathfrak{n}\bar{\mathfrak{n}} = (\det g_+)$ gives
$$\frac{\log N(\tau(\mathfrak{n}))}{[K : \mathbb{Q}]} = \frac{\log N(\det g_+)}{2[E : \mathbb{Q}]},$$
and the terms with $\det g_\pm$ cancel against those of the degree lemma. $\square$

Now let $X$ be the moduli space of a datum of signature $(s, t)$ over its coefficient field $F$, with uniformizations $U$ at level $r$ that satisfy the laws for a parameter $x$. For a component $Z$, $\tau \in \Psi$ and an archimedean section $\sigma$ of the flat weight of $\tau$ on $Z$, a function $\rho$ on the finite places of $F$ gives *constant valuations* if every valuation of $\sigma$ on $Z$ at a place $\lambda$ equals $\rho(\lambda)$. Then the *line degree* $\deg(\sigma, \rho)$ is the adelic degree of the line with the archimedean metric of $\sigma$ and the valuations $\rho$ ([heights](/learn/arakelov/#heights)). The *frame degree* condition with a number $\omega$ says: for all $Z$, $\tau$, $\sigma$ and $\rho$ with constant valuations, $\rho$ has finite support and
$$\deg(\sigma, \rho) = \frac{s - t}{2}\, \omega.$$

**Theorem 3.6.5 (Flat line degree as a difference of eigenlattice degrees).** Assume the laws. Let $Z$, $\tau \in \Psi$, $\sigma$ and $\rho$ be as above with constant valuations. Let $K$ be a number field over $F$ and $y$ a $K$-point of the moduli space on $Z$. Let $B$ be a polarized abelian scheme over $\mathcal{O}_K$ with $\mathcal{O}_E$-action, and let a semi-abelian scheme over $K$ map to both the universal object at $y$ and to $B$. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a generic fibre of $B$ with hermitian family $h$, and $\theta \colon \mathcal{O}_E \to \mathcal{O}_K$ the map through the embedding of $\tau$ into $F$ and $F \to K$. Then $\rho$ has finite support and
$$\deg(\sigma, \rho) = \deg_h L_\theta(\mathcal{F}) - \deg_h L_{\theta \circ \bar{\ }}(\mathcal{F}).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.PtDeg.lineDeg_eq_deg_eigLat_sub` (also `GlobalTheory.T.flatLineDegree_eq_eigenLattice_degree_sub`, a second name).

*Proof.* A valuation sum lemma, an input, gives for each prime $\mathfrak{P}$ of $\mathcal{O}_K$ the prime $\pi(\mathfrak{P})$ of $F$ below it and its ramification index $\epsilon(\mathfrak{P})$, with $\log N(\mathfrak{P})$ expressed through them. The map $\theta \circ \bar{\ }$ is the map of the conjugate embedding. Inputs give bases $u$ and $u'$ of $L_\theta(\mathcal{F})$ and $L_{\theta \circ \bar{\ }}(\mathcal{F})$ in frame coordinates, and show that at each $\mathfrak{P}$ the valuation of the flat section in these bases is $\epsilon(\mathfrak{P})\, \rho(\pi(\mathfrak{P}))$; in particular $\rho$ has finite support. The flat section does not vanish at $y$. A formula for the degree of the determinant line of two hermitian lattices, an input, expresses $\deg_h L_\theta - \deg_h L_{\theta \circ \bar{\ }}$ by these valuations and by the archimedean norm of the section in the bases $u$, $u'$. The archimedean norm is the frame norm of $\sigma$ at each complex embedding. Finally, the adelic degree of a line does not change under the finite extension $K/F$, which identifies the result with $\deg(\sigma, \rho)$. $\square$

**Theorem 3.6.6 (The frame degree in the definite case).** Assume that the degree theorem for definite CM objects holds. Let the host be a host field, $\chi$ its Kronecker character, and assume the laws for $U$ and $x$, and $t = 0$. Then the frame degree condition holds with $\omega = \operatorname{Re} \log \Omega_E$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.DefCoreLie.frameDeg_def`

*Proof.* Let $Z$, $\tau$, $\sigma$ and $\rho$ be given. An input gives a number field $K$, a $K$-point $y$ on $Z$ with CM good reduction, a CM object $X$ over $K$ of the signature of the datum, with a map from a semi-abelian scheme to the universal object at $y$ and to $B$, and $\rho_0 \in \mathcal{O}_K$ with $\tau'(\mathrm{rt}) = \rho_0$ for the embeddings $\tau'$ through $F$. Take a generic fibre $\mathcal{F}$ of $B$ and its unique hermitian family $h$. Let $\theta \colon \mathcal{O}_E \to \mathcal{O}_K$ be the map through $\tau$ and $F \to K$; it is integral, so it lands in $\mathcal{O}_K$, and $\theta(\mathrm{rt}) = \rho_0$. The theorem on flat line degrees gives finite support and $\deg(\sigma, \rho) = \deg_h L_\theta - \deg_h L_{\theta \circ \bar{\ }}$, and the assumed theorem with $t = 0$ gives the value $\tfrac{s - t}{2} \operatorname{Re} \log \Omega_E$. $\square$

**Remark 3.6.7 (Formalization).** The theorem on frame degrees takes the degree theorem for definite CM objects as a hypothesis instead of citing it ([ComponentPointLastIndex.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ComponentPointLastIndex.lean#L23)). The same file proves the Lie degree at the last level of the previous chapter, which supplies the theorem as this hypothesis (line 47); the file name does not describe either result. The Gram matrix $g_-$ of size $0$ is an argument of both lemmas but is never used ([CmDefiniteLieIndexIdealEqNormIdeal.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/CmDefiniteLieIndexIdealEqNormIdeal.lean#L20), [ExistsSelfDualLatticeCmHomomorphismsDefinite.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsSelfDualLatticeCmHomomorphismsDefinite.lean#L15)). In the degree theorem $t$ is a variable with the hypothesis $t = 0$, which the proof substitutes at once; the term at $\bar\tau$ is handled by an index lemma in rank $0$ (line 11 of [CmObjectDefiniteLieEigenLatticeDegreeDifference.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/CmObjectDefiniteLieEigenLatticeDegreeDifference.lean#L11)). The coordinate lattice of CM homomorphisms, the lemmas on independence, eigen decompositions, degrees of images, sublattice degrees, frame coordinate bases, constant valuations, determinant lines and adelic degrees, and the existence of CM points of good reduction are known to us by their statements. We have not unfolded the abelian schemes, their types, generic fibres, Gram matrices, hermitian families, archimedean sections or the laws.

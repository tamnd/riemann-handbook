## Depths along Kodaira-Spencer frames

This section defines the depth of a polynomial along a frame of a formal group, the depth of a factor at an integral point of the moduli space, and the oscillation of these depths at a prime. It then proves that at a prime $p$ that is unramified in the CM field, the formal group of an integral chart splits into typed parts: on each part the roots of unity of order $p^h - 1$ act through powers of Frobenius, and the factor depth can be computed on the part. The depth bound at odd inert primes of [the next section](/entry/fac-between-fine/) rests on this splitting, and the oscillation bound at every prime of [the section after it](/entry/osc-le-e-of/) collects the bounds.

Throughout the chapter, $E$ is a CM field with a host: a prime $d$, an element $\mathrm{rt} \in \mathcal{O}_E$ with $\mathrm{rt}^2 = -d$, the complex conjugation $a \mapsto \bar a$ of $E$, and a fixed complex number $\mathrm{rt}_0$ with $\mathrm{rt}_0^2 = -d$. The CM type is the set $\Psi$ of complex embeddings $\tau$ of $E$ with $\tau(\mathrm{rt}) = \mathrm{rt}_0$; it has one embedding from each conjugate pair, so $2|\Psi| \le [E : \mathbb{Q}]$. Let $K_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d}) \subseteq E$ ([kinds of number fields](/learn/number-fields/#kinds-of-number-fields)). A datum has a signature $(s, t)$ with $N = s + t$, and the multiplicity of $\tau$ is $\mathrm{mult}(\tau) = s$ if $\tau \in \Psi$ and $\mathrm{mult}(\tau) = t$ if not. (The previous chapter writes $(p_i, q_i)$ for the signature; here $p$ is a prime.) A *factor* is a pair $f = (\tau, j)$ with $j < \mathrm{mult}(\tau)$, and its *rank* is $\mathrm{rk}(f) = j + 1$. Let $X$ be the moduli space of the datum over its coefficient field $F$.

**Definition 3.1.1 (Logarithmic formal group).** Let $C$ be a field of characteristic $0$ with an additive valuation $v \colon C \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$. A power series over $C$ is *integral* if all its coefficients have $v \ge 0$. A *logarithmic formal group* $G$ of dimension $d$ over $(C, v)$ is a family of power series $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_d$ in $X_1, \dots, X_d$ with zero constant term and linear part $X_a$, the *logarithm*, together with integral power series $F_1, \dots, F_d$ in $2d$ variables with zero constant term such that $\lambda(F(X, Y)) = \lambda(X) + \lambda(Y)$. We write $\exp_G$ for the inverse of $\lambda$.

So $F$ is a formal group law over the valuation ring whose logarithm is $\lambda$ ([formal group laws](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws), [the logarithm](/learn/formal-groups/#the-invariant-derivation-and-the-logarithm)). An *integral homomorphism* $G' \to G$ with linear part $M$ is a family of integral power series $\varphi$ with zero constant term that is compatible with the logarithms through the matrix $M$.

**Definition 3.1.2 (Depth).** For a polynomial $P = \sum_\alpha P_\alpha X^\alpha$ and a power series $g$ in the same variables put
$$\partial_P(g) = \sum_\alpha P_\alpha\, \alpha!\, g_\alpha,$$
the value at $0$ of the differential operator $P(\partial)$ applied to $g$. The *content* of $P$ is $\min_\alpha v(P_\alpha\, \alpha!)$. Let $G_b$ be logarithmic formal groups of dimension $d_b$, for $b$ in a finite set, and let $u_{b,a} \in C^{d_b}$ be vectors. The *exponential along $u$* is the family of power series $\exp_{G_b}\bigl(\sum_a u_{b,a} X_{b,a}\bigr)$ in the variables $X_{b,a}$. The *depth* of a polynomial $P$ in the variables $X_{b,a}$ along $u$ is
$$\delta(P) = \inf_f v\bigl(\partial_P(f \circ \exp_u)\bigr),$$
over all integral power series $f$ in the coordinates of the groups $G_b$.

For $i \ge 1$ and $c \ge 0$ let $D_{i,c} = \det(X_{b,a})_{a,b \le i}^{\,c}$. For a group $G$ of dimension $d$ and vectors $u_1, \dots, u_i \in C^d$, the *determinant depth* $\delta_c(G, u)$ is the depth of $D_{i,c}$ along $i$ copies of $G$, with the vectors $u$ in each copy. The family $u$ is a *saturated frame* if all its entries are integral and some $i \times i$ minor of it has valuation $0$.

For example, let $G$ be the additive group of dimension $1$ ($\lambda = X$) and $i = 1$, $u_1 = 1$. Then $f \circ \exp_u = f$ and $\partial_{X^c}(f) = c!\, f_c$, so $\delta_c(G, u) = v(c!)$. If $v(p) = 1$, this is about $c/(p - 1)$. The bounds of the next sections have this shape.

**Definition 3.1.3 (Factor depth).** Let $T$ be a complete discrete valuation ring with an embedding of $F$ into its fraction field, $y$ an integral point of $X$ over $T$, and $\mathrm{Ch}$ an integral chart at $y$. A *valued extension* of $T$ over $p$ is a field $C$ with an additive valuation $v$ that extends the valuation of $T$, normalized by $v(p) = 1$. The chart gives a logarithmic formal group $G_{\mathrm{Ch}}$ over $C$ and *Lie vectors* $\ell_{\tau,j} \in C^d$, $j < \mathrm{mult}(\tau)$: the Kodaira-Spencer coordinates of the $\tau$-part of the Lie algebra. The *frame* of a factor $f = (\tau, j)$ is $u_f = (\ell_{\tau,0}, \dots, \ell_{\tau,j})$. Put
$$\nu_f^{\mathrm{Ch}, C}(k) = \delta_k(G_{\mathrm{Ch}}, u_f),$$
and let $\nu_f(y, k)$ be the infimum of these numbers over all integral charts at $y$ and all valued extensions. For a function $c$ from factors to $\mathbb{N}$, the *depth* of $y$ at $p$ is $\nu_p(y, c) = \sum_f \nu_f(y, c(f))$.

**Definition 3.1.4 (Depth bounds and oscillation).** Let $x$ be a point of the flag model at $p$, and say that $y$ lies over $x$ if $T$ lies over $x$. A factor $f$ is *between $A$ and $B$ at $x$* for $k$ if $A \le \nu_f(y, k)$ for all integral points $y$ over $x$, and $\nu_f(y, k) \le B$ if also $y$ is algebraic. In the same way $\nu_p(\cdot, c)$ is *between $A$ and $B$ at $x$*. The *oscillation at $p$ is at most $b$* for $c$ if for every point $x$ of the flag model at $p$ there is $A \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $\nu_p(\cdot, c)$ is between $A$ and $A + b$ at $x$.

If the oscillation is at most $b$ and $b \le b'$, it is at most $b'$. This is the only rule we use for oscillations besides the definition.

Now let $p$ be a prime that is *unramified* in $E$: $p \nmid \mathrm{disc}(E)$. Then $p \nmid d$, since the discriminant $-d$ of $K_0$ divides $\mathrm{disc}(E)$. For a valued extension $C$ and an embedding $\sigma$ of $E$ let $\iota_\sigma \colon \mathcal{O}_E \to C$ be the map through $\sigma$ and $F$, and let $w(\sigma)$ be the prime of $\mathcal{O}_E$ of elements $a$ with $v(\iota_\sigma(a)) > 0$. An embedding $\sigma'$ is a *Frobenius* of $\sigma$ if $v\bigl(\iota_{\sigma'}(a) - \iota_\sigma(a)^p\bigr) > 0$ for all $a \in \mathcal{O}_E$. We say $p$ is *split* in $K_0$ if $d \not\equiv 0$ and $-d$ is a square modulo $p$, and *inert* if $-d$ is not a square modulo $p$. The prime $2$ is split, since $-d \equiv 1 \pmod 2$.

**Theorem 3.1.5 (Typed parts at an unramified prime).** Let $p$ be unramified in $E$, $T$ and $C$ as above. Then:

1. if $\sigma'$ is a Frobenius of $\sigma$ and $p$ is split in $K_0$, then $\sigma' \in \Psi$ if and only if $\sigma \in \Psi$; if $p$ is inert in $K_0$, then $\sigma' \in \Psi$ if and only if $\bar\sigma \in \Psi$;
2. for every integral point $y$, every integral chart $\mathrm{Ch}$ at $y$ and every embedding $\tau$, the chart has a typed part at $\tau$ in the sense of the next lemma.

**Lemma 3.1.6 (Typed part).** Let $p$, $T$, $C$, $y$ and $\mathrm{Ch}$ be as above, $\tau$ an embedding and $w = w(\tau)$. Then there exist:

1. $h > 0$ with $|\mathcal{O}_E / w| = p^h$;
2. a map $r \mapsto \sigma_r$ from $\mathbb{Z}/h$ to the embeddings, injective, with $\sigma_0 = \tau$ and $\sigma_{r+1}$ a Frobenius of $\sigma_r$, whose image is the set of $\sigma$ with $w(\sigma) = w$;
3. a logarithmic formal group $G'$ of dimension $d'$ over $C$, a type $\mathrm{ty}(b) \in \mathbb{Z}/h$ for each coordinate $b$ of $G'$, and integral homomorphisms $G' \to G_{\mathrm{Ch}}$ and $G_{\mathrm{Ch}} \to G'$ with linear parts $I$ and $P$, such that $PI = 1$ and $IP$ fixes each Lie vector $\ell_{\sigma,j}$ with $w(\sigma) = w$ and kills the others;
4. for each $z \in C$ with $z^{p^h - 1} = 1$, integral power series $[z]$ with zero constant term such that $\lambda'_b \circ [z] = z^{p^{\mathrm{ty}(b)}} \lambda'_b$, where $\lambda'$ is the logarithm of $G'$;

such that for each $r \in \mathbb{Z}/h$ the integral span of the columns $b$ of $I$ with $\mathrm{ty}(b) = r$ is the integral span of the Lie vectors $\ell_{\sigma_r, j}$, the number of $b$ with $\mathrm{ty}(b) = r$ is $s$ if $\sigma_r \in \Psi$ and $t$ if not, and for each factor $f$ over $\sigma_r$ the frame $P u_f$ is saturated, vanishes in the coordinates of type other than $r$, and satisfies
$$\nu_f^{\mathrm{Ch}, C}(k) = \delta_k(G', P u_f) \quad \text{for all } k.$$

*Proof.* Since $p$ is unramified, the completion $E_w$ is unramified of degree $h$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with residue field $\mathcal{O}_E/w$ of $p^h$ elements ([the splitting of primes](/learn/number-fields/#the-splitting-of-primes), [completions](/learn/number-fields/#places-and-completions)). The embeddings $\sigma$ with $w(\sigma) = w$ are the embeddings of $E_w$ into $C$, and these are the $h$ Frobenius twists of $\tau$. This gives the orbit $\sigma_r$. Take an integral basis of the span of the Lie vectors $\ell_{\sigma_r, j}$. Let $z^{p^h - 1} = 1$. The ring $\mathcal{O}_E$ acts on $G_{\mathrm{Ch}}$, and $a$ acts on $\ell_{\sigma, j}$ by $\iota_\sigma(a)$. For each precision there are integers $a$ of $E$ with $\iota_{\sigma_r}(a)$ close to $z^{p^r}$ for all $r$, and the endomorphisms $[a]$ converge to an integral endomorphism of $G_{\mathrm{Ch}}$ that acts on the $\sigma_r$-directions by $z^{p^r}$. In the same way, integers that approximate the idempotent of $w$ in $\mathcal{O}_E \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p$ give an integral idempotent endomorphism. Its image is a logarithmic formal group $G'$, with maps $I$ and $P$ as in part 3, and the roots of unity act on it as in part 4, where the type of a coordinate is the $r$ of its direction. The coordinates of type $r$ correspond to the Lie vectors of $\sigma_r$, and there are $\mathrm{mult}(\sigma_r)$ of them. For a factor $f$ over $\sigma_r$ we have $u_f = IP u_f$, since $IP$ fixes the Lie vectors over $w$. Integral homomorphisms do not decrease the determinant depth, so the maps $P$ and $I$ give the two inequalities between $\delta_k(G_{\mathrm{Ch}}, u_f)$ and $\delta_k(G', P u_f)$. The frame $P u_f$ is saturated because $u_f$ is a frame of Lie vectors and $P$ is an isomorphism on their span. $\square$

*Proof of the theorem.* Part 2 is the lemma. For part 1, let $\mathcal{O}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{O}_E$ be the ring of integers of $K_0$. Whether $\sigma \in \Psi$ depends only on $\sigma$ on $K_0$. The two embeddings of $\mathcal{O}_0$ into $C$ differ modulo the maximal ideal, because their values on $\mathrm{rt}$ differ by $2\mathrm{rt}_0$ and $p$ is unramified in $K_0$ (for $p = 2$ use $(1 + \mathrm{rt})/2 \in \mathcal{O}_0$, whose two values differ by $\mathrm{rt}_0$, a unit). If $p$ is split, the residue field of $\mathcal{O}_0$ at $w(\sigma)$ is $\mathbb{F}_p$, so $\iota_\sigma(a)^p \equiv \iota_\sigma(a)$ for $a \in \mathcal{O}_0$, and $\sigma'$ agrees with $\sigma$ on $K_0$. If $p$ is inert, the residue field is $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ and the $p$-th power is its conjugation, so $\sigma'$ agrees with $\bar\sigma$ on $K_0$. $\square$

**Remark 3.1.7 (Formalization).** The theorem asks that the star of $\mathcal{O}_E$ is the conjugation of the host; the callers pass this as `fun _ => rfl` ([FactorDepthBoundedOddInertPrimeCmType.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/FactorDepthBoundedOddInertPrimeCmType.lean#L41)). The count of coordinates of type $r$ is written as $\mathrm{rank}(\Lambda) - t$ for $\sigma_r \in \Psi$, and a sanity example after the definition checks that this is $s$ ([TypedPartSplitPrimes.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/DivisorBase/TypedPartSplitPrimes.lean#L112)); another example there checks that $2$ is split. Part 1 is quoted from a Frobenius lemma whose proof we did not open; the argument above is ours. The steps of the lemma (the orbit, the approximation of the root of unity twists by integers, the limit of integral homomorphisms, the splitting by an idempotent and the comparison of depths under integral homomorphisms) are lemmas that we know by their statements. We have not unfolded the integral charts, their formal groups, their Lie vectors, the points of the flag model, the relation "lies over" or algebraic points.

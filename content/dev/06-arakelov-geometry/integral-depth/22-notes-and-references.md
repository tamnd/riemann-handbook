## Notes and references

The chapter proves local and lattice estimates that the chapter on canonical forms near the boundary quotes, and the inputs behind them. Sections 1 to 4 are $p$-adic: depths along Kodaira-Spencer frames of formal groups, the splitting into [typed parts](/entry/typed-part-at/) at unramified primes, the window at odd inert primes, the bound for the oscillation at every prime and its prime sum, and the bound of [reference exponents](/entry/sum-at-of-sizes/) by oscillations. Sections 5 to 8 are global: peak sections on the thick part, the degree of Lie eigenlattices of definite CM objects, the slope of eigenlattices of cusp forms in the definite case, and [the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel bound on a component](/entry/geo-bound-of-quoted-prime/). Sections 9 to 12 prove the geometric part of that bound: [the Bertini theorem](/entry/bertini/), the flat structures at good sections, the slope bound by the determinant method, and the assembly. Sections 13 to 21 prove further inputs of the boundary chapter: the separation of a fibre by adjoint sections, the canonical bundle of a toroidal component and its identification off the boundary, [the flat section of the Lie determinant line](/entry/exists-of-snc/), [the Riemannian Haar measure](/entry/riem-haar-eq-smul/) and the archimedean mass bound, the adelic probability measure and the equality of covolumes, the determinants of eigenlattices over a valuation ring, and the integrality of the theta key scalar.

The formal groups of Sections 1 to 4 are the formal completions of the universal abelian scheme along integral points, with the Lie algebra and its eigenparts under the CM action ([formal group laws](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws), [the logarithm](/learn/formal-groups/#the-invariant-derivation-and-the-logarithm)). The decomposition into typed parts at an unramified prime is the decomposition of a module over $\mathcal{O}_E \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p$ by the action of the roots of unity of order prime to $p$, which act through powers of Frobenius ([the splitting of primes](/learn/number-fields/#the-splitting-of-primes)). The depth and oscillation invariants, and the bound of reference exponents by oscillations, are particular to this development; we do not know a published source for them.

The peak sections of Section 5 use the $L^2$ method for the $\bar\partial$ operator of Hörmander, followed by a Poincaré series ([$L^2$ estimates](/learn/positivity-resolution/#l2-estimates)). The degree of the Lie eigenlattices in Section 6 is given by the logarithmic period of the Chowla-Selberg formula, which expresses the periods of CM elliptic curves by values of the gamma function ([the Chowla-Selberg formula](/learn/elliptic-curves/#the-chowla-selberg-formula)). The slopes of Sections 7, 8 and 11 are slopes of [hermitian lattices](/learn/arakelov/#hermitian-lattices-and-slopes).

The arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel theorem of Sections 8 to 12 is due to Gillet and Soulé, as a consequence of their arithmetic Riemann-Roch theorem ([arithmetic intersection numbers](/learn/arakelov/#arithmetic-varieties-and-intersection-numbers)). Abbes and Bouche gave a proof by Bergman kernels, Zhang proved a form for ample line bundles with semipositive metrics, and arithmetic volumes are studied by Yuan and by Moriwaki ([arithmetic volume and bigness](/learn/arakelov/#arithmetic-volume-and-bigness)). The bound of the chapter is a lower bound for the slope of the lattice of sections, and the proof is different. It cuts the image by $D$ general hyperplanes, filters the sections by their order of vanishing along the cut, and compares the determinant of a basis of products with a power of the Chow form. The heights of Chow forms are those of Bost, Gillet and Soulé ([heights](/learn/arakelov/#heights)). The Bertini theorem of Section 9 is the classical statement that a general linear section of a variety of degree $\delta$ and dimension $D$ is $\delta$ reduced points, in a form with an explicit exceptional hypersurface, so that a good section exists over a number field.

Section 13 uses Nadel vanishing for multiplier ideals and the point separation theorem of Angehrn and Siu ([vanishing theorems and resolution](/learn/geometry/#vanishing-theorems-and-resolution), [point separation](/learn/geometry/#point-separation)). Angehrn and Siu prove that adjoint bundles $K_X + L$ separate points when the intersection numbers of $L$ are large. The section uses the same method for the points of a fibre of a finite map, one of them very general, with $L$ a multiple of a nef and big bundle pulled back from the base. Smooth modifications exist by Hironaka's resolution of singularities ([resolution of singularities](/learn/geometry/#resolution-of-singularities)).

Sections 14 to 16 rest on toroidal compactifications: of Faltings and Chai in the Siegel case and of Lan for PEL type, with the logarithmic Kodaira-Spencer isomorphism ([toroidal compactifications](/learn/geometry/#shimura-varieties-and-toroidal-compactifications), [the Kodaira-Spencer map](/learn/abelian-varieties/#the-kodaira-spencer-map)). The identification of the canonical bundle with an automorphic line bundle of the canonical weight, twisted by the boundary, is the content of Mumford's extension of Hirzebruch proportionality to the noncompact case ([Mumford's proportionality theorem](/learn/abelian-varieties/#mumfords-proportionality-theorem)). The Kodaira-Spencer map at a point is that of the deformation theory of abelian schemes, in the form of Grothendieck and Messing. The flat section of Section 16 is the statement that the line $(\det \mathrm{Lie}_\chi)^{-1} \otimes \det \mathrm{Lie}_{\bar\chi}$ is trivial with a section that is flat for the complex uniformization; its extension to normal compactifications uses that a regular function on a normal proper geometrically connected variety is constant.

Sections 17 to 19 compute volumes. The Riemannian Haar measure on a compact Lie group is the measure of the Riemannian metric given by an invariant inner product on the Lie algebra; Macdonald computed the total volume of a compact Lie group in such a metric. Section 17 does not compute the total volume: it compares the Riemannian measure with a measure normalized at the identity by a constant, and the covolume of hermitian data with the mass of a fundamental set. The mass bound of Section 18 compares it with a product of Haar measures on the groups $U(p, q)$, by uniqueness of Haar measures and an estimate on small balls. The constant $(1/16)^{d r^2}$ is not sharp. The unfolding of the adelic quotient into a finite union of arithmetic quotients, used in Section 19, is the standard decomposition of $G(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}) / K$ by the class set of $K$.

The determinant relations of Section 20 compare the lattice of vectors that live at one point $a_\tau$ of an order $\mathcal{O}[T]/\prod_s (T - a_s)$ with the Lie quotient and the filtration at that point. The valuation of the constant is a sum of the valuations of the differences $a_\tau - a_s$, weighted by ranks. The lattices are lattices over a valuation ring, and the comparison is made by determinants of bases ([lattices over Dedekind domains](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#lattices-over-dedekind-domains)).

The key rule of Section 21, with the exponents $8$ and $4$ in its change of choices, has the form of the key formula of Moret-Bailly, which in the principally polarized case identifies the eighth power of the direct image of a symmetric rigidified line bundle with the fourth power of the inverse determinant of the Hodge bundle. Section 21 proves that the key scalar is a unit at all valued points of integral models, also at points near the boundary. Part 5 of the key rule compares the $L^2$ norm of the theta function with the norm of the Lie basis given by the periods ([the Hodge bundle](/learn/arakelov/#the-hodge-bundle-and-zarhins-trick)).

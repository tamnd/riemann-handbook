## The twisted comparison of Hodge ties

A Hodge tie joins a root model to the hermitian lines of a Hodge component. This section states how two Hodge ties compare when the second one is the image of the first one under a twist: an automorphism of the host that moves the CM types, together with an automorphism of the moduli space that carries the twisted universal semi-abelian scheme to the universal one. The result puts the two root models into one multiprojective space, with the same total degree on each side, and it shows that the pulled back singular lines of the two ties are isometric on an open set. The [final assembly](/entry/good-tower-volume-of-reduced/) uses this comparison to build the comparison pair, and through it the comparison enters [the good tower theorem](/entry/good-tower-and-volume/).

We keep the setting of the earlier sections: $E$ is a CM field with host $H$, $D$ is a datum with $N = p + q$, $l$ is a level, $X_o$ is a moduli space for $D$ and $l$ with structure map $s$, $c$ is a coefficient datum with field $F$, $\beta \colon O \to F$ is a ring map and $Y$ is a model of $X_o$ over $O$. A root index is a pair $r = (\tau, i)$ of a CM type $\tau$ and an integer $0 \le i < N$.

### Multiprojective data

Let $K$ be a finite set of blocks, and for each block $k$ let $\iota(k)$ be a finite set. We write $\Sigma\iota$ for the set of pairs $(k, j)$ with $j \in \iota(k)$, and $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ for the polynomial ring in the variables $x_{(k, j)}$. It is graded by $\mathbb{N}^K$, with one degree for each block.

**Definition 8.13.1 (Coordinate ideal).** Let $\Omega$ be a field and let $y \colon \Sigma\iota \to \Omega$ be a family of coordinates. The coordinate ideal $P(y)$ is the kernel of the ring map $\mathbb{Z}[x] \to \Omega[X_k : k \in K]$ that sends $x_{(k, j)}$ to $y_{(k, j)} X_k$.

So $P(y)$ is the multihomogeneous ideal of the point $y$ of a product of projective spaces, one for each block. We write $\mathbb{Q}(y)$ for the subfield of $\Omega$ generated by the quotients $y_a / y_b$ where $a$ and $b$ are in the same block. This is the field of the point.

**Definition 8.13.2 (Block map).** Let $(K_0, \iota_0)$ and $(K, \iota)$ be block data. A block map is a pair of an injective map $\kappa \colon K_0 \to K$ and an injective map $v \colon \Sigma\iota_0 \to \Sigma\iota$ such that $v$ sends the block $k$ into the block $\kappa(k)$, and such that every element of the block $\kappa(k)$ is in the image of $v$.

A block map identifies each block of the source with a full block of the target. So a family $y$ on the target restricts to the family $y \circ v$ on the source, and $\mathbb{Q}(y \circ v) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(y)$.

**Definition 8.13.3 (Pulled back hermitian line).** A hermitian line on $(K, \iota)$ is a degree $a \colon K \to \mathbb{Z}$ together with a function $h$ on $\mathbb{C}^{\Sigma\iota}$. Let $(\kappa, v)$ be a block map and let $L_0 = (a_0, h_0)$ be a hermitian line on the source. A hermitian line $L = (a, h)$ on the target is a pull back of $L_0$ along $(\kappa, v)$ if the degrees agree on the image blocks and $h(z) = h_0(z \circ v)$ for every point $z$.

**Definition 8.13.4 (Isometric lines).** Let $P$ be a prime ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, let $U$ be a set of complex points and let $L = (a, h)$ and $L' = (a', h')$ be hermitian lines. The lines are isometric on $U$ modulo $P$ if there are multihomogeneous polynomials $f$ and $g$ with these properties.

1. The degree of $f$ minus the degree of $g$ is $a - a'$, and $f/g$ is a unit at every relevant prime that contains $P$: $f, g \notin P$, and at each such prime $W$ there are $u, w \notin W$ with $uf - wg \in P$.
2. For each $z \in U$ and each pair $u, w$ with $uf - wg \in P$ and $u(z) \ne 0$,
$$
\log\left(\frac{|w(z)|}{|u(z)|} \cdot \frac{h'(z)}{h(z)}\right) = 0 .
$$

So $f/g$ is a rational section of the difference of the two lines that has norm $1$ on $U$. A prime ideal $P$ is an arithmetic variety of relative dimension $d$ if $K$ is not empty, $P$ is a multiprojective prime that is horizontal over $\mathbb{Z}$, and the Krull dimension of $\mathbb{Z}[x]/P$ is $d + |K|$.

### Hodge ties and twists

Let $S$ be a finite set of primes, let $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and let $M$ be a root model of type $(\Phi, N)$, with blocks $K_M$, index sets $\iota_M$ and ideal $P_M$. A Hodge tie $T$ of $Y$ over $S$ with weight $n$ for $M$ consists of a Hodge component $A$ over $S$, a flag of degree $\deg T$ that is a Hodge flag, the root lines, and for each root index $r$ a hermitian line $T_r$ on $(K_M, \iota_M)$ whose degree is the degree of $M$ at $r$. It also has an analytic system and a realization on $P_M$. Off the boundary, the logarithm of the norm of the combined line is the logarithm of the reference norm plus the potential of the system, and for small $t$ the coordinates of $M$ are the combination of the lines of the realization. We call $T_r$ the singular line of $T$ at $r$. We do not unfold the Hodge component, the flag or the realization.

A twist is the following data. An automorphism $g$ of the host, with its automorphism $\sigma_g$ of the CM field. An automorphism $\Phi$ of $X_o$ with $s \circ \Phi = s$. A map of semi-abelian schemes from the universal semi-abelian scheme twisted by $g$ to the universal one, over $\Phi$. A bijection $\pi$ of the CM types such that the embedding of $\pi(\tau)$ is the embedding of $\tau$ composed with $\sigma_g$. Two Hodge components $A$ and $A'$ are images under $\Phi$ if there are points $x$ and $x'$ of the generic fibre of $X_o$ with values in the fields of $A$ and $A'$, and an isomorphism of these fields, such that $\Phi$ maps $x$ to $x'$ compatibly with the isomorphism. We do not unfold this condition further.

We use two results from outside this chapter as hypotheses. The Hodge comparison compatibility states that, for two Hodge components of models of $X_o$ that are images under a twist and have the same value $R(p)$ on $S$, there is a linear isomorphism of their Lie spaces that is compatible with the tangent maps, with the twisted action of the host, with the eigenspaces at the cusps, with the interior points and with the Gram matrices. The theta uniformization of abelian varieties is the holomorphic uniformization of a polarized complex abelian variety by theta functions.

**Theorem 8.13.5 (Twisted comparison of Hodge ties).** Assume the Hodge comparison compatibility and the theta uniformization of abelian varieties. Let $Y$ be a model of $X_o$ over $O$ such that $\operatorname{Spec} \beta$ is a monomorphism. Let $(g, \Phi, \pi)$ be a twist, let $S$ be a finite set of primes, let $n \in \mathbb{N}$, let $M$ and $M'$ be root models, and let $T$ and $T'$ be Hodge ties of $Y$ over $S$ with weight $n$ for $M$ and $M'$. Assume that the Hodge components $A$ of $T$ and $A'$ of $T'$ are images under $\Phi$, and that $R_{A'}(p) = R_A(p)$ for every $p \in S$. Then there are finite block data $(K, \iota)$, block maps $(\kappa, v)$ from the blocks of $M$ and $(\kappa', v')$ from the blocks of $M'$, a field $\Omega$ of characteristic $0$, coordinates $y \colon \Sigma\iota \to \Omega$, a multihomogeneous polynomial $b$ of degree $d \colon K \to \mathbb{N}$, with these properties.

1. $P(y \circ v) = P_M$ and $P(y \circ v') = P_{M'}$.
2. $P(y)$ is an arithmetic variety of relative dimension $\dim \mathrm{Fl}(\Phi, N) + 1$, and $b \notin P(y)$.
3. The degree $[\mathbb{Q}(y) : \mathbb{Q}(y \circ v)]$ is not $0$, and
$$
\begin{aligned}
&[\mathbb{Q}(y) : \mathbb{Q}(y \circ v)] \cdot \deg T \\
&\quad = [\mathbb{Q}(y) : \mathbb{Q}(y \circ v')] \cdot \deg T' .
\end{aligned}
$$
4. Let $r = (\tau, i)$ be a root index, let $L$ be a pull back of $T_r$ along $(\kappa, v)$ and let $L'$ be a pull back of $T'_{(\pi(\tau), i)}$ along $(\kappa', v')$. Then $L$ and $L'$ are isometric modulo $P(y)$ on the set $U$ of complex points of $P(y)$ with $b(z) \ne 0$.

*Proof.* This result is proved outside this chapter, and we take it as given here. We do not unfold its proof. $\square$

The singular line at $(\tau, i)$ on the first side is compared with the singular line at $(\pi(\tau), i)$ on the second side. So the twist moves the CM types and keeps the index $i$. Property 3 says that the two flags have the same degree after we correct for the degrees of the two fields of definition. In the final assembly the block maps carry the singular lines of the two ties into the common space, and property 4 then shows that the combined lines differ by a section of norm $1$ on $U$.

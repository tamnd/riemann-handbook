## Notes and references

This chapter builds good towers of integral models and proves the volume theorem for them, and then it turns the volume theorem into the statements that the rest of the volume branch uses. Sections 1 to 9 give the good tower theorem and the [volume theorem](/entry/good-tower-volume-theorem-bis/), which feed [the fifth condition on global model towers](/entry/global-model-tower-fifth-condition/). Sections 10 to 13 give the nefness, the level covers, the lattice families and the twisted comparison that the [good tower theorem](/entry/good-tower-and-volume/) and the [final assembly](/entry/good-tower-volume-of-reduced/) use. Sections 14 to 17 turn the volume theorem into the bound for the mean volume slope, which is the form that [the deep statement from use](/entry/deep-st-r-of-use/) needs. Sections 18 and 19 prove the slope bound for Lie algebras of principally polarized semi-abelian schemes and assemble the top level inputs of the chapter. Sections 20 to 23 compute the flat degree at CM points and prove that the flat frame functions are algebraic with constant norms at all places, which the [covolume statement](/entry/top-covol-slim2-use-alt-alt/) uses.

### Notes per section

Section 1. Hermitian metrics on the Lie algebra of an abelian scheme over a ring of integers go back to Faltings, who used the Hodge line bundle with its $L^2$ metric to define the height of an abelian variety in his proof of the Mordell conjecture. For semi-abelian schemes and the behaviour of these metrics near the boundary we follow Moret-Bailly and Faltings and Chai. The slope bound for the Lie algebra in terms of a logarithm of its degree is the bound of Bost, proved by theta functions; Sections 18 and 19 give a proof of this type.

Section 2. The comparison of heights of points with degrees of graded pieces of flags is the formalism of hermitian lattices and slopes of Stuhler and Grayson, as used by Bost. Heights of points defined by hermitian lines are the heights of Arakelov theory, in the form of Faltings and of Bost, Gillet and Soulé.

Section 3. The bounds I5 and I6 are a lower bound for heights at every point and an upper bound on a dense set. Their use to bound an arithmetic volume follows Zhang, who proved the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel theorem and the theorem of successive minima for heights. The fibre input is a comparison of the type that Zhang uses for small points.

Sections 4 to 6. Period charts at the boundary come from the toroidal compactifications of Ash, Mumford, Rapoport and Tai, and in the setting of PEL type from Faltings and Chai and from Lan. The singular metric of the Hodge line at the boundary is of the type studied by Mumford for the proportionality theorem in the noncompact case, and by Burgos Gil, Kramer and Kühn for arithmetic intersection theory with log singular metrics. Plurisubharmonicity and growth of the potentials are standard pluripotential theory.

Sections 7 and 8. The arithmetic intersection numbers of the Hodge flag are the arithmetic intersection numbers of Gillet and Soulé, with the arithmetic Riemann-Roch theorem of Gillet and Soulé and the heights of Bost, Gillet and Soulé. The local Chern numbers at a prime in terms of the Frobenius action on the labels are of the type that appears in the computation of heights of CM points by Colmez.

Section 9. Root towers use root extensions of the field of definition and level covers, as in the semistable reduction theorem of Grothendieck for abelian varieties. The existence of good towers uses the toroidal compactifications above.

Section 10. Nefness of the line of a root model on the fibres at $p$ is proved on curves, by a slope bound for the flags of eigen subbundles of the Lie algebra. This is the method of Moret-Bailly for the positivity of the Hodge bundle, applied with the action of $\mathcal{O}_E$.

Sections 11 to 13. The bound of the Hodge metric of the abelian part by the Lie metric follows from the degeneration theory of Faltings and Chai. The lattice families at odd primes use the $p$-torsion of the semi-abelian scheme and the Kottwitz determinant condition for the action of $\mathcal{O}_E$ on the Lie algebra. The twisted comparison is a statement on automorphisms of Shimura varieties that move the CM types.

Sections 14 to 17. The descent of small sections and the count of small sections follow the method of Zhang for arithmetic volumes, with the Siegel lemma of Bombieri and Vaaler to produce small sections. The growth of the number of cusp sections is the Hirzebruch-Mumford proportionality theorem: the volume of a component times the degree of the flag variety, which is given by the Weyl dimension formula. The frame degrees at root origins use the denominators of the $p$-adic logarithm, which give the term $\frac{1}{p-1}$, and the different of $E$.

Sections 18 and 19. The method is the one of Bost for the slope bound of the Lie algebra of an abelian variety: theta functions of high level, their translates by torsion points, a jet basis at the origin, and a slope inequality. The theta group and the theta functions of level $m^2$ come from Mumford. The key formula for the mean square norm of a theta section is of the type of the key formula of Moret-Bailly. The absolute Siegel lemma is due to Roy and Thunder and to Zhang. The top level inputs collect the named results that the chapter assumes: resolution of singularities of Hironaka, GAGA of Serre, the Hodge-Tate decomposition of Faltings and Fargues, strong approximation for simply connected groups, and the bounds of Zhang.

Section 20. The flat degree at a CM point is a Chowla-Selberg formula: the periods of CM elliptic curves are values of the gamma function, and the logarithmic derivative of the $L$-function of the Kronecker character appears. The general form for abelian varieties with complex multiplication is the conjecture of Colmez; the formula used here is the case of products of CM elliptic curves.

Sections 21 to 23. The Hodge freeness of the de Rham module over $k \otimes \mathcal{O}_E$ is the condition of Rapoport for Hilbert-Blumenthal varieties, and the characteristic polynomial condition is the determinant condition of Kottwitz. The analytic eigen frames use resolution of singularities of Hironaka and GAGA of Serre, and the local exponentials in families are the exponential of a commutative complex Lie group, with the formal logarithm of a commutative formal group law.

**Remark 8.24.1 (Formalization).** The chapter follows the Lean development under `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar` and its second names under `GlobalTheory.T`; most `T.*` names are renamings proved by `first | exact @...` under `maxHeartbeats 1000000`. The classical results above enter the Lean code as named hypotheses collected in `Top.Given` (GoodOrigin.lean:67), such as `ResKStatement`, `GagaSections`, `LineOfLargeDegree.Statement` and the bounds of Zhang, or as proved results, such as the strong approximation statement and the key formula. The front matter lists `deepStR_of_volume`, which Section 15 already covers. We do not give a formal proof of any of the cited classical theorems.

## Raynaud flags and Hodge-Tate points

This section has two results on finite flat group schemes and $p$-divisible groups over the integers of an algebraically closed $p$-adic field. The first is a flag theorem for Hopf orders with an action of a finite field $F$, in the style of Raynaud's classification of group schemes of type $(p, \dots, p)$. It computes, for each character, the lengths of the cotangent spaces along a flag of the group. It is used for the [test facts of good towers](/entry/test-pt-test-facts/). The second result makes a Hodge-Tate point from a tower of Hopf orders with a polarization. It is used in the [volume chapter](/entry/deep-st-r-of-use/). For $p$-divisible groups see [the section on formal groups](/learn/formal-groups/#p-divisible-groups).

### Hopf orders with a field action

In this section $p$ is a prime, $C$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, and $v$ is a valuation on $C$ with ring of integers $R$ and $v(p) < 1$. Let $N$ be a finite abelian group.

**Definition 5.9.1 (Hopf order).** A Hopf order on $N$ is an $R$-lattice $A$ in the functions $N \to C$ such that every $f \in A$ takes values in $R$, $1 \in A$, $A$ is closed under products, the function $(x, y) \mapsto f(x + y)$ lies in the span of the products $g(x) h(y)$ with $g, h \in A$, and $x \mapsto f(-x)$ lies in $A$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.PGsh.L2.HopfOrder`.

So $A$ is the ring of functions of a finite flat group scheme over $R$ with generic fibre the constant group $N$. Let $F$ be a finite field of characteristic $p$, and let $N$ be an $F$-vector space. The order $A$ is $F$-stable if $x \mapsto f(a x)$ is in $A$ for all $a \in F$ and $f \in A$. A multiplicative character $\sigma \colon F \to R$ is fundamental if $\sigma(a + b) - \sigma(a) - \sigma(b) \in pR$ for all $a, b$.

**Definition 5.9.2 (Lengths and cotangent parts).** A module $M$ over $R$ has length $x$ if $M \cong \bigoplus_{i} R/(c_i)$ for finitely many $c_i$ with $\prod_i v(c_i) = x$. For a submodule $A$ of the functions on $N$ and a character $\chi$, the $\chi$-part of $A$ is the set of $g \in A$ with $g(a x) = \chi(a) g(x)$, and $\Omega_\chi(A)$ is the $\chi$-part modulo its intersection with the span of products of two functions of $A$ that vanish at $0$.

Lean: `ReciprocityTheory.PGsh.Up.HasLen` (also `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.GR.OmType` and `eig`).

For a subgroup $K$ and a vector $t \notin K$, the step order of $A$ at $(K, t)$ is the module of functions $a \mapsto g(a t)$ on $F$ where $g \in A$ is invariant under translation by $K$. For a module $S$ of functions on $F$ the ideal of $\chi$ is the set of $r \in R$ with $r \chi \in S$. The function $g$ is additive modulo $p$ if $g(x + y) - g(x) - g(y) \in pR$ for all $x, y$. The ideal $D_\chi(A, K, t)$ is the set of $c \in R$ such that there is $g \in A$ additive modulo $p$ with $g(a x) - \chi(a) g(x) \in pR$, with $g(k) \in pR$ for $k \in K$, and with $g(t) - c \in pR$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.GR.stepOrder` (also `stepOrderRes`, `xIdeal`, `idealX` and `idealD`).

The order $A$ is generated by additive functions on $K$ if the $K$-invariant functions in $A$ lie in the $R$-algebra generated by the $K$-invariant functions in $A$ that are additive modulo $p$. The order $A$ is tight if there are $c_A, c_N, c_O$ such that the quotient of the additive integral functions by the additive functions in $A$ plus $p$ has length $c_A$, the colength of $A$ is $c_N$, the cotangent space of $A$ has length $c_O$, and
$$c_A^{|N| (p - 1)} = c_N^2, \qquad c_O^{|N|} = c_N^2.$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.GR.IsTight` (also `GenAF`).

For a basis $b_0, \dots, b_{n-1}$ of $N$ let $N_j$ be the span of the $b_i$ with $i < j$.

**Definition 5.9.3 (Flag conclusion).** Let $A$ be a submodule of the functions on $N$, $b$ a basis and $\sigma$ a character. The flag conclusion holds for $(A, b, \sigma)$ if there are $c_{i,s}, \delta_{i,s} \in R$ for $i < n$ and $s \ge 0$ such that:
1. $c_{i,s} \neq 0$, and the ideal of $\sigma^{p^s}$ in the step order at $(N_i, b_i)$, the same ideal for the restricted step order, and $D_{\sigma^{p^s}}(A, N_i, b_i)$ are all equal to $(c_{i,s})$;
2. $c_{i,s}^p = \delta_{i,s}\, c_{i,s+1}$, and $\Omega_{\sigma^{p^{s+1}}}$ of the step order at $(N_i, b_i)$ has length $v(\delta_{i,s})$;
3. $\Omega_{\sigma^{p^{s+1}}}(A)$ has length $\prod_i v(\delta_{i,s})$;
4. for each $j$, $\Omega_{\sigma^{p^{s+1}}}$ of the restriction of $A$ to $N_j$ has length $\prod_{i < j} v(\delta_{i,s})$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.GR.FlagConcl`.

### The flag theorem

**Theorem 5.9.4 (Flag comparison).** Let $F$ be a finite field of characteristic $p$, $N$ a finite $F$-vector space, and $A$ an $F$-stable tight Hopf order on $N$. Let $b$ be a basis of $N$ such that $A$ is generated by additive functions on each $N_i$. Then for every fundamental character $\sigma$ the flag conclusion holds for $(A, b, \sigma)$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.GR.flagStatement_holds` (also `GlobalTheory.T.flag_comparison_theorem`, a second name).

*Proof.* Let $|F| = p^f$. The characters $\sigma^{p^s}$ are fundamental and depend on $s$ modulo $f$. For each $i$ the step order at $(N_i, b_i)$ is an $F$-stable Hopf order on $F$, and it equals the restricted step order. Its ideal at each character is principal, generated by $c_{i,s}$, and a Frobenius product formula gives $\delta_{i,s}$ with $c_{i,s}^p = \delta_{i,s} c_{i,s+1}$ and with $\Omega_{\sigma^{p^{s+1}}}$ of length $v(\delta_{i,s})$. For $A$ itself there are elements $d_{i,s}$ that generate the ideals $D$, and lengths $L_\chi$ of $\Omega_\chi(A)$ whose product is $c_O$. For a character that is not fundamental the $\chi$-part lies in the square of the augmentation ideal, so $L_\chi = 1$. A comparison along the basis gives $v(c_{i,s}) \le v(d_{i,s})$ and $\prod_i v(\delta_{i,s}) \le L_{\sigma^{p^{s+1}}}$. The two equations of tightness then force equality in both inequalities, by a counting lemma on products of real numbers. So $(c_{i,s}) = (d_{i,s})$, which gives the ideals $D$, and the lengths of $\Omega(A)$ are the products of the $v(\delta_{i,s})$. The lengths on the restriction to $N_j$ come from a colength lemma along the basis. $\square$

**Theorem 5.9.5 (Direct factors).** Assume that every abelian factor of the $p$-torsion has the following property: for every subgroup $K$ it is generated by additive functions on $K$. Let $R$ be $p$-adically complete. Let $A$ be a tight Hopf order on a group $N$ with $p N = 0$, and assume that $A$ comes from the $p$-torsion of an abelian scheme in the sense of the abelian factor condition. Let $N_w$ be an $F$-vector space with maps $i \colon N_w \to N$ and $\pi \colon N \to N_w$ such that $\pi \circ i = \mathrm{id}$ and $f \circ i \circ \pi \in A$ for $f \in A$. Let the image $A_w$ of $A$ under restriction along $i$ be $F$-stable. Then for every basis $b$ of $N_w$ and every fundamental character $\sigma$ the flag conclusion holds for $(A_w, b, \sigma)$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.GR.flagConcl_directFactor_of_subquotient` (also `GlobalTheory.T.abelian_factor_tight_field_action`, a second name).

*Proof.* Restriction along a retract sends a Hopf order to a Hopf order, sends tight orders to tight orders, and sends the generation property on $i(K)$ to the generation property on $K$. The first hypothesis gives the generation property for $A$ on every $i(N_j)$. Apply the flag comparison theorem to $A_w$. $\square$

We do not unfold the abelian factor condition. It asks for an abelian scheme over $R$ with a relatively ample line bundle and a family of $p$-torsion points whose functions give $A$.

### Hodge-Tate points

Let $O$ be the ring of integers of $C$, $p$-adically complete. Let $E$ be a number field with an involution $c$, and let $\Lambda$ be a module over the integers of $E$.

**Definition 5.9.6 (Polarized lattice).** A polarized lattice is a finite free $\mathbb{Z}$-module $\Lambda$ with an alternating unimodular form $e$ such that $e(a x, y) = e(x, c(a) y)$, of rank $N [E : \mathbb{Q}]$, with vectors $u_1, \dots, u_N$ that span $\Lambda$ over the integers of $E$ modulo $p \Lambda$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.HT.IsPolLattice`.

**Definition 5.9.7 (Hodge-Tate point).** Let $\kappa \in O$ be nonzero. A Hodge-Tate point with constant $\kappa$ on spaces $V$ and $\Omega$ over $C$ consists of:
1. a lattice $T \subset V$, free over the integers of $E$ on vectors $t_1, \dots, t_N$, with an alternating nondegenerate form $e$ that is perfect on $T$ and adjoint for $c$;
2. a lattice $\omega \subset \Omega$ stable under the integers of $E$;
3. an $E$-linear map $\mathrm{ht} \colon V \to \Omega$ with $\mathrm{ht}(T) \subseteq \omega$ and $\kappa \omega \subseteq \mathrm{ht}(T)$, whose kernel is equal to its orthogonal for $e$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.HT.HTPoint`.

An ordinary tower on $\Lambda$ is a family of Hopf orders $A_n$ on $\Lambda / p^n \Lambda$, compatible under multiplication by $p$, with a derivation relation from $A_n$ to the dual of a free $O$-module $L$ modulo $p^n$. We do not unfold its eleven conditions. The constant $\kappa$ is a Hodge-Tate constant if $v(\kappa)^{p-1} \le v(p)$.

**Theorem 5.9.8 (Fargues).** Let $G$ be a $p$-divisible group over $O$. If $v(\kappa)^{p-1} \le v(p)$ (and $v(\kappa) \le v(4)$ when $p = 2$), then $\kappa$ kills the cokernel of the Hodge-Tate map of $G$, which takes values in the invariant differentials of the dual of $G$. Given a compatible system of roots of unity $\zeta_n$ with $\zeta_1 \neq 1$, the kernel of the Hodge-Tate map on $O \otimes T(G)$ is the orthogonal, for the Weil pairing, of the kernel of the map $\mathrm{dlog}$ on $O \otimes T(G^\vee)$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.HT.FarguesHodgeTate`.

**Theorem 5.9.9 (Existence of Hodge-Tate points).** Assume the theorem of Fargues. Let $(\Lambda, e, u)$ be a polarized lattice, let $L$ be free of finite rank over $O$ with $2 \operatorname{rk} L = \operatorname{rk} \Lambda$, and let $X$ be an ordinary tower on $\Lambda$ with $L$ on which the integers of $E$ act through maps $\rho_L$. Let $\zeta_n$ be primitive $p^n$-th roots of unity with $\zeta_{n+1}^p = \zeta_n$, such that the pairing $\zeta_n^{e(a, b)}$ extends to the tower, and let $\kappa$ be a Hodge-Tate constant. Then there is a Hodge-Tate point $P$ on $C \otimes \Lambda$ and $C \otimes L^\vee$ with $t_i = u_i$, with the action and the form of $\Lambda$, with $\omega = O \otimes L^\vee$, and with the following property: if $f$ is the function $\zeta_n^{e(x, \cdot)}$ and $w$ is a derivative of $f$ in the tower, then $\mathrm{ht}(x) - w \in p^n \omega$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.HT.TowerPoint.exists_HTPoint` (also `GlobalTheory.T.polarised_divisible_group_exists`, a second name).

*Proof.* The polarized lattice gives a lattice tower. The ordinary tower gives a $p$-divisible group $G$ over $O$ with an isomorphism of its Tate module with $\mathbb{Z}_p \otimes \Lambda$, and the extension of the pairing gives a polarization $G \to G^\vee$. The tower is free, so the module of invariant differentials of $G$ is $L^\vee$, and the derivations of the tower are the Hodge-Tate map. Over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ the form $e$ stays alternating and perfect, and the Weil pairing of $G$ is $e$ times the fixed root of unity. The theorem of Fargues then says that $\kappa$ kills the cokernel of the Hodge-Tate map and that its kernel is Lagrangian. These are the conditions of a Hodge-Tate point. $\square$

**Remark 5.9.10 (Formalization).** The flag theorem has no hypothesis of $p$-adic completeness, while the theorem on direct factors asks for it ([PolarisedFlagStatement.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Statements/AlgebraStatements/PolarisedFlagStatement.lean#L31)). The theorem on direct factors takes the generation property of abelian factors as an explicit hypothesis ([AbelianFactorTightFieldActionStatement.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/AlgebraProofs/AbelianFactorTightFieldActionStatement.lean#L21)), and the existence of Hodge-Tate points takes the theorem of Fargues as one ([PolarisedDivisibleGroupExists.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/AlgebraProofs/PolarisedDivisibleGroupExists.lean#L398)). The theorem of Fargues is stated in [FarguesHodgeTate.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/PublishedTheorems/Algebra₂/FarguesHodgeTate.lean#L10) and is not proved. The second name of the existence theorem speaks of a polarized divisible group, but its conclusion is the Hodge-Tate point. The proofs of the step lemmas of the flag theorem were not opened.

## Conjugating the symmetric level

The first stage of the tower carries two level structures. One comes from the realisation data $R$: an open compact subgroup $J_R$ of the adelic group of the first stage, with a character $\chi_R$, cut out by a symmetric unipotent group at a finite set of places. The other comes from a Hida setting $X$: a level $K_2$ in the finite adelic unitary group with a character $\chi_2$. This section finds a finite adelic point $t$ whose conjugation carries the first pair onto the second, with $\chi_R$ going to $\overline{\chi_2}$. The step is linear algebra over the residue fields $\mathbb{Z}/g$: a change of basis modulo a place, followed by a diagonal rescaling that turns one generic character of the symmetric unipotent group into another. The point $t$ is the first output of [the theorem on spectral restrictions over the top stage](/entry/exists-s-rest-top/) of Section 2.

**Setting.** Let $E/F$ be the CM extension of a tower datum $D$, with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_E$. The datum has two type primes $g_0, g_1$. For $k \in \{0, 1\}$ it has a finite index set $V_k$ and, for $v \in V_k$, a place $w_{k,v}$ of $E$. Write $\Sigma = \{(k, v) : k \in \{0,1\},\ v \in V_k\}$, and for $\varkappa = (k, v) \in \Sigma$ write $g_\varkappa = g_k$ and $w_\varkappa = w_{k,v}$. The finite group of the datum is
$$\mathcal{G}^0 = \prod_{\varkappa \in \Sigma} \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{Z}/g_\varkappa).$$
The realisation data $R$ give a finite set $P_g$ of places of $E$, with residue fields $k_P$, the group $G_0(P_g) = \prod_{P \in P_g} \mathrm{GL}_r(k_P)$, a finite group $G'$ and a homomorphism $e_G \colon G' \to G_0(P_g)$. A *level datum* on $G_0(P_g)$ is a subgroup $H$ with a character $\chi \colon H \to \mathbb{C}^\times$.

**Definition 1.1.1 (Symmetric unipotent group).** Fix integers $\rho, k \ge 0$ and a commutative ring $\kappa$. Let $U_{\mathrm{sym}}(\kappa) \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_r(\kappa)$ be the group of upper unitriangular matrices $u$ with $u_{i, \rho + k} = 0$ for $\rho \le i < \rho + k$. An ordered pair $(i, j)$ is a *symmetric edge* if either $j = i + 1$ and $i + 1 \notin \{\rho, \rho + k\}$, or $i + 1 = \rho$ and $j = \rho + k$. The *edge sum* of $u$ is $\varepsilon(u) = \sum u_{ij}$ over the symmetric edges.

On $U_{\mathrm{sym}}(\kappa)$ the entries at the symmetric edges add under multiplication, so $\varepsilon$ is a homomorphism to the additive group of $\kappa$. For additive characters $\psi = (\psi_P)$ of the residue fields, the *symmetric level* $P^\sharp_{\mathrm{sym}}(\psi)$ is the level datum with $H = \prod_{P \in P_g} U_{\mathrm{sym}}(k_P)$ and $\chi(u) = \prod_P \psi_P(\varepsilon(u_P))$. The family $\psi$ is *generic* if $\psi_P \ne 1$ for each $P \in P_g$. A permutation $\pi$ of $\{0, \dots, r-1\}$ is the *symmetric order* if $\pi(a) = k + a$ for $a < \rho$, $\pi(a) = a - \rho$ for $\rho \le a < \rho + k$, and $\pi(a) = a$ otherwise. For $g \in G_0(P_g)$ the *reordering* $\mathrm{ro}_\pi(g)$ has entries $\mathrm{ro}_\pi(g)_{ij} = g_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}$ at each place.

**Setting (the Hida setting).** A Hida setting $X$ over $D$ consists of, among other data, a hermitian space $E^r$ with a lattice $L$, its finite adelic unitary group $G_f$ with the stabiliser $K$ of $\widehat{L}$, the principal congruence subgroup $K(g) \subseteq K$ for $g = g_0 g_1$, and a level $K_2 \subseteq K$ with a character $\chi_2$. It also gives vectors $e_0, f_0, z_0$ and weight vectors in $\widehat{L}$, and from them, for each place $w$, a *flag family* $s^w = (s^w_1, \dots, s^w_r)$ in $\widehat{L}$. We do not repeat the full list.

**Definition 1.1.2 (Matrices modulo a place).** Let $w$ be a place of $E$ over $g$, let $x \in G_f$ and let $u_1, \dots, u_r \in \widehat{L}$. A matrix $N$ over $\mathbb{Z}/g$ is *the matrix of $x$ on $u$ modulo $w$* if $x u_i - \sum_j N_{ji} u_j \in w \widehat{L}$ for every $i$. A *reduction basis* at $w$ is a family $b_1, \dots, b_r \in L$ such that each $y \in L$ satisfies $y \equiv \sum_i c_i b_i \pmod{wL}$ for some integers $c_i$.

**Definition 1.1.3 (Reduction homomorphism).** A map $\mathrm{red} \colon G_f \to \mathcal{G}^0$ is a *reduction homomorphism* if for $k \in K$ the component $\mathrm{red}(k)_\varkappa$ is the matrix of $k$ on some reduction basis at $w_\varkappa$, $\mathrm{red}$ is multiplicative on $K$, every element of $\mathcal{G}^0$ is $\mathrm{red}(k)$ for some $k \in K$, and for $k \in K$ we have $\mathrm{red}(k) = 1$ exactly when $k \in K(g)$. It is *fixed by* a family $b = (b_\varkappa)$ of reduction bases if $\mathrm{red}(k)_\varkappa$ is the matrix of $k$ on $b_\varkappa$ modulo $w_\varkappa$ for every $k \in K$ and every $\varkappa$.

**Theorem 1.1.4 (Conjugating the symmetric level).** Let $D$, $R$ and $X$ be as above, where the field of $X$ is $E$ and the instance of $X$ is the standard instance of $D$. Let $e_R \colon G' \to \mathcal{G}^0$ be an isomorphism, $p \colon \Sigma \to P_g$ a bijection, and $\iota_\varkappa \colon \mathbb{Z}/g_\varkappa \to k_{p(\varkappa)}$ ring isomorphisms, such that $p(\varkappa) = w_\varkappa$ as places and
$$e_G(y)_{p(\varkappa)} = \iota_\varkappa\big(e_R(y)_\varkappa\big) \quad (y \in G', \ \varkappa \in \Sigma).$$
Let $\psi_0$ be a generic family of additive characters, and let $\pi$ be the symmetric order. Let $\mathrm{red}$ be a reduction homomorphism fixed by a family $b$, and assume $g_\varkappa \in w_\varkappa$ for every $\varkappa$. For $c \in \mathcal{G}^0$ and $k \in K$ put
$$\theta_c(k) = \mathrm{ro}_\pi\, e_G\big(e_R^{-1}(c^{-1}\, \mathrm{red}(k)\, c)\big) \in G_0(P_g).$$
Then there is $c \in \mathcal{G}^0$ such that for every $k \in K$: $k \in K_2$ exactly when $\theta_c(k) \in H$, where $(H, \chi) = P^\sharp_{\mathrm{sym}}(\psi_0)$, and in that case $\chi(\theta_c(k)) = \overline{\chi_2(k)}$.

*Proof.* We use four facts about the Hida setting, proved with the construction of $X$. (a) There is $P \in \mathcal{G}^0$ with $s^{w_\varkappa}_{\pi^{-1}(j)} \equiv \sum_i P_{\varkappa, ij}\, b_{\varkappa, i} \pmod{w_\varkappa \widehat{L}}$ for all $\varkappa$ and $j$. (b) If $u \equiv b_\varkappa P$ in this sense, then for $k \in K$ the matrix of $k$ on $u$ modulo $w_\varkappa$ is $P^{-1}\, \mathrm{red}(k)_\varkappa\, P$. (c) For $k \in K$, let $N_\varkappa$ be the matrix of $k$ on $s^{w_\varkappa}$ modulo $w_\varkappa$. Then $k \in K_2$ exactly when $N_\varkappa \in U_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{Z}/g_\varkappa)$ for all $\varkappa$. (d) There are nontrivial additive characters $\psi'_\varkappa$ of $\mathbb{Z}/g_\varkappa$ and units $a_{\varkappa, ij}$ such that, for $k \in K_2$ with all $N_\varkappa$ symmetric unipotent,
$$\overline{\chi_2(k)} = \prod_{\varkappa} \psi'_\varkappa\Big(\sum_{(i,j)} a_{\varkappa, ij}\, (N_\varkappa)_{ij}\Big),$$
the inner sum over the symmetric edges.

*Rescaling.* Fix $\varkappa$. Since $g_\varkappa$ is prime, $\mathbb{Z}/g_\varkappa$ is a field. The character $\psi_0 \circ \iota_\varkappa$ is nontrivial, because $\psi_0$ is generic, and $\psi'_\varkappa$ is nontrivial by (d). Conjugation by a diagonal matrix $\delta(d) = \mathrm{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_r)$ multiplies the entry at $(i, j)$ by $d_i^{-1} d_j$. The rescaling lemma for edge characters over a finite field (it uses that a nontrivial additive character of a finite field is primitive) chooses the $d_i$ so that the weights $a_{\varkappa, ij}$ and the change of character are absorbed. This gives units $d_\varkappa$ with
$$\psi'_\varkappa\Big(\sum a_{\varkappa, ij} u_{ij}\Big) = \psi_0\big(\iota_\varkappa(\varepsilon(\delta(d_\varkappa)^{-1}\, u\, \delta(d_\varkappa)))\big)$$
for every $u \in U_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{Z}/g_\varkappa)$.

*The element $c$.* Put $c_\varkappa = P_\varkappa\, \delta(d_\varkappa \circ \pi^{-1})$. Fix $k \in K$ and let $N_\varkappa$ be the reordering by $\pi$ of $P_\varkappa^{-1}\, \mathrm{red}(k)_\varkappa\, P_\varkappa$. By (a) and (b), and since reordering a family reorders its matrix, $N_\varkappa$ is the matrix of $k$ on $s^{w_\varkappa}$ modulo $w_\varkappa$. Conjugation by a permuted diagonal matrix commutes with reordering up to the same permutation of $d$. With the compatibility of $e_G$ and $e_R$ this gives
$$\theta_c(k)_{p(\varkappa)} = \iota_\varkappa\big(\delta(d_\varkappa)^{-1}\, N_\varkappa\, \delta(d_\varkappa)\big).$$

*Membership.* The group $U_{\mathrm{sym}}$ is stable under conjugation by diagonal matrices, and $\iota_\varkappa$ carries $U_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{Z}/g_\varkappa)$ onto $U_{\mathrm{sym}}(k_{p(\varkappa)})$. Since $p$ is a bijection, $\theta_c(k) \in H$ exactly when every $N_\varkappa$ is symmetric unipotent, and by (c) this holds exactly when $k \in K_2$.

*Character.* Let $\theta_c(k) \in H$. The character of $P^\sharp_{\mathrm{sym}}(\psi_0)$ is a product over $P \in P_g$. Reindex it by $p$, and apply the displays above at each $\varkappa$. The product becomes the right side of (d), which is $\overline{\chi_2(k)}$. $\square$

To state the second result we need the two levels on the first stage. Let $Y_0$ be the first stage of the tower and $\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A})$ its adelic unitary group, with the frame $\beta_0$ of $R$. For a level datum $(H, \chi)$ on $G_0(P_g)$, the *base level* $J(H)$ is the set of finite $\kappa \in \mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A})$ whose finite part lies in the parahoric level of $\beta_0$ and whose reduction tuple at $P_g$ lies in $H$. Its character is $\chi$ of the reduction tuple. The *flag level* is $J(G_0(P_g))$. An element $\kappa$ is a *level lift* of $g_0 \in G_0(P_g)$ if it is finite, its finite part lies in the parahoric level, and its reduction tuple is $g_0$. The realisation $R$ assigns to the face $F$ a level datum $(H_F, \chi_F)$, and we write $J_R = J(H_F)$, $\chi_R$ for its character. Let $\iota_X \colon \mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A}) \to G_\infty \times G_f$ be the identification of $X$, and for $t \in \mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A})$ and $k \in G_f$ put $\kappa_t(k) = t^{-1}\, \iota_X^{-1}(1, k)\, t$.

**Definition 1.1.5 (A point that sees the row level).** A point $t \in \mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A})$ *sees the row level* of $(R, F, X)$ if: (i) $t$ is finite; (ii) for every $y \in \mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A})$, $y \in J_R$ exactly when $t y t^{-1} = \iota_X^{-1}(1, k)$ with $k \in K_2$, and then $\chi_R(y) = \overline{\chi_2(k)}$; (iii) there are an isomorphism $\sigma \colon \mathcal{G}^0 \to G'$ and a reduction homomorphism $\mathrm{red}$ such that: for $k \in K_2$, $\kappa_t(k) \in J_R$ and $\chi_R(\kappa_t(k)) = \overline{\chi_2(k)}$; for $k \in K$, every $T$ in the multiplicity space $\mathcal{M}(J_R, \chi_R)$ and every vector $v$ of the discrete series model,
$$T v\,(g\, \kappa_t(k)) = \mathrm{act}\big(e_G(\sigma(\mathrm{red}\, k))\big)(T v)(g);$$
and $\overline{e_0(\gamma)} = e(\sigma\gamma)$ for $\gamma \in \mathcal{G}^0$, where $e_0$ is the idempotent of $D$ in the group algebra of $\mathcal{G}^0$ and $e$ is the idempotent of the family of $R$, read in the coefficient field.

Here $\mathcal{M}(J, \chi)$ is the span of the maps $T$ from the model to forms on $\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A})$ with $T v\,(g \kappa) = \chi(\kappa)\, T v\,(g)$ for $\kappa \in J$, and $\mathrm{act}(g_0)$ is right translation by a level lift of $g_0$ on forms of flag level.

**Theorem 1.1.6 (A finite point that carries the row level).** Let $D$, $R$, $F$ and $X$ be as above, where the field of $X$ is $E$ and its instance is the standard instance of $D$. Assume: the level character of $R$ is the one of the family $A$; the group levels of $R$ at $F$ hold, that is, there are a generic $\psi_0$ and the symmetric order $\pi$ with $g \in H_F \Leftrightarrow \mathrm{ro}_\pi(g) \in H$ and $\chi_F(g) = \chi(\mathrm{ro}_\pi(g))$, for $(H, \chi) = P^\sharp_{\mathrm{sym}}(\psi_0)$; the group pin of $R$ holds, that is, there are $e_R$, $p$, $\iota_\varkappa$ as in the theorem above with $e(y) = e_0(e_R(y))$ in the coefficient field; and the coefficients of $e_0$ are real. Then some $t$ sees the row level.

*Proof.* *A transversal point.* By the construction of transversal points, there are a finite $t_0$, a reduction homomorphism $\mathrm{red}$ fixed by a family $b$, such that $\kappa_{t_0}(k)$ is a level lift of $e_G(e_R^{-1}(\mathrm{red}\, k))$ for every $k \in K$, and every element of the flag level is $\kappa_{t_0}(k)$ for some $k \in K$.

*Conjugation.* The theorem above gives $c$. The map $\mathrm{red}' = c^{-1}\, \mathrm{red}\, c$ is fixed by another family $b'$ (a change of the reduction bases by $c$), and it is again a reduction homomorphism: multiplicativity, surjectivity and the kernel pass through conjugation. Since $\mathrm{red}$ is onto, $c = \mathrm{red}(k_c)$ for some $k_c \in K$. Put $t = \iota_X^{-1}(1, k_c)\, t_0$. Then $\kappa_t(k) = \kappa_{t_0}(k_c^{-1} k k_c)$, and $\mathrm{red}(k_c^{-1} k k_c) = \mathrm{red}'(k)$ by multiplicativity. So $\kappa_t(k)$ is a level lift of $e_G(e_R^{-1}(\mathrm{red}'\, k))$ for every $k \in K$.

*The key step.* Let $k \in K$. A level lift of $g_0$ lies in $J(H_F)$ exactly when $g_0 \in H_F$, and then its character is $\chi_F(g_0)$. By the group levels, $e_G(e_R^{-1}(\mathrm{red}'\, k)) \in H_F$ exactly when its reordering lies in $H$, which is $\theta_c(k) \in H$. By the theorem above this holds exactly when $k \in K_2$, and then $\chi_R(\kappa_t(k)) = \chi(\theta_c(k)) = \overline{\chi_2(k)}$.

*Conclusion.* (i) $t = t_0\, \kappa_{t_0}(k_c)$ is a product of finite elements. (ii) If $y \in J_R$, then $y$ lies in the flag level, so $y = \kappa_t(k)$ for some $k \in K$ (conjugate the cover of $t_0$ by $k_c$). If $t y t^{-1} = \iota_X^{-1}(1, k)$ with $k \in K_2$, then $y = \kappa_t(k)$ and $k \in K$. In both cases the key step gives the claim. (iii) Take $\sigma = e_R^{-1}$ and $\mathrm{red}'$. The first clause is the key step. For the second, let $T$ be one of the spanning maps of $\mathcal{M}(J_R, \chi_R)$. Each $T v$ is right invariant under $J(\{1\})$, because this group lies in $J_R$ and $\chi_R$ is $1$ on it. The action formula of level lifts then gives $T v\,(g \kappa_t(k)) = \mathrm{act}(e_G(e_R^{-1}\, \mathrm{red}'\, k))(T v)(g)$, and the formula passes to sums and scalar multiples. For the third, the pin gives $e(e_R^{-1}\gamma) = e_0(\gamma)$, which is real. $\square$

**Remark 1.1.7 (Formalization).** In `conj_symLevel` five hypotheses are never used: that $\pi$ is the symmetric order, the level character of $R$, the equality $p(\varkappa) = w_\varkappa$, the divisibility of $R$ at $p(\varkappa)$, and that $\mathrm{red}$ is a reduction homomorphism ([ConjLevelConjCharacterSymmetricLevel.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ConjLevelConjCharacterSymmetricLevel.lean#L12)). The facts (a) to (d) are cited through statement files whose proofs we did not open, and we did not unfold the Hida setting, the flag family or the multiplicity space.

## The equations of the theta family

The kernel lattices of the construction carry a family of theta functions $e(L, L')$, one for each pair of self-dual hermitian lattices of even ranks. The family has three equations: it is equivariant under the unitary groups, and a change of one lattice multiplies it by a character value and the intertwining operator between the two theta spaces. In this section and the next we prove the three equations. This section reduces them to a square law for the intertwining operators, to two triangle laws, and to an arithmetic fact: $-1$ is a norm from $E$ modulo every positive integer. The square law follows from a split of each square of lattice pairs, and [Section 1.24.5](/entry/eq-p-car/) gives these splits. The result is an input of [the nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/). The background is [the Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation).

### The setting

Let $D$ be an ambient datum. Its host has a CM field $E$ with complex conjugation $x \mapsto \bar{x}$, a subfield $K$, an embedding $\varphi_0 : E \to \mathbb{C}$, a finite set $T$ of embeddings, and two primes $q \ne d$ with $q \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ and elements $\sqrt{-q}$, $\sqrt{-d}$ of $E$ with squares $-q$ and $-d$. All statements of this section hold for two kinds of ambient data with such a host, and the proofs are the same for both.

**Definition 1.23.1 (Curve ideal and twelfth root).** An ideal $\mathfrak{c}$ of $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a curve ideal if $\mathfrak{c} \ne 0$ and the norm of $\mathfrak{c}$ is prime to $2d$. Let $\Lambda(\mathfrak{c})$ be the subgroup of $\mathbb{C}$ generated by $\varphi_0(\mathfrak{c})$, and let $\Delta = g_2^3 - 27 g_3^2$ be the discriminant of a lattice. A number $\delta \in \mathbb{C}$ is a twelfth root for $\mathfrak{c}$ if $\delta^{12} = \Delta(\Lambda(\mathfrak{c}))$.

**Definition 1.23.2 (Splitting character).** An ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$ has norm one if $\mathfrak{a}\bar{\mathfrak{a}} = \mathcal{O}_E$. A function $\chi$ on the ideals of $\mathcal{O}_E$ is a splitting character if $\chi(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}) = \chi(\mathfrak{a})\chi(\mathfrak{b})$ for ideals of norm one, and
$$\chi(z\mathcal{O}_E) = \prod_{\tau \in T} \tau(z)^{-1}$$
for each $z \in E$ with $z\bar{z} = 1$.

A lattice $L \subset E^N$ is self-dual if it is a finitely generated $\mathcal{O}_E$-module that spans $E^N$, and $y \in L$ if and only if $\sum_i \bar{x}_i y_i \in \mathcal{O}_E$ for all $x \in L$. For lattices $L, L_1 \subset E^N$, let $\det(L, L_1)$ be the ideal generated by the determinants of the matrices that map $L$ into $L_1$.

Let $B$ be a base over $(D, \mathfrak{c}, \delta)$, with a torus datum. For self-dual lattices $L \subset E^N$ and $L' \subset E^{N'}$ with $N$ and $N'$ even, the base gives a lattice $\Lambda(L, L')$ in a complex space $V(N, N')$ and a space of theta functions for it. For two such lattices $\Lambda_1$ and $\Lambda_2$ there is an intertwining operator $I(\Lambda_1, \Lambda_2)$ from functions for $\Lambda_1$ to functions for $\Lambda_2$. For $\gamma \in U(N)$ and $\gamma' \in U(N')$ there is a pullback $\gamma^*$ of functions. A splitting character $\chi$ gives the theta family $e = e_\chi$ of $B$.

**Definition 1.23.3 (Equations of the theta family).** The theta family $e$ satisfies the equations if the following three laws hold for all even $N$, $N'$ and all self-dual lattices.

1. $(\gamma, \gamma')^* e(L, L') = e(\gamma L, \gamma' L')$ for $\gamma \in U(N)$ and $\gamma' \in U(N')$.
2. $e(L, L'') = \chi(\det(L', L''))^{-N/2}\, I\big(\Lambda(L, L'), \Lambda(L, L'')\big)\, e(L, L')$.
3. $e(L_1, L') = \chi(\det(L, L_1))^{N'/2}\, I\big(\Lambda(L, L'), \Lambda(L_1, L')\big)\, e(L, L')$.

### The reduction

Write $I(L, L' \to L_1, L_1')$ for $I(\Lambda(L, L'), \Lambda(L_1, L_1'))$, and let $S_N$ be the standard lattice $\mathcal{O}_E^N$. We use five conditions on the base. The square law: for all self-dual $L, L_1 \subset E^N$ and $L', L'' \subset E^{N'}$ and each theta function $G$ for $\Lambda(L, L')$,
$$I(L_1, L' \to L_1, L'')\, I(L, L' \to L_1, L') G = I(L, L'' \to L_1, L'')\, I(L, L' \to L, L'') G.$$
The left triangle law: for each theta function $G$ for $\Lambda(S_N, S_{N'})$, the operator $I(L, S_{N'} \to L_1, S_{N'})$ after $I(S_N, S_{N'} \to L, S_{N'})$ is $I(S_N, S_{N'} \to L_1, S_{N'})$ on $G$. The right triangle law is the same statement with the second lattice moving. The first constant law: for $\gamma \in U(N)$, the pullback of the vacuum function $\theta_0$ by $(\gamma, 1)$ is $(\prod_{\tau} \tau(\overline{\det \gamma}))^{N'/2}$ times the half moved function at $\gamma S_N$. The second constant law: the pullback of $\theta_0$ by $(1, \gamma')$ is $(\prod_\tau \tau(\det \gamma'))^{N/2}$ times the moved function at $(S_N, \gamma' S_{N'})$.

**Definition 1.23.4 (Norm condition).** The norm condition holds for $E$ if for each integer $m > 0$ there are $\lambda, s \in \mathcal{O}_E$ with $\bar{s} = s$ and $\bar{\lambda}\lambda = -1 + m s$.

**Lemma 1.23.5 (Minus one is a norm modulo $m$).** The norm condition holds.

*Proof.* Let $m > 0$. Since $q$ and $d$ are distinct primes with $q \equiv 3 \pmod 8$, there are integers $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta, s$ with
$$(\alpha^2 + q\beta^2)(\gamma^2 + d\delta^2) = -1 + m s.$$
Put $\lambda = (\alpha + \beta\sqrt{-q})(\gamma + \delta\sqrt{-d})$. It is integral, since $\sqrt{-q}$ and $\sqrt{-d}$ are roots of $X^2 + q$ and $X^2 + d$. Complex conjugation changes the signs of $\sqrt{-q}$ and $\sqrt{-d}$, so $\bar{\lambda}\lambda = (\alpha^2 + q\beta^2)(\gamma^2 + d\delta^2)$, and $\bar{s} = s$ since $s$ is an integer. $\square$

**Theorem 1.23.6 (Equations from the norm condition).** Let $\mathfrak{c}$ be a curve ideal, $\delta \ne 0$ a twelfth root for $\mathfrak{c}$, $B$ a standard base over $(D, \mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ and $\chi$ a splitting character. Assume the square law, the two triangle laws and the norm condition. Then the theta family satisfies the equations.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.Ker.Cd.PairingIndexBounds.Eq.J.eqP_of_neg` (also `Formalism.L2.PairingIndexBounds.Eq.J.eqP_of_neg`, the same statement for the second kind of datum).

*Proof.* The base has all its lattices, since $\mathfrak{c}$ is a curve ideal and $\delta$ is a nonzero twelfth root. The norm condition gives the Gauss sum rows of $B$. With the left triangle law, the Gauss rows give the first constant law; with the right triangle law they give the second. The vacuum lattice is a lattice and the vacuum functions span, since $B$ is standard. The equations follow from the square law, the triangle laws and the two constant laws. $\square$

**Corollary 1.23.7 (Equations from the paths).** In the theorem, the norm condition can be left out.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.Ker.Cd.PairingIndexBounds.Eq.J.eqP_of_paths` (also `Formalism.L2.PairingIndexBounds.Eq.J.eqP_of_paths`, the same statement for the second kind of datum).

*Proof.* The lemma gives the norm condition. $\square$

### Split squares

A square split for $B$ gives, for all self-dual $L, L_1 \subset E^N$ and $L', L'' \subset E^{N'}$, two subgroups $A$ and $A'$ of $V(N, N')$ that form a polarised family for the square of the four lattice pairs.

**Theorem 1.23.8 (Equations from a split square).** Let $\mathfrak{c}$, $\delta \ne 0$, $B$ and $\chi$ be as in the theorem, and assume that $B$ has square splits and the two triangle laws. Then the theta family satisfies the equations.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.Ker.Cd.PairingIndexBounds.Eq.Sq.eqP_of_split` (also `Formalism.L2.PairingIndexBounds.Eq.Sq.eqP_of_split`, the same statement for the second kind of datum).

*Proof.* We prove the square law and apply the corollary. Let $G$ be a theta function for $\Lambda(L, L')$. If $G = 0$, both sides are $0$. Otherwise $G$ is a nonzero fixed vector. The two paths of the square are two triples of admissible lattice pairs, each with triple data. The split square gives a split of each triple, and for a split triple the composed operator is the operator of the triple times a scalar $\mu$, with $\mu = 1$ when a nonzero fixed vector exists. The triple formula then gives both sides of the square law as the same function. $\square$

**Theorem 1.23.9 (Equations from square splits alone).** For the second kind of datum, if $B$ has square splits, then the theta family satisfies the equations.

Lean: `Formalism.L2.PairingIndexBounds.Eq.eqP_top`.

*Proof.* The left and right triangle laws hold for a standard base with a curve ideal and a nonzero twelfth root. Apply the theorem. $\square$

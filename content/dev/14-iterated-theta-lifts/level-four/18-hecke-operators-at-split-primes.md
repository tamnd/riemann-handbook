## Hecke operators at split primes

The series facts of a member need three properties of the Hecke operators at primes of $E$ that split over the real subfield. The first is integrality: a Hecke translate of an integral point is integral after a finite extension, and the values of integral sections at it are integral. The second is the existence of the transform: the Hecke operator is defined on sections of each weight and keeps the cusp forms. The third is the reading of a core by its constant $\kappa$ at each pinned frame. In this section we state the three properties and prove them from results of the chapters on twisted operators. They are used by [the local inputs of a member](/entry/exists-hyp-n-of-geometric-galois-compat/), and the transform property is unfolded in [the section transform](/entry/sec-transform/).

### Split-off primes

Let $E$ be a CM field with complex conjugation $x \mapsto \bar{x}$, and let $H$ be a host for $E$, $D_w$ a datum over $H$ with signature $(p, q)$, $\ell$ a level with level number $M'$, $X_o$ a moduli datum over $(D_w, \ell)$ and $c$ a coefficient system with field $F_c$.

**Definition 1.18.1 (Split-off prime).** Let $\mathfrak{P}$ be an ideal of $\mathcal{O}_E$ and $p$ a natural number. Then $\mathfrak{P}$ is split off over $p$ if $\mathfrak{P}$ is maximal, $\mathfrak{P} + \bar{\mathfrak{P}} = \mathcal{O}_E$, $p$ is prime, $p \in \mathfrak{P}$, and $p$ does not divide $M'$.

The condition $\mathfrak{P} + \bar{\mathfrak{P}} = \mathcal{O}_E$ says that $\mathfrak{P} \ne \bar{\mathfrak{P}}$, so the prime of the real subfield under $\mathfrak{P}$ splits in $E$. At such a prime the unitary group is a general linear group, and the Hecke operators come from correspondences of degree a power of $p$. For a split-off prime the norm of $\mathfrak{P}$ is not zero in $F_c$, since $p \in \mathfrak{P}$ and $p \ne 0$.

### Integrality

Let $T$ be a complete discrete valuation ring with valuation ring $V$ and fraction field $T_{\mathrm{fr}}$, with a map $\iota : F_c \to T_{\mathrm{fr}}$. A finite extension of $T$ is a complete discrete valuation ring $T'$ with a map $i : T_{\mathrm{fr}} \to T'_{\mathrm{fr}}$ that makes $T'$ a finite module, and that multiplies valuations by a positive integer $r$. An integral point of the moduli problem over $T$ is a point $y$ over $V$. Let $\Omega$ be an algebraically closed field over $F_c$ and $k : T_{\mathrm{fr}} \to \Omega$ an algebraic map. A point $z$ over $\Omega$ is Hecke related to $y$ at $(\mathfrak{P}, p, i)$ if there is a kernel datum $C$ of type $i$ at $\mathfrak{P}$ in the image $y_\Omega$ of $y$, and $z$ is the image of $y_\Omega$ under the Hecke correspondence defined by $C$.

**Definition 1.18.2 (Hecke integrality).** Let a class of integral points be given. Hecke frame integrality holds for this class if the following is true for each prime $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{O}_{F_c}$ with $p \notin \lambda$, each split-off $\mathfrak{P}$ over $p$, each $i$, each $T$ over $\lambda$, each algebraic integral point $y$ over $T$ in the class, and each $z$ Hecke related to $y$: there are a finite extension $T'$ of $T$, an algebraic integral point $y'$ over $T'$ in the class, and units $u_a$ of $V'$ such that $z$ is the image of $y'$ twisted by the units $u_a$. Hecke value integrality holds for the class if, for the same data, $z$ comes from a test point, and for each weight $a$, each degree $n$ and each section $f$ of weight $a$ and degree $n$ whose valuations at the algebraic points over $\lambda$ are nonnegative, the value $f(z)$ is integral over $V$. Hecke integrality is frame and value integrality, both for the class of good points and for the class of all points.

**Definition 1.18.3 (Hecke transform).** The section transform holds if, for every $E$, $H$, $D_w$, $\ell$, $X_o$, $c$, every split-off $\mathfrak{P}$ over $p$, every $i$ and every scalar $c_q \in F_c$, every weight $a$ and every even $n$, with $1 \le p + q$ and $i \le p + q$, there is a Hecke operator at $(\mathfrak{P}, p, i)$ with scalar $c_q$ on the sections of weight $a$ and degree $n$. The cusp transform holds if, for each core $T$ with the laws of modules and each index $j$ in the range, the Hecke operator at a split-off prime with $i \le r$ maps cusp forms of the moduli datum $X_o(j)$ to cusp forms. The Hecke transform is the conjunction of the two.

**Definition 1.18.4 (Hecke reading tie).** Let $D$ be an ambient datum, $G$ a finite group with a family $A$, $T$ a core with level $J$, $R$ a real datum over $A$ with the coefficient field of $T$, $F$ a face, $\kappa$ a family of weights and $v_0$ a family of vectors. The Hecke reading tie holds if the following is true whenever the level character of $R$ is the one of the reference tuple and the group, family, model and theta conditions of $R$ hold, and $T$, $R$, $\kappa$ and $v_0$ are tied: for each step $i$ and the distinguished embedding $\sigma_0$ of the field of $T$, if the space of $T$ at step $i + 1$ is the column space and the frame is pinned at $i$, then $T$ is read at $i$ by $\kappa(i)$.

**Theorem 1.18.5 (Hecke operators at split primes).** Hecke integrality holds, the Hecke transform holds, and the Hecke reading tie holds.

*Proof.* The three parts are proved separately. Integrality. For the class of all points and for the class of good points, frame integrality and value integrality are proved in the chapter on twisted operators, from the integrality of the frames and of the values for the two classes. We use that result as given. Transform. The theta generators are transported along the Hecke isogenies at split-off primes. This transport law gives the point laws of the Hecke operators: the kernels are finite, the correspondence is defined at all points, each point has one image, and sections are detected by their values at points. From the point laws the Hecke operator on sections of weight $a$ and degree $n$ exists, which is the section transform. The cusp transform holds with no further input. Reading tie. This follows from two facts for all ambient data. The first is a formula for the operators of a Weil datum in the model of a split place. The second says that at unramified places the stabilizers of the lattices fix the vacuum theta function. $\square$

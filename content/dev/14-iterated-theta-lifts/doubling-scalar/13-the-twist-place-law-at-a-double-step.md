## The twist place law at a double step

The laws of [the section on see-saw laws](/entry/laws-of-pin-resc/) concern a finite set $Q$ of places where a decoration is pinned. A second family of laws concerns the twist places themselves, where the level character of the chain is ramified. There the contracted theta function of a double step is not a vacuum, and the law says how to move the ramification from the theta function to the automorphic form: an operator $S_j$ acts on the form, an operator $\mathrm{op}_j$ acts on the theta function, and a monomial constant appears. This section proves this law at one inner position. It first proves that a contraction of two combinations of vacua is an operator applied to the contraction of the vacua. The law is used in [the twist data at one index](/entry/exists-choice/), which is a step of the twist place law for all tuples.

**Setting.** We keep the notation of the section on see-saw laws: common see-saw data $c$ with spaces $X_j$, global self-dual lattices $L_j$ of $X_j$, Bessel levels $B_{j, \rho}$ with spaces $V_0 = A \oplus L$, links $\kappa$ and volumes on the line parts; the data $v$ with lattices $N_j$, the set $P_T$ of twist places and coefficients $d_T(j)$; and a tuple $t$ with Weil representations. Points of a pair of spaces $(S, T)$ are matrices $u$ with adelic entries, and $\mathrm{H}(u)$ is the Heisenberg operator on functions of such points. A *combination* $e$ is a finite formal sum of points, and $e \cdot f = \sum_u e(u)\, \mathrm{H}(u) f$. It is *at* a set $P$ of places if its points have entries in the ideal of adeles supported at $P$, and *off* $P$ if they are at the complement. A function $f$ is *vacuum* for lattices $(L_S, L_T)$ at $P$ if it is a theta function and is the standard vacuum at each place of $P$. A function $C$ of two points is *bi-vacuum* if it is vacuum in each variable for the fixed other one. A *bi-operator* $\mathrm{op}$ is a finite list of pairs of combinations $(u_i, u_i')$, acting by $\mathrm{op}(C) = \sum_i \mathrm{bc}(u_i, u_i', C)$, where $\mathrm{bc}$ is the bi-combination. It is at $P$ if all $u_i$ and $u_i'$ are at $P$. For $f_1$ on points of $(V_0, S)$ and $f_2$ on points of $(S, V_0')$, the *contraction* is
$$\mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2)(u, u') = \int_{\mathcal{F}_L} f_2(u', x)\, f_1(u, \kappa(x))\, dx,$$
where $(u, y)$ is the point of $(V_0, S)$ with $A$-part $u$ and $L$-part $y$. The contracted theta function of a double step is $\mathrm{ctr}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2) = \mathrm{contr}(\Psi_1^\flat, \Psi_2^\flat)$, for the flattened functions $\Psi_i^\flat$.

The local data at the twist places form a *lattice shape datum* $o$. It has a nonarchimedean local field $k$ with a uniformiser $\varpi$; split points $p_i$ of $k$ for $i$ in a finite list $I$; additive characters $\chi_i$ of $k$ of conductor one; local frames $\beta_j(i)$ of $X_j$ and $\beta_0(i)$ of the Bessel spaces; pairs $Q_j(i)$ of lattices of $k^{n_j}$; and self-dual lattices $M_{j, \rho}$ of the Bessel spaces. The datum *fits* $v$ if the places of the $p_i$ are exactly the twist places, the frames are good and glued at each place, $d_T(j)$ represents at the twist places the product of the local functions $\phi_1(Q_j(i), Q_{j+1}(i))$, and the ramified set of $v$ at $j$ is the set $\mathrm{Ram}_j$ of continuous automorphic functions on the group of $X_j$ that are ramified for $(\beta_j(i), Q_j(i))$ at each $i$. For units $\varepsilon_i$ of $\mathcal{O}_k$, let $S_\varepsilon$ be the composite over $i \in I$ of the local twisting operators at $p_i$, built from $Q_j(i)$, $\varpi$, the residue character of $\chi_i$ and $\varepsilon_i$. For reals $r_i$, integers $e_i$ and a character $\chi$ of the ideles, put $\mathrm{mono}(r, e, \chi) = \prod_{i} r_i\, \chi(\varpi_i)^{e_i}$, where $\varpi_i$ is the idele of $\varpi$ at $p_i$.

**Theorem 2.13.1 (Contraction of combinations of vacua).** Let $p_i$ be split points whose places $P$ are distinct, let the additive character be standard, and let $B$ and $B'$ be Bessel levels with a link $\kappa$, a space $S$ and a volume on the line part. Let $M$, $L_S$ and $M'$ be self-dual lattices of $V_0$, $S$ and $V_0'$, and let the local frames of $V_0$ and $V_0'$ be sum frames that match $M$ and $M'$ with the lattices of the last kernel of given local lattices. Then for every pair of combinations $e_1, e_2$ at $P$ there is a bi-operator $\mathrm{op}$ at $P$ such that, for all $f_1$ vacuum for $(M, L_S)$ at $P$ and $f_2$ vacuum for $(L_S, M')$ at $P$,
$$\mathrm{contr}(e_1 \cdot f_1,\ e_2 \cdot f_2) = \mathrm{op}\big(\mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2)\big).$$

*Proof.* Write a point $u$ of $(V_0, S)$ as $(u_A, u_L)$ and a point $u'$ of $(S, V_0')$ as $(u'_A, u'_L)$. The parts of a point at $P$ are again at $P$.

1. *Line translates.* Let $y$ be an $L$-part at $P$. If the point $(0, y)$ lies in the lattice of $(M, L_S)$ at $P$, then it lies in the closure of the isotropic halves of the lattice, and so $\mathrm{H}(0, y)$ acts on all vacua by one scalar $c(y)$. If $(0, y)$ is not in that lattice, then $\mathrm{contr}(\mathrm{H}(0, y) f_1, f_2) = 0$, by the lemma on line translates off the lattice. Put $c(y) = 0$ in this case. In both cases
$$\mathrm{contr}(\mathrm{H}(0, y) f_1, f_2) = c(y)\, \mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2).$$
2. *Moving the right line part.* For $L$-parts $y$ and $w$ at $P$, the contraction is invariant under a Heisenberg translate of the right variable by $(0, w)$ and of the left variable by $(0, \kappa(w))$, by the Heisenberg invariance of the Bessel line contraction. The link $\kappa$ keeps points at $P$. Hence, with $\psi$ the additive character and $\langle\, ,\rangle$ the symplectic form,
$$\mathrm{contr}(\mathrm{H}(0, y) f_1, \mathrm{H}(0, w) f_2) = \psi\big(\langle -(0, \kappa w), (0, y)\rangle\big)\, c(y - \kappa w)\, \mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2).$$
Call this constant $a(u, u')$ when $y = u_L$ and $w = u'_L$.
3. *Splitting a point.* The two summands of $u = (u_A, 0) + (0, u_L)$ are orthogonal, so $\mathrm{H}(u) = \mathrm{H}(u_A, 0)\,\mathrm{H}(0, u_L)$, and the same holds for $u'$. The $A$-parts move out of the contraction as Heisenberg operators in its two variables. With step 2,
$$\mathrm{contr}(\mathrm{H}(u) f_1, \mathrm{H}(u') f_2) = a(u, u')\, \mathrm{H}(u_A) \otimes \mathrm{H}(u'_A)\, \mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2).$$
4. *Expansion.* The integrand of a contraction of two theta functions is continuous and periodic, so it is integrable on $\mathcal{F}_L$. Heisenberg translates at $P$ of theta functions are theta functions. Hence the contraction of $e_1 \cdot f_1$ and $e_2 \cdot f_2$ is the double sum over $u \in \mathrm{supp}\, e_1$ and $u' \in \mathrm{supp}\, e_2$ of $e_1(u)\, e_2(u')\, \mathrm{contr}(\mathrm{H}(u) f_1, \mathrm{H}(u') f_2)$.
5. *The operator.* Let $\mathrm{op}$ be indexed by the pairs $(u, u')$ of $\mathrm{supp}\, e_1 \times \mathrm{supp}\, e_2$. The pair of combinations at $(u, u')$ is the single point $u_A$ with coefficient $e_1(u)\, e_2(u')\, a(u, u')$, and the single point $u'_A$ with coefficient one. These are at $P$. A bi-combination of two single points is the product of the two Heisenberg operators and the coefficients. So steps 3 and 4 give the identity. $\square$

The operator does not depend on $f_1$ and $f_2$. This is why the scalars of step 1 are chosen once, for all vacua.

**Theorem 2.13.2 (Twist place law at a double step).** Assume the validity of $c$ and $v$, and let $o$ be a lattice shape datum that fits $v$. Let $j + 2(\sigma + 1) + 2 = m$. Then there are units $\varepsilon_i$ of $\mathcal{O}_k$, a bi-operator $\mathrm{op}$ at $P_T$, reals $r_i > 0$ and integers $e_i$ with this property. For every tuple $t$ that is valid, satisfies WeilOK for $v$ and is unramified at $P_T$, and for all $\Psi_1$ vacuum for $(N_j, N_{j+1})$ and $\Psi_2$ vacuum for $(N_{j+1}, N_{j+2})$ at $P_T$:

1. $\mathrm{op}(\mathrm{ctr}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2))$ is bi-vacuum for $(L_j, L_{j+1}, L_{j+2})$ at $P_T$;
2. for all coefficients $d, d'$ off $P_T$ and every $\varphi \in \mathrm{Ram}_j$,
$$\mathrm{bl}\big(\varphi, d, d', \mathrm{ctr}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2)\big) = \mathrm{mono}(r, e, \chi_W)\ \mathrm{bl}\Big(S_\varepsilon \varphi,\ d, d',\ \mathrm{bc}\big(d_T(j), d_T(j+1), \mathrm{op}(\mathrm{ctr}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2))\big)\Big),$$
where $\chi_W$ is the Weil character of the step $j$ of $t$.

*Proof.* Write $B = B_{j, \sigma+1}$ and $B' = B_{j+2, \sigma}$, with spaces $V_0$ and $V_0'$, and $M = M_{j, \sigma+1}$, $M' = M_{j+2, \sigma}$. The twist places are the places of the distinct points $p_i$.

1. *New lattices.* There are self-dual lattices $M_0$ of $V_0$ and $M_0'$ of $V_0'$ which, under the frames $\beta_0(i)$, are the last kernels of the local lattices of $Q_j(i)$ and $Q_{j+2}(i)$, by the lemma on self-dual lattices with prescribed completions at finitely many split places.
2. *Vacuum change.* There are combinations $e_1, e_1'$ at $P_T$ such that $e_1$ moves vacua for $(M, L_{j+1})$ to vacua for $(M_0, L_{j+1})$, $e_1'$ undoes $e_1$, and $e_1$ keeps the local representation of each vacuum. In the same way there are $e_2, e_2'$ for $(L_{j+1}, M')$ and $(L_{j+1}, M_0')$.
3. *Extension by zero.* By the lemma on extension by zero at split places, $V_0 = A \oplus L$ splits orthogonally for $M_0$ at each twist place, and the extension by zero of $d_T(j)$ to $V_0$ represents the local functions $\phi_1(Q_j(i), Q_{j+1}(i))$ tensored with the vector of the lattice $Q_{j+1}(i)$. The same holds for $d_T(j+1)$ on the right, with the dual vector.
4. *The contraction operator.* The last theorem, applied to $e_1'$ and $e_2'$ and to $M_0$, $L_{j+1}$ and $M_0'$, gives a bi-operator $\mathrm{op}_0$ at $P_T$ with $\mathrm{contr}(e_1' \cdot f_1, e_2' \cdot f_2) = \mathrm{op}_0(\mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2))$ for vacua $f_1, f_2$.
5. *Inversion.* By the lemma on bi-vacuum operators, either $\mathrm{op}_0$ is zero on all bi-vacua for $(L_j, L_{j+1}, L_{j+2})$ at $P_T$, or there is a bi-operator $\mathrm{op}'$ at $P_T$ with $\mathrm{op}'(\mathrm{op}_0(C)) = C$ for all such bi-vacua $C$.
6. *The constants.* The theorem on the value of small pairs of a double step at all glued split places gives the units $\varepsilon_i$, and then reals $r_i > 0$ and integers $e_i$ for the ranks of the step, with the identity of step 9 below.
7. *Reduction to vacua.* Let $t$ and $\Psi_1, \Psi_2$ be as in the theorem. Flattening keeps vacua, so $\Psi_1^\flat$ is vacuum for $(M, L_{j+1})$ and $\Psi_2^\flat$ for $(L_{j+1}, M')$. Put $f_1 = e_1 \cdot \Psi_1^\flat$ and $f_2 = e_2 \cdot \Psi_2^\flat$. Then $\Psi_i^\flat = e_i' \cdot f_i$, so by step 4
$$\mathrm{ctr}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2) = \mathrm{op}_0\big(\mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2)\big).$$
The contraction $\mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2)$ is bi-vacuum for $(L_j, L_{j+1}, L_{j+2})$, by the lemma on the vacuum property of the contraction after extension by zero, which uses the orthogonal splittings of step 3. Also $\Psi_1^\flat$ and $\Psi_2^\flat$ have the local representations of the extended coefficients $d_T(j)$ and $d_T(j+1)$ applied to $f_1$ and $f_2$, by steps 2 and 3.
8. *The zero case.* If $\mathrm{op}_0$ is zero on bi-vacua, then $\mathrm{ctr}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2) = 0$ by step 7. Take for $\mathrm{op}$ the empty bi-operator. Then item 1 holds since $0$ is bi-vacuum, and both sides of item 2 are lifts of $0$, which are $0$.
9. *The inverse case.* Otherwise take $\mathrm{op} = \mathrm{op}'$. By step 7, $\mathrm{op}'(\mathrm{ctr}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2)) = \mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2)$, which is bi-vacuum; this is item 1. For item 2, the bi-combination of $\mathrm{contr}(\Psi_1^\flat, \Psi_2^\flat)$ with $d, d'$ is the contraction of the extended combinations applied to the two functions. The lift of a contraction is the double see-saw lift $\mathrm{hand}_0$, by the theorem that the double step is two theta column lifts. The identity of step 6, with the local representations of step 7 and $\varphi \in \mathrm{Ram}_j$, gives
$$\mathrm{hand}_0\big(\varphi, d \cdot \Psi_1^\flat, d' \cdot \Psi_2^\flat\big) = \mathrm{mono}(r, e, \chi_W)\ \mathrm{hand}_0\big(S_\varepsilon \varphi,\ d \cdot d_T(j) \cdot f_1,\ d' \cdot d_T(j+1) \cdot f_2\big),$$
with the combinations extended by zero. The function $S_\varepsilon \varphi$ is continuous automorphic, since the twisting operators are bijections on these functions at the glued places. Read the right side back as a lift of a contraction, and then as the bi-combination with $d, d'$ of the bi-combination with $d_T(j), d_T(j+1)$ of $\mathrm{contr}(f_1, f_2) = \mathrm{op}(\mathrm{ctr}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2))$. $\square$

The units $\varepsilon_i$, the operator and the constants do not depend on the tuple $t$. Only the Weil character $\chi_W$ in the monomial depends on $t$.

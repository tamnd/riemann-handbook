## The twist place law at the last steps

[The last section](/entry/law-level-prime-double/) proved the twist place law at an inner double step. This section proves it at the last two steps of the chain, where the contracted theta function is replaced by the last see-saw datum and the law is an identity of integrals over the top group. The new input is local: at one split place, the last see-saw datum of a theta function with a given local datum can be moved to a bi-vacuum by explicit combinations, and moved back by the combinations that represent the local datum. We prove this at one place, then at finitely many places, and then the law. The law is used in [the twist data at one index](/entry/exists-choice/).

**Setting.** We keep the notation of the last section. Let $V_0 = A \oplus L$ and $X = T \oplus L''$ be orthogonal sums with lines $L$ and $L''$ spanned by line vectors $\zeta$ and $\zeta_0$, and let $S$ be a third space. Let $\tau \colon L'' \to L$ satisfy $\tau^* G_L \tau = -\xi_0 \bar\xi_0\, G_{L''}$ for the Gram matrices, with $\tau \zeta_0 = \lambda \zeta$. For $\Xi$ on points of $(V_0, S)$ and $\Phi_2$ on points of $(S, T)$, the *last see-saw datum* is the function of a point $u$ of $(A, S)$ and a point $x = (x_T, x_L)$ of $(S, X)$ given by
$$\mathrm{lst}(\Xi, \Phi_2)(u, x) = \Phi_2(x_T)\ \Xi\big(u, \kappa_\tau(x_L)\big),$$
where $\kappa_\tau$ carries the $L''$-part to an $L$-part through $\tau$. At a split point $p$, local frames identify points with matrices over $k$. For a vacuum $f$ and a Schwartz-Bruhat function $\Psi$ on matrices, a function $F$ *lies over* $\Psi$ relative to $f$ if $F$ is the image of $\Psi$ in the local model in which $f$ is the unit vector. A combination $c$ *represents* $\Psi$ if $c \cdot f$ lies over $\Psi$ relative to $f$ for every vacuum $f$. For finitely many points $p_i$ these notions are taken at each $p_i$ with data $\Psi_i$. For a pair $Q$ of a lattice of $k^n$ and a function on it, and a lattice $\Lambda$ of $k^{n''}$, let $\phi_1(Q, \mathrm{lp}(\Lambda))$ be the local function of the pair $Q$ and the last pair of $\Lambda$, and let $\Psi \otimes \iota_Q$ be the tensor of $\Psi$ with the indicator of $Q$ twisted by the residue character of $\chi$.

**Theorem 2.14.1 (Last see-saw datum at one split place).** Let $p$ be a split point with unit adelic image, $\varpi$ a uniformiser, $\chi$ a character of $k$ of conductor one that matches the standard additive character, and assume that $\lambda$ is a unit at $p$. Then there is a unit $\varepsilon$ of $\mathcal{O}_k$ with this property. Let the local frames be sum frames for the two orthogonal sums, let $Q$ and $\Lambda$ be as above, let $\Psi$ be a function on matrices, and let $L_A, L_S, L_T, L_X$ be self-dual lattices that match $Q$ and $\Lambda$ under the frames. Let $c$ and $c'$ be combinations at $p$ that represent $\Psi$ and $\phi_1(\varepsilon Q, \mathrm{lp}(\Lambda))$. Then there are combinations $e, e'$ at $p$ such that for all lattices $L_1, L_2$, every $\Xi$ that lies over $\Psi \otimes \iota_Q$ relative to some $f_1$, and every $\Phi_2$ vacuum for $(L_S, L_T)$ at $p$:

1. $\mathrm{bc}(e, e', \mathrm{lst}(\Xi, \Phi_2))$ is bi-vacuum for $(L_A, L_S, L_X)$ at $p$;
2. $\mathrm{lst}(\Xi, \Phi_2) = \mathrm{bc}\big(c, c', \mathrm{bc}(e, e', \mathrm{lst}(\Xi, \Phi_2))\big)$.

*Proof.* Write $C = \mathrm{lst}(\Xi, \Phi_2)$.

1. *The unit.* By the lemma that the last see-saw datum is a lattice pair over a vacuum, there is $\varepsilon$ such that, under the hypotheses, for every $u$ the row $x \mapsto C(u, x)$ lies over $\phi_1(\varepsilon Q, \mathrm{lp}(\Lambda))$ relative to some vacuum.
2. *Carrying back.* By the lemma on carrying a split place datum back to the base vacuum, there is $e$ at $p$ such that, whenever $F$ lies over $\Psi$ relative to a vacuum, $e \cdot F$ is vacuum and $c \cdot (e \cdot F) = F$. In the same way there is $e'$ for $c'$.
3. *The indicator.* The function $\iota_Q$ is not zero: its value at $0$ is $1$, since $0$ lies in the lattice and the character is $1$ at $0$.
4. *Columns.* By the lemma on slices along the line, which uses step 3, for every $L$-part $y$ the function $u \mapsto \Xi(u, y)$ lies over $\Psi$ relative to some vacuum. By the definition of $\mathrm{lst}$, each column $u \mapsto C(u, x)$ is the multiple $\Phi_2(x_T)$ of such a function. With step 2, $e \cdot C(\cdot, x)$ is vacuum and $c \cdot (e \cdot C(\cdot, x)) = C(\cdot, x)$.
5. *Rows.* By steps 1 and 2, $e' \cdot C(u, \cdot)$ is vacuum and $c' \cdot (e' \cdot C(u, \cdot)) = C(u, \cdot)$.
6. *Item 1.* For fixed $x$, the first variable of $\mathrm{bc}(e, e', C)$ is a finite sum, over the points of $e'$, of multiples of $e \cdot C(\cdot, x')$. These are vacua by step 4, and a finite sum of vacua is a vacuum. For fixed $u$ the same holds in the second variable by step 5.
7. *Item 2.* Combinations in the two variables commute. So $c'$ meets $e'$ in each row, and step 5 removes them. Then $c$ meets $e$ in each column, and step 4 removes them. $\square$

**Theorem 2.14.2 (Last see-saw datum at finitely many split places).** Let $p_i$, for $i$ in a finite list $I$, be split points at distinct places $P$ with unit adelic images, and let $\chi_i$ and $\lambda$ be as in the last theorem at each $p_i$. Then there are units $\varepsilon_i$ with this property. Let the local frames be sum frames, $Q_i$ and $\Lambda_i$ pairs and lattices, $\Psi_i$ functions, and $L_A, L_S, L_T, L_X, L_1$ self-dual lattices that match $Q_i$ and $\Lambda_i$. Let $c$ and $c'$ be at $P$ and represent $(\Psi_i)$ and $(\phi_1(\varepsilon_i Q_i, \mathrm{lp}(\Lambda_i)))$. Then there are $e, e'$ at $P$ such that for $f_1$ vacuum for $(L_1, L_S)$ at $P$, every $\Xi$ that lies over $(\Psi_i \otimes \iota_{Q_i})$ relative to $f_1$, and every $\Phi_2$ vacuum for $(L_S, L_T)$ at $P$, items 1 and 2 of the last theorem hold at $P$.

*Proof.* Again $C = \mathrm{lst}(\Xi, \Phi_2)$.

1. *Local units.* Let $\varepsilon_i$ be the unit of the last theorem at $p_i$.
2. *Local data are Schwartz-Bruhat.* Apply the representation property of $c$ to the zero vacuum. Its local steps show that each $\Psi_i$ is Schwartz-Bruhat. The same holds for $c'$.
3. *Local combinations.* By the lemma on change of base vacuum, there are combinations $r_i$ and $r_i'$ at $p_i$ that represent $\Psi_i$ and $\phi_1(\varepsilon_i Q_i, \mathrm{lp}(\Lambda_i))$ at $p_i$.
4. *Local inverses.* The last theorem at $p_i$, with $r_i$ and $r_i'$, gives $e_i$ and $e_i'$ at $p_i$.
5. *Commutation.* Points at distinct places have symplectic pairing zero. Hence combinations at $p_i$ and $p_{i'}$ commute for $i \neq i'$, and so do bi-combinations. A bi-combination at $p_{i'}$ keeps the bi-vacuum property at $p_i$.
6. *Product of local inverses.* For each $i$, $\Xi$ lies over $\Psi_i \otimes \iota_{Q_i}$ relative to some vacuum at $p_i$, since the other local steps commute and keep vacua at $p_i$. So step 4 applies at each $p_i$. The lemma on products of local inverses, with step 5, gives $e$ and $e'$ in the additive monoids generated by the points of the $e_i$ and $e_i'$, which are therefore at $P$, such that $\mathrm{bc}(e, e', C)$ is bi-vacuum at each $p_i$ and $C$ is the composite over $I$ of the $\mathrm{bc}(r_i, r_i', \cdot)$ applied to $\mathrm{bc}(e, e', C)$.
7. *Theta functions.* If $I$ is not empty, the rows and columns of $\mathrm{bc}(e, e', C)$ are theta functions by step 6. If $I$ is empty, then $\Xi = f_1$ and $\mathrm{bc}(e, e', C) = C$. The rows and columns of $C$ are then slices of the theta functions $f_1$ and $\Phi_2$, carried by $\kappa_\tau$ and multiplied by constants, and are theta functions.
8. *Item 1.* A theta function that is vacuum at each of finitely many distinct places is vacuum at all of them. With steps 6 and 7 this gives item 1.
9. *Item 2.* On vacua, $c \cdot f$ equals the composite of the $r_i \cdot$, by the uniqueness in the lemma on data as combinations at split places; the same holds for $c'$. By item 1 this applies to $\mathrm{bc}(e, e', C)$, and step 6 gives item 2. $\square$

**Theorem 2.14.3 (Twist place law at the last steps).** Assume the validity of $c$ and $v$, and let $o$ be a lattice shape datum that fits $v$. Let $j + 2 = m$. Then there are units $\varepsilon_i$, a bi-operator $\mathrm{op}$ at $P_T$, reals $r_i > 0$ and integers $e_i$ with this property. For every tuple $t$ that is valid, satisfies WeilOK for $v$, and is unramified at $P_T$ on its Bessel data and on its Weil representations, and for all $\Psi_1$ vacuum for $(N_j, N_{j+1})$ and $\Psi_2$ vacuum for $(N_{j+1}, N_T)$ at $P_T$:

1. $\mathrm{op}(\mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2))$ is bi-vacuum for $(L_j, L_{j+1}, L_{j+2})$ at $P_T$;
2. for all $d, d'$ off $P_T$, every $\varphi \in \mathrm{Ram}_j$ and every continuous automorphic $f$ on the top group,
$$\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathcal{F}} f(h)\, \mathrm{bl}\big(\varphi, d, d', \mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2)\big)(E(h, 1))\, dh \\ &\quad = \mathrm{mono}(r, e, \chi_W) \int_{\mathcal{F}} f(h)\, \mathrm{bl}\Big(S_\varepsilon \varphi, d, d', \mathrm{bc}\big(d_T(j), d_T(j+1), \mathrm{op}(\mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2))\big)\Big)(E(h, 1))\, dh.\end{aligned}$$

Here $\mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2)$ is the last see-saw datum of the flattened $\Xi = \Psi_1^\flat$ and of $\Phi_2$, the function $\Psi_2$ extended by zero along the Jacobi datum, with $V_0$ the Bessel space at $(j, 0)$ and $\tau$ the twist datum of $v$.

*Proof.* Let $M = M_{j, 0}$.

1. *The constants.* The fit of $o$ gives $\lambda$ with $\tau \zeta_0 = \lambda \zeta$, a unit at each $p_i$. The last theorem gives units $\varepsilon_i'$. The lemma on the last kernel datum in the last line frame gives lattices $\Lambda_i$, units $u_i$ and frames of $N_T$ that match $\Lambda_i$, such that $d_T(j+1)$ represents $\phi_1(u_i Q_{j+1}(i), \mathrm{lp}(\Lambda_i))$. Put $\varepsilon_i = (\varepsilon_i')^{-1} u_i$ and $Q_i = \varepsilon_i Q_{j+1}(i)$. Then $\varepsilon_i' Q_i = u_i Q_{j+1}(i)$, so $d_T(j+1)$ represents $\phi_1(\varepsilon_i' Q_i, \mathrm{lp}(\Lambda_i))$. The theorem on the pairing of small pairs at all glued places gives $r_i > 0$ and $e_i$.
2. *The combination $e_0$.* By the lemma on data as combinations, there is $e_0$ at $P_T$ that represents $\phi_1(Q_j(i), Q_{j+1}(i)) \otimes \iota_{Q_i}$ for $(M, L_{j+1})$.
3. *The local inverses.* The last theorem, with $c = d_T(j)$ and $c' = d_T(j+1)$, gives $e_1, e_2$ at $P_T$.
4. *The operator.* By the lemma on combinations of the last see-saw datum, there is $\mathrm{op}_0$ at $P_T$ with $\mathrm{lst}(e_0 \cdot \Xi, \Phi_2) = \mathrm{op}_0(\mathrm{lst}(\Xi, \Phi_2))$. By the lemma on bi-vacuum operators, there is $\mathrm{op}$ at $P_T$ with $\mathrm{op}(C) = \mathrm{bc}(e_1, e_2, \mathrm{op}_0(C))$ for all $C$.
5. *Item 1.* Let $t$, $\Psi_1$ and $\Psi_2$ be given. Flattening keeps vacua, so $\Xi$ is vacuum for $(M, L_{j+1})$ and $\Phi_2$ for $(L_{j+1}, N_T)$. By step 2, $e_0 \cdot \Xi$ lies over the data of step 3 relative to $\Xi$. By step 4, $\mathrm{op}(\mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2)) = \mathrm{bc}(e_1, e_2, \mathrm{lst}(e_0 \cdot \Xi, \Phi_2))$, which is bi-vacuum by step 3.
6. *The right side.* By item 2 of step 3, $\mathrm{bc}(d_T(j), d_T(j+1), \mathrm{op}(\mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2))) = \mathrm{lst}(e_0 \cdot \Xi, \Phi_2)$.
7. *Splitting the combinations.* By the same lemma on combinations of the last see-saw datum, there are $a_l, b_l$ off $P_T$, for $l$ in a finite set, with $\mathrm{bc}(d, d', \mathrm{lst}(\Xi', \Phi')) = \sum_l \mathrm{lst}(a_l \cdot \Xi', b_l \cdot \Phi')$ for all $\Xi', \Phi'$.
8. *Small pairs.* WeilOK gives the Weil data of the last step, and the unramified conditions on $t$ give characters unramified at $P_T$. By the additivity of the last double lift, the integral of $f$ against the lift of $\sum_l \mathrm{lst}(\Xi_l, \Phi_l)$ is the sum of the small pair values $\mathrm{sp}(\varphi, \Xi_l, \Phi_l, f)$. Apply this on both sides, with $\Xi_l = a_l \cdot \Xi$ and $a_l \cdot e_0 \cdot \Xi$, and $\Phi_l = b_l \cdot \Phi_2$. Combinations off $P_T$ keep vacua and local representations at $P_T$. The function $S_\varepsilon \varphi$ is continuous automorphic.
9. *Term by term.* The identity of the small pairs of step 1 gives $\mathrm{sp}(\varphi, a_l \cdot \Xi, b_l \cdot \Phi_2, f) = \mathrm{mono}(r, e, \chi_W)\, \mathrm{sp}(S_\varepsilon \varphi, a_l \cdot e_0 \cdot \Xi, b_l \cdot \Phi_2, f)$, since $a_l \cdot e_0 \cdot \Xi$ lies over the data of step 2 relative to $a_l \cdot \Xi$ and $\varphi$ is ramified. Summing over $l$ gives item 2. $\square$

## Good models of CM elliptic curves

Let $L \subset \mathbb{C}$ be a lattice with [[complex multiplication|427N6S6QQW]] by the ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, and let $\ell \geq 5$ be a prime that does not divide $d$. This section proves two facts about the curve $y^2 = 4x^3 - g_2(L)x - g_3(L)$ at $\ell$. First, global Artin reciprocity gives the curve a model with good reduction over the completed maximal unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. Second, if $\ell$ is inert in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, the formal group of a good model is isomorphic over the integers of that field to a fixed formal group of height two. The two results are the period inputs of [the Artin row of the proof](/entry/serv-h-row-of-artin/) and of [the formal isomorphism at a place of good reduction](/entry/exists-genus-one-formal-iso-of-unramified-good-reduction/). They are used in the step [unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/). The theta lifts of the rest of the chapter do not use them.

We use [complex multiplication](/learn/elliptic-curves/#complex-multiplication), [the Weierstrass function of a lattice](/learn/elliptic-curves/#lattices-and-the-weierstrass-function), [minimal models and reduction](/learn/elliptic-curves/#reduction-and-minimal-models), and [formal group laws](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws) with their [logarithms](/learn/formal-groups/#the-invariant-derivation-and-the-logarithm) and [height](/learn/formal-groups/#height).

**Definition 2.1.1 (CM lattice).** Let $d \geq 1$ be a squarefree integer with $d \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, and put $\omega_d = (1 + i\sqrt{d})/2$. A lattice $L \subset \mathbb{C}$ *has CM by* $d$ if $\omega_d L \subseteq L$. Then $\mathbb{Z}[\omega_d]$, the ring of integers of $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, acts on $L$. Write $g_2(L)$ and $g_3(L)$ for the Weierstrass invariants of $L$.

The identity $(1 - 2\omega_d)^2 = -d$ holds, because $1 - 2\omega_d = -i\sqrt{d}$. We use it below.

**Definition 2.1.2 (Unramified field and good model).** Fix a prime $\ell$ and let $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ be the completion of an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. Let $K^{\mathrm{ur}} \subset \mathbb{C}_\ell$ be the closure of the field generated by the roots of unity of order prime to $\ell$. This is the completion of the maximal unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. Fix a field isomorphism $\iota \colon \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}_\ell$. A pair $(g_2, g_3) \in \mathbb{C}_\ell^2$ is a *good model* of $L$ at $(\ell, \iota)$ if

- $g_2$ and $g_3$ lie in $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$, with $|g_2| \leq 1$ and $|g_3| \leq 1$;
- the discriminant is a unit: $|g_2^3 - 27 g_3^2| = 1$;
- there is $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}_\ell^\times$ with $g_2 = \lambda^4 \iota(g_2(L))$ and $g_3 = \lambda^6 \iota(g_3(L))$.

The last condition says that the curve $y^2 = 4x^3 - g_2 x - g_3$ is isomorphic over $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ to the image under $\iota$ of the curve of $L$. The first two say that this model has good reduction at $\ell$ (for $\ell \geq 5$).

**Theorem 2.1.3 (Global Artin reciprocity).** Let $E$ be a number field with an algebraic closure $\Omega$, and let $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ be the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension of $E$ in $\Omega$. There is a continuous homomorphism $\mathrm{art} \colon \mathbb{A}_E^\times \to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, open and onto, whose kernel is the closure of $E^\times$ times the connected component of the identity at the infinite places. For each finite place $v$, the image of the local units $\mathcal{O}_v^\times$ is the image of the inertia group at $v$, a uniformizer at $v$ maps to the inverse of a Frobenius at $v$, and the closure of the image of $E_v^\times$ is the image of the decomposition group at $v$. (J. Tate, *Global class field theory*, Chapter VII of J. W. S. Cassels and A. Fröhlich, *Algebraic Number Theory*.)

See also [global class field theory](/learn/class-field-theory/#global-class-field-theory) and [decomposition groups, inertia and Frobenius](/learn/galois-representations/#decomposition-groups-inertia-and-frobenius). We take this theorem as given.

**Theorem 2.1.4 (CM curves have good models).** Assume global Artin reciprocity. Let $d \geq 1$ be squarefree with $d \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, let $L$ have CM by $d$, let $\ell \geq 5$ be a prime with $\ell \nmid d$, and let $\iota \colon \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}_\ell$ be a field isomorphism. Then $L$ has a good model at $(\ell, \iota)$.

*Proof.* The proof has two inputs and one main lemma.

First, reciprocity gives an ideal class version of the Artin map. For each number field $E$ with algebraic closure $\Omega$ there is a homomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/E) \to \mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_E)$ to the [class group](/learn/number-fields/#ideals-and-the-class-group) ([[ideal class group|WQ46BD3ARY]]) with open kernel, which kills every inertia element, and which sends an arithmetic Frobenius at a finite place $v$ to the class of $v$. This follows from the theorem above, since the class group is the quotient of the ideles by $E^\times$, the units at the finite places and the infinite part.

Second, a form of the Chebotarev density theorem, proved without further hypotheses: for a finite set $S$ of finite places of $E$, an open normal subgroup $H$ of $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/E)$ that contains the inertia at each place outside $S$, and any $\sigma$, there is a place $v \notin S$ of degree one, with a valuation ring of $\Omega$ above it and an arithmetic Frobenius $\varphi$ there, such that $\sigma^{-1}\varphi \in H$.

[The theorem on good models of CM lattices](/entry/cm-lattice-good-model-unramified/) takes these two statements as its hypotheses and gives a good model of $L$ at $(\ell, \iota)$ for all $d$, $L$, $\ell$ and $\iota$ as above. This proves the claim. $\square$

**Definition 2.1.5 (The height two logarithm).** For a prime $\ell$ let
$$\lambda_\ell(X) = \sum_{j \geq 0} \frac{X^{\ell^{2j}}}{\ell^{j}} \in \mathbb{C}_\ell[[X]].$$
It is the logarithm of a formal group law of height two over $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, by the criterion of Honda. For $g_2, g_3$ in a field $k$ of characteristic zero, let $u(z) \in z + z^3 k[[z]]$ be the power series
$$u(z) = z \cdot \frac{1 + \sum_{k \geq 2} c_k z^{2k}}{1 - \sum_{k \geq 2} (k - 1) c_k z^{2k}},$$
where $c_2 = g_2/20$, $c_3 = g_3/28$ and $c_k = \frac{3}{(2k+1)(k-3)} \sum_{m=2}^{k-2} c_m c_{k-m}$ for $k \geq 4$.

The $c_k$ are the coefficients of the Laurent series $\wp(z) = z^{-2} + \sum_{k \geq 2} c_k z^{2k-2}$ of the Weierstrass function. A short computation with $\wp'(z) = -2z^{-3} + \sum_{k} (2k - 2) c_k z^{2k-3}$ shows that $u(z) = -2\wp(z)/\wp'(z)$. So $u$ is the expansion, in the elliptic logarithm $z$, of the formal parameter $u = -2x/y$ of the curve at its origin. That is, $u$ is the exponential of the formal group of the curve in the parameter $u$.

**Theorem 2.1.6 (Formal groups at an inert prime).** Let $d$, $L$, $\ell$ and $\iota$ be as in the last theorem, and assume also that $-d$ is not a square modulo $\ell$. Let $(g_2, g_3)$ be a good model of $L$ at $(\ell, \iota)$. Then there is $\varepsilon \in K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ with $|\varepsilon| = 1$ such that every coefficient of the power series $u(\varepsilon \lambda_\ell(X))$ lies in $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and has absolute value at most $1$.

So $X \mapsto u(\varepsilon\lambda_\ell(X))$ is an isomorphism, with integral coefficients, from the formal group with logarithm $\varepsilon\lambda_\ell$ to the formal group of the good model.

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$ be the ring of integers of $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$. The field $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is complete, its residue field is algebraically closed, and it has a Frobenius lift $\sigma$: a continuous automorphism that induces $x \mapsto x^\ell$ on the residue field. Let $f$ and $e$ be the logarithm and the exponential of the formal group of $y^2 = 4x^3 - g_2 x - g_3$ in the parameter $u$, over $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$; their images in $\mathbb{C}_\ell[[X]]$ are the logarithm of the curve and the series $u$. For $a \in K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ put $[a](X) = e(a f(X))$.

1. Since $\ell$ is odd, $|2| = 1$. With $|g_2|, |g_3| \leq 1$, Honda theory shows that $(f, e)$ is an integral pair: the formal group law $e(f(X) + f(Y))$ has coefficients in $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$.
2. Since $-d$ is not a square modulo $\ell$, the prime $\ell$ is inert in $K$. The element $w = \iota(\omega_d)$ lies in $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and $\sigma(w) = 1 - w$, since $\sigma$ acts on $\mathcal{O}_K \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ as the nontrivial automorphism. So $\sigma^2(w) = w$. Also $\sigma(w) - w = 1 - 2w$ and $(1 - 2w)^2 = -d$. Since $\ell \nmid d$, $|d| = 1$, so $|\sigma(w) - w| = 1$.
3. The series $[w]$ has coefficients of absolute value at most $1$. This is the integrality of the complex multiplication by $\omega_d$ on the good model at the inert prime.
4. The formal group has height at most two: there is $n \leq \ell^2$ such that the $n$th coefficient of $[\ell]$ is a unit. To see this, rescale $L$ by $\iota^{-1}(\lambda)$ to a lattice $L'$ with $\iota(g_2(L')) = g_2$ and $\iota(g_3(L')) = g_3$. Then good reduction of $L'$ bounds the [height](/learn/formal-groups/#height) of the reduction by two.
5. A theorem on formal groups of height two over $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$ now applies. Its inputs are an integral pair $(f, e)$, an endomorphism $[w]$ with integral coefficients, with $\sigma^2 w = w$ and $|\sigma w - w| = 1$, and height at most two. It gives $\varepsilon \in K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ with $|\varepsilon| = 1$ such that $e(\varepsilon \lambda_\ell(X))$ has coefficients in $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$.

Map the result from $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ to $\mathbb{C}_\ell$. The image of $e$ is $u$, and the image of $\lambda_\ell$ over $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is $\lambda_\ell$ over $\mathbb{C}_\ell$. This proves the claim. $\square$

**Remark 2.1.7 (Formalization).** The first theorem is one application of the main lemma, at [CmGoodModelOfGlobalArtinReciprocity.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DecoratedLatticeProofs/CmGoodModelOfGlobalArtinReciprocity.lean#L10). The ideal class Artin map and the Chebotarev statement enter as the statements of two other lemmas. We have not opened their proofs or the proof of the theorem on good models of CM lattices. The reciprocity map of the hypothesis sends a uniformizer to the inverse of a Frobenius, while the ideal class map sends an arithmetic Frobenius to the class of the place. In the second theorem, the hypothesis that $d$ is squarefree is used only to put $w$ in $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ with $\sigma w = 1 - w$ ([OneSubTwoOmegaSq.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DecoratedLatticeProofs/OneSubTwoOmegaSq.lean#L108)). We have not opened the proofs of steps 1 and 3 to 5. The same statement has a second proof elsewhere, `Formalism.L2.Th1.CM.cm3` in [CmAll.lean](lean:Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/CMGoodModels/CmAll.lean#L11).

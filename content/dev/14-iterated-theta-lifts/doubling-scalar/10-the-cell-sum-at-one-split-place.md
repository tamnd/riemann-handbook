## The cell sum at one split place

At a split place the two steps of a see-saw become two linear models of general linear groups over a local field. The iterated integral of the double step becomes a double quotient integral of a trilinear form against two [Weil representations](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation). This section computes such a double integral as a finite sum over cells, that is, over double cosets of a subgroup of position one by a level subgroup. The first theorem gives the sum at position one. The second theorem starts from a combination of symmetric double integrals at a higher position, lowers it to position one, and then applies the first theorem. The result is the main input of [the local law at one split place](/entry/val-one-all/) in the next section.

**Setting.** Fix a nonarchimedean local field $k$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}$, a Haar measure $\nu$ on $k$ with $\nu(\mathcal{O}) = 1$, and a continuous additive character $\psi$ of $k$ of conductor one. With these choices $\nu$ is self-dual for $\psi$. Fix characters $\mu_1, \mu_2$ of $k^\times$ with open kernel. For a finite set $n$ and an integer $c \geq 0$ write $G(n, c) = \mathrm{GL}_{n \sqcup c}(k)$, and $K(n, c)$ for its subgroup of integral matrices with integral inverse. For an integer $p \geq 1$ let $R_p \subset G(n, c)$ be the subgroup of position $p$: the product of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$, embedded in the upper left corner, and a unipotent group $N_p$ with its Haar measure built from $\nu$.

For Haar measures $m$ on $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ and $\mu$ on $G(n, c)$, a function $f$ on $G(n, c)$ has the *quotient integral* $z$ at position $p$ if there is a Schwartz-Bruhat function $F$ on $G(n, c)$ with
$$f(h) = \int_{\mathrm{GL}_n(k)} \int_{N_p} F(g u h)\, du\, dm(g) \quad \text{for all } h, \qquad z = \int_{G(n, c)} F\, d\mu.$$
We write $\int_{R_p \backslash G} f = z$. A *level* $L$ of $G(n, c)$ is a subgroup $J$ with a character $\lambda$ of $J$. A vector $v$ of a representation $\sigma$ has *type* $L$ if $\sigma(j)v = \lambda(j)v$ for all $j \in J$.

A finite set $T \subset G(n, c)$ *covers* a function $f$ at position $p$ for $J$ if the double cosets $R_p t J$ for $t \in T$ are distinct, and every $h$ with $f(h) \neq 0$ lies in one of them. For $\gamma \in G(n, c)$ let $C(J, \gamma)$ be the cell group of $\gamma$ in $J$, and let $\mathrm{pr}_X C(J, \gamma)$ be its image in $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$. The *cell weight* of $\gamma$ is $w_m(\gamma) = m(\mathrm{pr}_X C(J, \gamma))^{-1}$.

Let $n_0, n_1, n_2$ be finite sets and let $c, \rho$ satisfy $c + 1 = 2\rho$. Let $\Phi_1$ be a Schwartz-Bruhat function on the matrices of shape $(n_1 \sqcup c+1) \times (n_0 \sqcup c+2)$ and $\Phi_2$ one on the matrices of shape $(n_2 \sqcup c) \times (n_1 \sqcup c+1)$. The group $G(n_0, c+2) \times G(n_1, c+1)$ acts on the first by the linear model $\omega_{\mu_1}$, and $G(n_1, c+1) \times G(n_2, c)$ acts on the second by $\omega_{\mu_2}$. For $\alpha$ and $h$ the *flattened function* $\mathrm{fl}(\alpha, h)$ is the partial Fourier transform, with respect to $\psi$ and $\nu$, of $\omega_{\mu_1}(\alpha, h)\Phi_1$ in the last column block. It is a function on the matrices of shape $n_1 \times n_0$. For a level subgroup $J$, an element $b$ and representatives $\alpha, \gamma$, let $\xi(J, b, \alpha, \gamma)$ be the *cell entry*: the flattened function averaged over the cell of $\gamma$ and moved by $b$. The same notation is used for $\Phi_2$.

Let $\sigma$ be a smooth representation of $G(n_2, c)$ on a space $A$. A bilinear form $B$ in a Schwartz-Bruhat function on $n_2 \times n_1$ matrices and a vector of $A$ has *position* $p$ for $\mu_2$ if each $B(\Psi, \cdot)$ is equivariant for $N_p$ with respect to $\psi$, and $B$ transforms under $\mathrm{GL}_{n_2}(k)$ by $|\det g|^{(c+1)/2 - p}$. A trilinear form $T(\Psi_1, \Psi_2, v)$ is *positive* of position $\rho$ if each $T(\Psi_1)$ has position $\rho$, and $T$ is invariant under the diagonal action of $\mathrm{GL}_{n_1}(k)$ by $\omega_{\mu_1}$ and $\omega_{\mu_2}$. For a functional $l$ on $A$ of position $P$, let $l^{(1)}$ be the functional of position one obtained by $P - 1$ integrations over column unipotent groups.

**Definition 2.10.1 (Double integrals).** Let $T$ be positive of position $\rho$, let $F \in A$, and let $m_1, \mu_1^G, m_2, \mu_2^G$ be Haar measures on $\mathrm{GL}_{n_1}(k)$, $G(n_1, c+1)$, $\mathrm{GL}_{n_2}(k)$, $G(n_2, c)$.

1. The *double integral at position one* of $(T, F, \Phi_1, \Phi_2)$ has value $z$ if there is $w(h, \Psi)$, linear in $\Psi$, such that for all $h$ and $\Psi$ the function $h' \mapsto T(\Psi)^{(1)}$ of the flattened $\omega_{\mu_2}(h, h')\Phi_2$ and $\sigma(h')F$ has quotient integral $w(h, \Psi)$ at position one, and $h \mapsto w(h)$ applied to the flattened $\omega_{\mu_1}(1, h)\Phi_1$ has quotient integral $z$ at position one.
2. The *symmetric double integral* of $(T, F, \Phi_1, \Phi_2)$ has value $z$ if the same holds with the inner quotient integral at position $\rho$ and the outer integral a Fourier-Jacobi quotient integral at position $\rho$.

**Theorem 2.10.2 (Cell sum at position one).** Let $L_1 = (J_1, \lambda_1)$ and $L_2 = (J_2, \lambda_2)$ be levels of $G(n_1, c+1)$ and $G(n_2, c)$ with compact open $J_i$, and let $\mu_1^G(J_1) = \mu_2^G(J_2) = 1$. Let $b_1, b_2$ be elements of these groups, and let $m_i^{b}$ be $m_i$ rescaled by the volume of the integral points of $N_1$ conjugated by $b_i$. Let $T$ be positive of position $\rho$, let $F$ have type $L_2$, let $\omega_{\mu_1}(1, j)\Phi_1 = \lambda_1(j)^{-1}\Phi_1$ for $j \in J_1$, and let $\Phi_2$ have types $(L_1, L_2)$. Let $T_1$ cover $h \mapsto \mathrm{fl}(\alpha, h)$ for $J_1$, and let $T_2$ cover the flattened functions of $\Phi_2$ at every $\gamma \in T_1$ for $J_2$. Then the double integral at position one of $(T, F, \omega_{\mu_1}(\alpha, 1)\Phi_1, \Phi_2)$ for the measures $m_1^b, \mu_1^G, m_2^b, \mu_2^G$ has value $z$ if and only if
$$z = \sum_{\gamma \in T_1} \sum_{\beta \in T_2} w_{m_1}(\gamma)\, w_{m_2}(\beta)\, T\big(\xi(J_1, b_1, \alpha, \gamma), \xi(J_2, b_2, \gamma, \beta)\big)^{(1)}\big(\sigma(\beta)F\big).$$

*Proof.* The idea is to reduce both quotient integrals to one known cell sum each, and to use that quotient integrals are unique.

1. *Source representation.* The action $\omega_{\mu_2}(h, 1)$ on Schwartz-Bruhat functions of shape $(n_2 \sqcup c) \times (n_1 \sqcup c+1)$ is a smooth representation $\rho_1$ of $G(n_1, c+1)$.
2. *The middle bilinear form.* By the existence theorem for bilinear forms at the middle vertex, there is a bilinear form $b(\Psi, \Phi)$ of position $\rho$ for $\rho_1$ such that for every $\Psi$ and every $\Phi$ the inner double quotient integral equals $b(\Psi, \Phi)$. Lowering $b$ to position one gives a form $B'$ of position one with $B'(\Psi) = b(\Psi)^{(1)}$.
3. *The inner forms.* For each $\Psi$ the form $B_1(\Psi) = T(\Psi)^{(1)}$ has position one for $\mu_2$, since lowering keeps the positivity.
4. *Uniqueness.* For the rescaled measures, a function has at most one quotient integral at position one. This uses $1 \leq c$.
5. *The inner integral.* For every $h$ and $\Psi$, the inner function of item 1 of the definition has quotient integral $B'(\Psi, \rho_1(h)\Phi_2)$. This is step 2 rewritten with step 3.
6. *The outer cell sum.* The vector $\Phi_2$ has type $L_1$ for $\rho_1$, by the type condition on $\Phi_2$. The cell sum theorem at position one for $B'$, $\Phi_1$ and the cover $T_1$ gives the outer quotient integral as $\sum_{\gamma} w_{m_1}(\gamma)\, B'(\xi(J_1, b_1, \alpha, \gamma), \rho_1(\gamma)\Phi_2)$.
7. *The inner cell sums.* Fix $\gamma \in T_1$. If the cell entry $\xi(J_1, b_1, \alpha, \gamma)$ is a Schwartz-Bruhat function, then the cell sum theorem for $B_1$ of this entry, the cover $T_2$ and $F$ gives a second quotient integral of the same inner function. By uniqueness,
$$B'(\xi_1, \rho_1(\gamma)\Phi_2) = \sum_{\beta \in T_2} w_{m_2}(\beta)\, T(\xi_1, \xi(J_2, b_2, \gamma, \beta))^{(1)}(\sigma(\beta)F).$$
If the entry is not a Schwartz-Bruhat function, both forms are zero on it by convention, and both sides are zero.
8. *The two directions.* Suppose the double integral has value $z$ with the function $w$. By step 5 and uniqueness, $w(h, \Psi) = B'(\Psi, \rho_1(h)\Phi_2)$. So the outer function is the one of step 6, and uniqueness with steps 6 and 7 gives the formula for $z$. Conversely, if $z$ is given by the formula, take $w(h, \Psi) = B'(\Psi, \rho_1(h)\Phi_2)$. Step 5 gives the inner integrals, and steps 6 and 7 give the outer integral with value $z$. $\square$

A combination of group elements can replace the lowering operator $l \mapsto l^{(1)}$ on vectors of a fixed type. This is the content of the next definition.

**Definition 2.10.3 (One-step combination).** Let $L = (J, \lambda)$ be a level of $G(n, c)$, let $P \geq 1$ and $\alpha \in G(n, c)$. A finite combination $e$ of elements of $G(n, c)$ is a *one-step combination* at $\alpha$ for $L$ and position $P$ if, for every smooth representation $\sigma$, every functional $l$ that is equivariant of position $P$, and every vector $v$ of type $L$,
$$l^{(1)}(\sigma(\alpha)v) = \sum_g e(g)\, l(\sigma(g)v).$$

**Theorem 2.10.4 (Symmetric double integrals as a cell sum).** Let $\mu_1^G(K(n_1, c+1)) = \mu_2^G(K(n_2, c)) = 1$. Let $L_0, L_1, L_2$ be levels of $G(n_0, c+2)$, $G(n_1, c+1)$, $G(n_2, c)$, with $J_1$ and $J_2$ compact open and contained in the integral subgroups. Let $T$ be positive of position $\rho$, let $F$ have type $L_2$, let $\Phi_1$ have types $(L_0, L_1)$ and $\Phi_2$ types $(L_1, L_2)$, and let $T_1, T_2$ be covers as in the last theorem, now with $b_1 = b_2 = 1$. Let $e_0$ be a one-step combination at $\alpha$ for $L_0$ and position $\rho + 1$, and for each $\beta \in T_2$ let $e_2(\beta)$ be a one-step combination at $\beta$ for $L_2$ and position $\rho$. If, for each $a$ in the support of $e_0$, the symmetric double integral of $(T, F, \omega_{\mu_1}(a, 1)\Phi_1, \Phi_2)$ has value $z(a)$, then
$$\begin{aligned} \sum_a e_0(a)\, z(a) &= \frac{1}{[K_1 : J_1]\,[K_2 : J_2]} \sum_{\gamma \in T_1} \sum_{\beta \in T_2} w_{m_1}(\gamma)\, w_{m_2}(\beta) \\ &\qquad \sum_b e_2(\beta)(b)\, T\big(\xi_1(\alpha, \gamma), \xi_2(\gamma, \beta)\big)(\sigma(b)F),\end{aligned}$$
where $K_i$ are the integral subgroups and $\xi_i$ the cell entries with $b_i = 1$.

*Proof.* We pass from the symmetric double integrals to one double integral at position one, then apply the cell sum.

1. *Source representation.* The action $\omega_{\mu_1}(g, 1)$ is a smooth representation $\rho_0$ of $G(n_0, c+2)$ on Schwartz-Bruhat functions. The function $\Phi_1$ has type $L_0$ for $\rho_0$.
2. *Shift by $\alpha$.* Let $e_0'$ be $e_0$ moved by right multiplication with $\alpha^{-1}$. Then $\sum_g e_0'(g) z(g\alpha) = \sum_a e_0(a) z(a)$. The one-step property of $e_0$ at $\alpha$ says that $e_0'$ represents the lowering operator on the vector $\rho_0(\alpha)\Phi_1$.
3. *Lowering to position one.* For $g$ in the support of $e_0'$, the symmetric double integral of $\omega_{\mu_1}(g, 1)\rho_0(\alpha)\Phi_1$ is $z(g\alpha)$. The theorem that lowers a finite combination of symmetric double integrals to position one then gives that the double integral at position one of $(T, F, \omega_{\mu_1}(\alpha, 1)\Phi_1, \Phi_2)$ has value $\sum_a e_0(a) z(a)$, for the measures $m_1, \mu_1^G, m_2, \mu_2^G$.
4. *Index and volume.* Since $J_i$ is open in the compact group $K_i$, the index $r_i = [K_i : J_i]$ is finite and not zero, and $r_i\, \mu_i^G(J_i) = 1$.
5. *Rescaling.* If the double integral has value $z$ for $\mu_1^G, \mu_2^G$, it has value $r_1 r_2 z$ for $r_1\mu_1^G, r_2\mu_2^G$: the inner function is multiplied by $r_2$ and the outer integral by $r_1$. The new measures give volume one to $J_1$ and $J_2$. For $b = 1$ the rescaling of $m_i$ in the last theorem is trivial, since $\nu(\mathcal{O}) = 1$ and the integral points of $N_1$ have volume one.
6. *Cell sum.* The last theorem, with $b_1 = b_2 = 1$, gives
$$r_1 r_2 \sum_a e_0(a)z(a) = \sum_{\gamma, \beta} w_{m_1}(\gamma)\, w_{m_2}(\beta)\, T(\xi_1(\alpha, \gamma), \xi_2(\gamma, \beta))^{(1)}(\sigma(\beta)F).$$
7. *The combinations $e_2$.* The functional $T(\xi_1, \xi_2)$ is equivariant of position $\rho$, by the positivity of $T$, and $F$ has type $L_2$. So the one-step property of $e_2(\beta)$ at $\beta$ replaces the lowered functional at $\sigma(\beta)F$ by $\sum_b e_2(\beta)(b)\, T(\xi_1, \xi_2)(\sigma(b)F)$. If a cell entry is not a Schwartz-Bruhat function, both sides are zero.
8. Divide by $r_1 r_2 \neq 0$. $\square$

The index factor $([K_1 : J_1][K_2 : J_2])^{-1}$ is the volume $\mathrm{vol}_J$ of the split place datum of the section on [the double step](/entry/val-q/), and the cell weights are its weights $w_1, w_2$.

We have not unfolded the position subgroups, the cell groups, the flattened functions and the cell entries; their descriptions above follow their names and the statements that use them.

## The last step law for pinned decorations

The section on [see-saw laws at twist places](/entry/laws-of-pin-resc/) proved the iterated integral law for pinned decorations, and used the last step law for pinned decorations without proof. This section gives that proof. At the last two steps of the chain there is no Bessel subgroup and no iterated quotient integral: the law is an identity of integrals over a fundamental domain of the top group. The proof is algebra on top of one identity, the local factorisation of the last see-saw datum over the twist places. The result is used in [the laws for decorations pinned up to roots of unity](/entry/laws-of-pin-resc/).

**Setting.** We keep the notation of the section on see-saw laws: common see-saw data $c$ for a datum with chain length $m$, twist place data $v$ with lattices $N_j$ and a top lattice $N_T$, a tuple $t$ with index sets $\mathrm{Ch}_j$, and a decoration $w$ with set of places $Q$. We assume the standing conditions of that section. Fix $j$ with $j + 2 = m$. Let $\omega_j$ be the Weil representation of the step $j$ of the tuple. For theta functions $\Psi_1, \Psi_2$ let $\mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2)$ be the *last see-saw datum*: the contracted theta function of the last two steps, built from the twist datum $\tau_j$ of $v$. For a function $\varphi$ on the group of $X_j$ write $\mathrm{bl}(\varphi, \Theta)$ for the lift of $\varphi$ against $\Theta$, and $\mathrm{bl}(\varphi, d, d', \Theta)$ for the lift against the bi-combination of $\Theta$ with $d, d'$, as before. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the fundamental domain of the top group with its measure, and let $E$ be the embedding of the top group into the group of the last space.

**Definition 2.12.1 (Last step law).** The decoration $w$ satisfies the *last step law* if, for $j + 2 = m$, all theta functions $\Psi_1, \Psi_2$ that are vacuum at $Q$ for the lattices $(N_j, N_{j+1})$ and $(N_{j+1}, N_T)$, all continuous automorphic $\varphi_\alpha \in F_X(j, \alpha)$, and every automorphic $f$ in the top set $F_T$ of the decoration,
$$\begin{aligned} &\sum_{\alpha} \sum_{g} \varepsilon(j, \alpha)(g) \int_{\mathcal{F}} f(h)\, \mathrm{bl}\Big(\varphi_\alpha, \mathrm{lst}\big(\omega_j(g, 1)\, e_Q(j)\Psi_1,\ e_{Q,T}(j)\Psi_2\big)\Big)(E(h, 1))\, dh \\ &\quad = c_Q(j) \sum_{\beta} \int_{\mathcal{F}} \big(\varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta) \cdot f\big)(h) \sum_{\alpha, \gamma} \mathrm{bl}\big(\varphi_\alpha, d_Q(j, \alpha, \gamma), d_Q(j+1, \gamma, \beta), \mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2)\big)(E(h, 1))\, dh. \end{aligned}$$

For a pinned decoration the top data are simple. By the definition of the pin, $\varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta)$ is the single point $1$ with coefficient $\prod_i \mathrm{wt}_i(j+2, \beta_i)$, and at the top each local weight $\mathrm{wt}_i(j+2, \cdot)$ is the constant $n_i(j+2)$. The index of a local datum at the top has one element.

**Theorem 2.12.2 (Core identity at the last steps).** Assume the standing conditions, let $w$ be pinned by $Q_1, \dots, Q_r$, and let $\Psi_1, \Psi_2$ be vacuum as in the last step law and $\varphi$ continuous automorphic. For $\alpha \in \mathrm{Ch}_j$ and $\beta \in \mathrm{Ch}_{j+2}$, let $P(\alpha) = \prod_i \mathrm{wt}_i(j, \alpha_i)$ and let $G(\alpha)$ be the lift $\mathrm{bl}(\varphi, \mathrm{lst}(\sum_g \varepsilon(j, \alpha)(g)\, \omega_j(g, 1)e_Q(j)\Psi_1,\ e_{Q,T}(j)\Psi_2))$. Then
$$G(\alpha) = c_Q(j)\, \varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta)(1) \sum_{\gamma} \mathrm{bl}\big(\varphi, d_Q(j, \alpha, \gamma), d_Q(j+1, \gamma, \beta), \mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2)\big).$$

*Proof.* By the pin, $e_Q(j)\Psi_1$ and $e_{Q,T}(j)\Psi_2$ are theta functions, and so is each $\omega_j(g, 1)e_Q(j)\Psi_1$, since the Weil representation preserves theta functions. The lift $\mathrm{bl}(\varphi, \mathrm{lst}(\cdot, Y))$ is linear on finite sums of such theta functions, by the lemma on the integrability and linearity of the last double theta lift.

1. *The left side.* By the pin, $\varepsilon(j, \alpha) = P(\alpha) \cdot E(\alpha)$, where $E(\alpha)$ is the convolution of the local combinations $\varepsilon^{(i)}(\alpha_i)$. If $P(\alpha) = 0$ both sides below are zero. Otherwise the supports agree, and linearity gives $G(\alpha) = P(\alpha)\, G_E(\alpha)$, where $G_E(\alpha)$ is the same lift with $E(\alpha)$ in place of $\varepsilon(j, \alpha)$.
2. *The right side.* By the pin, $d_Q(j, \alpha, \gamma)$ and $d_Q(j+1, \gamma, \beta)$ are the products of weights $P(\alpha)$ and $\prod_i \mathrm{wt}_i(j+1, \gamma_i)$ times the convolutions $d(\alpha, \gamma)$ and $d(\gamma, \beta)$ of the local coefficients. Scalars move out of a bi-combination and out of the lift.
3. *The local factorisation.* The theorem on the last two steps at all twist places says
$$\prod_i n_i(j+1) \cdot G_E(\alpha) = \sum_{\gamma} \prod_i \mathrm{wt}_i(j+1, \gamma_i)\, \mathrm{bl}\big(\varphi, d(\alpha, \gamma), d(\gamma, \beta), \mathrm{lst}(\Psi_1, \Psi_2)\big).$$
4. *The constants.* We have $\varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta)(1) = \prod_i n_i(j+2)$ and $c_Q(j) = \prod_i (n_i(j+1)\, n_i(j+2))^{-1}$. The indices are not zero, by the lemma on the last two level indices. Hence $c_Q(j)\, \varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta)(1) \prod_i n_i(j+1) = 1$. Multiply the identity of step 3 by $P(\alpha)\, c_Q(j)\, \varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta)(1)$ and use steps 1 and 2. $\square$

**Theorem 2.12.3 (Last step law for pinned decorations).** Assume the standing conditions. If $w$ is pinned, then $w$ satisfies the last step law.

*Proof.* Fix $j$, $\Psi_1, \Psi_2$, the functions $\varphi_\alpha$ and $f$ as in the law. Put $X = e_Q(j)\Psi_1$ and $Y = e_{Q,T}(j)\Psi_2$.

1. *Integrability.* An automorphic function is continuous, so $f$ is continuous automorphic. By the lemma on the last double theta lift, for each $\alpha$ and $g$ the function $h \mapsto f(h)\, \mathrm{bl}(\varphi_\alpha, \mathrm{lst}(\omega_j(g, 1)X, Y))(E(h, 1))$ is integrable on $\mathcal{F}$.
2. *The left side.* Let $G(\alpha)$ be as in the core identity, with $\varphi = \varphi_\alpha$. By linearity, $f\, G(\alpha)$ is the finite sum $\sum_g \varepsilon(j, \alpha)(g)\, f\, \mathrm{bl}(\varphi_\alpha, \mathrm{lst}(\omega_j(g, 1)X, Y))$. So $f\, G(\alpha)$ is integrable, and the integral commutes with the finite sums over $g$ and over $\alpha$. The left side of the law becomes $\int_{\mathcal{F}} \sum_\alpha f(h)\, G(\alpha)(E(h, 1))\, dh$.
3. *One top index.* Under the bijection of the pin, $\mathrm{Ch}_{j+2}$ is the product of the top index sets of the local data. Each of them has one element, so $\mathrm{Ch}_{j+2}$ has one element.
4. *The top translate.* The combination $\varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta)$ is a single point $1$ with coefficient $\varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta)(1)$. So the translate $\varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta) \cdot f$ is the multiple $\varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta)(1)\, f$; when the coefficient is zero, both are zero.
5. *Conclusion.* For each $\beta$, move $c_Q(j)$ and $\varepsilon_T(j+2, \beta)(1)$ inside the integral on the right side. The core identity, applied to each $\alpha$, turns the integrand into $\sum_\alpha f(h)\, G(\alpha)(E(h, 1))$. So each term of the sum over $\beta$ equals the left side, and by step 3 there is one such term. $\square$

The law does not use the set $F_T$: the identity holds for every automorphic $f$. Of the conditions $\varphi_\alpha \in F_X(j, \alpha)$, only the continuity of $\varphi_\alpha$ enters.

**Remark 2.12.4 (Formalization).** The core identity and the law are at [SeeSawIteratedIntegralLawLastStepsOfLocalFactori.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DecoratedLatticeProofs/SeeSawIteratedIntegralLawLastStepsOfLocalFactori.lean#L126) (lines 65 and 126), with the scalar lemmas of steps 1 and 2 of the core identity at lines 18 to 48 of the same file. The last step law is defined in [PlaceLaws.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/LatticeFrames/PlaceLaws.lean#L243), and the pin in [CosInj.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/LatticeFrames/CosInj.lean#L152). In the formal proof of the law the hypothesis $f \in F_T$ is introduced and not used, and of the hypothesis on $\varphi_\alpha$ only the continuity part is used. We have read the statements, not the proofs, of the preservation of theta functions by the normalised combinations, the integrability and linearity of the last double theta lift, the theorem on the last two steps at all twist places, and the lemma on the last two level indices. We have not unfolded the last see-saw datum, the lift, the top group and its fundamental domain; their descriptions above follow their names and the statements that use them.

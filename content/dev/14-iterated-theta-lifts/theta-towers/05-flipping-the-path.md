## Flipping the path through the grid

This section proves the flip formula: the twisted theta function of a theta datum, at the lift of a vector $y$, is a constant times the lift of the form $F_y$ along the opposite path of the grid. The proof starts from the given path, where the twisted theta function is a lift by definition up to a unit, and moves the path across the grid one square at a time. Each square costs a nonzero constant. The flip formula is the second of the four steps of the row formula in [Theorem 3.6.6](/entry/theta-lift-row-properties-conj/).

We keep the notation of [Section 3.4.4](/entry/twisted-iterate-eventually-large/). Let $c$ be a choice of the row, with grid $G$, Weil datum $W$, model $\mathcal M$, real matching $\rho$ and frames $B$. We fix the local inputs of [ThetaLocalInputs](/step/theta-local-inputs-def/) and assume that lifts of Fock vectors keep cusp forms. Recall that $\Xi_t \subseteq \mathbb C^\times$ is the subgroup generated by the local values of the character $\xi_t$.

**Definition 3.5.1 (Twisted theta function).** For a theta datum $\Phi$ of the grid, the choice gives a twisted theta function $\theta_\chi(\Phi)$, a function on the node $(m, 0)$ built from the Weil datum, the model and the real matching. For an index $t$ and a vector $y$ of the target space at $t$, let $\ell_t(y)$ be the lift of $y$ that the choice fixes. We have not unfolded the twisted theta function or the lift $\ell_t$.

**Definition 3.5.2 (Finite tensor representation).** Let $t$ be an index and $p$ a path in the grid from $(0, m)$. A finite combination $\tau$ of test data along $p$ has a finite tensor representation with respect to the stage tensors of $\Phi$ if it meets the finiteness condition of the frames $B$ and the real matching $\rho$ at $t$, with the stage tensors of $\Phi$ at each place $w$ as the local factors. We have not unfolded this condition.

The given path has a natural set of test data. Along its first part, the column $(0, m) \to \cdots \to (0, 0)$, a chain of Schwartz vectors $f = (f_j)_{j < m}$ on the down arrows is given. We say that $f$ is a chain of vectors if it meets the conditions of the first column at $t$; we have not unfolded them. The chain and $\Phi$ give a pure test datum $g(f, \Phi)$ on the given path.

**Lemma 3.5.3 (Finite representation at the start).** At large scale, the following holds. For every index $t$ and every chain of vectors $f$ at $t$ there is $z \in \Xi_t$ such that for every theta datum $\Phi$, the combination $z \cdot \delta_{g(f, \Phi)}$ has a finite tensor representation along the given path with respect to the stage tensors of $\Phi$.

*Proof.* Three facts hold at large scale. For each arrow $j$ of the first column there is $c_j \in \Xi_t$ such that $c_j$ times the given function at the arrow $(0, j + 1) \to (0, j)$ is a local vector for every idempotent sum $e$ over the local places of $t$ and every $\Phi$, with $f_j$ as its Schwartz part. For $j + 1 < m$ this comes from the inner chain step on the column, and for $j + 1 = m$ from the highest vector at the start of the flip. The third fact says that the coset functions of the row are local vectors. An idempotent sum $e$ over the local places exists. Put $z = (\prod_j c_j)^{-1}$; it lies in $\Xi_t$ since $\Xi_t$ is a group. The local vectors on the column and on the row, with the scalars $c_j$ whose product cancels $z$, are the data of a finite tensor representation of $z \cdot \delta_{g(f, \Phi)}$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.5.4 (The flip sequence starts).** At large scale, the following holds. There is a combination $P_g$ of polynomials such that for every index $t$ there is $z \in \Xi_t$ with this property. For every theta datum $\Phi$ there is a combination $\tau_g$ of test data along the given path that has a finite tensor representation with respect to the stage tensors of $\Phi$, such that for every volume datum $M$ good at $t$ and every $y$:

1. the lift along the given path with the test vectors $P_g \otimes \tau_g$ converges at $F_y$;
2. the twisted theta function satisfies

$$\theta_\chi(\Phi)\bigl(\ell_t(y)\bigr) = z^{-1}\, \Lambda_{\mathrm{giv}}(P_g \otimes \tau_g)(F_y).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.FlipStart.flipSequence_start_condition` (also `GlobalTheory.T.flipSequence_initial_term`, a second name).

*Proof.* Choose chain polynomials $q$ and a chain of vectors $f$ at $t$; both exist for every choice. Put $P_g = \delta_{g(q)}$, the polynomial combination of $q$ with coefficient $1$. Take $z$ from [Lemma 3.5.3](/entry/theta-first-occurrence-finite-swapped-pair/) and put $\tau_g = z \cdot \delta_{g(f, \Phi)}$; by that lemma it has a finite tensor representation. The given path is the first column followed by the bottom row, and the lift along it is the composite of the two lifts. Two facts at large scale describe the parts. On the column, the lift with $f$ and $q$ converges at $F_y$ and gives the leading form at $(0, 0)$. On the row, the first occurrence of the twisted theta function says that the lift of this leading form along the bottom row converges and equals $\theta_\chi(\Phi)(\ell_t(y))$. So the lift along the given path with $P_g \otimes \delta_{g(f, \Phi)}$ is $\theta_\chi(\Phi)(\ell_t(y))$. Multiply the test vector by $z$; the lift is linear, so it becomes $z\, \theta_\chi(\Phi)(\ell_t(y))$, and the formula follows. $\square$

To move from the given path to the opposite path we flip squares. A square of the grid at $(i, j)$ has the two paths $(i, j + 1) \to (i, j) \to (i + 1, j)$ and $(i, j + 1) \to (i + 1, j + 1) \to (i + 1, j)$. The flip of the square replaces the first by the second.

**Definition 3.5.5 (Square constants).** The square constants are complex numbers $c_{ij}(t)$, one for each square and each index $t$, attached to the choice. The constant condition says that at large scale, for all $i$, $j$ and $t$, the square at $(i, j)$ meets the nonvanishing condition of its square and $c_{ij}(t) \neq 0$. The total constant $c_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)$ is built from the square constants. We have not unfolded the nonvanishing condition of a square or the total constant.

**Theorem 3.5.6 (Flip formula).** Assume the convergence of the Weil representations on the squares, the agreement of the theta integrals of [ThetaIntegralsAgree](/step/theta-integrals-agree-def/), and the constant condition. Then at large scale there are a combination $\tau_i$ of pairs of polynomials on the row and the last column and elements $z_t \in \Xi_t$, for each index $t$, with this property. For every $t$ and every theta datum $\Phi$ there is a combination $\tau$ that is flip-finite for $\Phi$, such that for every volume datum $M$ good at $t$ and every $y$:

1. the lift along the opposite path with $\tau_i \otimes \tau$ converges at $F_y$;
2. the twisted theta function satisfies

$$\theta_\chi(\Phi)\bigl(\ell_t(y)\bigr) = \bigl(z_t\, c_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)\bigr)^{-1}\, \Lambda_{\mathrm{opp}}(\tau_i \otimes \tau)(F_y).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.FlipRun.twist_sign_flip_succ` (also `GlobalTheory.T.twisted_lift_flip_invariant`, a second name).

*Proof.* We use six facts at large scale.

1. The twisted square commutes: for a square at $(i, j)$, the lifts along its two paths, with matched test data, differ by the factor $c_{ij}(t)$. This uses the convergence on the squares and the agreement of the theta integrals.
2. The cusp step of [Theorem 3.3.6](/entry/cusp-step-of-large-flip-run/): lifts along the arrows of the grid converge on listed cusp forms and give listed cusp forms.
3. The leading form at the end of the column is a listed cusp form.
4. The flip sequence starts, by [Lemma 3.5.4](/entry/flip-sequence-start-condition/).
5. The local data of each flip stage are tensors over the places, to the depth that the places need.
6. The constant condition, of which we use only $c_{ij}(t) \neq 0$.

The squares are flipped in a fixed order, the flip order of the $m^2$ squares, which turns the given path into the opposite path. We prove by induction on $k \le m^2$ that after the first $k$ flips the twisted theta function at $\ell_t(y)$ is $z^{-1} (\prod c)^{-1}$ times a convergent lift along the current path, where the product runs over the constants of the $k$ squares flipped so far. For $k = 0$ this is fact 4. In the step, the current path contains the first path of the next square. By facts 2 and 3 the lift up to that square is a listed cusp form, so the lifts on both paths of the square converge. Fact 1 replaces the first path of the square by the second, at the cost of the factor $c_{ij}(t)$; we may divide by it since $c_{ij}(t) \neq 0$. Fact 5 keeps the test data in the tensor form that fact 1 needs, so that the new data are again flip-finite. After $m^2$ flips we reach the opposite path, and the product of the constants is $c_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)$. $\square$

**Remark 3.5.7 (Formalization).** The names do not fit the statements. The first lemma is named for a first occurrence and a swapped pair, but it states a finite tensor representation at the start of the flips. The theorem is named for a sign and a successor, but it is the whole flip formula. Of the constant condition only $c_{ij}(t) \neq 0$ is used ([TwistedLiftFlipInvariant.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/LocalTypeDictionary/TwistedLiftFlipInvariant.lean#L21)), and lines 15 to 19 of that file are an empty section. The induction over the flip order is in a lemma that we read only in outline. The five facts other than the constant condition are lemmas that we have not opened.

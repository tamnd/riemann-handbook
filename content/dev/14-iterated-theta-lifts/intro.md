This volume proves the results on theta lifts that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses on the definite side. The object is a tower of [global theta lifts](/learn/theta/#the-global-theta-lift) from a unitary group $U(X_0)$ to unitary groups $U(X_m)$ of larger Hermitian spaces. The volume computes the scalars of the tower by the [doubling method](/learn/theta/#the-doubling-method) and the [Rallis inner product formula](/learn/theta/#the-rallis-inner-product-formula). It fits Fourier coefficients and square constants along the tower. It proves an index bound for lattices of cusp forms, and it builds the level-four data for the membership statement. Volume 1 gives the definitions and states the standard facts: the [Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation), [theta towers](/learn/theta/#theta-towers), [see-saw pairs](/learn/theta/#seesaw-pairs) and the [Siegel-Weil formula](/learn/theta/#the-siegel-weil-formula). This volume proves the forms of these results that the main proof needs.

The proof uses the volume in [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/), below the step [HypsAlt is nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/). Branch 1 uses the injectivity of the up map from Chapter 2, through [the member bundles](/entry/member-bundles-large-unramified-exponents-alt/). Branch 2 uses the index bound of Chapter 4, through [the net inputs](/entry/inputs-net-rec-plus/), which are the main part of [the run theorem](/entry/run-holds-of-par-ok/). Branch 3 uses the see-saw laws of Chapter 2, through [the rescaled laws](/entry/laws-resc-alt/) of the open orbit chapter. Chapter 3 supplies the step [the hypotheses are not empty](/step/nonempty-hyps/) with separating functionals, row properties and a Fourier coefficient fit. Chapter 1 supplies [the membership statement from the construction steps](/entry/member-e-closing-alt-alt/), [the step from level three to level four](/entry/has4-of-has3-of-s-ext-none/) and the equations of the theta family for the same step.

Chapter 1 builds the level-four objects and the data for the membership statement. It conjugates the symmetric level into a row level, [Theorem 1.1.4](/entry/conj-sym-level/), and finds a finite [adelic point](/learn/groups/#adelic-points-and-compact-open-subgroups) that carries it, [Theorem 1.1.6](/entry/see-at-exists/). It gives the level-four saturation of a [discrete series](/learn/representations/#discrete-series), [Theorem 1.2.8](/entry/sre-sat-of-discrete-series/) and [Corollary 1.2.10](/entry/up-s-sat-of-discrete-series/). Sections 3 and 4 put two-step [Haar measures](/learn/analysis/#haar-measure) on unitary stabilizers and prove that they do not depend on the path, [Theorem 1.3.5](/entry/two-step-adelic-swap/). They give a gauge for each CM extension, [Theorem 1.4.3](/entry/exists-is-gauge-of-is-cmext/). The kernel entry conditions for true columns are [Theorem 1.5.10](/entry/ker-ent-hyp-true/), and the kernel data at served places are [Theorem 1.6.5](/entry/ker-data-at-served/). Section 7 shows that the upward Hecke operator is rational and invertible on ramified forms, which gives the column layer conditions, [Theorem 1.7.10](/entry/col-lay-col-t/). Section 8 proves the membership statement from the construction steps with a second pass, [Theorem 1.8.8](/entry/member-e-of-steps2/). The later sections give the inputs of the other branches. Section 9 writes a Bessel period on a definite space as a map that is conjugate linear in one form and linear in the other, [Theorem 1.9.4](/entry/pos-lin/), and Section 10 gives theta data on true rows and columns, [Theorem 1.10.5](/entry/th-ex-main/). Section 16 takes a fine adelic datum from seven results of the literature, [Theorem 1.16.4](/entry/exists-fine-adelic-datum-of-hypotheses/). Section 18 treats Hecke operators at split primes, [Theorem 1.18.5](/entry/hecke-int-transform-read-tie/). Sections 23 and 24 reduce the equations of the theta family to a square law, two triangle laws and the fact that $-1$ is a norm from $E$ modulo every positive integer, [Theorem 1.23.6](/entry/cd-pairing-index-bounds-eq-j-eq-p-of-neg/), and then prove them for every base over a curve ideal, [Theorem 1.24.5](/entry/eq-p-car/).

Chapter 2 computes the scalars of a chain of theta lifts. The operator $\Theta\Theta^*$ acts on each Hecke eigenpart of the first stage by a positive constant times a product of L-values, one for each step. So the total up map is injective. Section 1 proves that a CM elliptic curve has a good model at a prime, by global Artin reciprocity, [Theorem 2.1.4](/entry/cm-good-model-of-global-artin-reciprocity/), and that at an inert prime its [formal group](/learn/formal-groups/#height) has height two, [Theorem 2.1.6](/entry/honda-cm3-cm3/). Sections 2 and 3 compute the local doubling integral at a twist place with parahoric level, [Theorem 2.2.6](/entry/local-zeta-twisted/), and its local factor, [Theorem 2.3.3](/entry/local-factor/). Section 4 peels the twist places, [Theorem 2.4.2](/entry/q-peel-holds/). Section 5 gives the Hecke parameters along the chain, [Theorem 2.5.5](/entry/up-q-holds/). Section 6 applies the Rallis inner product formula, so each step acts by a positive multiple of an L-value, [Theorem 2.6.6](/entry/scalars/). The injectivity is [Theorem 2.7.3](/entry/up-inj/). Sections 8 to 16 prove the see-saw laws at twist places for decorations pinned up to roots of unity, [Theorem 2.9.5](/entry/laws-of-pin-resc/), with the local and finite integral laws, [Theorem 2.16.5](/entry/pins-finite-integrals/). The kernels keep ramified forms, [Theorem 2.17.2](/entry/kernel-keeps-ram-t/), and the theta kernel is a jet of the classical theta function, [Theorem 2.20.2](/entry/theta-kernel-eq-jet-th-c/). Section 26 gives the diagonal row bound. The pairs of corank one need the Siegel-Weil formula, [Theorem 2.26.3](/entry/row-diag-of-siegel-weil-unitary/), and the degenerate terms are bounded without a hypothesis, [Theorem 2.26.4](/entry/degenerate-and-collisions-uncond/). Section 27 computes the mean of the norms of theta nulls, a result of [Chowla-Selberg](/learn/elliptic-curves/#the-chowla-selberg-formula) type, [Theorem 2.27.2](/entry/theta0-mean/).

Chapter 3 studies twisted theta lifts along a grid of Hermitian spaces on the definite side. A $K$-finite cusp form lies in the sum of its irreducible $(\mathfrak g, K)$-submodules, [Theorem 3.1.4](/entry/cusp-form-mem-i-sup-irreducible-g-k/). The lifts keep cusp forms, and a cusp form is close to a finite sum of lifts of Fock vectors, [Theorem 3.2.4](/entry/cusp-type-lift-approx-fock-space/), so a path can be flipped one square at a time. Together with the unfolding formula, [Theorem 3.4.7](/entry/twisted-theta-integral-unfolding/), this gives the five row properties, [Theorem 3.6.6](/entry/theta-lift-row-properties-conj/): a sum condition, constants for each square, convergence, an unfolding formula with explicit constants, and rationality of sums. Real functionals separate the rows, by unramified Howe duality and the Siegel-Weil formula, [Theorem 3.7.8](/entry/functionals-separate-of-howe-unram-of-siegel-weil/). The Petersson norm of a lift is a Rallis integral, [Theorem 3.8.2](/entry/theta-lift-petersson-norm-eq-rallis-integral/). For bottom lifts it is a product of [Hecke L-values](/learn/class-field-theory/#tates-thesis-and-hecke-l-functions), [Theorem 3.8.6](/entry/rallis-bottom-formula-large-rank/). With the row properties this gives the bound that makes a constant function fit, [Theorem 3.9.6](/entry/fourier-coefficient-fit-of-join-alt/). Section 13 gets equal cuspidal multiplicities from ten published results, [Theorem 3.13.12](/entry/twisted-multiplicity-eq/). The tower of square constants is [Theorem 3.14.6](/entry/tower-alt-bis/). The bottom field is a number field, [Theorem 3.18.8](/entry/bottom-field-is-number-field/). The squares match locally, [Theorem 3.20.6](/entry/twisted-square-local-matching/), and the bottom lift is not zero at the first occurrence, [Theorem 3.21.2](/entry/bottom-theta-lift-ne-zero/).

Chapter 4 proves the quotient index bound for a lattice $M$ of cusp forms. Let $S^\flat$ be the forms whose low coefficients vanish at the maximal cusps. Suppose forms with small sup norms span $S$ together with $S^\flat$. Then the index of their span in $M/(M\cap S^\flat)$ is bounded by a polynomial in the size of the weight. The tool is a CM functional: evaluate a form along a formal arc through a CM point and read one coefficient. Sections 1 to 3 show that these functionals detect $S^\flat$. A form lies in $S^\flat$ if and only if its low coefficients vanish, [Theorem 4.1.5](/entry/mem-iff-low-coeff-vanish/). A split point has a nonzero [Fourier-Jacobi coefficient](/learn/automorphic-forms/#fourier-coefficients-and-whittaker-functions), [Theorem 4.2.2](/entry/exists-fj-ne-zero-at-split-point-prime/), and the functionals detect, [Theorem 4.3.6](/entry/detect-q-of-known/). Section 4 bounds the functionals at the finite places by an integral model, [Theorem 4.4.8](/entry/cf-of-fin-bound/). Section 5 bounds them at the infinite places by the Cauchy inequalities, so each functional is numerically good, [Theorem 4.5.5](/entry/num-good-of-fam-q/). Section 6 builds a bridge and applies the bridge theorem of Volume 13, [Theorem 7.9.5 of Volume 13](/entry/qat-of-bridge-prime/), to get [Theorem 4.6.3](/entry/qat-of-kill-detect/). It then supplies the constants from a page, [Theorem 4.6.4](/entry/q-at-run/).

The four chapters do not cite each other. Each can be read alone, in any order. They use other volumes. Chapter 1 uses the kernel entry conditions and the column comparisons of Volume 13, and the section transform of Volume 8. Chapter 2 uses the theorem on good models of CM lattices from Volume 13. Chapter 3 takes the work on multiplicities from Volume 8, [the multiplicity theorem](/entry/mult-eq-of-sides/). Chapter 4 uses the bridge theorem of Volume 13. All four chapters feed Volume 15. A reader can take Chapter 2 or Chapter 3 first, because they set out the doubling method and the theta tower in their plainest form. Chapter 4 is short and stands apart. Chapter 1 is the longest and is best read last.

This volume proves the results on the theta correspondence that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. The volume builds the [Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation) of a unitary dual pair over a CM extension $E/F$, in its Schrödinger, Fock and mixed models. It proves Howe duality at the unramified and at the real places, the transformation law of theta series of Hermitian lattices, and the [Siegel-Weil formula](/learn/theta/#the-siegel-weil-formula) in the convergent range, at the centre and in a regularised form. With the [doubling method](/learn/theta/#the-doubling-method) and the [Rallis inner product formula](/learn/theta/#the-rallis-inner-product-formula) it shows that some theta lifts are not zero. The last two chapters unfold [Bessel and Fourier-Jacobi periods](/learn/periods/#bessel-and-fourier-jacobi-periods) along [theta towers](/learn/theta/#theta-towers) and prove that Bessel periods climb a tower. Volume 1 gives the definitions and recalls these statements. This volume proves them.

The proof uses the volume in four of its chapters. In [the theta matching chapter](/proof/theta-matching/), the step [theta integrals agree and local matching](/step/theta-integrals-agree-and-local-matching-of-literature/) comes from Chapters 2 and 3. In [the Bessel seesaw chapter](/proof/bessel-seesaw/), the steps [the nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/), [every character from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) and [every character of the row](/step/every-character-of-row/) use the unfolding laws and local inputs of Chapter 7 and the ascent of Chapter 8. The first of these steps also uses the Galois conjugates of rational theta lifts of Chapter 1. In [the unitary hypotheses chapter](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/), Chapter 5 gives the non-vanishing of the down lift and Chapter 2 gives the real lift splitting. Chapter 4 sits inside [the Siegel-Weil chapter](/proof/siegel-weil/) of the proof. Its archimedean results go to the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/), and its formula at the centre goes to the branch from the literature.

Chapter 1 builds the Weil representation of a unitary dual pair over a CM field. The local part proves that the norm residue sign of a quadratic extension is multiplicative, [Theorem 1.1.6](/entry/mul-mem-range-norm/), computes the Gauss integral of a Hermitian form, [Theorem 1.2.4](/entry/has-gauss-herm/), and proves the big cell relation, [Theorem 1.3.4](/entry/weyl-cell-relation/). From these it builds the Schrödinger model of $\mathrm{U}(n,n)$ with the splitting of Kudla, [Theorem 1.4.4](/entry/kudla-splitting-split-unitary/). At the real places it proves Howe duality in the Fock model, [Theorem 1.5.10](/entry/howe-duality-real-holds/), and the density of the polynomial Fock vectors in the Schwartz space, [Theorem 1.6.4](/entry/pol-dense/). The global part proves Weil's theorem that rational points fix the theta distribution, [Theorem 1.8.7](/entry/theta-sum-om-rat/), and the rationality of theta lifts under Galois conjugation, [Theorem 1.11.9](/entry/conj-rat-lift5/). The last sections build cusp forms from Poincaré series and prove that the bottom period of the theta tower is not zero, [Theorem 1.18.7](/entry/period-ne-of-eigen/).

Chapter 2 proves the parts of the theta correspondence that the proof uses directly. It proves an integral Witt theorem for Hermitian matrices over an unramified quadratic extension, [Theorem 2.1.2](/entry/witt-mod-pow/), and writes sums over a Gram class as combinations of self-dual vacua, [Theorem 2.2.3](/entry/gram-class-sum-mem-span-self-dual-vac/). This gives unramified Howe duality, [Theorem 2.3.4](/entry/howe-unram-holds/): the spherical vectors of one member of the dual pair lie in the span of the translates of the vacuum under the other. Section 4 computes the unramified doubling integral at inert places, [Theorem 2.4.1](/entry/triv/), and proves the rapid decay of cuspidal differences, [Theorem 2.4.7](/entry/sig-rapid/). Section 5 constructs the global Weil representation, [Theorem 2.5.5](/entry/exists-is-weil/). If Eisenstein continuation and the Siegel-Weil formula hold, then theta integrals agree and local matching holds, [Theorem 2.6.5](/entry/theorem-k-head2/). Section 7 treats the Rallis inner product and the injectivity of lifts, [Theorem 2.7.9](/entry/lift-rows-injective-and-type-properties-of-nonvanishing/). Section 8 proves results quoted from the literature, among them the Siegel-Weil formula for unitary pairs, [Theorem 2.8.4](/entry/weil-unitary/).

Chapter 3 studies theta series of Hermitian lattices over a CM field. A unitary change of columns multiplies the theta function by a number $t$ with $t^2 \prod_i \sigma_i(\det h)$ a root of unity, [Theorem 3.1.5](/entry/is-mu-multiplier-sq-mul-det/). A change of representative changes the short theta values by a root of unity, [Theorem 3.2.4](/entry/change-root-of-chart-indep/). The chapter proves the Siegel-Weil formula for definite Hermitian lattices in the range $4N < n$ by Hecke operators and a contraction, [Theorem 3.4.5](/entry/siegel-weil-definite/). With the kernel identity, [Theorem 3.5.4](/entry/kernel-identity/), it shows that theta series with harmonic polynomial coefficients span the bounded holomorphic forms, [Theorem 3.6.4](/entry/theta-span/), also at a level, [Theorem 3.6.6](/entry/level-theta-span/). The last two sections give the spherical forms at inert places, [Theorem 3.7.6](/entry/sph-fun-of-mod-st/), and the spherical closure, [Theorem 3.8.2](/entry/close-sph/), which derives the agreement of theta integrals and local matching from fourteen assumptions.

Chapter 4 proves the Siegel-Weil formula for unitary dual pairs: a Siegel Eisenstein series on the section of a Schwartz function equals the average of its theta function over the unitary group. It first proves the formula in measure form, as an identity between the theta measure of a Gram fibre and an Euler product of local fibre measures. The singular case is [Theorem 4.2.6](/entry/sw-free-singular/). The anisotropic case goes by induction on the rank from the rank one and binary cases, [Theorem 4.10.7](/entry/e1/). A Fourier coefficient is an Euler product integral, [Theorem 4.3.8](/entry/whit-eq-integral/), and the comparison of coefficients gives Weil's formula in the convergent range $2n < m$, [Theorem 4.4.4](/entry/weil-siegel-formula/). The formula at the point $s_0$ is [Theorem 4.5.5](/entry/field/), and the chapter reduces it to three inputs, [Theorem 4.5.15](/entry/top7/). The archimedean results include the holomorphy of leading terms, [Theorem 4.7.3](/entry/hol-lead-b/), the absolute convergence theorem, [Theorem 4.8.8](/entry/abs-thm-of/), and the Whittaker integral at a real place, [Theorem 4.9.6](/entry/wr/). The chapter also proves the standard Siegel-Weil formula, [Theorem 4.15.10](/entry/siegel-weil-std/).

Chapter 5 proves the first term identity of the regularised Siegel-Weil formula in the form that the proof uses. Let $B$ be the sum of a Hermitian space $W$ of rank $r$ and $t$ hyperbolic planes, with $1 \le r < t$. The Siegel Eisenstein series of a section from $B$ has at most a simple pole at $s = t/2$, and its residue lies in the closed span of the theta functions of $W$, [Theorem 5.7.3](/entry/res-core/). The inputs include the Fourier-Jacobi coefficient of an Eisenstein series, [Theorem 5.2.8](/entry/fje-fj-eis/), the completeness of theta functions, [Theorem 5.3.8](/entry/th-tot-th-total/), and the residue functional as an orbit integral, [Theorem 5.6.3](/entry/eis-meas/). The last section computes the unramified doubling integral at an inert place, [Theorem 5.8.2](/entry/inert-zeta-top-unram-doubling-inert/), and proves with the doubling method that the theta lift down to $\mathrm{U}(W)$ is not zero, [Theorem 5.8.3](/entry/down-ne-of-core/).

Chapter 6 proves that the global theta lift from a definite unitary group of rank $n$ to a definite unitary group of rank $d$ is not zero in the stable range, for test vectors that it constructs. It builds the Weil representation of a good unitary pair, [Theorem 6.1.4](/entry/exists-is-weil-k/), and proves the Siegel-Weil formula for a definite space, [Theorem 6.4.1](/entry/siegel-weil-std-k/). With bumps, [Theorem 6.5.4](/entry/bump-any-k/), and the Rallis inner product identity it proves the lift in the stable range, [Theorem 6.6.6](/entry/t-lift-holds-k/). An automorphic representation of even rank then has two lifts of odd size whose extra Hecke characters are disjoint, [Theorem 6.7.4](/entry/two-up-ok-of-k/). The last section works over finite fields: the Shintani lift of a product of cuspidal characters is in the span of the base change product, [Theorem 6.8.5](/entry/kills-lift-all/).

Chapter 7 proves the unfolding laws behind the seesaw identities for Bessel periods of theta lifts. A chain of theta lifts climbs a tower of Hermitian spaces. Its Bessel and Fourier-Jacobi periods unfold into each other, rung by rung, and the chain closes with a seesaw identity at the bottom. The chapter starts with the mixed model of the Weil representation, [Theorem 7.1.5](/entry/mixed-source/). It proves the evaluation form of the Bessel unfolding, [Theorem 7.2.6](/entry/eval-bf-of-descent/), and the eight unfolding laws of a chain, [Theorem 7.3.3](/entry/unfoldings-of-chain/). The local inputs are the flat maps at split places, [Theorem 7.4.4](/entry/flat-fb-holds/) and [Theorem 7.4.5](/entry/flat-bf-of-laws/), the archimedean facts, [Theorem 7.5.1](/entry/arch-on-record-closed/), and the standard form of the Weil datum at an inert place, [Theorem 7.5.4](/entry/std-cayley-of-inert/). The last result is a quantitative Mahler criterion, which bounds the growth of automorphic functions near the cusps, [Theorem 7.6.3](/entry/ht-min-of-reduced/).

Chapter 8 proves that Bessel periods climb a theta tower. If a column chain of cusp forms has a nonzero period against a row form at the bottom of a test grid, then a theta lift of the row form has a nonzero Bessel period at the top, [Theorem 8.13.12](/entry/bessel-period-nonvanishing-ascent-of-depth-data/). The tools come first: the constant term along a Witt tower, [Theorem 8.2.9](/entry/constant-term-lift-isotropic-subspace/), the positivity and decay of discrete series coefficients on the lowest $K$-type, [Theorem 8.3.9](/entry/lowest-type-positive-and-decays/), and the span of theta series in the bounded holomorphic functions, [Theorem 8.5.7](/entry/mem-span-theta-series-of-bounded-holomorphic/). Then come the test grid, [Theorem 8.6.5](/entry/exists-grid-pre/), cuspidal lifts, [Theorem 8.8.3](/entry/lifts-cuspidal/), the flat dictionary, [Theorem 8.10.7](/entry/flat-at-big/), the periods on one line, [Theorem 8.11.4](/entry/row-test-real-places-definite-periods-on-one-line/), and the reduction of periods to Fock vectors, [Theorem 8.12.9](/entry/period-reduction-to-fock-vectors-large-depth/). The last section climbs the tower one rung at a time, from a Bessel period to a Fourier-Jacobi period and back, [Theorem 8.13.4](/entry/bessel-to-fourier-jacobi-nonvanishing-tower/).

The chapters use each other in a few places. Theorem 4.4.4 gives Theorem 2.8.4. Chapters 2 and 3 depend on each other: the results of Section 2.4 are inputs of the spherical closure 3.8.2, and the spherical closure proves Theorem 2.6.5. Theorem 5.8.2 gives the inert local factor 2.4.1. Section 1.18 takes its real branching input from Theorem 2.8.3. Chapter 6 repeats Theorem 2.5.5 and the constant term split of Section 4.15 over other base data. In the last block, the unfolding laws of Theorem 7.3.3 feed the row chain of Section 8.12, and the archimedean facts of Section 8.3 feed Theorem 7.5.1. So a reader can take Chapters 4 and 5 first, then Chapters 2 and 3 together, and Chapter 1 after Section 2.8. Chapter 6 stands alone and can come at any point after Chapter 4. Chapters 7 and 8 form one block, with Section 8.3 before Section 7.5.

## Gaussian laws at a real place

The reduction of [the Siegel-Weil formula at $s_0$](/entry/lit/) needs two facts about the Weil representation at one real place $\tau$. The first is a Gaussian law: the local operators of $U(n,n)(\mathbb{R})$ carry a Gaussian with a Siegel matrix $Z$ to a multiple of the Gaussian with matrix $y \cdot Z$, by an automorphy factor. The second is the dilation property: the theta integral along a one-parameter family of dilations is the restriction of a holomorphic function, whose derivatives are theta integrals of polynomial-Gaussian functions. This section proves both facts from the Schrödinger formulas, and it proves the property of local instances used in [the third reduction](/entry/top5/).

We fix a context: standard data as in [the previous section](/entry/lit/), a real place $\tau$ of $F$, and an embedding $\iota$ of the real group into $G(\mathbb{A})$ at $\tau$. Here the real group is
$$G_n(\mathbb{R}) = \{ y \in \mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C}) : y^{H} J_n y = J_n \},$$
for the standard skew-Hermitian form $J_n$ of $U(n,n)$. At $\tau$ we choose an orthogonal frame of $V \otimes_\tau \mathbb{C}$ for the Cartan involution $s_\tau$, with signs $\varepsilon_a \in \{+, -\}$, $a = 1, \dots, m$. A Schwartz function $f$ on $\mathrm{Mat}_{m \times n}(\mathbb{C})$ and a function $\Psi$ of the other components give a pure tensor $f \otimes \Psi$ in the Schwartz space.

**Definition 4.6.1 (Local operator).** A family $L(y)$, $y \in G_n(\mathbb{R})$, of maps on functions on $\mathrm{Mat}_{m \times n}(\mathbb{C})$ is a local operator for $\omega$ at $\tau$ if each $L(y)$ maps Schwartz functions to Schwartz functions and
$$\omega(\iota y)(f \otimes \Psi) = L(y) f \otimes \Psi$$
for all Schwartz $f$ and all admissible $\Psi$ away from $\tau$.

**Definition 4.6.2 (Siegel Gaussians).** For $y \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times n}(\mathbb{C})$ and a sign $b$ let $Q_b(y)$ be the $n \times n$ matrix with entries $\sum_{\varepsilon_a = b} \bar y_{ai} y_{aj}$. An $n \times n$ complex matrix $Z$ is a Siegel matrix if $-\tfrac{i}{2}(Z - Z^H)$ is positive definite. For $c > 0$ and $t = \pm c$ the Siegel Gaussian of $Z$ is
$$g_Z(y) = \exp\bigl(i c \operatorname{tr}(Z Q_+(y)) - i c \operatorname{tr}(Z^H Q_-(y))\bigr),$$
where the signs are $\varepsilon$ if $t > 0$ and $-\varepsilon$ if $t < 0$.

For $Z = i \cdot 1$ this is $\exp(-c \sum |y_{ai}|^2)$, the standard Gaussian. Write $y = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}$ in blocks. The group acts on Siegel matrices by $y \cdot Z = (AZ + B)(CZ + D)^{-1}$, with automorphy factor
$$j_{e,p,q}(y, Z) = \det(y)^e \det(CZ + D)^{-p} \det(C Z^H + D)^{-q}.$$

**Theorem 4.6.3 (Gaussian law).** Assume that the Weyl element $w = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ lies in $G$, and assume the Fourier action: for each context, $\omega(w)$ and $\omega(w^{-1})$ act on pure tensors $f \otimes \Psi$, with $f$ at $\tau$, by $\kappa$ times a partial Fourier transform of $f$ and $\kappa^{-1}$ times its inverse, for some $\kappa \ne 0$. Then for every context and every local operator $L$, there is an integer $e$ such that for all $y \in G_n(\mathbb{R})$ and all Siegel matrices $Z$,
$$L(y) g_Z = j_{e,p,q}(y, Z) \, g_{y \cdot Z},$$
where $p$ and $q$ are the numbers of signs $+$ and $-$ in the frame (with the sign change for $t < 0$).

*Proof.* The group $G_n(\mathbb{R})$ is generated by the upper unipotent elements, the Levi elements and $w$. It suffices to prove the law for these generators and to check that it is stable under products. Stability under products is the cocycle relation of the factor $j$ together with the multiplicativity of $L$; for this we need that pure tensors determine their factors, which holds since the functions $\Psi$ away from $\tau$ are admissible. For the upper unipotent element $n(b)$, the Schrödinger formula multiplies $f$ by a character of the Gram matrix, which changes $Z$ to $Z + b$ with factor 1. For the lower unipotent elements we write them through $w$ and $n(b)$, and the Fourier action computes the partial Fourier transform of a Siegel Gaussian, which is a Siegel Gaussian of $-Z^{-1}$ times $\det$ factors. For the Levi elements $m(a)$, the law is a change of variables, and the factor $\det(a)^e$ comes from the character $\chi$ at $\tau$. Each Siegel Gaussian is a Schwartz function, so all steps stay in the Schwartz space. $\square$

**Corollary 4.6.4 (Gaussian law holds).** For every context and every local operator $L$ there is an integer $e$ with $L(y) g_Z = j_{e,p,q}(y, Z) \, g_{y \cdot Z}$ for all $y$ and all Siegel $Z$.

*Proof.* The element $w$ lies in $G$, and the Fourier action is a proved property of the Schrödinger model. Apply [the Gaussian law](/entry/gaussian-law-of-weil/). $\square$

Next, the dilation property. For a real vector $X \in \mathbb{R}^n$, a dilation family is a map $t \mapsto a(t)$ into $G_n(\mathbb{R})$ that acts by $e^{t X}$ on the Levi factor. Let $\delta_X$ be the operator on polynomials that differentiates the Gaussian times the polynomial along this family. For a polynomial $P$, write $\Theta_\Psi(P)(g)$ for the theta integral at $g$ of the pure tensor $(P \cdot \text{Gaussian}) \otimes \Psi$.

**Theorem 4.6.5 (Dilation property).** Assume Weil's convergence theorem for theta integrals, and the following properties of each context: the decomposition $g = x \iota(y)$ with $x$ trivial at $\tau$ and commuting with $\iota(G_n(\mathbb{R}))$; the action away from $\tau$; local factorisation; the Gaussian law; admissibility of the functions away from $\tau$; and the local isometry, which says that each $L(y)$ multiplies $L^2$ norms of Schwartz functions by a constant $\kappa > 0$. Then for every context, every admissible $\Psi$, every $g$, $X$ and dilation family $a(t)$, there are an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ containing $\mathbb{R}$ and a function $F(y, z)$ on $G_n(\mathbb{R}) \times U$, continuous on $G_n(\mathbb{R}) \times U$ and holomorphic in $z$, such that
$$F(y, t) = \Theta_\Psi(1)\bigl(g \, \iota(y \, a(t))\bigr), \qquad t \in \mathbb{R},$$
and the $r$-th derivative of $F(y, \cdot)$ at 0 is $\Theta_\Psi(\delta_X^r 1)(g \, \iota(y))$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.Glob.gIdil_of`.

*Proof.* Write $g = x \iota(y_0)$ as in the decomposition. Then $g \iota(y a(t)) = x \iota(y_0 y a(t))$, and the action away from $\tau$ moves $x$ to the factor $\Psi$. By local factorisation, the function at $\tau$ is $L(y_0 y a(t))$ applied to the standard Gaussian $g_{i}$. By the Gaussian law at $Z = i \cdot 1$, this is $j(y_0 y a(t), i) \, g_{(y_0 y a(t)) \cdot i}$. The dilation $a(t)$ acts on $Z$ by $e^{tX}$-scaling, so for complex $z$ in a strip $\{ |\operatorname{Im} z| < \delta \}$ the matrix $(y_0 y a(z)) \cdot i$ is still a Siegel matrix and the Gaussian depends holomorphically on $z$. The theta integral of this holomorphic family of Schwartz functions converges by Weil's theorem, locally uniformly; the local isometry controls the $L^2$ norms and so the Schwartz seminorms of the family. This gives $F$, holomorphic on the strip and continuous in $(y, z)$. Its derivatives at 0 are computed under the integral: differentiating the Gaussian $r$ times along the family gives $\delta_X^r 1$ times the Gaussian, and Schwartz functions of polynomial-Gaussian form are Schwartz. $\square$

**Corollary 4.6.6 (Dilation property holds).** Assume Weil's convergence theorem and that $G(\mathbb{A})$ is generated by $n(b)$, the Levi elements and the lower unipotent elements. Then the dilation property holds for every context.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.Glob.gIdil_gen`.

*Proof.* The decomposition, local factorisation, admissibility and the local isometry are proved properties of the Schrödinger model, and the Gaussian law holds by [its corollary](/entry/gauss-law/). The action away from $\tau$ follows from generation: each generator trivial at $\tau$ acts only on the factor away from $\tau$. Apply [the dilation property](/entry/g-idil-of/). $\square$

The last result gives the local instances used in the third reduction. A local operator in the polynomial sense is a family $L(y)$ with $\omega(\iota y)((P \cdot \text{Gaussian}) \otimes \Psi) = L(y)(P \cdot \text{Gaussian}) \otimes \Psi$ for all polynomials $P$.

**Theorem 4.6.7 (Local instances).** Assume the action away from $\tau$, and that the pure tensors $(P \cdot \text{Gaussian}) \otimes \Psi$ lie in both the Schwartz space and the Fock space. Then for every context and every local operator $L$ in the polynomial sense there are a linear map $P \mapsto s_L(P)$ from polynomials to functions on $G_n(\mathbb{R})$, a representation $\rho$ of $G_n(\mathbb{R})$ on polynomials, integers $p, q \ge 0$ and $e$, with the following properties: $(p, q)$ is a good signature for $n$; the local data of the frame hold for $(p, q, e, s_L, \rho)$; $s_L(P)(y) = L(y)(P \cdot \text{Gaussian})(0)$; and $L(k)(P \cdot \text{Gaussian}) = \rho(k)P \cdot \text{Gaussian}$ for each $k$ in the maximal compact subgroup.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.Glob.locInst_of_away`.

*Proof.* Put $s_L(P)(y) = L(y)(P \cdot \text{Gaussian})(0)$; this is linear in $P$. The action on polynomial-Gaussian functions comes from a cell decomposition of the action: the continuity of the operators, the Gaussian law for the vacuum (the case $P = 1$), and the vacuum property of the Gaussian, which is fixed by the maximal compact subgroup up to the character $\det(k)^e \det(\ldots)^{-p} \det(\ldots)^{-q}$. From these, the compact subgroup maps polynomial-Gaussian functions to polynomial-Gaussian functions through a representation $\rho$, and the section $s_L(P)$ has the equivariance of the local data. The membership of pure tensors in the two spaces lets us apply these identities to the factor at $\tau$. $\square$

**Remark 4.6.8 (Formalization).** The dilation property takes a hypothesis that Siegel Gaussians are Schwartz and never uses it; the argument is unnamed ([IteratedDerivMulConst.lean](lean:Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/SiegelWeilProof/HolomorphicContinuation/GlobalGaussLaws/IteratedDerivMulConst.lean#L185)). The good signature condition, the local data of a frame and the admissible class of functions away from $\tau$ are not unfolded here. The sketches of the proofs follow the names of the cited lemmas; the Gaussian law at the generators and the strip argument are given by those lemmas, which we have not all opened.

This theorem proves `LC`, a statement about polynomials and Gaussian moments, from `FFT`, the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for the unitary group. It is a step of the [proof of EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/): the theorem `absThm` on absolute convergence uses it, with `FFT` proved as `fft`. The proof moves `LC` through a Fock map to the statement `CoreLC`, which is proved in [`coreLC_of`](/entry/core-lc-of/). That entry has the main argument.

## Statement

Fix $m,n$, a sign $\varepsilon\colon\{1,\dots,m\}\to\{\pm1\}$ and $c>0$. Let $P_{m,n}$ be the complex polynomials in independent variables $x_{ai}$ and $\bar x_{ai}$, with $a\le m$ and $i\le n$. Evaluate $\bar x_{ai}$ as the complex conjugate of $x_{ai}$, and put

$$\operatorname{mom}_c(P)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}}P(x)\,e^{-c\sum|x_{ai}|^2}\,dx,\qquad Q_{ij}=\sum_{a}\varepsilon_a\,\bar x_{ai}x_{aj}.$$

Call $P$ *null* if $\operatorname{mom}_c(RP)=0$ for every $R$ in the algebra generated by the $Q_{ij}$. For $a,b\le m$ let

$$H_{ab}P=\sum_i\Bigl(x_{bi}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_{ai}}-\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b\,\bar x_{ai}\frac{\partial P}{\partial \bar x_{bi}}-c(1-\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b)\,x_{bi}\bar x_{ai}P\Bigr).$$

`FFT` says: if a polynomial in vectors $y_1,\dots,y_r$ and $z_1,\dots,z_s$ of $\mathbb{C}^k$ is unchanged when each $y$ moves by a unitary $u$ and each $z$ by $\bar u$, then it is a polynomial in the pairings $\sum_i y_{a,i}z_{b,i}$. This is Weyl's first fundamental theorem for $U(k)$ (H. Weyl, *The Classical Groups*, 1939, Ch. II).

**Theorem.** Assume `FFT`. Let $\lambda$ be a linear form on $P_{m,n}$ with $\lambda(H_{ab}P)=0$ for all $a,b,P$. Then $\lambda(P)=0$ for every null $P$.

## Proof

The proof is `lc_of_core` applied to `coreLC_of h`. `FFT` is used only in the second.

1. The Fock map $\Phi_c$ sends a monomial in the variables to the product of the creation operators $-\partial_k+2c\,\bar k$ applied to $1$, where $\bar k$ is multiplication by the conjugate of the variable $k$. It is linear and bijective. It turns the operators $H_{ab}$ into simpler operators $D_{ab}$: $H_{ab}\Phi_c=\Phi_c D_{ab}$.
2. Put $\Lambda=\lambda\circ\Phi_c$. Then $\Lambda$ kills every $D_{ab}p$.
3. `CoreLC` gives, for each $p$, an element $w$ of the algebra $\mathcal{K}_\varepsilon$ generated by the operators $K_{ij}$, with $\Lambda(p)=\operatorname{contr}_c(wp)$. Here $\operatorname{contr}_c$ is a linear form that sees only balanced monomials.
4. The null polynomials are stable under $\Phi_c\circ w$ for $w\in\mathcal{K}_\varepsilon$.
5. The moment of $\Phi_c(q)$ is $\operatorname{mom}_c(1)\operatorname{contr}_c(q)$, and $\operatorname{mom}_c(1)\neq0$. If $P=\Phi_c(p)$ is null, so is $\Phi_c(wp)$, and its moment is $0$. So $\operatorname{contr}_c(wp)=0$ and $\lambda(P)=0$.

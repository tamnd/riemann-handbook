## Gaussian moments and invariant theory

The absolute convergence theorem gives the holomorphic value at $s_0$ of an Eisenstein series whose section at a real place is a polynomial times a Gaussian. Its proof needs a description of the polynomials that are invisible to the theta integral: those whose Gaussian moments against all invariant polynomials vanish. This section proves two such descriptions from the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for the unitary group, and then the absolute convergence theorem. The theorem is the first input of [the main reduction](/entry/glob-top/), and it is used in [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/).

Fix $m, n \ge 1$, signs $\varepsilon_a \in \{+, -\}$ for $a = 1, \dots, m$, and $c > 0$. Let $\mathcal{P}_{m,n}$ be the ring of complex polynomials in independent variables $x_{ai}$ and $\bar x_{ai}$ ($a \le m$, $i \le n$). We evaluate a polynomial on $x \in \mathbb{C}^{m \times n}$ by reading $\bar x_{ai}$ as the complex conjugate of $x_{ai}$.

**Definition 4.8.1 (Gaussian moments and null polynomials).** The Gaussian moment of $P$ is
$$\operatorname{mom}_c(P) = \int_{\mathbb{C}^{m \times n}} P(x) \, e^{-c \sum |x_{ai}|^2} \, dx.$$
For a sign $b$ put $Q^b_{ij} = \sum_{\varepsilon_a = b} \bar x_{ai} x_{aj}$, and $Q_{ij} = Q^+_{ij} - Q^-_{ij}$. A polynomial $P$ is null if $\operatorname{mom}_c(RP) = 0$ for every $R$ in the algebra generated by the $Q_{ij}$.

The $Q_{ij}$ are the entries of the Gram matrix of the $n$ columns of $x$ for the form of signature $\varepsilon$. A null polynomial is one whose moments against all functions of the Gram matrix vanish, so it does not contribute to a theta integral.

**Definition 4.8.2 (Raising operators).** For $a, b \le m$ let
$$\begin{aligned} H_{ab} P = \sum_i \Bigl( & x_{bi} \frac{\partial P}{\partial x_{ai}} - \varepsilon_a \varepsilon_b \, \bar x_{ai} \frac{\partial P}{\partial \bar x_{bi}} \\ & - c (1 - \varepsilon_a \varepsilon_b) \, x_{bi} \bar x_{ai} P \Bigr). \end{aligned}$$

These operators are the action of the Lie algebra of $U(\varepsilon)$ on polynomial-Gaussian functions, written on the polynomial factor.

**Theorem 4.8.3 (First fundamental theorem for the unitary group).** Let $P$ be a polynomial in vectors $y_1, \dots, y_r$ and $z_1, \dots, z_s$ of $\mathbb{C}^k$ such that $P(u y_1, \dots, u y_r, \bar u z_1, \dots, \bar u z_s) = P(y, z)$ for every unitary $u$. Then $P$ is a polynomial in the pairings $\sum_i y_{a,i} z_{b,i}$.

This is Weyl's theorem: H. Weyl, *The Classical Groups*, Chapter II. It is proved in the development, and we use it as an input below.

We split the variables in two families. The first family $F_1$ holds the $x_{ai}$ with $\varepsilon_a = +$ and the $\bar x_{ai}$ with $\varepsilon_a = -$; the second family $F_2$ holds the rest. A box is the span of the $F_1$ monomials with fixed row degrees; it has finite dimension. A differential operator with polynomial coefficients in the first family has a symbol $s$, and $\operatorname{op}(s)$ is the operator. For $i, j \le n$ let
$$K_{ij} = \sum_{\varepsilon_a = +} x_{ai} \partial_{x_{aj}} - \sum_{\varepsilon_a = -} \bar x_{aj} \partial_{\bar x_{ai}},$$
and let $\mathcal{K}_\varepsilon$ be the algebra generated by the $K_{ij}$. The commutator of $K_{ij}$ with $\operatorname{op}(s)$ is $\operatorname{op}(\kappa_{ij}(s))$ for a symbol $\kappa_{ij}(s)$.

**Lemma 4.8.4 (Invariant operators).** Assume the first fundamental theorem. Let $s$ be a symbol in the first family with $\kappa_{ij}(s) = 0$ for all $i, j$. Then there is $w$ in the algebra generated by the first family operators $E^c_{ab}$ ($a, b \le m$) with $\operatorname{op}(s) f = w f$ for every $f$ in $F_1$.

*Proof.* The condition $\kappa_{ij}(s) = 0$ says that the symbol is invariant under the infinitesimal action of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ on the column index. In the symmetric form of the symbol, the coefficients become polynomials in the row vectors and their duals that are invariant under the unitary group in the column variable. The first fundamental theorem writes them as polynomials in the pairings, and the pairings are the symbols of the operators $E^c_{ab}$. The composition rule for symbols turns this into an identity of operators on $F_1$. $\square$

For the core statement, define the operators $D_{ab}$ on $\mathcal{P}_{m,n}$ by
$$D_{ab} = \sum_i \bigl( \bar x_{bi} \partial_{\bar x_{ai}} - x_{ai} \partial_{x_{bi}} \bigr) \quad (\varepsilon_a = \varepsilon_b),$$
$$D_{ab} = \sum_i \bigl( 2c \, \partial_{x_{bi}} \partial_{\bar x_{ai}} - \tfrac{1}{2c} x_{ai} \bar x_{bi} \bigr) \quad (\varepsilon_a \ne \varepsilon_b).$$
Let $\operatorname{contr}_c$ be the linear form that sends a monomial $\prod x^{s} \bar x^{t}$ to $\prod s! \, (2c)^{s}$ if $s = t$, and to 0 otherwise.

**Theorem 4.8.5 (Core identity).** Assume the first fundamental theorem. Let $\Lambda$ be a linear form on $\mathcal{P}_{m,n}$ with $\Lambda(D_{ab} p) = 0$ for all $a, b, p$. Then for each $p$ there is $w \in \mathcal{K}_\varepsilon$ with $\Lambda(p) = \operatorname{contr}_c(w p)$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.LC.coreLC_of`.

*Proof.* There is an operator $B$ that keeps each box, commutes with the first family part of the $D_{ab}$, and satisfies $\Lambda(f g) = \operatorname{contr}_c(B f \cdot g)$ for $f \in F_1$ and $g \in F_2$. A weighted inner product on $\mathcal{P}_{m,n}$ is Hermitian and positive, $K_{ij}$ and $K_{ji}$ are adjoint for it, and each $K_{ij}$ keeps each box. Fix $p$ and the box $V$ of its row degrees. An operator that keeps $V$ and commutes with all $K_{ij}$ has, by density, a symbol in the first family on $V$, and this symbol satisfies $\kappa_{ij}(s) = 0$. By [the lemma on invariant operators](/entry/inv-op/), it agrees on $V$ with an element of the algebra of the $E^c_{ab}$, so it commutes with $B|_V$. The double commutant theorem for a family closed under adjoints on a finite dimensional space with a positive Hermitian form puts $B|_V$ in the algebra generated by the $K_{ij}|_V$, and this lifts to some $w \in \mathcal{K}_\varepsilon$. Splitting each monomial of $p$ into its $F_1$ and $F_2$ parts and summing gives the identity. $\square$

**Theorem 4.8.6 (Null polynomials and raising operators).** Assume the core identity. Let $\lambda$ be a linear form on $\mathcal{P}_{m,n}$ with $\lambda(H_{ab} P) = 0$ for all $a, b, P$. Then $\lambda(P) = 0$ for every null polynomial $P$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.LC.lc_of_core` (also `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.lc_of`, the same conclusion from the first fundamental theorem through the core identity).

*Proof.* The Fock map $\Phi_c$ sends a monomial to the product of the creation operators $-\partial_k + 2c \bar k$ applied to 1. It is linear and bijective, and $H_{ab} \Phi_c = \Phi_c D_{ab}$. So $\Lambda = \lambda \circ \Phi_c$ kills every $D_{ab} p$. Let $P = \Phi_c(p)$ be null. [The core identity](/entry/core-lc-of/) gives $w \in \mathcal{K}_\varepsilon$ with $\lambda(P) = \operatorname{contr}_c(w p)$. The null polynomials are stable under $\Phi_c \circ w \circ \Phi_c^{-1}$, so $\Phi_c(w p)$ is null, and in particular its moment is 0. The moment of $\Phi_c(q)$ is $\operatorname{mom}_c(1) \operatorname{contr}_c(q)$ with $\operatorname{mom}_c(1) \ne 0$. So $\operatorname{contr}_c(w p) = 0$ and $\lambda(P) = 0$. The second form follows by composing with [the core identity](/entry/core-lc-of/), which holds under the first fundamental theorem. $\square$

**Theorem 4.8.7 (Averaging).** Assume the first fundamental theorem. For each polynomial $P$ there is $R$ in the algebra generated by the $Q^+_{ij}$ and $Q^-_{ij}$ such that $P - R$ is null.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.cyc_of`.

*Proof.* Let $R$ be the average of $P$ over the compact group $U(\varepsilon_+) \times U(\varepsilon_-)$ acting on the rows of each sign; this average is taken against Haar measure. The moments against invariant polynomials do not change under averaging, so $P - R$ is null. The average $R$ is invariant under the product of unitary groups, and the first fundamental theorem, applied separately to the rows of each sign, puts $R$ in the algebra generated by the $Q^b_{ij}$. $\square$

To state the absolute convergence theorem we need one more definition. For $t \ne 0$ and a Hermitian $b$ put
$$\sigma_P(b) = \int P(x) \, e^{-c \sum |x_{ai}|^2} \, e^{i t \operatorname{tr}(b \, Q(x))} \, dx.$$
We say that the null criterion holds if $P$ is null exactly when $\sigma_P(b) = 0$ for all Hermitian $b$. We say that local vacuum nonvanishing holds if for each good signature $(p, q)$ for $n$ and each compact group $K_g$ with a continuous homomorphism onto $K$, there are a singular test $\psi$ and group elements $a, b$ with
$$\int_{K_g} \Bigl( \int f_{p,q}\bigl(\iota(k) a \, n(\operatorname{herm} x) \, b\bigr) \psi(x) \, dx \Bigr) f_{p,q}(\iota(k))^{-1} \, dk \ne 0,$$
in the notation of [the section on leading terms](/entry/hol-lead-b/).

**Theorem 4.8.8 (Absolute convergence theorem).** Assume local vacuum nonvanishing, the conclusion of the averaging theorem, the conclusion of the theorem on null polynomials, and the null criterion. Let local data of signature $(p, q)$ and weight $e$ be given, with sections $s_L(P)$ and a representation $\rho$, and let $(p, q)$ be good for $n$. Let $G$, $P$, $\Gamma$, $\iota$, $s_0$, a height $H$, linear families of global sections $P \mapsto \sec(P)$ and theta integrals $P \mapsto I(P)$, and a set $\Lambda$ of linear forms on functions on $G$ satisfy the global data conditions. Then for every polynomial $P$ and every $g$, the function
$$z \mapsto \sum_{\gamma \in P \backslash \Gamma} H(\gamma g)^{z - s_0} \sec(P)(\gamma g)$$
has holomorphic value $I(P)(g)$ at $s_0$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.AbsSt.absThm_of`.

*Proof.* The null criterion shows that the sections of null polynomials vanish. The spherical theory of the real group (the Iwasawa decomposition and multiplicity one for the $K$-types) gives the spherical expansion of the theta integrals of polynomial-Gaussian sections; this uses the derivative of the vacuum section in the parameter, the holomorphy in the parameter and the theorem on null polynomials. Local vacuum nonvanishing then gives, through the local operators, a vacuum output identity at $s_0$. The averaging theorem gives the cyclicity of the sections: each section is a section of a Gram polynomial plus a null one. The global data conditions, which include that every $l \in \Lambda$ applied to the Eisenstein series of $\sec(1)$ has holomorphic value $l(I(1))$ at $s_0$, and that the forms of $\Lambda$ see only nondegenerate coefficients, glue these local facts into the holomorphic value for every $P$. $\square$

**Remark 4.8.9 (Formalization).** In the core identity, the lifting step takes a density statement for first family symbols that the proof also uses, and a semisimplicity fact that is passed and not used; the positive form comes with a clause that the weighted form of $(1/2, 0, 0)$ is 0, which is trivial ([CoreTop.lean](lean:Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/SiegelWeilProof/HolomorphicContinuation/PolynomialDerivatives/CoreTop.lean#L13)). The second name of the theorem on null polynomials only composes it with the core identity ([LcOf.lean](lean:Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/SiegelWeilProof/HolomorphicContinuation/PolynomialDerivatives/LcOf.lean#L6)). The global data conditions of the absolute convergence theorem are not unfolded here beyond the two clauses quoted, and the proof sketch follows the names of the cited lemmas.

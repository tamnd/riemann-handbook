## Test vectors and bumps

The proof that a global [[theta correspondence|6WB6TKE70H]] lift is not zero evaluates the Rallis inner product on a special vector. That vector is a pure tensor: at the infinite places it is a polynomial times a Gaussian in the [Fock model](/learn/theta/#the-fock-model), and at a finite set of finite places it is a *bump*, a Schwartz-Bruhat function that is fixed by a small open subgroup and that sees every element outside that subgroup. This section makes the two kinds of local vectors. At the infinite places the choice comes from the classical decomposition of polynomials on matrices under $GL_n \times GL_d$ (Kashiwara and Vergne 1978): the product of leading minors is a joint highest weight vector, and the weights of the two sides are related by a shift. At the finite places the bump is a Heisenberg translate of the vacuum vector by a point of a lattice divided by a high power of a uniformizer. Both results feed into [the non-vanishing of the theta lift](/entry/t-lift-of-tools-k/).

We work with the unitary dual pair of [the Weil representation of a good unitary pair](/entry/exists-is-weil-k/). So $E$ is a CM field with maximal real subfield $F$, $X$ is a Hermitian space of dimension $n$ and $W$ is a skew-Hermitian space of dimension $d$ over $E$, both definite (signature $(n, 0)$ and $(d, 0)$), and $\omega$ is the Weil representation of $U(X)(\mathbb{A}) \times U(W)(\mathbb{A})$ on the [[Schwartz space|GE020MY43M]] of $n \times d$ matrices over $\mathbb{A}_E$, made from a Weil datum with splitting characters $\chi_X$ and $\chi_W$ (see [splitting characters](/learn/theta/#splitting-characters)). We assume $1 \le n$. For a finite place $v$ let $U(X)_v$ be the image of $U(X)(F_v)$ in $U(X)(\mathbb{A})$, and let $\rho(u)$ be the Heisenberg operator of a local matrix $u$, which acts on the Schrödinger model by translation and a character.

**Definition 6.5.1 (Bump).** Let $v$ be a finite place. A *bump at $v$* is a pair $(H, \phi)$ of a subgroup $H$ of $U(X)(\mathbb{A})$ and a function $\phi$ on $(F_v)^{n \times d}$ with these properties.

1. $\phi$ is a Schwartz-Bruhat function and $\phi \ne 0$.
2. $H \subseteq U(X)_v$, and every element of $H$ is integral at $v$.
3. There is an open set $O$ that contains $1$ with $U(X)_v \cap O \subseteq H$.
4. $\omega(g, 1)\phi = \phi$ for all $g \in H$.
5. *Separation.* For each $g \in U(X)_v$ with $g \notin H$ there are a local matrix $u$ and constants $c \ne c'$ with $\rho(u)\phi = c\,\phi$ and $\rho(gu)\phi = c'\phi$.

Condition 5 is the reason for the name. When the local vector $\phi$ is put into the coefficient of the Rallis inner product, condition 5 makes the coefficient vanish at each $g$ outside $H$, because the two eigenvalues force a scalar $c/c' \ne 1$ to equal $1$. Condition 3 says that $H$ is open in $U(X)_v$, so $H$ has positive measure.

**Definition 6.5.2 (Lift weights).** Let $\lambda = (\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_n)$ be a dominant weight of $U(n)$, let $m_X$ and $m_W$ be integers, and let $\lambda' = (\lambda'_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda'_d)$ be a dominant weight of $U(d)$. Then $\lambda'$ is a *lift weight* of $\lambda$ for $(m_X, m_W)$ if for each $k = 1, \dots, d$
$$2\lambda'_k = \begin{cases} 2\lambda_k - d - m_W + n + m_X & \text{if } k \le n,\\ n + m_X & \text{if } k > n.\end{cases}$$

The integers $m_X$ and $m_W$ are the exponents of the splitting characters at an infinite place $w$: the character $\chi_X$ is $z \mapsto (z/\lvert z \rvert)^{m_X}$ at $w$, and likewise for $\chi_W$. These are the weights of [Hecke characters](/learn/class-field-theory/#hecke-characters).

**Theorem 6.5.3 (Real test vectors).** Assume $n \le d$, $X$ has Gram matrix $1$, $W$ has Gram matrix $\delta \cdot 1$, and $\chi_X$ and $\chi_W$ have exponents $m_X$ and $m_W$. Let $U$ be an [[automorphic form|68SJQEC11F]] representation of $U(n)$ with weight $\lambda_w$ at each infinite place $w$, and assume that $d + m_{W,w} \le 2\lambda_{w,k}$ for all $w$ and $k$. Then there are a nonzero vector $f_0$ of the polynomial Fock space $\mathcal{P}$ at the infinite places and a nonzero $\varphi \in U$ such that:

1. $f_0$ and $\varphi$ are images of one vector $x_0$ of an irreducible finite-dimensional representation $V$ of the maximal compact subgroup $K_\infty$ of $U(n)$, by maps $V \to \mathcal{P}$ and $V \to U$ that are $K_\infty$-equivariant (for $\omega$ and for right translation);
2. at each infinite place $w$ there is a lift weight $\lambda'_w$ of $\lambda_w$ for $(m_{X,w}, m_{W,w})$ such that the irreducible representation of $U(d)$ of weight $\lambda'_w$ maps $U(d)$-equivariantly into $\mathcal{P}$ with $f_0$ in the image.

*Proof.* The Fock model at the infinite places is a tensor product over $w$ of the space $\mathcal{P}_{n,d}$ of complex polynomials on $n \times d$ matrices. The standard model gives an injective linear map $\Phi$ from $\bigotimes_w \mathcal{P}_{n,d}$ onto $\mathcal{P}$ and integers $a_w$ and $b_w$ with $2a_w = d + m_{W,w}$ and $2b_w = n + m_{X,w}$. Under $\Phi$, an element $k$ of $U(n)$ at $w$ acts by $\det(k)^{a_w}$ times the left substitution $P(Z) \mapsto P({}^t k Z)$, and an element $k'$ of $U(d)$ acts by $\det(k')^{b_w}$ times the right substitution $P(Z) \mapsto P(Z k')$. This is the [Bargmann transform](/learn/theta/#the-fock-model) of the Gaussian model, with the $\det$ factors that come from the splitting characters.

Put $\mu_{w,k} = \lambda_{w,k} - a_w$. The assumption $d + m_{W,w} \le 2\lambda_{w,k}$ gives $\mu_{w,k} \ge 0$, and $\mu_w$ is non-increasing because $\lambda_w$ is dominant. So $\mu_w$ is a partition with at most $n$ parts. For $s = 1, \dots, n$ let $\Delta_s(Z)$ be the leading $s \times s$ minor of $Z$, and put
$$J_w(Z) = \prod_{s=1}^{n} \Delta_s(Z)^{\mu_{w,s} - \mu_{w,s+1}}, \qquad \mu_{w,n+1} = 0.$$
This is a nonzero polynomial because each $\Delta_s$ is nonzero and the exponents are non-negative. By the classical $(GL_n, GL_d)$ duality on $\mathcal{P}_{n,d}$ (Kashiwara and Vergne 1978), $J_w$ is a highest weight vector for both groups: it is a highest weight vector of weight $\mu_w$ for the left substitution and of weight $(\mu_w, 0, \dots, 0)$ for the right substitution. So there is an equivariant map from the irreducible representation of $U(n)$ of weight $\mu_w + a_w = \lambda_w$ (with the $\det^{a_w}$ twist) into $\mathcal{P}_{n,d}$ that hits $J_w$, and an equivariant map from the irreducible representation of $U(d)$ of weight $(\mu_w, 0) + b_w$ (with the $\det^{b_w}$ twist) into $\mathcal{P}_{n,d}$ that hits $J_w$.

On the automorphic side, the representation $U$ contains an irreducible $K_\infty$-type of weight $(\lambda_w)_w$, that is, an injective equivariant map $j$ from $\bigotimes_w V_{\lambda_w}$ into $U$. Let $x_1 = \bigotimes_w x_{1,w}$ be the tensor of the vectors that map to $J_w$, put $f_0 = \Phi(\bigotimes_w J_w)$ and $\varphi = j(x_1)$. A pure tensor of nonzero vectors is nonzero, and $\Phi$ and $j$ are injective, so $f_0 \ne 0$ and $\varphi \ne 0$. The tensor product of the maps of the first step, followed by $\Phi$, is the map $V \to \mathcal{P}$ of property 1, and $j$ is the map $V \to U$.

For property 2 put $\lambda'_{w,k} = \mu_{w,k} + b_w$ for $k \le n$ and $\lambda'_{w,k} = b_w$ for $k > n$. This is dominant. For $k \le n$ we have $2\lambda'_{w,k} = 2\lambda_{w,k} - 2a_w + 2b_w = 2\lambda_{w,k} - d - m_{W,w} + n + m_{X,w}$, and for $k > n$ we have $2\lambda'_{w,k} = n + m_{X,w}$. So $\lambda'_w$ is a lift weight. The map of the second step at $w$, tensored with the vectors $J_{w'}$ at the other places $w'$ and followed by $\Phi$, is the map of property 2, and it hits $f_0$. $\square$

The weight condition $d + m_W \le 2\lambda_k$ is a condition of the stable range: it says that the $K_\infty$-type of $U$ occurs in the Fock space. Without it the partition $\mu_w$ has a negative part and no polynomial has the right weight.

**Theorem 6.5.4 (Bumps exist).** Assume $2n \le d$. Let $v$ be a finite place and let $N$ be a neighbourhood of $1$ in $U(X)(\mathbb{A})$. Then there is a bump $(H, \phi)$ at $v$ with $H \subseteq N$.

Lean: `UpperTheory.TP.bumpAny_k`.

*Proof.* The local Weil representation at $v$ is the Schrödinger model, and the vacuum vector $\phi_0$ of the good Weil datum at $v$ is a nonzero Schwartz-Bruhat function. At each lattice point $\gamma$ the Heisenberg operator acts on the vacuum by a scalar: $\rho(\gamma)\phi_0 = Q(\gamma)\phi_0$ with $Q(\gamma) = \psi(\tfrac12 \langle \gamma, \gamma \rangle_\theta)$, where $\langle\,,\rangle$ is the symplectic form and $\theta$ is the element of $E$ of the good datum. Since $2n \le d$, there is a *lattice datum* at $v$: a lattice $\Lambda$ of $n \times n$ matrices that is a ring and is stable under multiplication by integers, a uniformizer $\varpi$, an $n \times d$ lattice matrix $x_1$ and a $d \times n$ matrix $r$ with $x_1 r = 1$, such that $\langle a x_1, b x_1 \rangle = 0$ for all $a, b \in \Lambda$, such that $1 + \varpi p$ is a unit with inverse in $1 + \Lambda$ for each $p \in \Lambda$, and such that the congruence subgroups for $\varpi^k \Lambda$ shrink to $1$. The lattice datum comes from an $n \times d$ matrix $Y$ with $Y J_W {}^t\bar Y = 0$ and a right inverse, that is, from a totally isotropic subspace of dimension $n$ in $W$ at $v$. The condition $2n \le d$ gives this subspace, with one argument at the places that split in $E$ and one at the inert places.

The vacuum is fixed by an open set of $U(X)(\mathbb{A}) \times U(W)(\mathbb{A})$ around $1$, and the integral elements at $v$ form an open set around $1$. Choose $k_0$ such that each element $g$ of $U(X)_v$ with $g - 1, g^{-1} - 1 \in \varpi^{k_0}\Lambda$ is in $N$, fixes the vacuum and is integral, put $k = k_0 + 1$ and
$$H = \{ g \in U(X)_v : g - 1 \in \varpi^k \Lambda,\ g^{-1} - 1 \in \varpi^k \Lambda \}, \qquad x_0 = \varpi^{-k} x_1, \qquad \phi = \rho(x_0)\phi_0.$$
Then $H \subseteq N$, every element of $H$ is integral, and $H$ contains a neighbourhood of $1$ in $U(X)_v$ by the shrinking property. The function $\phi$ is a translate of $\phi_0$ times a character, so it is Schwartz-Bruhat and nonzero. For a lattice point $\gamma$ the Heisenberg relation gives
$$\rho(\gamma)\phi = \psi(\langle \gamma, x_0 \rangle)\, Q(\gamma)\, \phi. \tag{$\ast$}$$

*$H$ fixes $\phi$.* Let $g \in H$ with $g - 1 = \varpi^k a$, $a \in \Lambda$. Then $g x_0 = x_0 + a x_1$. The Weil representation intertwines translations, so $\omega(g)\phi = \rho(g x_0)\omega(g)\phi_0 = \rho(x_0 + a x_1)\phi_0$. Since $x_0$ is a multiple of $x_1$, the form $\langle x_0, a x_1 \rangle$ vanishes, so $\rho(x_0 + a x_1) = \rho(x_0)\rho(a x_1)$, and $Q(a x_1) = 1$ for the same reason. Hence $\omega(g)\phi = \rho(x_0)\phi_0 = \phi$.

*Separation.* Let $g \in U(X)_v$ with $g \notin H$, and put $y = g^{-1}x_0 - x_0$. Assume first that $y$ is not of the form $\alpha + g^{-1}\beta$ with lattice points $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Then a lattice duality argument gives a lattice point $u$ with $gu$ also a lattice point and $\psi(\langle u, y \rangle) \ne 1$. The map $g$ preserves $Q$ and the symplectic form, so by $(\ast)$ the eigenvalues of $\rho(u)$ and $\rho(gu)$ on $\phi$ are $c = \psi(\langle u, x_0 \rangle)Q(u)$ and $c' = \psi(\langle u, g^{-1}x_0 \rangle)Q(u)$, and $c'/c = \psi(\langle u, y \rangle) \ne 1$. Now assume that $y = \alpha + g^{-1}\beta$ with lattice points $\alpha = a x_1$ and $\beta = b x_1$. Multiply by $\varpi^k$ and by $r$ on the right: $g^{-1}(1 - \varpi^k b') = 1 + \varpi^k a'$ with $a', b' \in \Lambda$. Since $k = k_0 + 1$, both $1 - \varpi^k b'$ and $1 + \varpi^k a'$ are units with inverses in $1 + \varpi^k\Lambda$, and then $g - 1$ and $g^{-1} - 1$ are in $\varpi^k\Lambda$. So $g \in H$, which is false. $\square$

The condition $2n \le d$ is the stable range. It is used only for the isotropic subspace of the lattice datum, and with it the bump has the separation property at every element outside $H$.

**Remark 6.5.5 (Formalization).** The test vector theorem takes the whole list of fourteen tools of the chapter as one hypothesis, and uses only the two Lie algebra tools, through the Fock model; these two fields are the copies for all base data, not the copies for the good base data ([GalTPDefs.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/GalTPDefs.lean#L41)). The bump theorem also takes the full list, and uses only the field that gives an open set fixing the vacuum ([TPGBumpAny.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/TPGBumpAny.lean#L182)). The file of the bump theorem has 22 private helper lemmas for the lattice computations, and the file of the test vector theorem has a private lemma for the action of the identity matrix ([TPGRealPair.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/TPGRealPair.lean#L26)). We have not opened the proofs of the Fock model, of the two highest weight maps, of the lattice datum or of the lattice duality step, so the proofs above follow the structure of the formal proofs and give the classical reason for these four steps.

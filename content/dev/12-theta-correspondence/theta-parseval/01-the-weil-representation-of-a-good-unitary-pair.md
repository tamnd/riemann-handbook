## The Weil representation of a good unitary pair

A theta lift needs a [[Weil representation|H3Q2703KKV]] of the adelic group $U(X)(\mathbb{A}) \times U(W)(\mathbb{A})$ for a hermitian space $X$ and a skew-hermitian space $W$. This section proves that such a representation exists, for a CM extension with a good base datum and a pair of splitting characters. The theorem is one of the tools of [the theta lift](/entry/t-lift-holds-k/), and its proof has three layers: a Weil datum attached to a self-dual lattice, a twist of that datum by the splitting characters, and the passage from the datum to the representation in a fixed Darboux basis.

Let $F$ be a totally real number field, $E/F$ a CM extension and $\sigma$ the nontrivial automorphism of $E$ over $F$. Let $\mathbb{A}_F$ be the [[adele ring|NYJFYZ0B4M]] of $F$ and $\mathbb{A}_E = \mathbb{A}_F \otimes_F E$. A *base datum* $B$ consists of a nontrivial additive character $\psi$ of $\mathbb{A}_F/F$, a finite set $P$ of finite places of $F$, and for each infinite place $w$ of $F$ an embedding of $E$ into $\mathbb{C}$ above $w$.

**Definition 6.1.1 (Good base datum).** The base datum $B$ is *good* for $E/F$ if these conditions hold:

1. $E/F$ is a CM extension with conjugation $\sigma$;
2. $E$ contains an element $x$ with $x^2 = -d$ for some integer $d > 0$;
3. the extension of rings of integers $\mathcal{O}_E/\mathcal{O}_F$ is unramified;
4. $\psi$ is the standard additive character;
5. at each finite place $v$ of $F$ that is inert in $E$, $v$ does not lie over $2$, the norm of $v$ is greater than $3$, and $\psi$ is unramified at $v$;
6. $\psi$ is unramified at each place of $P$.

Condition 2 says that $E$ contains an imaginary quadratic field. It is used in [the Siegel-Weil formula](/entry/siegel-weil-std-k/) of this chapter, which is proved for good base data in this sense.

**Definition 6.1.2 (Hermitian and skew-hermitian spaces).** A *space* of dimension $n$ over $E$ is $E^n$ with a Gram matrix $J \in M_n(E)$, an $\mathcal{O}_E$-lattice $L \subset E^n$, a matrix $\theta_w$ for each infinite place $w$, and a basis of $L$ at each place of $P$. Write $\langle x, y \rangle = x^{T} J \sigma(y)$. The space is *hermitian of signature $(r, s)$* if $\sigma(J)^{T} = J$; if at each infinite place $w$ the complex matrix $J_w$ obtained through the embedding of $B$ at $w$ has signature $(r, s)$; if $L$ is self-dual, that is, $y \in L$ exactly when $\langle y, x \rangle$ is integral for all $x \in L$; if $\theta_w$ is a Cartan involution for $J_w$; and if the bases at $P$ are split. It is *skew-hermitian of signature $(r, s)$* if $\sigma(J)^{T} = -J$, if $i J_w$ has signature $(r, s)$ at each $w$, if $L$ is self-dual for the forms $\operatorname{tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(o \langle y, x \rangle)$ with $o \in \mathcal{O}_E$, and if the same two conditions on $\theta_w$ and on the bases hold. The *unitary group* of the space is $U(J) = \{ g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E) : \sigma(g)^{T} J g = J \}$, see [unitary groups](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups) and [[unitary group|0R9R0Y1HDK]].

For a hermitian space $X$ of dimension $n$ and a skew-hermitian space $W$ of dimension $d$, the space $X \otimes_E W$ is a symplectic space over $F$ of dimension $2nd$. A *tensor model* $M$ for $(X, W)$ fixes a Darboux basis $e$ of this symplectic space, a measure $\mu$ and a set $D$ in $U(X)(\mathbb{A})$, and a vector $\phi_v^0$ at each finite place $v$. The model is *good* if $e$ sends each rational matrix to a point with rational coordinates, if each $\phi_v^0$ is a vacuum vector (a nonzero Schwartz-Bruhat function on which the lattice acts by an explicit character), if $\phi_v^0$ is the indicator function of the integral matrices for almost all $v$, if $\mu$ is a Haar measure, and if $D$ is a fundamental domain for $U(X)(F)$. The space of *polynomial test functions* of $M$ is the smallest space of Schwartz functions at the infinite places that contains the Gaussians of the Cartan involutions and is stable under the Heisenberg translates along real lines.

**Definition 6.1.3 (Splitting character).** Let $\eta$ be the quadratic character of $\mathbb{A}_F^\times/F^\times$ attached to $E/F$ by [class field theory](/learn/class-field-theory/#hecke-characters): $\eta(a) = 1$ if $a \in F^\times N(\mathbb{A}_E^\times)$ and $\eta(a) = -1$ otherwise. A Hecke character $\chi$ of $\mathbb{A}_E^\times/E^\times$ is a *splitting character of parity $n$* if $\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_F^\times} = \eta^n$: that is, $\chi(a) = 1$ for $a \in F^\times N(\mathbb{A}_E^\times)$ and $\chi(a) = (-1)^n$ for the other $a \in \mathbb{A}_F^\times$.

These are the characters that split the metaplectic cover over a unitary group (S. S. Kudla, "Splitting metaplectic covers of dual reductive pairs", Israel J. Math. 87 (1994), 361 to 401). A character $\chi_X$ of parity $n = \dim X$ and a character $\chi_W$ of parity $d = \dim W$ give a Weil representation of the product of the two unitary groups, see [splitting characters](/learn/theta/#splitting-characters).

**Theorem 6.1.4 (Existence of the Weil representation).** Let $E/F$ be a CM extension and $B$ a good base datum. Let $X$ be a hermitian space of dimension $n$ and signature $(r, s)$, and $W$ a skew-hermitian space of dimension $d$ and signature $(r', s')$. Let $M$ be a good tensor model for $(X, W)$. Let $\chi_X$ and $\chi_W$ be Hecke characters of $\mathbb{A}_E^\times$, splitting characters of parity $n$ and $d$. Then there is a Weil representation $\omega$ of $U(X)(\mathbb{A}) \times U(W)(\mathbb{A})$ on the [[Schwartz space|GE020MY43M]] of $\mathbb{A}_E^{n \times d}$ for $M$, $\psi$, $\chi_X$ and $\chi_W$.

Here a *Weil representation for $M$* is a map $\omega$ from the product of the two adelic unitary groups to operators on Schwartz functions, such that each factor acts by a continuous, smooth and unitary Weil representation in the Schrödinger model of the Darboux basis of $M$, with the splitting given by the two characters, and such that the two actions satisfy the equivariance and doubling laws of a dual pair. The proof uses three results, which we state first.

A *Weil datum* is the global object behind the Schrödinger model. Let $J_S \in M_{n_S}(E)$ and $J_T \in M_{n_T}(E)$ be hermitian and nondegenerate, and let $\xi_0 \in E^\times$ with $\sigma(\xi_0) = -\xi_0$. Then $\xi_0$ and the pair $(J_S, J_T)$ define a symplectic form on $M_{n_T \times n_S}(E)$ and a Heisenberg group. A *Weil datum* for $(J_S, J_T, \xi_0, \psi, \chi_S, \chi_T)$ is an action $\omega$ of $U(J_S)(\mathbb{A}) \times U(J_T)(\mathbb{A})$ on theta functions such that: $\chi_S$ and $\chi_T$ are Hecke characters with the restrictions $\eta^{n_S}$ and $\eta^{n_T}$; $\omega$ preserves theta functions and intertwines the Heisenberg translates; a rational element does not change the value at $0$ of a theta function; $\omega$ is continuous and preserves the $L^2$ norm over a fundamental domain; and at a split idempotent the linear forms that are invariant under one part act by a scalar times a splitting factor.

**Proposition 6.1.5 (A Weil datum from a self-dual lattice).** Let $B$ be a good base datum. Let $J_S$, $J_T$ be hermitian and nondegenerate, and $\xi_0 \neq 0$ with $\sigma(\xi_0) = -\xi_0$. Let $\Lambda \subset M_{n_T \times n_S}(E)$ be an $\mathcal{O}_E$-lattice that is good and rationally self-dual for the symplectic form. Assume that the theta functions of the pair are closed under Heisenberg translates, and let $(w, \lambda, \Omega)$ be a lattice representation for $\Lambda$: an element $w \in \mathcal{O}_E$ with $w + \sigma(w) = 1$, a multiplicative function $\lambda$ on $\mathbb{N}$, and an action $\Omega$ on theta functions that commutes with the lattice operators and acts on the vectors fixed by $\Lambda$ by a normalized index factor. Let $\eta_E$ be a Hecke character of $\mathbb{A}_E^\times$ with restriction $\eta$ to $\mathbb{A}_F^\times$, with exponent $1$ at each infinite place, and unramified at the inert places. Then there is a Weil datum $\omega$ for $(J_S, J_T, \xi_0, \psi, \eta_E^{n_S}, \eta_E^{n_T})$.

*Proof.* The proof takes three inputs: the existence of a quadratic character $\kappa$ attached to $\lambda$, a product formula for $\eta_E$ against $\kappa$, and a sign law for lattice indices. The character $\kappa$ of $\mathbb{A}_E^\times$ is continuous, with $\kappa^2 = 1$, trivial on $\mathbb{A}_F^\times$, trivial on the ideles that are units at the places not fixed by $\sigma$, and $\lambda(N\mathfrak{P}) = \kappa(z)\sqrt{N\mathfrak{P}}$ for an idele $z$ that is a uniformizer at such a place $\mathfrak{P}$ and a unit elsewhere. The product formula gives a sign $\varepsilon$, and with it, the lattice action $\Omega$ and the sign law give a finite part $\omega_f$: a Weil representation at the finite places for the characters $\eta_E^{n_S}$ and $\eta_E^{n_T}$, which on each element is a scalar multiple of $\Omega$. The standard sign of $\psi$ and a chart at each archimedean place give an infinite part $\omega_\infty$ in the Schrödinger model, with its vacuum vector. The two parts commute on theta functions. The product $\omega = \omega_f \omega_\infty$ is a Weil datum as soon as each rational pair $(\gamma, \gamma')$ fixes the value at $0$ of each theta function. The rational points are generated by elements that commute with a Cartan element, and the set of rational elements on which $\omega$ acts on the value at $0$ by the scalar $1$ is closed under products. On an element that commutes with a Cartan element this scalar is $1$, by the rational self-duality of $\Lambda$. So $\omega(\gamma, \gamma')f(0) = f(\gamma^{-1} \cdot 0 \cdot \gamma'^{-1}) = f(0)$. $\square$

**Lemma 6.1.6 (Weil datum with given splitting characters).** Let $B$ be a good base datum. Let $J_S$, $J_T$ be hermitian and nondegenerate, and $\xi_0 \neq 0$ with $\sigma(\xi_0) = -\xi_0$, such that the pair has a good rationally self-dual lattice. Let $\chi_S$ and $\chi_T$ be Hecke characters with restrictions $\eta^{n_S}$ and $\eta^{n_T}$ to $\mathbb{A}_F^\times$. Then there is a Weil datum $\omega$ for $(J_S, J_T, \xi_0, \psi, \chi_S, \chi_T)$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.ThKW.S.exists_isWeilDatumOF_k`.

*Proof.* Take a Hecke character $\eta_E$ as in the [Proposition 6.1.5](/entry/datum-k-k/), with restriction $\eta$, exponent $1$ and unramified at the inert places. Take the lattice $\Lambda$ that the hypothesis gives. The theta functions of a good base datum are closed under Heisenberg translates, and the lattice has a lattice representation $(w, \lambda, \Omega)$. The Proposition gives a Weil datum $\omega_0$ for the characters $\eta_E^{n_S}$ and $\eta_E^{n_T}$. The characters $\eta_E^{-n_S}\chi_S$ and $\eta_E^{-n_T}\chi_T$ are trivial on $\mathbb{A}_F^\times$, since both factors restrict to $\eta^{n_S}$ and $\eta^{n_T}$. A Weil datum twisted by such characters is again a Weil datum, and its characters are $\eta_E^{n_S} \cdot \eta_E^{-n_S}\chi_S = \chi_S$ and $\chi_T$. $\square$

**Lemma 6.1.7 (From a Weil datum to the pair representation).** Let $B$, $J_S$, $J_T$, $\xi_0$, $\chi_S$, $\chi_T$ be as in the last Lemma, and let $\omega$ be a Weil datum for them. Let $J_A = J_T^{T}$, $J_B \in M_{n_S}(E)$, and $\xi \in \mathbb{A}_E$ with $\xi J_B = \xi_0 J_S^{-1}$. Let $e$ be a Darboux basis of the symplectic space of $(\xi, J_A, J_B)$ that sends each rational matrix to a point with rational coordinates. Then there is a unitary Weil pair representation for $(\psi, \xi, J_A, J_B, e, \chi_T, \chi_S)$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.ThKW.S.exists_isUWeilPair_cm_k`.

*Proof.* The Weil datum gives a representation of the pair in the Schrödinger model of $e$. It is continuous and smooth, it is unitary, and it is equivariant for the action of the two groups. These four properties make a splitting of the pair, and its two restrictions give the Weil representations of the two factors. The doubling property of each factor comes from a doubled target for the datum: the pair embeds into the doubled unitary group, and the Weil representation of the doubled group restricts to the tensor product. $\square$

*Proof of the Theorem.* Choose $\xi_0 \in E^\times$ with $\sigma(\xi_0) = -\xi_0$; it exists since $E \neq F$. The Gram matrices $J_X$ and $J_W$ are invertible, since $X$ has a self-dual lattice and $J_W$ has a signature at each infinite place. So $J_X^{T}$ is hermitian and nondegenerate, and so is $-\xi_0 J_W^{-1}$, because $\sigma(-\xi_0 J_W^{-1})^{T} = \xi_0 (-J_W)^{-1} = -\xi_0 J_W^{-1}$. The CM condition gives a good rationally self-dual lattice for the pair $(J_S, J_T) = (-\xi_0 J_W^{-1}, J_X^{T})$. The [Lemma on splitting characters](/entry/exists-is-weil-datum-of-k/) with $\chi_S = \chi_W$ of parity $d$ and $\chi_T = \chi_X$ of parity $n$ gives a Weil datum $\omega_0$. Now
$$\xi_0 \bigl(-\xi_0 J_W^{-1}\bigr)^{-1} = \xi_0 \cdot (-\xi_0)^{-1} J_W = -J_W,$$
so $J_B = J_W$ and $\xi = -1$ satisfy $\xi J_B = \xi_0 J_S^{-1}$. The Darboux basis of $M$ sends rational matrices to rational points, since $M$ is good. So the [Lemma on the pair representation](/entry/exists-is-uweil-pair-cm-k/) with $J_A = J_X$ and $J_B = J_W$ gives the Weil representation for $M$. $\square$

The Theorem is used once, in the tools of the theta lift: there it is joined with the existence of a good tensor model, so that each pair $(X, W)$ in the theta lift has both a model and a Weil representation.

**Remark 6.1.8 (Formalization).** The statement has the same text as the theorem [s-weil-ex](/entry/s-weil-ex/) of Volume 10, with the condition $x^2 = -d$ in place of the condition that $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois; the proof is the same line for line. The condition $x^2 = -d$ is a field of the good base datum with the name `galois` ([SplitStab.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/HalfIndexLattices/SplitStab.lean#L20)). Of the conditions of a good tensor model, only the rationality of the Darboux basis is used ([WpSTop.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/WpSTop.lean#L41)). The pair lemma calls the doubled target lemma twice, once directly and once inside the doubling of the second factor ([WpSPair.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/WpSPair.lean#L34)). A structure `IsLatRepK` in [SplitStab.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/HalfIndexLattices/SplitStab.lean#L26) repeats the lattice representation and is not used. We have not unfolded the good lattice, the rational self-duality, the doubling part of the pair representation, or the three inputs of the Proposition.

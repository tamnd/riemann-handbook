## The constant term split

The Siegel-Weil formula of this chapter is proved by induction on the rank $n$ of the space $Y$. The induction step compares a Fourier coefficient of the Siegel Eisenstein series of rank $n$ with a Fourier coefficient of rank $n - 1$. This section proves that comparison, the *constant term split*: after an integration over a unipotent group, the coefficient of rank $n$ is the coefficient of rank $n - 1$ plus a second term, and the second term changes by an explicit power under a dilation. The split is the only input of [the Siegel-Weil formula from the constant term split](/entry/siegel-weil-std-of-ct-k/), and through it of [the Siegel-Weil formula](/entry/siegel-weil-std-k/). The same argument for the copy over the other good base data is in [the constant term split](/entry/ct-split-of/) of the Siegel-Weil chapter.

We use the notation of [the Siegel-Weil formula](/learn/theta/#the-siegel-weil-formula) and of [Siegel Eisenstein series](/learn/automorphic-forms/#siegel-eisenstein-series). A *Siegel-Weil datum* $d$ consists of a good base datum for $E/F$, a definite space $A$ of dimension $m$ and a space $Y$ of dimension $n$ (one hermitian, one skew-hermitian), the doubled unitary group $G = U(Y \oplus -Y)$ with its Siegel parabolic $P$, a frame of $Y \oplus -Y$, the standard maximal compact subgroup $K$ of $G(\mathbb{A})$, the height $\mathrm{Ht}$ on $G(\mathbb{A})$ with $\mathrm{Ht}(pk) = \lvert \det t \rvert$ for $p \in P(\mathbb{A})$ with Levi part $t$ and $k \in K$, a Hecke character $\chi$, and a Weil representation $\omega$ of $G(\mathbb{A})$ with its doubling functional. Let $f$ be a section of the induced representation of $G(\mathbb{A})$ from $P$ and $\chi$ at a point $s_1$. For $z \in \mathbb{C}$ put
$$\varphi_z(x) = \mathrm{Ht}(x)^{z - s_1} f(x), \qquad E(x, z) = \sum_{\gamma \in P(F) \backslash G(F)} \varphi_z(\gamma x).$$
We assume that this [[Eisenstein series|3NZBZXYWAJ]] converges absolutely for $\operatorname{Re} z$ large.

**Definition 6.2.1 (Fourier coefficient).** *Coefficient data* for $d$ consist of a coordinate map $\beta \mapsto n(\beta)$ from the hermitian matrices of size $n$ over $\mathbb{A}_F$ onto the unipotent radical $N(\mathbb{A})$ of $P$, a [Haar measure](/learn/analysis/#haar-measure) $\lambda$, and a fundamental domain $\mathcal{G}$ for the rational matrices of volume $1$. For a function $\varphi$ on $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A})$ and a rational hermitian matrix $T$, the *$T$-th Fourier coefficient* of $\varphi$ is
$$\varphi_T(x) = \int_{\mathcal{G}} \varphi\bigl(n(\beta)\, x\bigr)\, \psi\bigl(-\operatorname{tr}(T\beta)\bigr)\, d\lambda(\beta).$$

**Definition 6.2.2 (Lower rank datum).** A *lower rank datum* $L$ for $d$ is a Siegel-Weil datum $d'$ for a space $Y'$ of dimension $n - 1$, an index $i_0$ of the frame, and an embedding $j : G' \to G$ of the doubled group of $Y'$ that fixes the frame vectors at $i_0$. Two rational hermitian matrices $T$ of size $n$ and $T'$ of size $n - 1$ are *related*, $T \sim T'$, if $T$ is zero in row $i_0$ and in column $i_0$ and $T'$ is the matrix that remains. *Unipotent data* for $L$ are a map $u \mapsto u_M(u)$ onto a unipotent subgroup of $G(\mathbb{A})$, a measure $\nu$ and a domain $\mathcal{U}$ for the rational points.

For each coset $\gamma \in P(F) \backslash G(F)$ we look at the block of $\gamma$ in the lower left corner, in the frame. The coset is in the *first orbit* if the entries $(i, i_0)$ of this block are $0$ for every $i$. The *second orbit term* is
$$c_2(x) = \sum_{\gamma \notin \text{first orbit}} \int_{\mathcal{U}} \int_{\mathcal{G}} \varphi_z\bigl(\gamma\, n(\beta)\, u_M(u)\, x\bigr)\, \psi\bigl(-\operatorname{tr}(T\beta)\bigr)\, d\lambda(\beta)\, d\nu(u).$$
An element $y$ of the real unitary group of rank $n$ is a *dilation of parameter $\tau$* in the direction $e_{i_0}$ if it acts on the line of $e_{i_0}$ by $e^{\tau}$ in the sense of the frame; for a real place $w$ let $\iota_w(y)$ be its image in $G(\mathbb{A})$ at $w$.

**Theorem 6.2.3 (Constant term split).** Let $d$ be a Siegel-Weil datum, $L$ a lower rank datum and $f$ a section at $s_1$ whose Eisenstein series converges. There is $t \in \mathbb{R}$ such that for each $z$ with $\operatorname{Re} z > t$, all coefficient data for $d$ and for $L$, all unipotent data for $L$ and all related $T \sim T'$, there is a function $c_2$ on $G(\mathbb{A})$ with
$$\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathcal{U}} E_T\bigl(u_M(u)\, x, z\bigr)\, d\nu(u) &= \Bigl(\sum_{g \in P'(F) \backslash G'(F)} \mathrm{Ht}\bigl(j(g) x\bigr)^{z - s_1} f\bigl(j(g) x\bigr)\Bigr)_{T'} + c_2(x), \\ c_2\bigl(\iota_w(y)\, x\bigr) &= e^{\tau(n - 2z)}\, c_2(x) \end{aligned}$$
for each $x$, each real place $w$ and each dilation $y$ of parameter $\tau$. Here the coefficient on the right is the $T'$-th Fourier coefficient on $G'$, taken at $1$.

The first term on the right is a Fourier coefficient of an Eisenstein series of rank $n - 1$, for the section $g \mapsto f(j(g)x)$. The second term has the law that the induction needs: at the point $s_0$ of the Siegel-Weil formula its exponent decides whether it can contribute. The proof rests on an unfolding of $c_2$, which we state first.

**Theorem 6.2.4 (Unfolding of the second orbit term).** Let $d$, $L$ and $f$ be as above, and fix $z$. Take coefficient data for $d$ and for $L$, unipotent data for $L$, and related $T \sim T'$. Assume that the terms of the double sum and integral that define $c_2$ are integrable, and that their norms are summable over the cosets. Let $w_1 \in G(F)$ and a continuous map $c \mapsto c_M(c)$ with an additive Haar measure $\kappa$ satisfy these conditions: in the frame $w_1$ is the identity except for the block at the two positions $i_0$, which is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & a \\ b & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $a, b \in E^\times$; and for some $\delta \in E^\times$ each $c_M(u, r)$ is block upper triangular, with upper left block $1 + \sum_j u_j e_{i_0, e(j)}$ and upper right block $r \delta\, e_{i_0 i_0}$. Then there is $K \in \mathbb{C}$ such that for all $x$
$$c_2(x) = K \sum_{q' \in P'(F) \backslash G'(F)} \int_{\mathcal{G}'} \Bigl( \int \varphi_z\bigl(w_1\, c_M(c)\, j(q'\, n'(\beta))\, x\bigr)\, d\kappa(c) \Bigr)\, \psi\bigl(-\operatorname{tr}(T'\beta)\bigr)\, d\lambda'(\beta).$$

*Proof.* The rational points of the groups and the sets of cosets are countable. The unipotent radical of $P$ splits into three blocks of coordinates: the part of rank $n - 1$, the row and column $i_0$, and the corner entry. Under this splitting $\lambda$ is a constant $c_P$ times the product measure, and $\kappa$ is a constant $c_K$ times the measure on the last two blocks. Choose a fundamental domain for $F$ in $\mathbb{A}_F$. The cosets outside the first orbit are in bijection with pairs of a coset $q'$ of $P'(F) \backslash G'(F)$ and a rational point $c$, so that each coset has a representative $p\, w_1\, c_M(c)\, j(q')$ with $p \in P(F)$. Since $\varphi_z$ is left invariant under $P(F)$ up to the character, which is trivial on rational points, the term of the coset is the integral of $\varphi_z(w_1 c_M(c) j(q') \cdots)$. So $c_2$ is one integral of a sum over pairs $(q', c)$. The summability of the norms allows us to exchange the sum and the integral (Fubini for sums). The sum over the rational $c$ and the integrals over the fundamental domains in the second and third blocks fold into one integral over the adelic points of these blocks, that is, into the integral against $\kappa$. The constant is $K = c_P\, c_K^{-1}$. $\square$

The element $w_1$ and the map $c_M$ have a second property, the *change of variables law*: for each dilation $y$ of parameter $\tau$, the element $\iota_w(y)$ commutes with $j(G')$; $w_1 \iota_w(y) = p\, w_1$ for some $p \in P(\mathbb{A})$ with Levi part $t$, $\lvert \det t \rvert = e^{-2\tau}$ and $\chi(\det t) = 1$; and $c_M(c)\, \iota_w(y) = \iota_w(y)\, c_M(s(c))$ for a change of variables $s$ with $s_* \kappa = e^{2n\tau} \kappa$.

**Theorem 6.2.5 (Unfolding with the change of variables law).** Assume that for each Siegel-Weil datum and section as above there is a bound $t$ such that for $\operatorname{Re} z > t$ and fundamental domains with compact closure the terms of $c_2$ are integrable with summable norms. Assume also that each lower rank datum has $w_1$, $c_M$ and $\kappa$ as in the last theorem that satisfy the change of variables law. Then for $\operatorname{Re} z > t$ and fundamental domains with compact closure there are $w_1$, $c_M$ and $\kappa$ with the change of variables law and a constant $K$ such that the formula of the last theorem holds for all $x$.

*Proof.* Take $t$ from the first assumption, and $w_1$, $c_M$, $\kappa$ and $\delta$ from the second. The shapes of $w_1$ and $c_M$ and the shape of $u_M$ are the hypotheses of the [unfolding theorem](/entry/unf-k/), which gives $K$ and the formula. The change of variables law passes to the conclusion unchanged. The second assumption holds for all data: an explicit element $w_1$ and an explicit map $c_M$ have the required shape and law. This gives the second form of the theorem. $\square$

*Proof of the Constant term split.* The proof combines five facts, for $\operatorname{Re} z$ large.

1. The integral $\int_{\mathcal{U}} E_T(u_M(u)x, z)\, d\nu(u)$ does not depend on the fundamental domains $\mathcal{G}$ and $\mathcal{U}$, and the first term of the statement does not depend on $\mathcal{G}'$.
2. There are fundamental domains with compact closure.
3. On such domains the terms of the unfolded integral, one for each coset of $P(F) \backslash G(F)$, are integrable, and their norms are summable over the cosets.
4. The sum of the terms over the cosets in the first orbit is the first term of the statement, the $T'$-th coefficient of rank $n - 1$.
5. The second orbit term has the law $c_2(\iota_w(y)x) = e^{\tau(n - 2z)} c_2(x)$.

Take $t$ as the maximum of the three bounds in 1, 3 and 5. By 1 and 2 we may use domains with compact closure. On these, 3 lets us write the integral as the sum over cosets of $P(F) \backslash G(F)$ of the integrals of the terms. Split the sum into the first orbit and its complement. By 4 the first part is the coefficient of rank $n - 1$, and by definition the second part is $c_2$. Fact 5 follows from the [unfolding with the change of variables law](/entry/sec-unf-of-k/), which applies by 3. In the unfolded formula, replace $x$ by $\iota_w(y)x$. The element $\iota_w(y)$ commutes with $j(G')$. It moves past $c_M(c)$ by the change of variables $s$, which multiplies $\kappa$ by $e^{2n\tau}$, and then past $w_1$ to an element $p$ of $P(\mathbb{A})$ with $\lvert \det t \rvert = e^{-2\tau}$ and $\chi(\det t) = 1$. Since $f$ is a section, $\mathrm{Ht}(pg) = e^{-2\tau}\mathrm{Ht}(g)$ and $\chi(\det t) = 1$ give $\varphi_z(pg) = e^{-\tau(2z + n)} \varphi_z(g)$. Together this gives the factor $e^{2n\tau} \cdot e^{-\tau(2z + n)} = e^{\tau(n - 2z)}$. $\square$

**Remark 6.2.6 (Formalization).** The constant term split is proved with two hypotheses, the existence of auxiliary data for Siegel-Weil data and a coefficient existence statement, and its proof has the form `fun _ _ => …`, so neither is used ([SiegelWeilActTop.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/SiegelWeilActTop.lean#L17)). The unfolding with the change of variables law takes a hypothesis that the closure of $\mathcal{G}'$ is compact, and does not use it ([SecUnfOfCLawK.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/SecUnfOfCLawK.lean#L24)). A second structure with the same name and fields as the hypothesis of the unfolding theorem is in [FirstOrbitParabolicLeft.lean](lean:Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/FirstOrbitParabolicLeft.lean#L31). We have not opened the proofs of facts 1 to 5, of the existence of $w_1$ and $c_M$, or of the bijection of cosets, and we have not unfolded the definitions of a section and of a dilation.

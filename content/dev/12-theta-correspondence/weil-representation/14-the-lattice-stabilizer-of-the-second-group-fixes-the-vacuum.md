## The lattice stabilizer of the second group fixes the vacuum

This section proves the companion of the [theorem](/entry/vac-fixed-left/) of the previous section for the skew-Hermitian space $W$: at an inert place $v$ outside the bad set, the stabilizer in $\mathrm{U}(W)(F_v)$ of the lattice of $W$ fixes the local vacuum, both for the Weil representation $\omega'$ of $(V', W)$ and for the Weil representation $\omega$ of $(V, W)$. With the previous section this gives the spherical vacuum at inert places that the step [every character of the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) uses. The method is again doubling, but now of $W$: the stabilizer of the vacuum tensor in the doubled unitary group contains enough of a Siegel parabolic and of a unipotent group to contain the lattice stabilizer.

We keep the setting of the previous section: a seesaw triple $(\omega, \omega')$, a finite place $v$ of $F$ inert in $E$ with $v \notin T$, the local vacua $\varphi^\circ_v$ and $\varphi'^\circ_v$ of $\omega$ and $\omega'$ at $v$, and the notion of an operator that takes one local function to another at $v$.

### The doubled space

**Definition 1.14.1 (Doubled space).** The *doubled space* of $W$ is $W^\square = W \oplus (-W)$, with Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(J_W, -J_W)$. Its *diagonal* is the image of the columns $\binom{1}{1}$ and its *antidiagonal* the image of $\binom{1}{-1}$. Both are Lagrangians of $W^\square$. For $k \in \mathrm{U}(W)$ write $\iota(k) = \operatorname{diag}(k, 1) \in \mathrm{U}(W^\square)$.

Write $K^\square_v$ for the stabilizer in $\mathrm{U}(W^\square)$ of the doubled lattice $L \oplus L$ at $v$ (elements $g$ with $g - 1$ supported at $v$, with $g$ and $g^{-1}$ preserving $L \oplus L$). Let $P_{\mathrm{diag}}$ be the stabilizer of the diagonal, so that $p \in P_{\mathrm{diag}}$ means $p\binom{1}{1} = \binom{1}{1} t$ for an invertible $t$. Let $P_{\mathrm{anti}}$ be the stabilizer of the antidiagonal. Let $N_v$ be the set of $n \in K^\square_v$ that fix the antidiagonal pointwise and satisfy $n\binom{1}{1} = \binom{1}{1} + \binom{1}{-1}b$ for some matrix $b$: the integral unipotent radical of the parabolic of the antidiagonal.

For a Schwartz-Bruhat function $\Psi$ on $\mathbb{A}_F^{\,N}$ put
$$(\Psi \boxtimes \bar\Psi)(x, y) = \Psi(x)\, \overline{\Psi(y)},$$
a function on $\mathbb{A}_F^{\,N} \times \mathbb{A}_F^{\,N}$.

The Weil property of $\omega'$ includes a doubled Weil representation $\omega'^\square$ of $\mathrm{U}(V') \times \mathrm{U}(W^\square)$ with a *restriction law*: for $k \in \mathrm{U}(W)$,
$$\omega'^\square(\iota(k))(\Psi \boxtimes \bar\Psi) = \big(\omega'(1, k)\Psi\big) \boxtimes \bar\Psi.$$
The same holds for $\omega$. This is the seesaw of the doubling method: on $V' \otimes W^\square = (V' \otimes W) \oplus (V' \otimes (-W))$, the Weil representation of the doubled group restricts to $\omega' \otimes \bar\omega'$.

### The pair $(V', W)$

**Lemma 1.14.2 (The scalar is one for $\omega'$).** Let $k \in K_v(W)$, the lattice stabilizer of $W$ at $v$, and $c \in \mathbb{C}$. If $\omega'(1, k)$ takes $\varphi'^\circ_v$ to $c\,\varphi'^\circ_v$ at $v$, then $c = 1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.InertScalar.scalar_eq_one` (also `GlobalTheory.T.weil_vacuum_scalar_eq_one_inert_place`, a second name).

*Proof.* Choose a function $R$ on the other places with $R(0) \neq 0$, and put $\Psi = \varphi'^\circ_v \otimes R$, a Schwartz-Bruhat function on $\mathbb{A}_F^{\,a' \times (a+a')}$. Let $H$ be the stabilizer of $\Psi \boxtimes \bar\Psi$ in $\mathrm{U}(W^\square)$ under $\omega'^\square$. It is a subgroup. Two facts about the doubled vacuum at an inert place $v \notin T$ show that $H$ contains $K^\square_v \cap P_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and $N_v$: the integral elements of the Siegel parabolic of the diagonal fix $\Psi \boxtimes \bar\Psi$, and so do the integral unipotent elements of $N_v$. In the Schrödinger model of the diagonal polarization these elements act through their Levi part and by multiplication with $\psi$ of a quadratic form, and at an unramified inert place both factors are trivial on the doubled vacuum. Next, $\iota(k)$ lies in the subgroup generated by $K^\square_v \cap P_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and $N_v$. So $\iota(k) \in H$. By the restriction law and $\omega'(1, k)\Psi = c\Psi$,
$$c\, (\Psi \boxtimes \bar\Psi) = \omega'^\square(\iota(k))(\Psi \boxtimes \bar\Psi) = \Psi \boxtimes \bar\Psi.$$
Choose $y$ with $\varphi'^\circ_v(y) \neq 0$ and lift it to an adele $x$ supported at $v$. Then $\Psi(x) = \varphi'^\circ_v(y) R(0) \neq 0$. Evaluate the identity at $(x, x)$: $c\,|\Psi(x)|^2 = |\Psi(x)|^2$, so $c = 1$. $\square$

**Theorem 1.14.3 (The lattice stabilizer of $W$ fixes the vacuum of $\omega'$).** Let $(\omega, \omega')$ be a seesaw triple, $v$ a finite place of $F$ inert in $E$ with $v \notin T$, and $k \in K_v(W)$. Then $\omega'(1, k)$ takes $\varphi'^\circ_v$ to itself at $v$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.InertSph.vac_fixed'` (also `GlobalTheory.T.inertPlace_latticeStabilizer_fixes_vacuum`, a second name).

*Proof.* Since $k - 1$ is supported at $v$, the operator $\omega'(1, k)$ acts locally at $v$ and takes $\varphi'^\circ_v$ to some $\varphi_1$. The element $k$ preserves the lattice of $W$ and the form, so $\varphi_1$ satisfies the vacuum condition of $\varphi'^\circ_v$ for the lattice $L_v$. At an inert place the functions with this condition form a line, so $\varphi_1 = c\,\varphi'^\circ_v$. The [lemma](/entry/inert-scalar-scalar-eq-one/) gives $c = 1$. $\square$

### The pair $(V, W)$

**Lemma 1.14.4 (The eigenvalue is one for $\omega$).** Let $k \in K_v(W)$ and $c \in \mathbb{C}$. If $\omega(1, k)$ takes $\varphi^\circ_v$ to $c\,\varphi^\circ_v$ at $v$, then $c = 1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.DblVac.eigen_eq_one` (also `GlobalTheory.T.latticeStabilizer_vacuum_eigenvalue_eq_one_inertPlace`, a second name).

*Proof.* Choose a function $r$ on the other places such that $V_0 = \varphi^\circ_v \otimes r$ is a nonzero Schwartz-Bruhat function, and put $S = V_0 \boxtimes \bar V_0$. Then $S(x, x) = |V_0(x)|^2 \neq 0$ for some $x$. Let $H$ be the stabilizer of $S$ in $\mathrm{U}(W^\square)$ under $\omega^\square$.

1. $H$ contains $K^\square_v \cap P_{\mathrm{diag}}$. An element $p$ of this set scales $S$ by some $\mu$, since it preserves the doubled lattice and the vacuum condition, and for $p$ in the stabilizer of the diagonal the scalar $\mu$ is $1$.
2. $H$ contains $K^\square_v \cap P_{\mathrm{anti}}$. The element $w_0 = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1)$ lies in $K^\square_v$ and exchanges the diagonal and the antidiagonal, so $w_0 q w_0^{-1} \in K^\square_v \cap P_{\mathrm{diag}}$ for $q \in K^\square_v \cap P_{\mathrm{anti}}$. The element $w_0$ scales $S$ by some $\mu$, and $w_0^{-1}$ by $\mu^{-1}$. So $\omega^\square(q)S = \mu^{-1}\mu S = S$.
3. At an inert place, $K^\square_v$ is generated by $K^\square_v \cap P_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and $K^\square_v \cap P_{\mathrm{anti}}$, and $\iota(k) \in K^\square_v$. So $\iota(k) \in H$.

By the restriction law, $c\, S = \omega^\square(\iota(k)) S = S$. Evaluating at a point where $S \neq 0$ gives $c = 1$. $\square$

**Theorem 1.14.5 (The lattice stabilizer of $W$ fixes the vacuum of $\omega$).** Let $(\omega, \omega')$ be a seesaw triple, $v$ a finite place of $F$ inert in $E$ with $v \notin T$, and $k \in K_v(W)$. Then $\omega(1, k)$ takes $\varphi^\circ_v$ to itself at $v$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.InertSph.vac_fixed` (also `GlobalTheory.T.inert_place_lattice_stabiliser_fixes_vacuum`, a second name).

*Proof.* As for $\omega'$: $\omega(1, k)$ takes $\varphi^\circ_v$ to a function $\varphi_1$ at $v$, and $\varphi_1$ has the vacuum type of $\varphi^\circ_v$, since $k$ preserves the lattice. The tensor lattice at an inert place is self-dual, so the functions fixed by the Heisenberg group of this lattice (with its phase) form a line, and $\varphi_1 = c\,\varphi^\circ_v$. The [lemma](/entry/eigen-eq-one/) gives $c = 1$. $\square$

**Remark 1.14.6.** In the classical language the theorems say that the vacuum vector at an unramified inert place is $K_v$-spherical for both members of the dual pair. For the characteristic function of a self-dual lattice and the unramified splitting this is the computation in S. Kudla, *Splitting metaplectic covers of dual reductive pairs*, Israel J. Math. 87 (1994). The doubling argument here avoids any formula for the splitting: it only uses that the vacuum tensor is fixed by an integral Siegel parabolic.

**Remark 1.14.7 (Formalization).** All four second names are `first | exact` wrappers. There are two files with near-identical names, InertPlaceLatticeStabilizerFixesVacuum.lean (the theorem for $\omega'$, `vac_fixed'`) and InertPlaceLatticeStabiliserFixesVacuum.lean (the theorem for $\omega$, `vac_fixed`), which differ in one letter. The two lemmas on the scalar use different subgroups: the lemma for $\omega'$ uses $K^\square_v \cap P_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and $N_v$, through `inertPlace_doubledFirstFactor_mem_siegelClosure`, and the lemma for $\omega$ uses the two parabolics, through `inertPlace_lattice_stabiliser_generated_diag_antidiag`. The doubled Weil representation and the restriction law are taken from the Weil property of $\omega$ and $\omega'$ in the triple; the facts on the action of the integral parabolic and unipotent elements are results that the proofs cite and that are proved elsewhere, and the account in the proofs above gives their statements, not their proofs.

## Notes and references

The chapter builds the Weil representation of a unitary dual pair over a CM field and proves the facts about it that the theta correspondence of the main argument uses. Its results feed [the moderate growth of theta functions](/entry/theta-moderate-growth/), [the invariance of the theta sum under rational points](/entry/theta-sum-omega-rational/), [the existence of an algebraic vector with nonzero definite theta lift](/entry/exists-algebraic-vector-definite-theta-ne/), [the constant ratio at the bottom of the theta tower](/entry/theta-tower-bottom-constant-ratio/) and [the identity of the Petersson norm of a theta lift with a Rallis integral](/entry/theta-lift-petersson-norm-eq-rallis-integral/). The global Weil datum of Section 16 and the vacuum results of Sections 13 and 14 are used in the step [every character of the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/), the Galois conjugates of Section 11 in the step [the hypotheses are nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/), and the nonzero bottom period of Section 18 in [real functionals separate](/entry/real-functionals-separate-of-hypothesis/). The published inputs of Sections 17 and 18 are fields of the bundle of [published local inputs](/step/published-results-first/).

Section 1 is the local class field theory of a quadratic extension: the norm residue symbol identifies $F^\times/\mathrm N E^\times$ with a group of order $2$, and the sign is multiplicative. See J.-P. Serre, *Local class field theory*, in the volume of Cassels and Fröhlich, Section 2.

Sections 2 to 4 build the local Weil representation of a split unitary group $\mathrm{U}(n,n)$ on a Schrödinger model without the metaplectic group. The Gauss integral of a Hermitian form and the constant of the Weyl element go back to A. Weil, *Sur certains groupes d'opérateurs unitaires* (1964), Chapter I, where the Weil index appears. The big cell relation and the action of the Siegel Levi through a character $\chi$ with $\chi|_{F^\times} = \eta^m$ are the splitting of S. Kudla, *Splitting metaplectic covers of dual reductive pairs* (1994), Section 3 and Theorem 3.1. The normalization by Hecke characters whose restriction is a power of $\eta_{E/F}$ is the one of M. Harris, S. Kudla and W. J. Sweet, *Theta dichotomy for unitary groups* (1996), Section 1. For the general theory of the Weil representation over a local field and of the local theta correspondence, see C. Mœglin, M.-F. Vignéras and J.-L. Waldspurger, *Correspondances de Howe sur un corps p-adique*, Lecture Notes in Math. 1291.

Sections 5, 6 and 12 treat the archimedean places in the Fock model. The Fock model and the Bargmann transform are in V. Bargmann (1961) and in G. B. Folland, *Harmonic Analysis in Phase Space*, Chapter 4. The Howe duality statement of Section 5 is the case of a character of R. Howe, *Transcending classical invariant theory* (1989), Theorem 2.1. The density of the polynomial Fock vectors in Section 6 is, for a positive definite form, the density of the Hermite functions in the Schwartz space; see M. Reed and B. Simon, Volume I, Theorem V.13. Section 12 is the existence of a Fock model of the Weil representation of a real unitary dual pair, read in frames adapted to the Cartan involutions.

Sections 7, 8, 9 and 15 are global. The decay bound of Section 7 is the adelic form of the estimates of A. Weil, *Sur la formule de Siegel dans la théorie des groupes classiques* (1965), Chapter I, which give the convergence of theta series. The invariance of the theta distribution under rational points in Sections 8 and 15 is the theorem of Weil (1964) for the metaplectic group, restricted to a unitary dual pair through the splitting of Kudla. The proof here replaces the generators of $\mathrm{U}(W)(F)$ by the parabolics of a doubled group, in the spirit of the doubling method. Section 9 says that the finite part of the Weil representation does not touch the archimedean variable, so that the Fock and Schrödinger pictures agree there.

Section 10 uses the classification of Hermitian forms over number fields by rank, determinant and signatures: W. Landherr (1936), and W. Scharlau, *Quadratic and Hermitian Forms*, Chapter 10, Theorem 6.9. Section 11 is a form of the rationality of theta lifts between unitary groups and of the action of $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)$ on them, compare M. Harris, *L-functions and periods of polarized regular motives* (1997).

Sections 13 and 14 prove that the vacuum vector at an unramified inert place is fixed by the lattice stabilizers of both members of the dual pair. For the characteristic function of a self-dual lattice and an unramified splitting this is in the computations of Kudla (1994). The proofs here use the doubling method and the invariance of the doubled vacuum under an integral Siegel parabolic, and need no formula for the splitting. Section 16 assembles a global Weil representation with prescribed splitting characters from a finite part and an infinite part, as in Weil (1964), Kudla (1994) and Harris, Kudla and Sweet (1996).

Sections 17 and 18 prove that the bottom period of the theta tower is not zero. The cusp forms are Poincaré series of discrete series matrix coefficients, and the vanishing of their constant terms comes from the theorem of Harish-Chandra that integrable matrix coefficients are cusp forms on the real group; see Harish-Chandra, *Automorphic forms on semisimple Lie groups*, Chapter I. The inputs taken as given are the theory of Harish-Chandra modules (Harish-Chandra, 1953), the globalization theorem of Casselman and Wallach (W. Casselman, 1989; N. Wallach, *Real Reductive Groups II*, Chapter 11) and the rapid decay of cusp forms (C. Mœglin and J.-L. Waldspurger, *Spectral Decomposition and Eisenstein Series*, Section I.2). The real branching input of Section 18 is a quantitative branching statement for discrete series from $\mathrm{U}(n+1)$ to $\mathrm{U}(n)$; for compact groups the interlacing condition is the classical branching rule, see H. Weyl, *The Classical Groups*.

**Remark 1.19.1 (Formalization).** The declaration listed for this section is `GlobalTheory.howeDualityReal_holds` ([HoweDualityRealHolds.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/HoweDualityRealHolds.lean#L19)), the Howe duality statement of Section 5, where its hypotheses `ResEtaPow` and `IsHecke` are bound as `_` and not used. Across the chapter the second names of the form `GlobalTheory.T.*` are `first | exact ...` wrappers under `maxHeartbeats`. Several statements are weaker or wider than their names suggest: the Fock data of Section 12 drop the continuity part of the Weil property, the lemma on the Lagrangian in Section 13 does not use the hypothesis $v \notin T$, the target automorphy of Section 15 covers only elements of the form $(1, \gamma')$, and the extension of the global Weil datum in Section 16 acts by the identity on a complement chosen by choice. The analytic results of Sections 17 and 18 rest on the three published inputs `HarishChandraModules`, `CasselmanWallach` and `CuspRapidDecay`, and on cited results on the depth of the grid and on the transfer of a nonzero period to $K$-finite vectors.

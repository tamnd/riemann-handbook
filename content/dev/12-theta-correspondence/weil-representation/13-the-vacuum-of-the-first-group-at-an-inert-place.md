## The vacuum of the first group at an inert place

This section proves that at a finite place $v$ of $F$ that is inert in $E$ and outside the bad set of the characters, the stabilizer in $\mathrm{U}(V')(F_v)$ of the standard lattice fixes the local vacuum vector of the Weil representation of the pair $(V', W)$. The result is the spherical property of the vacuum at inert places that the step [every character of the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) needs: there, the theta lift of an unramified character must be spherical at every inert place. The proof shows first that the vacuum is not in the augmentation submodule of the unitary group at $v$. This uses a linear form on the local Schwartz-Bruhat space that is invariant under a Lagrangian, and a doubling argument shows that the form is invariant under $\mathrm{U}(V')(F_v)$.

### The setting

Let $F$ be a number field, $E/F$ a quadratic extension with involution $\sigma$, and $a > a'$ natural numbers with $a + a'$ even. Let $V$ be a Hermitian space of rank $a$, $V'$ a Hermitian space of rank $a'$ and $W$ a skew-Hermitian space of rank $a + a'$ over $E$, each with an $\mathcal{O}_E$-lattice. For the pair $(V, W)$ let $\omega$ be a Weil representation of $\mathrm{U}(V) \times \mathrm{U}(W)$ over the adeles on Schwartz-Bruhat functions on $\mathbb{A}_F^{\,a \times (a+a')}$, and for $(V', W)$ let $\omega'$ be a Weil representation of $\mathrm{U}(V') \times \mathrm{U}(W)$ on Schwartz-Bruhat functions on $\mathbb{A}_F^{\,a' \times (a+a')}$. The splitting characters are Hecke characters $\beta, \beta/\alpha$ for $\omega$ and $\alpha, \alpha/\beta$ for $\omega'$, for a standard character $\psi$ of $\mathbb{A}_F/F$. Each model comes with a local vacuum $\varphi^\circ_v$ at each finite place $v$, a Schwartz-Bruhat function on $F_v^{\,a' \times (a+a')}$ in the case of $\omega'$.

We call this a *seesaw triple* when the following conditions hold. They are the conditions of section 10 with the base data of the chapter.

1. At each finite place $v$ of $F$ inert in $E$: $v$ does not divide $2$, the norm of $v$ is larger than $3$, and $\psi$ is unramified at $v$.
2. The signatures of $V, V', W$ and the ranks satisfy the size conditions of the base data, and the local vacua are vacuum vectors in the sense below.
3. $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are Hecke characters with the prescribed restrictions to $\mathbb{A}_F^\times$ and the prescribed infinity types, unramified outside a finite set $T$ of places.
4. $\omega$ and $\omega'$ have the Weil property for $\psi$ and their splitting characters.

Let $v$ be a finite place of $F$, $\mathcal{O}_v$ the ring of integers of $F_v$, and $L_v$ the subgroup of $M_{a' \times (a+a')}(\mathbb{A}_E)$ generated by the elements $t \cdot x\, {}^ty$, with $t$ an adele supported at $v$ whose $v$-component lies in $\mathcal{O}_v$, $x$ in the lattice of $V'$ and $y$ in the lattice of $W$. It is the tensor product lattice at $v$. For $u$ in the Heisenberg group, write $\rho(u)$ for its action in the model.

**Definition 1.13.1 (Local action).** Let $T$ be an operator on Schwartz-Bruhat functions on $\mathbb{A}_F^{\,a' \times (a+a')}$, and $\varphi, \varphi_1$ Schwartz-Bruhat functions on $F_v^{\,a' \times (a+a')}$. Then $T$ *takes $\varphi$ to $\varphi_1$ at $v$* if $T(\varphi \otimes r) = \varphi_1 \otimes r$ for each function $r$ on the other places.

**Definition 1.13.2 (Vacuum).** A Schwartz-Bruhat function $\varphi$ on $F_v^{\,a' \times (a+a')}$ is a *vacuum at $v$* if $\varphi \neq 0$ and there is $\vartheta \in \mathcal{O}_E$ with $\vartheta + \sigma(\vartheta) = 1$ such that each $\gamma \in L_v$ takes $\varphi$ to
$$\psi\big(\tfrac12\, \langle \gamma, \gamma \rangle_\vartheta\big)\, \varphi$$
at $v$. Here $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_\vartheta$ is the symplectic form $\operatorname{tr}_{E/F}(\vartheta \cdot)$ of the Hermitian and skew-Hermitian forms.

For $\vartheta$ fixed, the vacuum is the function fixed by the lattice $L_v$ up to a phase: it is a version of the characteristic function of a self-dual lattice. At an inert place the lattice $L_v$ is self-dual for the symplectic form, and the space of vacua at $v$ is a line.

**Definition 1.13.3 (Lattice stabilizer, augmentation).** Let $\mathrm{U}(V')_v$ be the group of elements $g \in \mathrm{U}(V')(\mathbb{A}_F)$ with $g - 1$ supported at $v$. The *lattice stabilizer* $K_v \subset \mathrm{U}(V')_v$ is the set of $g$ with $g L \subset L$ and $g^{-1} L \subset L$ for the lattice $L$ of $V'$ at $v$. The *augmentation submodule* $\mathfrak{a}_v$ is the span of the differences $\varphi_1 - \varphi$, where $g \in \mathrm{U}(V')_v$ takes $\varphi$ to $\varphi_1$ at $v$ under $\omega'(g, 1)$.

The quotient by $\mathfrak{a}_v$ is the space of coinvariants of $\mathrm{U}(V')_v$. A vector outside $\mathfrak{a}_v$ has a nonzero image in the coinvariants.

**Definition 1.13.4 (Lagrangian at $v$).** A matrix $C$ of size $(a + a') \times m$ is a *Lagrangian at $v$* if, at the place $v$, ${}^tC\, J_W\, \bar C = 0$ and there is a second matrix $C'$ with ${}^tC' J_W \bar C' = 0$ such that $(C \mid C')$ is invertible. Then $m = (a+a')/2$. For such $C$ the *Lagrangian subgroup* $\Lambda_C$ is the set of $u = z\,{}^tC$ supported at $v$.

**Definition 1.13.5 (Heisenberg invariant form).** A linear form $l$ on the Schwartz-Bruhat functions on $F_v^{\,a' \times (a+a')}$ is *Heisenberg invariant* for $C$ if $l(\varphi_1) = l(\varphi)$ whenever $u \in \Lambda_C$ takes $\varphi$ to $\varphi_1$ at $v$.

On the Schrödinger model attached to a polarization, such forms are the integrals over the Lagrangian. Their space has dimension at most $1$, by the uniqueness of a Heisenberg invariant functional for a Lagrangian. This is the Stone-von Neumann theorem in the form of A. Weil, *Sur certains groupes d'opérateurs unitaires*, Acta Math. 111 (1964), Chapter I.

### The Lagrangian form

**Lemma 1.13.6 (A Lagrangian form that sees the vacuum).** Let $(\omega, \omega')$ be a seesaw triple, $v$ a finite place of $F$ inert in $E$ with $v \notin T$, and $\varphi^\circ_v$ the vacuum of $\omega'$ at $v$. Then there are a Lagrangian $C$ at $v$ with $(a+a')/2$ columns and a Heisenberg invariant form $l$ for $C$ with $l(\varphi^\circ_v) \neq 0$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.exists_lagrangian_invariantForm_inertVacuum`.

*Proof.* Since $v$ is inert and does not divide $2$, the skew-Hermitian space $W_v$ has a Lagrangian $C$ with $(a+a')/2$ columns. Let $\Lambda$ be the image of $\Lambda_C$ in the local symplectic space $F_v^{\,2a'(a+a')}$ and $\Gamma$ the image of $L_v$. Then $\Lambda$ is isotropic, since the symplectic form vanishes on $\Lambda_C$, and $\Gamma$ is a self-dual lattice, since $v$ is inert. The vacuum is an eigenvector of $\Gamma$ for the character $\gamma \mapsto \psi(\tfrac12 \langle \gamma, \gamma\rangle_\vartheta)$, and this character is trivial on $\Gamma \cap \Lambda$ since $\Lambda$ is isotropic. So $\varphi^\circ_v$ is fixed by $\Gamma \cap \Lambda$. Local uniqueness for Lagrangian invariant forms gives a Heisenberg invariant form $l$ for $C$, the integral over the Lagrangian, which is nonzero on each nonzero vector fixed by $\Gamma \cap \Lambda$ with this eigencharacter. So $l(\varphi^\circ_v) \neq 0$. $\square$

### The form is invariant

**Proposition 1.13.7 (The transforming character is trivial).** Let $(\omega, \omega')$ be a seesaw triple, $v$ inert with $v \notin T$, $C$ a Lagrangian at $v$ with $m$ columns, and $l$ a Heisenberg invariant form for $C$ with $l(\varphi) \neq 0$ for some $\varphi$. Let $c \colon \mathrm{U}(V')(\mathbb{A}_F) \to \mathbb{C}$ be a function such that, for all $g \in \mathrm{U}(V')_v$, $l(\varphi_1) = c(g)\, l(\varphi)$ whenever $\omega'(g, 1)$ takes $\varphi$ to $\varphi_1$ at $v$. Then $c(g) = 1$ for all $g \in \mathrm{U}(V')_v$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.InertLagChar.char_eq_one` (also `GlobalTheory.T.inert_place_transforming_character_eq_one`, a second name).

*Proof.* We use the doubling method. Let $V'^{\square} = V' \oplus (-V')$, so that $\mathrm{U}(V') \times \mathrm{U}(V')$ embeds in $\mathrm{U}(V'^\square)$, and the Weil representation of $\mathrm{U}(V'^\square) \times \mathrm{U}(W)$ restricts on functions $\varphi \otimes \varphi_2$ to $\omega' \otimes \bar\omega'$. The diagonal of $V'^\square$ is a Lagrangian, and with $C$ it gives a Lagrangian of $V'^\square \otimes W$. Integration over the diagonal, followed by $l$ in the remaining variables, defines a linear form $\Lambda$ on Schwartz-Bruhat functions on the doubled space. It has three properties.

1. $\Lambda(\varphi \otimes \varphi_2)$ is a fixed multiple of $l(\varphi)$, for a test function $\varphi_2$ supported on a compact open set, and there is a pair with $\Lambda(\varphi_0 \otimes \varphi_0) \neq 0$.
2. For $k \in \mathrm{U}(V')_v$, the element $\operatorname{diag}(k, 1)$ of $\mathrm{U}(V'^\square)$ scales $\Lambda$ by $c(k)$, by the hypothesis on $l$ and the first property.
3. Each element $p$ of the Siegel parabolic of $\mathrm{U}(V'^\square)$ at $v$ (the stabilizer of the diagonal) scales $\Lambda$ by $1$. If the Levi part of $p$ is $t$, the scalar is $(\alpha/\beta)(\det t)\, |N(\det t)|_v^{m}$ times the Jacobian $|\det t|_v^{-(a+a')}$ of the change of variables. At an inert place $v \notin T$ one has $(\alpha/\beta)(\det t) = 1$, since $\alpha$ and $\beta$ agree there, and $|N \det t|_v^{(a+a')/2} = |\det t|_v^{a+a'}$, with $m = (a+a')/2$. So the scalar is $1$.

Now let $g \in \mathrm{U}(V')_v$. Since the residue field at $v$ has more than $3$ elements, there is $k \in \mathrm{U}(V')_v$ such that $k - 1$ and $kg - 1$ are invertible (a scalar shift of $g$). For such $k$ a block computation in $\mathrm{U}(V'^\square)$ gives
$$\operatorname{diag}(g, 1) = \operatorname{diag}(k, 1)\, p_1\, \operatorname{diag}(k^{-1}, 1)\, p_2$$
with $p_1, p_2$ in the Siegel parabolic. Hence $c(g) = c(k)\, c(k^{-1})$. The product $\operatorname{diag}(k, 1)\operatorname{diag}(k^{-1}, 1) = 1$ scales $\Lambda$ by $c(k)c(k^{-1})$ and by $1$, and $\Lambda \neq 0$, so $c(k)c(k^{-1}) = 1$. So $c(g) = 1$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.13.8 (The Lagrangian form is invariant).** Let $(\omega, \omega')$ be a seesaw triple, $v$ inert with $v \notin T$, $C$ a Lagrangian at $v$ with $m$ columns, and $l$ a Heisenberg invariant form for $C$. If $g \in \mathrm{U}(V')_v$ and $\omega'(g, 1)$ takes $\varphi$ to $\varphi_1$ at $v$, then $l(\varphi_1) = l(\varphi)$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.InertVacLag.inv_of_heisInv`.

*Proof.* If $l = 0$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, for $g \in \mathrm{U}(V')_v$ let $S_g$ be the local operator of $\omega'(g, 1)$ at $v$, defined on the local Schwartz-Bruhat space through the action on $\varphi \otimes r$ for a fixed function $r$ at the other places. Since $g$ commutes with $\mathrm{U}(W)$ and normalizes the Heisenberg group through its action on $V' \otimes W$, which preserves $\Lambda_C$, the form $l \circ S_g$ is again Heisenberg invariant for $C$. The space of Heisenberg invariant forms has dimension at most $1$, so $l \circ S_g = c(g)\, l$ for a scalar $c(g)$. The proposition on the transforming character gives $c(g) = 1$. $\square$

**Corollary 1.13.9 (The vacuum is not in the augmentation).** Let $(\omega, \omega')$ be a seesaw triple and $v$ inert with $v \notin T$. Then $\varphi^\circ_v \notin \mathfrak{a}_v$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.InertVacLag.vac_not_mem_augAt` (also `GlobalTheory.T.weil_vacuum_not_mem_augmentation_inert_place`, a second name).

*Proof.* Take $C$ and $l$ from the [lemma](/entry/exists-lagrangian-invariant-form-inert-vacuum/). By the [proposition](/entry/inv-of-heis-inv/), $l$ vanishes on each generator $\varphi_1 - \varphi$ of $\mathfrak{a}_v$, so $\mathfrak{a}_v \subset \ker l$. Since $l(\varphi^\circ_v) \neq 0$, the vacuum is not in $\mathfrak{a}_v$. $\square$

### The vacuum is fixed

**Lemma 1.13.10 (The scalar is one).** Let $(\omega, \omega')$ be a seesaw triple, $v$ inert with $v \notin T$, $k \in K_v$ and $c \in \mathbb{C}$. If $\omega'(k, 1)$ takes $\varphi^\circ_v$ to $c\, \varphi^\circ_v$ at $v$, then $c = 1$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.InertScalarLeft.scalar_eq_one` (also `GlobalTheory.T.inertPlace_latticeStabilizer_vacuumScalar_eq_one`, a second name).

*Proof.* The difference $c\varphi^\circ_v - \varphi^\circ_v$ lies in $\mathfrak{a}_v$ by definition. If $c \neq 1$, then $\varphi^\circ_v = (c-1)^{-1}(c\varphi^\circ_v - \varphi^\circ_v) \in \mathfrak{a}_v$, which contradicts the [corollary](/entry/vac-not-mem-aug-at/). $\square$

**Theorem 1.13.11 (The lattice stabilizer of the first group fixes the vacuum).** Let $(\omega, \omega')$ be a seesaw triple, $v$ a finite place of $F$ inert in $E$ with $v \notin T$, and $k \in K_v$. Then $\omega'(k, 1)$ takes $\varphi^\circ_v$ to itself at $v$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.InertSphLeft.vac_fixed_left`.

*Proof.* The operator $\omega'(k, 1)$ acts locally at $v$, since $k - 1$ is supported at $v$: it takes $\varphi^\circ_v$ to some Schwartz-Bruhat function $\varphi_1$ at $v$. Since $k$ preserves the lattice of $V'$, it preserves $L_v$ and the form $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_\vartheta$, so $\varphi_1$ satisfies the same eigen condition for $L_v$ as the vacuum. The functions with this eigen condition form a line at an inert place, so $\varphi_1 = c\,\varphi^\circ_v$ for some $c \in \mathbb{C}$. The [lemma](/entry/inert-scalar-left-scalar-eq-one/) gives $c = 1$. $\square$

**Remark 1.13.12.** The classical statement is that the characteristic function of a self-dual lattice spans the fixed vectors of its stabilizer, and that the stabilizer acts on it by a character which is trivial when the splitting is unramified. For unitary pairs the triviality of the character is part of the unramified computation of the splitting in S. Kudla, *Splitting metaplectic covers of dual reductive pairs*, Israel J. Math. 87 (1994). The proof here does not compute the splitting: it shows that the coinvariants of $\mathrm{U}(V')_v$ see the vacuum, which forces the character to be trivial.

**Remark 1.13.13 (Formalization).** The lemma is the `first | exact` wrapper `exists_lagrangian_invariantForm_inertVacuum` (under `maxHeartbeats 1000000`) around `GlobalTheory.InertVacLagP.exists_lagr_inv_vac`, which does not use the hypothesis $v \notin T$ ([ExistsLagrangianInvariantFormInertVacuum.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsLagrangianInvariantFormInertVacuum.lean#L93), the `_` in the `intro`). The other second names are wrappers of the same kind. The name `inv_of_heisInv` says only that invariance follows from Heisenberg invariance, while the statement is invariance under $\mathrm{U}(V')_v$. There are two theorems named `scalar_eq_one`, in the namespaces `InertScalarLeft` (this section) and `InertScalar` (the next section), and the file of the theorem is named InertPlaceStabilizerFixesVacuumLeft.lean, without "Lattice". The scalar shift, the block identity and the doubling functional are results that the proof cites (`exists_unitaryScalar_inertPlace_invertible_shifts`, `idempotent_block_unipotent_identity`, `exists_admitted_diagonal_integral_linearForm`); the account above gives their content as far as their statements show it.

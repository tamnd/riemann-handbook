## Generators of the level subgroup

The residue of the Eisenstein series is a function on $G_n$ that is invariant under a compact open subgroup at the finite places. To compare two such functions, or to show that a set of functions is stable under the level subgroup, it is enough to check a small set of generators. This section proves that the level subgroup away from a finite set $S$ of places is generated by upper unipotent elements, Levi elements and conjugates of upper unipotent elements by the Weyl element, all of level $S$, as soon as $S$ contains the places above $2$. The result is used in the proof of [Theorem 5.7.3](/entry/res-core/). There it shows that the [Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation) fixes suitable Schwartz functions under the level subgroup, and that the functions of the theta class with a suitable value at $1$ are right invariant under it: in each case one checks the generators, and the set of elements that fix the function is closed and contains $1$.

We keep the notation of the first section and work in the group $G_n$ of [adelic points](/learn/groups/#adelic-points-and-compact-open-subgroups) of $U(n, n)$. For a finite place $v$ of $F$, let $\mathcal{O}_{E,v}$ be the integral closure of $\mathcal{O}_{F,v}$ in $E_v = E \otimes_F F_v$.

**Definition 5.4.1 (Level subgroup away from $S$).** Let $S$ be a finite set of finite places of $F$. The *level subgroup away from $S$* is the subgroup $K^S \subset G_n$ of the elements $k$ with:

1. $k_\infty = 1$ at the archimedean places;
2. $k_v = 1$ for $v \in S$;
3. the entries of $k_v$ lie in $\mathcal{O}_{E,v}$ for each finite $v \notin S$.

**Definition 5.4.2 (Elementary elements).** An element $g \in G_n$ is *upper unipotent* if $g = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ for an $n \times n$ matrix $x$ over $\mathbb{A}_E$, and a *Levi element* if $g = \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & (a^*)^{-1} \end{pmatrix}$ for some $a \in GL_n(\mathbb{A}_E)$. An element $w \in G_n$ is a *Weyl element* if $w = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

Since $g$ is unitary, $x$ in an upper unipotent element is Hermitian. A conjugate $w u w^{-1}$ of an upper unipotent $u = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is the lower unipotent element $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -x & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

The main step is a factorisation of each element of $K^S$. It is the adelic form of the Bruhat decomposition of $U(n,n)$ over a local ring, and it uses the following local fact at each place $v \notin S$, which needs $2$ to be a unit at $v$: if $\begin{pmatrix} c & d \end{pmatrix}$ is the bottom row of blocks of an integral unitary matrix, then there is an integral Hermitian matrix $x$ over $\mathcal{O}_{E,v}$ such that $\det(d + cx)$ is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_{E,v}$.

**Lemma 5.4.3 (Factorisation).** Let $S$ be a finite set of finite places of $F$ such that $2$ is a unit at every finite place $v \notin S$. Let $k \in K^S$. Then there are $g_1, g_2, g_3, g_4 \in K^S$ with $k = g_1 g_2 g_3 g_4$ such that:

1. $g_1$ and $g_4$ are upper unipotent;
2. $g_2$ is a Levi element;
3. $g_3 = w u w^{-1}$ for a Weyl element $w$ and an upper unipotent $u \in K^S$.

*Proof.* Write $k = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$. At each finite $v \notin S$ the blocks of $k_v$ are integral, and $2$ is a unit, so the local fact gives an integral Hermitian $x_v$ with $\det(d_v + c_v x_v)$ a unit. For $v \in S$ put $x_v = 0$; there $c_v = 0$ and $d_v = 1$. Since each $x_v$ is integral, these local choices glue to a Hermitian $X$ over $\mathbb{A}_E$ with $X_\infty = 0$, $X_v = 0$ for $v \in S$, and $X_v$ integral for $v \notin S$. Then $u = d + cX$ is invertible and $u_v^{-1}$ is integral at every finite $v$.

Let $n(X) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & X \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Then $n(X) \in K^S$ and
$$k\, n(X) = \begin{pmatrix} a & \beta \\ c & u \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \beta = aX + b.$$
Since $k\, n(X)$ is unitary with an invertible lower right block, the matrices $\beta u^{-1}$ and $u^{-1} c$ are Hermitian, and
$$k\, n(X) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \beta u^{-1} \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a - \beta u^{-1} c & 0 \\ 0 & u \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ u^{-1} c & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Put $g_1 = n(\beta u^{-1})$, $g_3 = w\, n(-u^{-1} c)\, w^{-1}$, which is the lower unipotent factor on the right, $g_4 = n(-X)$, and $g_2 = g_1^{-1} k\, n(X)\, g_3^{-1}$, which is the middle factor. Then $k = g_1 g_2 g_3 g_4$, and $g_2$ is a Levi element because it is unitary and block diagonal.

It remains to check the level. At the archimedean places $X = 0$, $b = c = 0$ and $a = 1$, so $\beta u^{-1} = 0$ and $u^{-1} c = 0$. At $v \in S$ the same holds. At $v \notin S$ the matrices $\beta u^{-1}$, $u^{-1} c$ and $X$ are integral, because $a$, $b$, $c$, $X$ and $u^{-1}$ are. So $g_1$, $g_3$, $g_4$ and $n(-u^{-1}c)$ lie in $K^S$, and so does $g_2$, as a product of elements of $K^S$. $\square$

**Theorem 5.4.4 (Generators of the level subgroup).** For each $n$ there is a finite set $S_0$ of finite places of $F$ with the following property. Let $S \supseteq S_0$ be finite, and let $A \subseteq G_n$ be a closed set with $1 \in A$. Assume that $gA \subseteq A$ for each $g \in K^S$ that is upper unipotent, or a Levi element, or of the form $w u w^{-1}$ with $w$ a Weyl element and $u \in K^S$ upper unipotent. Then $K^S \subseteq A$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rsw.L.genK`.

*Proof.* Let $S_0$ be the set of finite places above $2$; it is finite because $2$ has finitely many prime factors. For $S \supseteq S_0$, the number $2$ is a unit at each finite $v \notin S$. Let $k \in K^S$, and write $k = g_1 g_2 g_3 g_4$ by the [Lemma 5.4.3](/entry/factor/). Each $g_i$ lies in $K^S$ and is of one of the three kinds. Starting from $1 \in A$, the hypothesis gives $g_4 \in A$, then $g_3 g_4 \in A$, then $g_2 g_3 g_4 \in A$, and finally $k \in A$. $\square$

**Remark 5.4.5 (Formalization).** The theorem assumes that $A$ is closed, but the proof does not use this; it shows that every element of $K^S$ is a product of four generators. The lemma raises the heartbeat limit to one million and the limit for instance search to 400,000 for the whole file ([LGenKI.lean](lean:Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/RegularisedSiegelWeil/SingularNullSets/LGenKI.lean#L15)). We have not opened the proof of the local fact on Hermitian matrices; we only use its statement.

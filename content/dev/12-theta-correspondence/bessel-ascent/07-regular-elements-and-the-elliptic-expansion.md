## Regular elements and the elliptic expansion

The trace formula comparison behind the ascent needs two facts about a unitary group $U_J$. The first is the structure of the centralizer of a regular element: it is the norm one torus of an étale algebra, its split rank counts the split factors, and its adelic quotient is compact exactly when no factor splits. The second is the elliptic expansion: the integral of the elliptic part of the kernel is a finite sum, over characteristic polynomials, of a torus volume times a product of local stable orbital integrals. The structure theorem feeds [the direct elliptic side](/entry/elliptic-side-direct/), and the expansion feeds [the elliptic part](/entry/elliptic-part-x/). For the background see [unitary groups](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups) and [orbit integrals and the relative trace formula](/learn/periods/#orbit-integrals-and-the-relative-trace-formula).

### The centralizer of a regular element

Let $F$ be a field of characteristic $0$ and let $E$ be a commutative $F$-algebra with an $F$-algebra map $\sigma : E \to E$. We say that $\sigma$ is a *quadratic involution* if $E$ is étale of dimension $2$ over $F$, $\sigma^2 = 1$ and $\sigma \ne 1$. Let $n$ be a finite index set and let $J$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $E$ with $\sigma(J)^{T} = J$ and $\det J$ a unit. For a matrix $l$ put $\mathrm{adj}(l) = J^{-1} \sigma(l)^{T} J$. For an $F$-algebra $R$ let $U_J(R)$ be the group of $g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E \otimes_F R)$ with $\mathrm{adj}(g) g = 1$.

**Definition 8.7.1 (Regular element).** An element $\gamma \in U_J(F)$ is *regular* if $\sigma$ is a quadratic involution, $J$ is Hermitian with unit determinant, and the characteristic polynomial of $\gamma$ is separable.

For regular $\gamma$ let $E[\gamma]$ be the $E$-subalgebra of matrices generated by $\gamma$, and let

$$E[\gamma]^{+} = \{\, l \in E[\gamma] : \sigma(l)^{T} J = J l \,\}.$$

This is an $F$-subalgebra. For an $F$-algebra $R$ let $C_\gamma(R)$ be the centralizer of $\gamma$ in $U_J(R)$. A *split subtorus of rank $k$* is a family of injective group maps $(R^\times)^k \to C_\gamma(R)$, natural in $R$. The *split rank* of $\gamma$ is the largest $k$ for which a split subtorus of rank $k$ exists.

**Theorem 8.7.2 (Regular elements of unitary groups).** Let $\gamma$ be regular.

1. There is an isomorphism $E[X]/(\chi_\gamma) \to E[\gamma]$ that maps $X$ to $\gamma$, where $\chi_\gamma$ is the characteristic polynomial. The algebra $E[\gamma]$ is étale and free of rank $|n|$ over $E$, and there is a vector $v$ such that $l \mapsto l v$ is a bijection $E[\gamma] \to E^n$. The map $\mathrm{adj}$ preserves $E[\gamma]$, $\mathrm{adj}(\gamma) = \gamma^{-1}$ and $\mathrm{adj}(e) = \sigma(e)$ for $e \in E$.
2. The algebra $E[\gamma]^{+}$ is étale and free of rank $|n|$ over $F$, and the map $E[\gamma]^{+} \otimes_F E \to E[\gamma]$ is bijective.
3. For each $F$-algebra $R$, $C_\gamma(R)$ is the image of the norm one elements of $E[\gamma] \otimes_F R$, and this map is injective and carries the conjugation to $\mathrm{adj}$. An element $g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ is in $C_\gamma(F)$ if and only if $g \in E[\gamma]$ and $g\, \mathrm{adj}(g) = 1$. Over an algebraically closed field $\Omega$, $E[\gamma] \otimes_F \Omega$ is isomorphic to $(\Omega \times \Omega)^n$, with the conjugation acting by the swap of the two factors.
4. There is a decomposition $E[\gamma]^{+} \cong K_1 \times \dots \times K_k$ into fields. For each such decomposition, the split rank of $\gamma$ is the number of $i$ for which $K_i \otimes_F E$ is not a field, $k \ge 1$ if $n$ is nonempty, and $E[\gamma]$ is a field if and only if $k = 1$ and $K_1 \otimes_F E$ is a field.
5. The group $C_\gamma(R)$ is commutative for each $R$. If $E$ is a field, then for each split subtorus $A$ of rank $s \ge 1$ there are $g \in U_J(F)$ and $t \in (F^\times)^s$ with $g A(t) \ne A(t) g$.
6. There are a unit $x$ of $E[\gamma]^{+}$ and a vector $v$ as in clause 1 such that, for all $y, y' \in E[\gamma]$, the Hermitian form of $J$ satisfies

$$\langle y v, y' v \rangle = \operatorname{tr}_{E[\gamma]/E}\bigl(x\, y\, \mathrm{adj}(y')\bigr).$$

Now let $F$ be a number field. Then, for each decomposition $E[\gamma]^{+} \cong \prod_i K_i$ into fields, the quotient $C_\gamma(\mathbb{A}_F)/C_\gamma(F)$ is compact if and only if $K_i \otimes_F E$ is a field for each $i$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.regularSemisimple_unitaryGroup_concrete` (also `GlobalTheory.RegSSObj.regularSemisimple` and `GlobalTheory.RegSS.regularSemisimple`, the proof).

*Proof.* A separable characteristic polynomial gives a cyclic vector over a field, and then over the semilocal algebra $E$. A cyclic vector identifies $E[\gamma]$ with $E[X]/(\chi_\gamma)$, which gives clause 1. Clause 2 follows from the étale descent of $E[\gamma]$ along $\sigma$. The centralizer is the commutant of $\gamma$, and the commutant of a cyclic matrix is $E[\gamma]$. With the adjoint rule this gives clause 3, and the splitting over $\Omega$ follows since an étale algebra splits over an algebraically closed field. For clause 4, each factor $K_i$ with $K_i \otimes_F E$ split gives an idempotent and a copy of the multiplicative group, so the split rank is at least the number of split factors. Conversely, the characters of a split subtorus are monomials in Laurent polynomials, and the conjugate factors separate points, which gives the upper bound. Clause 5 uses that the commutant of $U_J(F)$ consists of scalars. Clause 6 is the perfectness of the trace form of the reduced free algebra $E[\gamma]$.

For the adelic statement: if each $K_i \otimes_F E$ is a field, the norm one units of each factor have bounded adelic translates by small multiples, so the centralizer points are bounded matrices and the quotient is compact. If a factor splits, its idempotent gives a split torus whose determinant on the range of the idempotent is unbounded, so the quotient is not compact. $\square$

### The elliptic expansion

Now let $E$ be a CM field with totally real subfield $F$ and complex conjugation $\sigma$, and assume that $\mathcal{O}_E$ is unramified over $\mathcal{O}_F$. Let $J$ be a nondegenerate Hermitian $r \times r$ matrix over $E$, let $G = U_J$, and let $L \subset E^r$ be a lattice. Let $K_v$ be the stabilizer of $L$ in $G(F_v)$.

Let $\mu_w$ and $\mu_v$ be Haar measures on $G(F_w)$ for the infinite places $w$ and on $G(F_v)$ for the finite places $v$, with $\mu_v(K_v) = 1$ for almost all $v$, and let $\mu$ be their restricted product. Let $f_w$ be continuous with compact support on $G(F_w)$, and let $f_v$ be compactly supported and smooth on $G(F_v)$ with $f_v = \mathbf{1}_{K_v}$ for almost all $v$. Put $f(x) = \prod_w f_w(x_w) \prod_v f_v(x_v)$.

Let $\mathcal{P}_r$ be the set of irreducible polynomials of degree $r$ that occur as characteristic polynomials of regular elements of $G(F)$. For $P \in \mathcal{P}_r$ let $T_P$ be the torus of the class of $P$, with local measures $dt_w$, $dt_v$ and their product $dt$ as for $G$. Let $\mathrm{vol}(T_P)$ be the volume of $T_P(F) \backslash T_P(\mathbb{A}_F)$. For each place let $\Phi_v(f_v)$ be the stable orbital integral of $f_v$ along the local class of $P$: the sum, over the local conjugacy classes with characteristic polynomial $P$, of the orbital integrals of $f_v$ with the measures $\mu_v$ and $dt_v$.

**Definition 8.7.3 (Polynomial kernel).** For $P \in \mathcal{P}_r$ the *polynomial kernel* of $f$ is

$$K_P(x) = \sum_{\delta} f(x^{-1} \delta x), \qquad x \in G(\mathbb{A}_F),$$

where $\delta$ runs over the elements of $G(F)$ with characteristic polynomial $P$. It is left invariant under $G(F)$, so it descends to $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F)$.

**Theorem 8.7.4 (The polynomial kernel integrates to stable orbital integrals).** The function $K_P$ is integrable on $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F)$, and

$$\int K_P = \mathrm{vol}(T_P) \prod_{v} \Phi_v(f_v) \prod_{w} \Phi_w(f_w).$$

*Proof.* The rational conjugacy classes with characteristic polynomial $P$ correspond one to one to the families of local classes, one at each place, that are trivial at almost all places. This uses that $\mathcal{O}_E$ is unramified over $\mathcal{O}_F$. There are finitely many local classes at each place, and at almost all places only the trivial class meets the support of $f_v$. Since $f$ has compact support, only finitely many rational classes give a nonzero term. Split $K_P$ into the class kernels of these classes. For each class, the integral of its class kernel is the torus volume times the product of the local orbital integrals. Sum over the families of local classes. The sum of products is the product of the local sums, which are the stable orbital integrals. $\square$

**Theorem 8.7.5 (The elliptic expansion).** Let $K(x) = \sum_\delta f(x^{-1} \delta x)$, where $\delta$ runs over the elements of $G(F)$ with irreducible characteristic polynomial. Let $t(P) = \mathrm{vol}(T_P) \prod_v \Phi_v(f_v) \prod_w \Phi_w(f_w)$. Then:

1. for each $P$, $\mathrm{vol}(T_P)$ is finite, each local stable orbital integral is defined, and $\Phi_v(f_v) = 1$ for almost all $v$;
2. $t(P) \ne 0$ for only finitely many $P$;
3. $K$ is integrable on $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F)$, and

$$\int K = \sum_{P} t(P).$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.TrEllSum.elliptic_expansion`.

*Proof.* Since $f$ is continuous with compact support, only finitely many characteristic polynomials of elements of $G(F)$ meet the conjugates of its support. Each element with irreducible characteristic polynomial has a torus class $P$, so $K$ is the finite sum of the kernels $K_P$. The previous theorem integrates each $K_P$. The torus quotient is compact, so its volume is finite. The local integrals are defined since $f_v$ and $f_w$ are continuous with compact support. At almost all places the only local class is trivial, $f_v = \mathbf{1}_{K_v}$ and the measures give volume $1$, so $\Phi_v(f_v) = 1$. If $P$ is not one of the finitely many polynomials, $K_P = 0$, so $t(P) = 0$. $\square$

**Remark 8.7.6 (Formalization).** The structure theorem is a conjunction of two Prop-valued definitions, and its proof takes eighteen separate lemmas as arguments ([RegularSemisimpleOf.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/RegularSemisimpleOf.lean#L194)). The structure theorem has three names: a statement wrapper, an object alias and the proof. The expansion is in [EllipticPart.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/EllipticPart.lean#L47), and the kernel integral in [IntegralCharpolyKernelEqStableOrbitalIntegrals.lean](lean:Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/IntegralCharpolyKernelEqStableOrbitalIntegrals.lean#L112). Both assume that $\mathcal{O}_E$ is unramified over $\mathcal{O}_F$. We have not unfolded the set $\mathcal{P}_r$ of polynomial classes, the torus measures, the definition of the local stable orbital integral or the conditions under which it is defined.

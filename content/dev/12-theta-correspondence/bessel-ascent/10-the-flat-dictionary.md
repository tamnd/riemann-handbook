## The flat dictionary

This section proves that the unfolded integrand of the row test, at the representative of a coset family, is a scalar times a combination of one real family of bi-vacua. The scalar is fixed on the staircase cosets. The result is the *flat dictionary*. It turns the unfolded theta integral into a finite sum of periods of one fixed family, and it is used in [the unfolding of the twisted theta integral](/entry/twisted-theta-integral-unfolding/). The proof works one place at a time in the [Schrödinger model](/learn/theta/#the-schrdinger-model) and then joins the places by a product over the local places.

### Choices, integrands and combinations

All statements in this section hold *for all large choices*. A *choice* $\mathrm{ch}$ fixes a host, size data, a grid with a grid choice $S$ and a Weil family $W$, a real match with vectors $v_j$ on the top row, and data $\Phi_a$. The choice is *large* if its size and its scale are large enough. Let $m$ be the number of rows. For a type $t$, let $P_t$ be the set of local places of the grid at $t$, and let $W_0$ be the fixed set of places of the grid.

For a volume $M$ at $t$, a family $f$ of theta functions on the down edges of the last row, and points $h$ of the last row, the *contracted family* $\mathrm{con}_M(f, h)$ is a family $C$ of bi-vacua: for even $j < m$, $C_j$ is a vector of the theta functions at the lattices $\Lambda_j, \Lambda_{j+1}, \Lambda_{j+2}$ of the top row. For a polynomial datum $P$ on the down edges, let $\mathrm{fock}(f, P)$ be the Fock vectors of $f$ and $P$, as in [the Fock model](/learn/theta/#the-fock-model). The evaluation $\mathrm{ev}(C, g)(u, y)$ pairs $C$ at a column point $u$ with a row function $g$ at $y$. A *combination datum* $c$ is a finite combination of translates on each column and row edge, and $\mathrm{comb}_c(C)$ is the family $C$ with these combinations applied.

A *flip expansion* at $t$ is a finite sum $\tau$ of pairs $(d_1, d_2)$, in which $d_2$ is a family of theta functions on the down edges and $d_1$ is a row function. A coset family $\beta$ has a *representative* $b$ for a sign $s$ if $b$ is a point of the last row that reads $s \cdot \beta$ at the places of $P_t$. A point $b_0$ is a *base point* if it reads the inverse staircase element at the column $0$ and $1$ elsewhere. The family $\beta$ is a *staircase coset* if at each place of $W_0$ it is the coset of the staircase element at each column. Let $\Xi_t$ be the subgroup of $\mathbb{C}^\times$ generated by the local values of the character $\xi_t$ of the level datum.

**Definition 8.10.1 (Local and flat dictionaries).** The choice has the *local dictionary* for a sign $s$ if there are $\kappa \in \mathbb{C}$ and $z_t \in \Xi_t$ with this property. Let $\tau$ be a flip expansion at $t$, $M$ a volume at $t$, $b$ a representative of $\beta$ for $s$, $b_0$ a base point, and $\tau'$ an expansion of the term at $\beta$ with combination data $c$. Then there is $\zeta \in \mathbb{C}$, equal to $\kappa z_t^{-1}$ if $\beta$ is a staircase coset, such that for each $P$, each real point $p$ and all $u, y$,

$$\begin{aligned}
&\sum_d \tau_d\, \mathrm{ev}\bigl(\mathrm{con}_M(\mathrm{fock}(d_2, P), p b),\, d_1\bigr)(u, y) \\
&\quad = \zeta \sum_d \tau'_d\, \mathrm{ev}\bigl(\mathrm{comb}_{c(d)} C_0,\, \mathrm{comb}_{c(d)}\bigr)(u, y),
\end{aligned}$$

with $C_0 = \mathrm{con}_M(\mathrm{fock}(v, P), p b_0)$. The choice has the *flat dictionary* for $s$ if the same holds with $z_t$ in place of $\kappa^{-1} z_t$ and with one real family $C$, which depends only on $P$ and $p$, in place of $C_0$.

The *place dictionary* for $s$ is the same identity at one place $w \in P_t$, for a local coset $\beta_w$ and a scalar $\zeta_w$. It holds with a family $\kappa_w$ that is $1$ outside $W_0$, with $z_{t,w} \in \Xi_t$, and with $\zeta_w = \kappa_w z_{t,w}^{-1}$ on staircase cosets. The local integrand is a flat tensor of the moved local datum, and its row edges carry the constant function $1$.

The choice is *linear in the contraction* if, for each finite combination of row functions $f_i$ and down families $\Phi_i$ of theta functions with

$$\sum_i x_i \prod_n f_{i,n}(y_n) \prod_j \Phi_{i,j}(v_j) = 0$$

at all points, the same combination of the evaluated contracted families $\mathrm{ev}(\mathrm{con}_M(\mathrm{fock}(\Phi_i, P), h), f_i)$ is $0$.

### From one place to all places

**Theorem 8.10.2 (The place dictionary).** For all large choices there is a sign $s$ such that the place dictionary holds for $s$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.exists_sign_flatDictionaryAtOnePlace_largeDepth` (also `GlobalTheory.FlatPlace.big_placeAt`, the proof).

*Proof.* Take $s = -1$. Ten local facts hold for all large choices: the frame lattice points are integral matrices, the local character components, the dictionary points, the Bessel and Jacobi frames of the grid lattice, the scalar of the dictionary, the local model of the moved arrows, the vacuum combinations, and the double step and the last two steps at a place. Fix $w$, a local coset and local points. The double step and the last two steps say that each pair of columns $(2l, 2l + 1)$ of the local integrand expands as a combination of the pair at the base point. The local flat tensor of the pure tensor is the product of its local factors over the pairs of columns, and the row factors are $1$. A product of pairwise linear expansions is a sum of products. So the integrand at $b$ is $\zeta_w$ times the combination at $b_0$. $\square$

**Theorem 8.10.3 (Column induction).** For all large choices the following holds. Let $s$ be a sign, $\beta$ a coset family at $t$, and $\zeta_w$ scalars such that the place identity holds at each $w \in P_t$ with $\zeta_w$. Let $b$ be a representative of $\beta$ for $s$, $b_0$ a base point, $e$ an idempotent sum for $P_t$, $\varphi_w$ natural local data at the places, and $f$ a down family whose components are local vectors of the top row vectors for $\prod_w \varphi_w$. Assume that at each $w$, a finite combination of column data for an idempotent $e_w$ of $\{w\}$ expands the local flat tensor of $\varphi_w$ with the row edges set to $1$. Then there is one finite combination of column data for $e$ that expands the product over $w$ of these local flat tensors. For each $P$ and each real point $p$, the evaluated family at $p b$ is $\prod_w \zeta_w$ times this combination of the family at $p b_0$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.rowTest_columnInduction_localToGlobalExpansion` (also `GlobalTheory.LocAsm.columnInduction`, the proof).

*Proof.* Write $e$ as the sum of the single place idempotents $e_w$. The double coset points of the row test form a chain place by place, and the place vectors split along a subset of places. Fock vectors pass through the place models, and there is a combination operator on the column product. Combinations of columns compose, and the product of finite data is a finite datum. Each local column reads only its own place part, so the local flat tensor at $w$ does not change when another idempotent acts on its argument. Induct on the places of $P_t$. At each step multiply the combination so far with the local combination at the new place, and multiply the scalars. The vacuum datum gives the trivial combination at the start. $\square$

**Theorem 8.10.4 (One product over all places).** For all large choices the following holds. Let $s$, $\beta$, $\zeta_w$, $b$, $b_0$, $e$ and $\varphi_w$ be as in the previous theorem. Let $f_d$ be a down family and $f_r$ a row family whose components are local vectors of the top and row vectors for $\prod_w \varphi_w$. Then there is a finitely supported $\sigma$ on pairs of pure data and combination data such that:

1. the data in the support of $\sigma$ carry $e$ and are combinations of the data;
2. at the points fixed by $e$, the sum $\sum_k \sigma_k \prod_j f_{k,j} \prod_n f'_{k,n}$ is the product over $w \in P_t$ of the local flat tensors of $\varphi_w$;
3. for each $P$, each real point $p$ and all $u, y$,

$$\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{ev}\bigl(\mathrm{con}_M(\mathrm{fock}(f_d, P), p b),\, f_r\bigr)(u, y) \\
&\quad = \Bigl(\prod_w \zeta_w\Bigr) \sum_k \sigma_k\, \mathrm{ev}\bigl(\mathrm{comb}_k C_0,\, \mathrm{comb}_k\bigr)(u, y).
\end{aligned}$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.localPureTensor_product_expansion_all_places` (also `GlobalTheory.LocAsm.oneProduct`, the proof).

*Proof.* The row data give local pure tensors $d_r$ with combinations $c_r$, and the local flat tensor splits as a column factor times a row factor. Each place $w$ has a single place idempotent $e_w$, since $w \in P_t$. The column flat tensor at $w$ expands as a local combination. Column induction joins these into one combination for $e$. Put $\sigma$ the sum of the single points $((d_a, d_r), (c_a, c_r))$ with the coefficients of column induction. The row factor of the evaluation is the product of the row functions, so clause 3 follows from column induction. $\square$

**Theorem 8.10.5 (The local dictionary from the place dictionary).** For all large choices, if the place dictionary holds for $s$ and the choice is linear in the contraction, then the local dictionary holds for $s$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.flat_dictionary_relative_base_vacua_large_depth` (also `GlobalTheory.LocAsm.locAt_of_placeAt`, the proof).

*Proof.* Put $\kappa = \prod_{w \in W_0} \kappa_w$, $z_t = \prod_{w \in P_t} z_{t,w}$ and $\zeta = \prod_w \zeta_w$. On staircase cosets, $\zeta_w = \kappa_w z_{t,w}^{-1}$, and $\kappa_w = 1$ for $w \notin W_0$, so $\zeta = \kappa z_t^{-1}$. For the identity, the flipped finite tensor writes the flip expansion as a sum of local pure tensors $\varphi_a$ with coefficients $x_a$. For each $a$, the previous theorem gives $\sigma_a$. Let $\mu$ be the difference of $\tau'$, with its combinations, and $\sum_a x_a \sigma_a$. At the points fixed by $e$, the product functions of both parts equal the product of the local unfolded terms, so the product function of $\mu$ is $0$. The vacua at the base point are real data. A combination whose product function is $0$ gives the evaluation $0$. So the evaluation of $\tau'$ equals the sum over $a$, and the previous theorem gives the claim. $\square$

### The global statements

**Theorem 8.10.6 (The local dictionary).** For all large choices there is a sign $s$ such that the local dictionary holds for $s$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.T.flat_dictionary_base_vacua_large_depth` (also `GlobalTheory.RowVal.exists_locAt_big`, the proof).

*Proof.* For all large choices, the place dictionary holds for some $s$, the choice is linear in the contraction because the unfolded product depends only on the data product, and the previous theorem applies. $\square$

**Theorem 8.10.7 (The flat dictionary).** For all large choices there is a sign $s$ such that the flat dictionary holds for $s$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.RowFlatAsm.flatAt_big`.

*Proof.* Take $s$, $\kappa$ and $z^{(1)}_t$ from the local dictionary. For each $t$ there is a base point $b_0$. The unfolded vacua are real up to a twist: there are $z^{(2)}_{t,j} \in \Xi_t$ and a real family $C^0$ with $C_{0,j} = z^{(2)}_{t,j} C^0_j$ for even $j < m$. Put $C_0' = \kappa C^0_0$ and $C_j' = C^0_j$ for $j > 0$, and

$$z_t = z^{(1)}_t \Bigl(\prod_{l < m/2} z^{(2)}_{t,2l}\Bigr)^{-1}.$$

The scalars pass through the combination. If $\beta$ is a staircase coset this gives $\zeta = z_t^{-1}$. Each coset in the support is a staircase coset. If $\beta$ is not in the support, then $\tau' = 0$ is also an expansion at $\beta$, so the local dictionary makes the left side $0$, and $\zeta = 0$ works. $\square$

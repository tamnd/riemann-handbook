This chapter proves that Bessel periods climb a theta tower. If a column chain of cusp forms has a nonzero period against a row form at the bottom of a test grid, then a theta lift of the row form has a nonzero Bessel period at the top. The chapter first builds the tools: isotropic means and constant terms of theta functions along a Witt tower, lowest K types of discrete series, unramified and level computations, the density of theta series in the cusp forms, the test grid and its elliptic expansion, cuspidal lifts and core chains, the level invariance of the pairing, the flat dictionary, the multiplicity one result that puts all periods on one line, and the cell masses that reduce the periods to Fock vectors. The last section climbs the tower one rung at a time and alternates Bessel and Fourier-Jacobi periods.

The chapter needs these results from Volume 1: [the Weil representation](/learn/theta/#the-weil-representation), [the Fock model](/learn/theta/#the-fock-model), [seesaw pairs](/learn/theta/#seesaw-pairs), [theta towers](/learn/theta/#theta-towers), [the global theta lift](/learn/theta/#the-global-theta-lift), [constant terms and cusp forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/#constant-terms-and-cusp-forms), [discrete series](/learn/representations/#discrete-series), [highest weights and branching](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching), [Bessel and Fourier-Jacobi periods](/learn/periods/#bessel-and-fourier-jacobi-periods) and [the Heisenberg and metaplectic groups](/learn/groups/#the-heisenberg-and-metaplectic-groups). The result is used in [the separation of real functionals](/entry/real-functionals-separate-of-hypothesis/), and through it in the steps [the nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/) and [every character from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/).

## Notes and references

The chapter feeds the proof at three points. The ascent of Section 13 feeds [the separation of real functionals](/entry/real-functionals-separate-of-hypothesis/). Sections 9 to 12 feed [the twisted pairing identity](/entry/twisted-pairing-identity/), [the unfolding of the twisted theta integral](/entry/twisted-theta-integral-unfolding/), [the eventual largeness of the twisted iterate](/entry/twisted-iterate-eventually-large/) and [the sign flip step](/entry/twist-sign-flip-succ/). Section 8 feeds [the cuspidality of pair lifts](/entry/theta-pair-lift-cuspidal-of-hypotheses/) and [the onto property of the twisted core](/entry/twist-core-surjective-of-local-components/), Section 4 feeds [the frames from a realisation](/entry/frames-ex-of-realisation-alt/), and Section 3 feeds [the closed archimedean record](/entry/arch-on-record-closed/). Through these entries the chapter serves the steps [the nonempty hypotheses](/step/nonempty-hyps/) and [every character from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/).

The theta functions of Section 1 are those of Weil's 1964 paper, which builds the Weil representation on the Schwartz space of a symplectic space and its Heisenberg group. Weil's 1965 paper proves the Siegel-Weil formula, and it uses the same means over isotropic subspaces to compute constant terms. The scalar of absolute value $1$ in Section 1 is the cocycle of the metaplectic group in the Schrödinger model. For the local models, the lattice models at the finite places and the metaplectic group, see Mœglin, Vignéras and Waldspurger, Chapter 2.

The constant term formula of Section 2 is the tower property of Rallis. It is in his paper on the Howe duality conjecture and is used in his paper on injectivity. The first occurrence of a cusp form in a Witt tower is cuspidal by this property. Kudla and Rallis use the tower property to regularize the Siegel-Weil formula. For the splitting characters of unitary dual pairs, see Kudla's 1994 paper and Harris, Kudla and Sweet.

The discrete series of Section 3 are those of Harish-Chandra. The lowest $K$-type of a discrete series and its multiplicity one are in Schmid's 1975 paper and in Hecht and Schmid, who prove Blattner's formula. Vogan's 1979 paper puts the lowest $K$-type at the center of the classification of irreducible $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-modules. The Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt filtration of an induced module is the filtration of the enveloping algebra by degree, see Dixmier, Chapter 2. The positivity and Gaussian decay of the matrix coefficients for large gaps are specific to the proof.

The unramified theta lift of Section 4 sends Satake parameters to Satake parameters as in Rallis's 1982 paper and in Kudla's paper on the local theta correspondence. The doubling integrals are those of Gelbart, Piatetski-Shapiro and Rallis, Part A. Li computes the unramified doubling zeta integral for unitary groups and uses theta lifts to construct cohomology classes. The norm of a level vector and the frames in normal form are bookkeeping for the proof.

The holomorphic discrete series of Section 5 and their realization in the Fock model are in Kashiwara and Vergne. Howe's 1979 paper describes the theta correspondence on $K$-finite vectors through harmonic polynomials in the Fock model. The density of theta series in a space of cusp forms of fixed type and level, as proved in Section 5, follows the method of these papers. The level vectors as translates of sums of Heisenberg translates are specific to the proof.

The test grid of Section 6 is a family of seesaw pairs, in the sense of Kudla's seesaw identity, set out on a grid of Hermitian spaces. We know no classical source for the grid itself.

The structure of regular centralizers in Section 7 is the standard description of maximal tori in unitary groups as norm one tori of étale algebras. The elliptic expansion of the kernel integral into orbital integrals is the elliptic part of the trace formula, and its stable form follows Kottwitz.

The core chains of Section 8 move $K$-finite vectors along the tower by theta lifts, as in Rallis's papers on the tower. The level invariance of Section 9 and the flat dictionary of Section 10 are computations in the Schrödinger model at each place, see Mœglin, Vignéras and Waldspurger, Chapter 2, for the formulas of the Weil action.

The multiplicity one result of Section 11 rests on the branching law for $U(n) \subset U(n + 1)$. A representation of $U(n + 1)$ of highest weight $\lambda$ restricts to $U(n)$ with multiplicity one, and the weights that occur are those that interlace $\lambda$. This is due to Weyl, and its iteration gives the patterns of Gelfand and Tsetlin. The multiplicity one of Bessel and Fourier-Jacobi models in the sense of Gan, Gross and Prasad is the analogue for the noncompact groups.

The cell masses of Section 12 are local volumes of the kind that enter the Siegel mass formula, which Weil's 1965 paper recovers from the Siegel-Weil formula. The rapid decay of cusp forms in Section 12 is in Mœglin and Waldspurger, Chapter I.

The ascent of Section 13 alternates Bessel and Fourier-Jacobi periods along a tower, as in the descent method of Ginzburg, Rallis and Soudry. For unitary groups, Ginzburg, Jiang and Rallis use the same alternation to relate the nonvanishing of periods of theta lifts to central $L$-values. The periods and the conjectures that relate them to central $L$-values are in Gan, Gross and Prasad, and the refined form for special orthogonal groups is due to Ichino and Ikeda. The tame lemma of Section 13 uses the Bargmann transform and its reproducing kernel. See Bargmann's paper and Folland, Chapter 1, for the Fock model of the Heisenberg group, and Folland, Chapter 4, for the metaplectic representation.

This theorem gives the polynomial $P$ that the [intersection number theorem `a18`](/entry/a18/) uses: the multigraded Hilbert polynomial of the model $V$, which also counts the sections of $\mathcal{O}(\beta + c\,e_{k_0})$ for large $c$. We use the notation of that entry: $V$, $h_V$, $\Gamma(m)$, $k_0$ and $e_{k_0}$.

## Statement

**Theorem.** Suppose that $y$ is a model with base block $k_0$ (`IsModelWithBase`). Then there is a polynomial $P \in \mathbb{Q}[t_k : k \in K]$ such that:

1. $h_V(m) = P(m)$ for all $m \in \mathbb{Z}^K$ large in every coordinate;
2. for each $\beta \in \mathbb{Z}^K$, $\dim_F \Gamma(\beta + c\,e_{k_0}) = P(\beta + c\,e_{k_0})$ for all large $c \in \mathbb{N}$.

Part 1 says that $P$ is the multigraded Hilbert polynomial of $V$. Part 2 says that the same polynomial counts sections along the ray in the direction $e_{k_0}$, where only one coordinate grows. On $V$, the bundle $\mathcal{O}(e_{k_0})$ comes from $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ on $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$, so it is ample, and the higher cohomology dies along this ray.

## Proof

Let $\check H^i(m)$ be the Čech cohomology in degree $m$ of the graded module $F[x]/V$ for the cover by the standard open cells (the Lean `cechHc`), and let $\chi(m) = \sum_i (-1)^i \dim_F \check H^i(m)$.

1. `a12` (`EulerPoly`): each $\check H^i(m)$ has finite dimension, they vanish for $i > B$, and $\chi$ is a polynomial $P$ on all of $\mathbb{Z}^K$.
2. `HZeroRowPlaceData.full`: $\check H^0(m) \cong \Gamma(m)$. With it, `finrank_sectionsOver_eq_H0` gives $\dim \Gamma(m) = \dim \check H^0(m)$.
3. So $\dim \Gamma(m) = P(m)$ at each $m$ where $\check H^i(m) = 0$ for all $i \geq 1$.
4. `a13` (`MultiSerre`): for $m$ large in every coordinate, $\check H^i(m) = 0$ for $i \geq 1$ and $\dim \check H^0(m) = h_V(m)$. This gives part 1.
5. [`a16`](/entry/a16/) (`RayVanish`): for each $\beta$ and large $c$, $\check H^i(\beta + c\,e_{k_0}) = 0$ for $i \geq 1$. With step 3 this gives part 2.

## In the Lean code

The theorem is in [A12.lean](lean:Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/HodgeTatePoints/A12.lean#L125). Its proof is `SectionsPoly.proof HZeroRowPlaceData.full a12 a13 a16`. `SectionsPoly.proof` and `finrank_sectionsOver_eq_H0` are in [FinrankSectionsOverEqH0.lean](lean:GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/KleimanAmpleness/FinrankSectionsOverEqH0.lean#L35); the statement is in [ImportsSideExtra.lean](lean:GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/KleimanAmpleness/ImportsSideExtra.lean#L11).

The Čech complex needs linear orders on $K$ and on each $\iota_k$. The statement has none, so the proof picks them from enumerations of the finite sets. The result does not depend on this choice.

The remark on ampleness in the Statement section is our reading. The Lean proof never names ampleness; it uses the vanishing of `a16` directly.

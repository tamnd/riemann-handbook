This theorem proves the vanishing statement $\mathrm{Van}(Z)$ for every commutative reduced subalgebra $Z$ of $M_n(k)$, when $k$ has characteristic zero. It is the induction that holds the proof together; five lemmas supply the steps. Its only caller is [ThmT in characteristic zero](/entry/t-final-thm-t/), which restates it.

## Statement

The notation ($X_n$, $B_Z$, $G_Z$, distributions, $\mathrm{Van}$) is as in [the parent entry](/entry/t-final-thm-t/). Let $k$ be a non-archimedean local field with $\operatorname{char} k = 0$.

**Theorem.** For all $m, n \in \mathbb{N}$ and each commutative reduced subalgebra $Z \subseteq M_n(k)$ with $\dim_k B_Z = m$, $\mathrm{Van}(Z)$ holds.

## Proof

We use strong induction on $m = \dim_k B_Z$. For $x \in B_Z$ let $p_Z(x)$ be the vector of traces $\operatorname{tr}(b_j x^i)$, for $0 \le i \le 2n$ and $b_j$ in a fixed basis of $Z$. Its fibres are $G_Z$-stable. Fix $Z$, and let $\xi$ be supported on $B_Z \times k^n$ and $G_Z$-invariant.

1. By `nilpB`, it is enough to show that $\xi$ kills test functions supported where $v \neq 0$ and $x \notin Z + \{\text{nilpotents}\}$.
2. By `locStep`, it is enough to show, for each value $y$ of $p_Z$, one of two things: the fibre $p_Z^{-1}(y) \cap B_Z$ lies in $c + \{\text{nilpotents}\}$ for one $c \in Z$; or each $G_Z$-invariant distribution supported on that fibre times $k^n$ kills $\{v \neq 0\}$.
3. By `dich`, either the first case holds, or there is $s \in B_Z \setminus Z$ with $s$ semisimple such that each $x$ in the fibre has a $G_Z$-conjugate $x'$ with $x' - s$ nilpotent and $x' s = s x'$.
4. In the second case let $Z[s]$ be the algebra generated by $Z$ and $s$. By `adjP`, $Z[s]$ is commutative and reduced, and $\dim_k B_{Z[s]} < \dim_k B_Z$. The induction gives $\mathrm{Van}(Z[s])$.
5. By `descA`, $\mathrm{Van}(Z[s])$ gives the vanishing for distributions on the fibre.

Step 4 is the descent: the centralizer of $Z[s]$ is the centralizer of $s$ inside $B_Z$, a smaller algebra. Step 3 is a form of the Jordan decomposition $x = s + (x - s)$.

## In the Lean code

The theorem is at [LocStep.lean](lean:Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/UnitaryDualProof/FrobeniusReciprocity/LocStep.lean#L20). The statements `Dich`, `Adj`, `LocStep`, `DescA` and `NilpB`, and the map `pZ`, are in [CompactOrbits/LocStep.lean](lean:Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/UnitaryDualProof/CompactOrbits/LocStep.lean#L36). The lemmas, each a second name in the same file:

- `dich` is `T.Alg.dich`, in MemBzOfAdjoin.lean.
- `adjP` is `adjunction_char_zero`, in AdjunctionCharZero.lean.
- `locStep` is `local_descent_holds`, in LocalDescentHolds.lean. It has no hypothesis on the characteristic.
- `descA` is `T.Desc.Final.descA`, in DescFinal.lean. It comes from `jcPoly` and a slice lemma `sliceVan`, which uses Frobenius reciprocity and a lemma `SLF.Un2.unimod`.
- `nilpB` is `T.Nil.Final.nilpB`, in NilpB.lean.

We have not unfolded their proofs. The other four carry the hypothesis `CharZero k`. This fits the fact that over a field that is not perfect, such as $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$, a matrix need not have a Jordan decomposition over the field. The [characteristic $p$ proof](/entry/tp-final-thm-t/) uses a different induction, on the invariant $\mu$ of [the descent statement](/entry/desc-final-desc/).

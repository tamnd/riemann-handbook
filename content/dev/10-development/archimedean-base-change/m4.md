This theorem proves `M4`, a matching of functions for unitary base change at a finite place $w$ of a CM field that does not split over the maximal real subfield. On the unitary side the function is the indicator of one Iwahori double coset. On the side of $\mathrm{GL}_a \times \mathrm{GL}_{n-a}$ it is an explicit sum of Iwahori double coset indicators. The spine entry [archimedean base change, fourth part](/entry/archimedean-base-change-fourth/) is this theorem under a second name, and [`hM4`](/entry/h-m4/) is a third name for it.

## Statement

Let $E$ be a CM field with maximal real subfield $F$, and suppose that each finite place of $E$ is unramified over $F$. Let $w$ be a finite place of $E$ that is not split over $F$, and let $c$ be the continuous extension of complex conjugation to $E_w$. Let $q_w$ be `qV E w` (not unfolded). Fix $n$, $a \le n$, a matrix $b \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_w)$ with $\overline{b}^{\,T} b = J$ ($J$ antidiagonal), and a uniformizer $\varpi$ with $c(\varpi) = \varpi$. Let $x \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ be strictly decreasing, and put $l_i = x_i - x_{n-1-i}$ for $i < \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$. Suppose $l_i \ge 2$ and $l_i - l_{i+1} \ge 2$.

Let $U \subset \mathrm{GL}_n(E_w)$ be the unitary group of the form, $K_U = U \cap \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_w)$, and $I_U = U \cap b I b^{-1}$ with $I$ the standard Iwahori. Write $t(m) = \mathrm{diag}(\varpi^{m_i})$. Then:

- $f_U$ is the indicator of $I_U \, b\, t(l_1, \dots, l_{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor}, [0], -l_{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor}, \dots, -l_1)\, b^{-1} I_U$ in $U$;
- $\phi_x(y_1, y_2)$ is the sum

  $$\begin{aligned}&{\sum_s (-q_w^{1/2})^{-v(s)} [K_U : I_U]^{-1} [K_a : I_a][K_{n-a} : I_{n-a}]}\\ &{\quad \cdot 1_{I_a t(x|_{S}) I_a}(y_1) \, 1_{I_{n-a} t(x|_{S'}) I_{n-a}}(y_2).}\end{aligned}$$

The sum runs over sets $s$ of $\lfloor a/2 \rfloor$ indices below $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$, under a parity condition. $S$ is the set of indices $i$ with $i$ or $n-1-i$ in $s$, plus the middle index when $n$ and $a$ are odd. $S'$ is the same set for the complement of $s$. The exponent $v(s)$ is $-2\sum_{i \in s, j \notin s} \max(l_i, l_j)$, with a further sum of the $l_j$ when $n$ is odd.

**Theorem.** $f_U$ and $\phi_x$ match with the trivial character. That is, for monic $Q_1, Q_2$ of degrees $a$, $n-a$, conjugate self-reciprocal, with $Q_1 Q_2$ separable, the stable twisted orbital integral of $\phi_x$ at $(Q_1, Q_2)$ equals $\Delta(Q_1, Q_2)$ times the $\kappa$-orbital integral of $f_U$ at $Q_1 Q_2$. Also, $\phi_x(y) \ne 0$ implies that the product of the characteristic polynomials of the norms $y_i \theta(y_i)$ is separable.

## Proof

Case A: some $s$ gives polynomials $Q_1$, $Q_2$ whose roots have valuations $x_i - x_{\mathrm{rev}\, i}$ along $S$ and $S'$. Then `asmL_A` computes the left side as $(-q_w^{1/2})^{-v(s)} [K_U : I_U]^{-1}$. `asmR_A` gets the same value on the right: the transfer factor is $(-q_w^{1/2})^{-v(s)}$ (`v1`), the sign $\kappa$ is $1$ (`k1`), and the orbit is one class (`uniqR`) with integral $[K_U : I_U]^{-1}$ (`orbR`). Otherwise `asmL_B` and `asmR_B` show that both sides are $0$.

For regularity, `asmReg` uses `s1`: on the double coset of $t(z)$ the roots of the norm have valuations $z_i - z_{\mathrm{rev}\, i}$. These are distinct because $x$ is strictly decreasing (`asmReg_sep`).

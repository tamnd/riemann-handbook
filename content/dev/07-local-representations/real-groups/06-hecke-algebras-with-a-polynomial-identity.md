## Hecke algebras with a polynomial identity and the tensor product theorem

An irreducible unitary representation of the adelic unitary group should be a restricted tensor product of irreducible unitary representations of the local groups. This is Flath's theorem, and its main local input is admissibility: in an irreducible unitary representation of a $p$-adic group, the vectors fixed by a compact open subgroup form a finite-dimensional space. This section proves a uniform form of admissibility through a polynomial identity in Hecke algebras, starting from a Cartan decomposition of $p$-adic unitary groups, and then the tensor product theorem. The results are used in [the Flath factorisation](/entry/flath-factorisation-of-literature/) and in [the tensor product form of it](/entry/flath-tensor-product-of-literature/).

Background: [smooth representations of $p$-adic groups](/learn/representations/#smooth-representations-of-p-adic-groups), [adelic points and compact open subgroups](/learn/groups/#adelic-points-and-compact-open-subgroups), [automorphic representations](/learn/automorphic-forms/#automorphic-representations), [[unitary representation|74BPYJNZTM]].

### The polynomial identity

For elements $E, a_1, \dots, a_M$ of a ring put
$$s_M(E; a) = \sum_{\pi \in S_M} \operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\, E\, a_{\pi(1)}\, E\, a_{\pi(2)}\, E \cdots E\, a_{\pi(M)}\, E.$$
If $E^2 = E$, this is the standard polynomial of degree $M$ evaluated at $E a_1 E, \dots, E a_M E$ in the corner ring $E R E$.

**Definition 2.6.1 (Averaging operator).** Let $G$ be a group, $K \leq G$ a subgroup, and $\sigma$ a representation of $G$ by bounded operators on a Hilbert space $H$. A bounded operator $E$ on $H$ *averages over $K$* if $E v$ is $K$-fixed for all $v$, $E v = v$ for every $K$-fixed $v$, and $E \sigma(k) = E$ for $k \in K$.

**Definition 2.6.2 (Polynomial identity for $K$).** We say that $K$ *has the identity of degree $M$* if for every Hilbert space $H$, every representation $\sigma$ of $G$ by isometries of $H$, every $E$ that averages over $K$, and all $g_1, \dots, g_M \in G$, we have $s_M(E; \sigma(g_1), \dots, \sigma(g_M)) = 0$.

If $K$ has the identity of degree $M$ and $\sigma$ is irreducible and unitary, then $\dim H^K \leq M$. Indeed, the commutant of $\sigma(G)$ consists of scalars, so by the double commutant theorem the span of the $\sigma(g)$ is strongly dense in the bounded operators. By multilinearity and continuity, $s_M(E; A_1, \dots, A_M) = 0$ for all bounded $A_i$, where $E$ is the orthogonal projection on $H^K$. If $H^K$ contained a subspace of large finite dimension $k$, the matrix units of that subspace would give a nonzero value, since $M_k(\mathbb{C})$ satisfies no standard identity of degree less than $2k$ (S. A. Amitsur and J. Levitzki, *Minimal identities for algebras*, Proc. AMS 1 (1950)). The bound is uniform in $\sigma$.

### The Cartan decomposition of a $p$-adic unitary group

Let $L$ be a nontrivially normed field of characteristic $0$ with an ultrametric norm whose closed balls are compact, so a finite extension of some $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Let $s$ be a nontrivial involution of $L$ that preserves the norm, and $J \in \mathrm{GL}_n(L)$ with $(J^s)^T = J$. Put
$$U(s, J) = \{ g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(L) : (g^s)^T J g = J \},$$
with the topology from $\mathrm{GL}_n(L)$.

**Definition 2.6.3 (Cartan structure).** A subgroup $K$ of a group $G$ has a *Cartan structure* if there are $c_1, \dots, c_d \in G$ with $c_i K c_i^{-1} = K$ and commuting $t_1, \dots, t_l \in G$ such that, with $S$ the monoid generated by the $t_j$,

1. $G = \bigcup_{i,j} K c_i\, S\, c_j K$;
2. $t k t' \in K t t' K$ for all $t, t' \in S$ and $k \in K$.

The classical Cartan decomposition $G = K_0 A^+ K_0$, for a special maximal compact subgroup $K_0$ and the dominant part $A^+$ of a maximal split torus, holds for every reductive $p$-adic group (F. Bruhat and J. Tits, *Groupes réductifs sur un corps local*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 41 (1972), §4.4). Condition 2 is the property of the Iwahori factorisation that makes the Hecke algebra of a congruence subgroup close to commutative.

**Theorem 2.6.4 (Cartan decomposition for unitary groups).** With $L$, $s$, $J$ as above, every open subgroup of $U(s, J)$ contains a compact open subgroup $K$ with a Cartan structure.

Lean: `CompanionTheory.Rg.Fl.B.stCartanU`.

*Proof.* Let $\Lambda$ be a lattice in $L^n$ that is maximal among the lattices on which the Hermitian form is integral; it exists by a sandwich argument between $\Lambda$ and its dual. Choose a Witt decomposition $L^n = \bigoplus_i (L e_i \oplus L f_i) \oplus W$ adapted to $\Lambda$, with $e_i, f_i$ hyperbolic pairs and $W$ anisotropic. It is built from Hensel's lemma (to produce isotropic vectors), the pairing of $\Lambda$ with its dual, and a splitting of hyperbolic planes. For units $u_i$ of $L$ there is an isometry acting by $u_i$ on $e_i$, by $s(u_i)^{-1}$ on $f_i$ and trivially on $W$. Every isometry is $k\, t\, k'$, where $k, k'$ fix $\Lambda$ and $t$ is the torus element with $e_i \mapsto \varpi^{r_i} e_i$, with $r$ decreasing. Let $K$ be a congruence subgroup of the stabiliser $K_0$ of $\Lambda$, inside the given open subgroup; the congruence subgroups are compact, open, normal in $K_0$ and cofinal. Take for the $c_i$ coset representatives of $K$ in $K_0$, and for the $t_j$ the torus elements for the basis vectors of the cone of decreasing $r$. Condition 1 is the decomposition. For condition 2, each $k \in K$ factors as $a b$ with $t a t^{-1} \in K$ and $t'^{-1} b t' \in K$ for dominant $t, t'$ (the Iwahori factorisation), so $t k t' = (t a t^{-1})\, t t'\, (t'^{-1} b t')$. $\square$

### Small subgroups with a polynomial identity

Let $E/F$ be a CM extension of number fields with complex conjugation $\sigma$, $J$ a nondegenerate Hermitian $n \times n$ matrix over $E$, $G = U(J)$, and $v$ a finite place of $F$.

**Theorem 2.6.5 (Hecke algebras with a polynomial identity).** Every open subgroup $U$ of $G(F_v)$ contains a subgroup $K$ that has the identity of degree $M$ for some $M$.

Lean: `CompanionTheory.Rg.Fl.B.locPI`.

The proof gives $K$ compact and open, but the statement does not say so. The classical result behind it is Bernstein's theorem that a reductive $p$-adic group is uniformly admissible: for every compact open $K$ there is $C$ with $\dim H^K \leq C$ for every irreducible unitary $H$ (I. N. Bernstein, *All reductive p-adic groups are tame*, Funct. Anal. Appl. 8 (1974)). Here $K$ is chosen small inside $U$.

**Lemma 2.6.6 (From a Cartan structure to the identity).** Let $K \leq G$ be a subgroup such that every double coset $K g K$ is a finite union of left cosets, with a Cartan structure of sizes $d, l$, and separated in this sense: for every finite set $S \subseteq G/K$ there is a subgroup $\Gamma$ with finitely many orbits and finite stabilisers on $G/K$ whose orbits separate the points of $S$. Then $K$ has the identity of degree $C^2 + 1$, where $C$ depends only on $d$ and $l$.

Lean: `CompanionTheory.Rg.Fl.hasPI_of`.

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{H}$ be the Hecke algebra, the commutant of $G$ acting on finitely supported functions on $G/K$. The Cartan structure gives $x_i, y_j \in \mathcal{H}$ ($i, j \leq d$) and commuting $a_1, \dots, a_l$ such that $\mathcal{H}$ is spanned by the products $x_i\, c\, y_j$ with $c$ in the algebra generated by the $a$'s. For such an algebra every simple finite-dimensional module $V$ has dimension at most some $C(d, l)$. By Jacobson density, $\mathcal{H}$ maps onto $\mathrm{End}(V)$, so $(\dim V)^2 \leq d^2 \dim C_V$, where $C_V$ is the image of the commutative part. A commutative algebra of operators generated by $l$ elements is small: for each $N$ there is $C_N$, depending only on $l$ and $N$, with $N \dim C_V \leq (\dim V)^2 + C_N \dim V$. Taking $N = 2 d^2$ gives $\dim V \leq 2 C_N$. For $h \in \mathcal{H}$ and the finite set of cosets in the support of $h$, a separating $\Gamma$ gives the finite-dimensional space of functions on $\Gamma \backslash G / K$, with the inner product weighted by the inverse stabiliser orders. The Hecke algebra acts there by a representation closed under adjoints, in which $h$ acts nonzero. A representation closed under adjoints is semisimple, so its image is a product of matrix algebras of size at most $C$, each of dimension at most $C^2$; an alternating multilinear expression in $C^2 + 1$ elements of such an algebra vanishes. So $s_{C^2+1}$ vanishes on $\mathcal{H}$. Finally the identity passes from $\mathcal{H}$ to every representation by isometries: on $K$-fixed vectors, $E \sigma(g) E$ is the action of the Hecke operator of $K g K$ divided by the number of left cosets in $K g K$. $\square$

*Proof of the theorem.* By a dictionary for local unitary groups, $G(F_v)$ is isomorphic as a topological group to $\mathrm{GL}_n(L)$ for a $p$-adic field $L$ (if $v$ splits in $E$) or to a group $U(s, J')$ as in the previous theorem (otherwise). In both cases every open subgroup contains a compact open $K$ with a Cartan structure: for $\mathrm{GL}_n(L)$ by the Cartan decomposition with congruence subgroups, for $U(s, J')$ by the theorem above, and the property moves along isomorphisms. A compact open subgroup has finite double cosets modulo $K$. For separation, replace $J$ by a positive definite $J'$ with an isomorphic local group at $v$; for definite $J'$ the group $\Gamma$ of elements of $U(J')$ with entries integral away from $v$ is discrete and cocompact in $G(F_v)$, and its congruence subgroups separate any finite set of cosets. Separation also moves along the isomorphism. The lemma gives the identity. $\square$

**Corollary 2.6.7 (Admissibility).** Every irreducible unitary representation of $G(F_v)$ has finite-dimensional $K$-fixed space for every compact open $K$.

Lean: `CompanionTheory.Rg.Fl.B.unitaryIrreducibleAdmissible`.

*Proof.* Choose $K' \leq K$ as in the theorem. The orthogonal projection on $H^{K'}$ averages over $K'$, so $\dim H^{K'} \leq M$ by the remark after the definitions, and $H^K \subseteq H^{K'}$. $\square$

### The restricted tensor product

The classical statement is the following (D. Flath, *Decomposition of representations into tensor products*, Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 33, Part 1 (1979)). Let $\pi$ be an irreducible admissible representation of $G(\mathbb{A}_F)$. Then there are irreducible admissible representations $\pi_v$ of $G(F_v)$, with $\pi_v$ unramified for almost all $v$, such that $\pi \cong \bigotimes'_v \pi_v$, the restricted tensor product with respect to spherical vectors. The unitary form replaces admissible representations by unitary ones and the algebraic tensor product by its Hilbert completion.

**Definition 2.6.8 (Restricted tensor factorisation).** Let $\pi$ be a representation of $G(\mathbb{A}_F)$ on $H$, $R_v$ representations of $G(F_v)$ on $H_v$ for all places $v$, and $\xi = (\xi_v)$ with $\xi_v \in H_v$. A family $y = (y_v)$ is *restricted* if $y_v = \xi_v$ for almost all $v$. A map $B$ from all families to $H$ is a *factorisation* of $\pi$ if:

1. $\|\xi_v\| = 1$ for almost all $v$;
2. for each $g \in G(\mathbb{A}_F)$, $R_v(g_v) \xi_v = \xi_v$ for almost all $v$;
3. $\langle B y', B y \rangle = \prod_v \langle y'_v, y_v \rangle$ for restricted $y, y'$;
4. the span of the $B y$, $y$ restricted, is dense in $H$;
5. $\pi(g) B y = B\big((R_v(g_v) y_v)_v\big)$ for restricted $y$.

Condition 3 forces $B$ to be linear in each restricted slot, because the norm of the defect of linearity is computed from inner products and vanishes. So $B$ is the canonical map from restricted pure tensors, and it extends to a unitary isomorphism from the Hilbert restricted tensor product $\bigotimes'_v (H_v, \xi_v)$ onto $H$.

**Theorem 2.6.9 (Flath tensor product).** Let $\pi$ be a topologically irreducible unitary representation of $G(\mathbb{A}_F)$ on a Hilbert space $H$. Then there are irreducible unitary representations $R_v$ of $G(F_v)$, for all places $v$ of $F$, vectors $\xi_v$, and a factorisation $B$ of $\pi$ in the above sense.

Lean: `CompanionTheory.Rg.Fl.B.flathTensorProduct`.

*Proof.* Fix a place $v$ and restrict $\pi$ to $G(F_v)$, a direct factor of $G(\mathbb{A}_F)$ that commutes with the kernel of the projection to $G(F_v)$. We first find a nonzero self-adjoint projection $P$, commuting with the intertwiners of the restriction, whose corner satisfies a polynomial identity. At an archimedean place this is a separate proved input on the real group, whose proof goes through principal series; we did not read it. At a finite place, $\pi$ restricted to $G(F_v)$ has a nonzero vector fixed by some open subgroup $U$; the theorem above gives $K \leq U$ with an identity, and the averaging projection on $H^K$ is nonzero and has the required properties. A corner with a polynomial identity forces the restriction to contain an irreducible subrepresentation $W$, and irreducibility of $\pi$ under the commuting product then shows that the restriction is isotypic of a single type $R_v$. Finally, with all restrictions isotypic, the admissibility corollary, and fixed vectors at almost all places, the tensor product construction builds $\xi_v$ (a unit $K_v$-fixed vector at almost every $v$) and the map $B$. $\square$

**Remark 2.6.10 (Lean form of Flath's theorem).** The Lean statement asks for irreducible unitary $R_v$ at all places, including the archimedean ones, and for a factorisation as above, but it does not say that $\xi_v$ is fixed by a hyperspecial subgroup or that $R_v$ is unramified: condition 2 only asks that each $g$ fixes almost all $\xi_v$. It does not state uniqueness of the $R_v$, and $B$ is a map on all families, constrained only on restricted ones.

**Remark 2.6.11 (Formalization).** All three results are assemblies of proved inputs whose names end in "Statement" or "Theorem" but which are theorems, not hypotheses ([FinalB.lean](lean:CompanionTheory/RealGroups/UnitaryAdmissibility/ArchimedeanUnitaryDual/FinalB.lean#L49)). The polynomial identity for $K$ is stated for representations by isometries without any continuity, and the degree is $C^2 + 1$ instead of the Amitsur-Levitzki degree $2C$. The statement of the second theorem does not say that $K$ is compact or open.

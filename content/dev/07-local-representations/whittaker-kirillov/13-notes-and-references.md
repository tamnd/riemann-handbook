## Notes and references

The local results of the chapter feed the step [EveryCharacter from the Galois row](/step/every-character/) (Sections 1, 2 and 6), [Theorem](/entry/toral-of-lower/) (Section 3), [Theorem](/entry/bad-share-win/) (Section 4) and the step [HypsAlt is nonempty from the closed bound](/step/nonempty-hyps/) (Section 5). The global results feed [Theorem 8.7.6 of Volume 8](/entry/vanish/) (Sections 7 and 8), [Theorem 7.21.2 of Volume 8](/entry/loc-iff/) (Section 9), [Theorem 7.24.3 of Volume 8](/entry/gen-fe/) (Section 10), [Theorem 3.4.7 of Volume 5](/entry/has-good-reduction-of-infinite-torsion-unramified/) (Section 11) and [Theorem](/entry/on-of-has-at-places/) (Section 12). For the background on Whittaker models, see [generic representations and local factors](/learn/representations/#generic-representations-and-local-factors) and [Fourier coefficients and Whittaker functions](/learn/automorphic-forms/#fourier-coefficients-and-whittaker-functions).

The uniqueness of Whittaker models (Sections 1 and 2) is due to Gelfand and Kazhdan, through distributions invariant under the anti-involution $g \mapsto w\,{}^t g\,w$; Shalika proved it independently and used it for the multiplicity one theorem. The exposition of Bernstein and Zelevinsky gives the theory of $l$-spaces, distributions and the Kirillov model over a non-archimedean field. Here the anti-involution argument is carried out for one fixed involution and the contragredient is identified separately. Jacquet modules and the subquotient theorem of Section 3 are in the same exposition. Multiplicity one for Bessel models (Section 5) is due to Aizenbud, Gourevitch, Rallis and Schiffmann over non-archimedean fields and to Sun and Zhu over archimedean fields; Section 5 proves only the form for one discrete series that the proof needs, from a decay estimate and a symmetry under an anti-involution.

The factorization theorem of Dixmier and Malliavin writes a smooth vector of a representation of a Lie group as a finite sum of smoothed vectors; Section 6 proves its one-variable core with the bounds on the derivatives fixed first. The archimedean Kirillov theorem of Section 7 replaces the finite sums of translates of the non-archimedean theory by integral operators with rapidly decreasing kernels; the induction over rows is the archimedean form of the induction over the mirabolic chain in Bernstein and Zelevinsky. The tensor product theorem of Section 9 is due to Flath, and the proof here follows his argument, with the multiplicity one of spherical vectors from the Satake isomorphism. The zeta integral of Section 10 is that of Godement and Jacquet; their theorem also identifies the integral with $L(s, \pi)$ times an entire function, which is not proved here. The criterion of Section 11 is a special case of the criterion of Néron, Ogg and Shafarevich in the form of Serre and Tate: an abelian variety has good reduction if and only if the inertia group acts trivially on its Tate module. The form here, with rational $m$-torsion for infinitely many $m$ over a strictly henselian base, is proved by counting torsion on a smooth model. Section 12 has no classical source; it is a lemma on localization of lattices over a Dedekind domain.

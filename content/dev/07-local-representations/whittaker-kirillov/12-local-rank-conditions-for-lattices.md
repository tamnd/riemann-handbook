## Local rank conditions for lattices

The index bounds of the indefinite tree are proved from a long list of conditions on a family of lattices over the ring of integers of a number field. One of these conditions asks for a global inclusion of two lattices. This section shows that the inclusion is only needed after localization at each place of a finite set: the bound then holds with the same error terms. The argument replaces the larger lattice by the sum of the two, which does not change any local index. The result is used in the proof of [Theorem 7.9.3 of Volume 15](/entry/on-of-has-at-places/). For lattices over Dedekind domains and their localizations, see [lattices over Dedekind domains](/learn/hermitian-lattices/#lattices-over-dedekind-domains).

### Lattices and localization

Let $K$ be a number field with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space.

**Definition 4.12.1 (Lattice in a subspace).** Let $W \subset V$ be a $K$-subspace. An $\mathcal{O}_K$-submodule $A \subset V$ is a *lattice in* $W$ if $A$ is finitely generated and its $K$-span is $W$.

**Definition 4.12.2 (Localization at a place).** Let $v$ be a finite place of $K$, with prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}_v$. For an $\mathcal{O}_K$-submodule $L \subset V$, its *localization* at $v$ is
$$L_{(v)} = \{ x \in V : s x \in L \text{ for some } s \in \mathcal{O}_K \setminus \mathfrak{p}_v \}.$$

So $L \subset L_{(v)}$, and $L_{(v)}$ is the $\mathcal{O}_{K,(v)}$-module generated by $L$. Two lattices with the same localization at $v$ have the same local index at $v$ in any third lattice: the local index at $v$ is computed from the localizations, and the factors of the index at other primes do not enter it.

### The data

The data of the index bound consist of a finite set $T$ of finite places, with an involution $w \mapsto \bar w$ (the conjugate place in a CM extension); a finite index set $J$; and an object $\mathcal{O}$ that gives, for each place $v$ and each $j \in J$:

1. two $\mathcal{O}_K$-lattices $\Theta_0(v, j)$ and $\Theta_J(v, j)$ in $K^{n_j}$;
2. a $K$-linear map $e_j$ on $K^{n_j}$ and a subspace $Z_j$ (an eigenspace of $e_j$ met with a fixed subspace);
3. the image $e_J(v, j) = e_j(\Theta_J(v, j))$, and further lattices and numbers that enter the other conditions.

We say that a condition $Q$ on places holds *at the pairs of $T$* if $Q(w)$ and $Q(\bar w)$ hold for each $w \in T$.

**Definition 4.12.3 (Rank conditions).** The *global rank condition* holds if, at the pairs of $T$ and for each $j$,

1. $e_j(\Theta_0(v, j))$ is a lattice in $Z_j$;
2. $e_J(v, j)$ is a lattice in $Z_j$;
3. $\Theta_0(v, j) \subseteq \Theta_J(v, j)$.

The *local rank condition* holds if the same is true with condition 3 replaced by
$$\Theta_0(v, j)_{(v)} \subseteq \Theta_J(v, j)_{(v)}.$$

The global condition implies the local one, since localization is monotone.

The conclusion of the index bound is a statement $\mathrm{ON}(\mathrm{err})$ about a family of error terms $\mathrm{err}(w, j)$ attached to $\mathcal{O}$. It says three things: for each $w \in T$, the sum over $j$ of the indices at $w$ and $\bar w$ is bounded by twice the main term $F_0$ plus the error terms and a term $2/q_w^2$, all times a fixed degree and ramification factor; the error terms are nonnegative; and for each $j$, a weighted sum over $w \in T$ of the error terms and a second main term $J_0$ is at most a constant $E_1(j)$.

We use the core index bound: if the object $\mathcal{O}$ satisfies the global rank condition and a list of about twenty-five further conditions (on the ranks of the rows, Mackey and support conditions, spans, projections, coordinates, a bound on lattice indices, identity and twisting conditions, depth conditions at good, ramified and level places, pages, sizes, a share of bad places and sums of the $J$-terms), then $\mathrm{ON}(\mathrm{err})$ holds for its error terms.

**Theorem 4.12.4 (Index bound under local rank conditions).** Let $\mathcal{O}$ satisfy the local rank condition and all the further conditions of the core index bound, with the same constants. Then $\mathrm{ON}(\mathrm{err})$ holds for the error terms of $\mathcal{O}$.

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{O}'$ be the object with the same data as $\mathcal{O}$, except that $\Theta_J(v, j)$ is replaced by
$$\Theta'_J(v, j) = \Theta_J(v, j) + \Theta_0(v, j).$$
We check that $\mathcal{O}'$ satisfies all the conditions of the core bound.

1. *Global rank condition.* Condition 3 holds for $\mathcal{O}'$ by construction. Condition 1 does not involve $\Theta_J$. For condition 2, $e_j(\Theta'_J) = e_J + e_j(\Theta_0)$ is a sum of two lattices in $Z_j$, so it is finitely generated and its $K$-span is $Z_j$.
2. *Local indices.* At a place $v$ of the pairs of $T$, the local rank condition gives $\Theta'_J(v, j) \subseteq \Theta_J(v, j)_{(v)}$, so $\Theta'_J(v, j)_{(v)} = \Theta_J(v, j)_{(v)}$. Applying $e_j$, the images have the same localization at $v$. So the local index at $v$ of $e_j(\Theta'_J(v, j))$ in any lattice equals that of $e_J(v, j)$.
3. *The twisting condition.* The first twisting condition bounds a local index at $v$ of a lattice by the local index at $v$ of $e_J(v, j)$ plus a multiple of an error term. By step 2 the right side is the same for $\mathcal{O}'$, so the condition holds for $\mathcal{O}'$.
4. *The other conditions.* No other condition and no error term involves $\Theta_J$. So they hold for $\mathcal{O}'$ as for $\mathcal{O}$, and the error terms of $\mathcal{O}'$ are those of $\mathcal{O}$.

The core index bound for $\mathcal{O}'$ gives $\mathrm{ON}(\mathrm{err})$ with the error terms of $\mathcal{O}$. $\square$

So the theorem is a reduction: a global inclusion is replaced by a family of local inclusions, which are what one can check place by place.

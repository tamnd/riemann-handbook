This chapter proves the facts about unitary and generic representations of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ that the proof needs at the local places, and three global facts that go with them. Over a nonarchimedean local field it proves Bernstein's vanishing theorem for distributions invariant under the mirabolic subgroup, in characteristic zero and in characteristic $p$, through Frobenius reciprocity for distributions. From it come Theorems A and B, the Leibniz rule for derivatives, the lift of invariant forms and the exponent bound for unitarisable representations, and Bernstein's theorem that the product of two irreducible unitarisable representations is irreducible. It then proves the reducibility of $\rho \times \rho\nu$, Zelevinsky's segment representations and the classification of generic representations by unlinked segments. At the real places it proves He's branching theorem for the discrete series of $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$ and Harish-Chandra's formula for their formal degrees. The last section proves Clozel's theorem that regular algebraic cuspidal representations are cohomological, the Cauchy identity for unramified Rankin and Selberg Euler products, and Kottwitz's base change fundamental lemma for the unit element. The chapter needs from Volume 1 the [smooth representations of $p$-adic groups](/learn/representations/#smooth-representations-of-p-adic-groups), [parabolic induction and the Jacquet module](/learn/representations/#parabolic-induction-and-the-jacquet-module), [cuspidal representations and segments](/learn/representations/#cuspidal-representations-and-segments), [generic representations](/learn/representations/#generic-representations-and-local-factors), the [representations of real groups](/learn/representations/#representations-of-real-groups), the [discrete series](/learn/representations/#discrete-series) and [highest weights and branching](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching).

The results are used in the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/), where the local components of unitary cuspidal representations of $\mathrm{GL}_N$ are written as irreducible products of segment representations with bounded exponents. The branching theorem is [the real input E](/entry/real-input-e-holds/), the formal degree formula is [the formal degree statement of the literature](/entry/formal-degree-compact-dual-of-literature/), Clozel's theorem enters the step [EveryCharacter from Row0b](/step/every-character-of-row/), and the Cauchy identity enters [Theorem 9.14.5 of Volume 3](/entry/rs-pole/).

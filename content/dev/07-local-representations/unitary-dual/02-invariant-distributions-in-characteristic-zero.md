## Bernstein's vanishing theorem in characteristic zero

Let $k$ be a nonarchimedean local field. Bernstein's key theorem on invariant distributions says that a distribution on $M_n(k) \times k^n$ that is invariant under the action $g \cdot (x, v) = (g x g^{-1}, g v)$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ vanishes off the set $\{v = 0\}$. The proof runs over a family of centralizers, so the theorem is stated for every commutative reduced subalgebra $Z$ of $M_n(k)$. This section proves it when $k$ has characteristic zero, by descent to centralizers of semisimple elements and a homogeneity argument with the Fourier transform on the nilpotent part. The next section treats characteristic $p$. The theorem is the input of Bernstein's Theorem A in the section on [Theorems A and B](/entry/thm-a/), and through it of the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/).

Background: [smooth representations of $p$-adic groups](/learn/representations/#smooth-representations-of-p-adic-groups).

### The setting

Fix $n$ and write $W = M_n(k) \times k^n$. Let $Z \subseteq M_n(k)$ be a subalgebra that is commutative and reduced. Let
$$B_Z = \{ x \in M_n(k) : x a = a x \text{ for all } a \in Z \}$$
be its centralizer, and $G_Z = B_Z^\times$ the group of invertible elements of $B_Z$. The group $G_Z$ acts on $W$ by $g \cdot (x, v) = (g x g^{-1}, g v)$. Test functions are the locally constant compactly supported functions on $W$, as in the [section on Frobenius reciprocity](/entry/frobenius-reciprocity/).

**Definition 7.2.1 (Vanishing property).** Say that $Z$ has the *vanishing property* $(\mathrm{V}_Z)$ if the following holds. Let $\xi$ be a linear functional on the functions $W \to \mathbb{C}$ such that

1. $\xi(f) = 0$ for every test function $f$ that vanishes on $B_Z \times k^n$, and
2. $\xi(g \cdot f) = \xi(f)$ for every test function $f$ and every $g \in G_Z$.

Then $\xi(f) = 0$ for every test function $f$ supported in $\{(x, v) : v \neq 0\}$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Van`.

**Theorem 7.2.2 (Theorem T in characteristic zero).** Let $k$ have characteristic zero. Then every commutative reduced subalgebra $Z \subseteq M_n(k)$ has the vanishing property $(\mathrm{V}_Z)$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Final.thmT` (also `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Final.van_all`).

For $Z = k \cdot 1$ this is Bernstein's statement: $B_Z = M_n(k)$ and $G_Z = \mathrm{GL}_n(k)$.

### Three facts on the semisimple part

Let $b_1, \dots, b_r$ be a basis of $Z$. For $x \in B_Z$ let $p_Z(x)$ be the vector of traces $\mathrm{tr}(b_j x^i)$ for $0 \leq i \leq 2n$ and $1 \leq j \leq r$. In characteristic zero the traces of powers fix the characteristic polynomial, so a fibre of $p_Z$ is a finite union of sets with one semisimple part up to conjugacy. Let $Z_N = \{ x : x - c \text{ is nilpotent for some } c \in Z \}$, the elements of $B_Z$ whose semisimple part lies in $Z$.

**Proposition 7.2.3 (Dichotomy).** Let $y$ be a value of $p_Z$. Then one of the following holds.

1. There is $c \in Z$ such that $x - c$ is nilpotent for every $x \in B_Z$ with $p_Z(x) = y$.
2. There is a semisimple $s \in B_Z \setminus Z$ such that every $x \in B_Z$ with $p_Z(x) = y$ is $G_Z$-conjugate to some $x'$ that commutes with $s$ and has $x' - s$ nilpotent.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Final.dich` (also `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Final.adjP`).

In case 2, let $Z[s]$ be the algebra generated by $Z$ and $s$. It is commutative and reduced, and its centralizer has smaller dimension than $B_Z$, because $s \notin Z$ and $s$ is semisimple.

**Proposition 7.2.4 (Localization).** Assume that every fibre of $p_Z$ is of the first kind above, or that every functional supported on that fibre and invariant under $G_Z$ kills the test functions supported in $\{v \neq 0\}$. Then every $\xi$ as in the definition of $(\mathrm{V}_Z)$ kills the test functions supported in $\{v \neq 0\} \cap \{x \notin Z_N\}$.


Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Final.locStep`.

**Proposition 7.2.5 (Descent).** In case 2 of the dichotomy, assume $(\mathrm{V}_{Z[s]})$. Then every $G_Z$-invariant functional supported on the fibre kills the test functions supported in $\{v \neq 0\}$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Final.descA`.

The descent comes from [Frobenius reciprocity](/entry/frobenius-reciprocity/): near the fibre, $B_Z \times k^n$ is fibred over the orbit $G_Z / G_{Z[s]}$, and the fibre over the base point is a neighbourhood of $s$ in $B_{Z[s]} \times k^n$.

### The nilpotent part

**Theorem 7.2.6 (Nilpotent step).** Let $k$ have characteristic zero and let $Z$ be commutative and reduced. Assume that every $\xi$ as in the definition of $(\mathrm{V}_Z)$ kills the test functions supported in $\{v \neq 0\} \cap \{x \notin Z_N\}$. Then $Z$ has the vanishing property $(\mathrm{V}_Z)$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Final.nilpB` (also `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Nil.Final.nilpB`).

The proof uses a Fourier transform and dilations. Let $q_Z(x) = (\mathrm{tr}(b_i x))_i$, and let $\pi_Z(x)$ be the element of $Z$ with $q_Z(\pi_Z(x)) = q_Z(x)$. Put $d = \dim B_Z - \dim Z$. For $t \in k^\times$ let
$$\sigma_t(x, v) = (\pi_Z(x) + t\,(x - \pi_Z(x)),\ v), \qquad (t_* \xi)(f) = \xi(f \circ \sigma_t).$$

**Proposition 7.2.7 (Fourier transform).** There is a linear map $\mathcal{F}$ on functionals with these properties.

1. $\mathcal{F}$ preserves support in $B_Z \times k^n$, support in each set $(B_Z \cap q_Z^{-1}(y)) \times k^n$, and invariance under $G_Z$.
2. For $\xi$ supported in $B_Z \times k^n$, $\xi$ kills the test functions supported in $\{v \neq 0\}$ if and only if $\mathcal{F}\xi$ does.
3. If $\xi$ kills all test functions, so does $\mathcal{F}\xi$.
4. For $t \neq 0$ and every test function $f$, $\mathcal{F}(t_* \xi)(f) = |t|^{-d}\, (t^{-1})_* (\mathcal{F}\xi)(f)$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Nil.Four.Final.fourP`.

$\mathcal{F}$ is the Fourier transform on $B_Z$ along the complement of $Z$, with respect to the trace form. Property 4 is the homogeneity of the Fourier transform on a space of dimension $d$.

**Proposition 7.2.8 (Eigenvalues).** Let $d > 0$ and $0 < |t| < 1$. For each $y$ there is a finite list of real numbers $a_1, \dots, a_m$, all with $2 a_i < d$, such that the following holds. If $\xi$ is supported in $(B_Z \cap q_Z^{-1}(y)) \times k^n$, is invariant under $G_Z$, and kills the test functions supported in $\{v \neq 0, x \notin Z_N\}$, then $\prod_i (t_* - |t|^{-a_i})\, \xi$ kills the test functions supported in $\{v \neq 0\}$.

Lean: `PositivityTheory.RgUd.T.Nil.Orb.Final.orbP`.

This is the statement that nilpotent orbital integrals have homogeneity degrees below half the dimension.

*Proof of the nilpotent step.* If $d = 0$, then $B_Z = Z$ and $\pi_Z$ is the identity on $B_Z$. This base case has its own short proof. Let $d > 0$ and fix $t \in k$ with $0 < |t| < 1$. It is enough to treat $\xi$ supported on one set $(B_Z \cap q_Z^{-1}(y)) \times k^n$, by a localization in $y$. Let $P(X) = \prod_i (X - |t|^{-a_i})$ be the polynomial of the eigenvalue proposition for $\xi$. By property 1, $\mathcal{F}\xi$ satisfies the same hypotheses, so there is a list $b_1, \dots, b_l$ with $2 b_j < d$ and $\prod_j (t_* - |t|^{-b_j})\, \mathcal{F}\xi$ kills $\{v \neq 0\}$. By property 4, $t_* \mathcal{F}\xi = |t|^{-d}\, \mathcal{F}((t^{-1})_* \xi)$. So $\mathcal{F}$ of $\prod_j (|t|^{-d} (t^{-1})_* - |t|^{-b_j})\, \xi$ kills $\{v \neq 0\}$, and by property 2 so does the functional itself. Apply $t_*^{\,l}$, which preserves the condition because $\sigma_t$ does not change $v$. Then $Q(t_*)\, \xi$ kills $\{v \neq 0\}$, where $Q(X) = \prod_j (|t|^{-d} - |t|^{-b_j} X)$. The roots of $Q$ are $|t|^{-(d - b_j)}$ with $d - b_j > d/2$. The roots of $P$ are $|t|^{-a_i}$ with $a_i < d/2$. Since $|t| < 1$ the two sets of roots are disjoint, so there are polynomials $U$ and $V$ with $U P + V Q = 1$. Hence $\xi = U(t_*) P(t_*) \xi + V(t_*) Q(t_*) \xi$ kills $\{v \neq 0\}$. $\square$

### The induction

*Proof of Theorem T in characteristic zero.* Use strong induction on $\dim_k B_Z$. Let $Z$ be commutative and reduced, and assume $(\mathrm{V}_{Z'})$ for every commutative reduced $Z'$ with $\dim B_{Z'} < \dim B_Z$. By the nilpotent step, it is enough to show that every $\xi$ as in the definition of $(\mathrm{V}_Z)$ kills the test functions supported in $\{v \neq 0\} \cap \{x \notin Z_N\}$. By the localization proposition, it is enough to treat each fibre of $p_Z$. If the fibre is of the first kind, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise the dichotomy gives a semisimple $s \notin Z$. The algebra $Z[s]$ is commutative and reduced with $\dim B_{Z[s]} < \dim B_Z$, so $(\mathrm{V}_{Z[s]})$ holds by induction. The descent proposition then gives the vanishing on the fibre. $\square$

The functional $\xi$ is defined on all functions on $W$, and every condition is imposed on test functions only. The localization step does not need characteristic zero; the nilpotent step does.

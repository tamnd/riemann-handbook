## A model from an induced function

The multiplicity argument of Volume 8 compares an abstract pair module with a concrete one. This section builds the concrete one. For a decreasing weight $\lambda$ and a block structure, we write down an explicit smooth function $F$ on $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$, equivariant on the left under a block parabolic, and we show that the space spanned by its derivatives is a pair module with a highest weight vector of weight $\mu_Q$, the expected infinitesimal characters, and all its $K$-weights in a cone above $\mu_Q$. This is the classical construction of a $K$-finite vector in a principal series representation, written out with explicit minors. The result is an input of [Theorem 7.1.12 of Volume 8](/entry/mult-g-of-mult-for/) in Volume 8, on the way to the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/). We use the notation of the two previous sections: pair modules, the operators $k_{ab}$, the weights $\rho$, $\rho^U$, $\mu_Q = 2\lambda + 2\rho^U$, and the highest weight spaces $V^{\mathrm{hw}}(\mu)$. For background see [highest weights and branching](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching).

### Models

**Definition 1.3.1 (Cone of a level function).** Let $\mathrm{lev}$ be a level function. A vector $Q \in \mathbb{R}^n$ lies in the *cone* of $\mathrm{lev}$ if $Q = \sum c_{ab}(e_a - e_b)$ with $c_{ab} \in \mathbb{N}$, and $c_{ab} \neq 0$ only if $\mathrm{lev}(a) < \mathrm{lev}(b)$.

**Definition 1.3.2 (Model).** Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ and let $\mathrm{lev}$ be a level function. A *model for $(\lambda, \mathrm{lev})$* is a complex inner product space $W$ with a pair module structure $F^1_{ab}, F^2_{ab}$ and a vector $f \in W$ such that:

1. $\langle k_{ab} x, y \rangle = \langle x, k_{ba} y \rangle$, where $k_{ab} = F^1_{ab} + F^2_{ab}$;
2. every vector lies in a finite-dimensional subspace stable under all $k_{ab}$;
3. the infinitesimal characters of the two copies are those of condition 3 of the definition of an admissible module, attached to $\lambda$;
4. $f \in W^{\mathrm{hw}}(\mu_Q)$ and $f \neq 0$;
5. if $x \neq 0$ lies in $W^{\mathrm{hw}}(\nu)$, then $\nu - \mu_Q$ lies in the cone of $\mathrm{lev}$.

Condition 5 says that $\mu_Q$ is the lowest $K$-type of $W$ in a strong sense: every other highest weight is obtained from it by adding roots of $\mathfrak{u}$.

### A criterion

The next lemma reduces the existence of a model to the existence of a generating vector and a linear form that behaves like evaluation at the identity. For a pair module, write $E'_{ab} = -F^2_{ba}$ as in the previous section; this family also satisfies the relations of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$.

**Lemma 1.3.3 (Model from a cyclic vector).** Let $(\lambda, \mathrm{lev})$ be adapted. Let $W$ be an inner product space with a pair module structure satisfying condition 1, let $f \in W$, and let $\epsilon : W \to \mathbb{C}$ be linear. Put $y_a = \lambda_a - (\mu_Q)_a$. Assume:

1. every $x \in W$ is $T f$ for some $T$ in the algebra generated by the $F^1_{ab}$ and $F^2_{ab}$;
2. $f$ lies in a finite-dimensional subspace stable under all $k_{ab}$, and $f \in W^{\mathrm{hw}}(\mu_Q)$, and $\epsilon(f) \neq 0$;
3. for all $v$: $\epsilon(F^1_{ab} v) = 0$ and $\epsilon(E'_{ab} v) = 0$ when $a \neq b$ and $\mathrm{lev}(b) \leq \mathrm{lev}(a)$; $\epsilon(F^1_{aa} v) = \lambda_a\, \epsilon(v)$; and $\epsilon(E'_{aa} v) = y_a\, \epsilon(v)$;
4. a finite-dimensional subspace stable under all $k_{ab}$ on which $\epsilon$ vanishes is $0$.

Then $(W, F^1, F^2, f)$ is a model for $(\lambda, \mathrm{lev})$.

*Proof.* Condition 2 of a model follows from hypotheses 1 and 2: the $k$-finite vectors form a stable subspace that contains $f$, and it is all of $W$ because $W$ is generated by $f$. Conditions 1 and 4 are hypotheses.

For condition 3, let $z$ be a Gelfand invariant $\sum_a (F^m)_{aa}$ of the first copy. It commutes with the first copy (it is central there) and with the second copy (the copies commute). Since $W$ is generated by $f$ and locally finite, and since hypothesis 4 separates vectors, $z$ acts by a scalar as soon as $\epsilon(z v) = c\, \epsilon(v)$ for all $v$. By hypothesis 3, $\epsilon$ sees $F^1$ as a module of lowest weight type with diagonal values $\lambda_a$, and the value of a Gelfand invariant on such a vector is the classical expression in the shifted parameter $\lambda_a + \frac{n-1}{2} - a$. For the second copy we use $E'$ and the values $y_a$. Since $(\lambda, \mathrm{lev})$ is adapted, there is a permutation $\sigma$ with $y_a + \frac{n-1}{2} - a = -\gamma_{\sigma(a)}$, where $\gamma_a = \lambda_a + \frac{n-1}{2} - a$; the Gelfand values are invariant under permutation of the parameter.

For condition 5, let $x \neq 0$ lie in $W^{\mathrm{hw}}(\nu)$. By hypothesis 3, $\epsilon$ kills $k_{ab} v$ for $a \neq b$ in the same block (since $k_{ab} = F^1_{ab} - E'_{ba}$), and $\epsilon(k_{aa} v) = (\mu_Q)_a\, \epsilon(v)$, since $(\mu_Q)_a = \lambda_a - y_a$. If $\epsilon$ vanished on the $k$-span of $x$, which is finite-dimensional, then $x = 0$ by hypothesis 4. So some $T x$ with $T$ in the algebra of the $k_{ab}$ has $\epsilon(T x) \neq 0$. Now we apply the cone statement for highest weights: if $\epsilon$ kills $k_{ab}$ inside each block, $\epsilon(k_{aa} v) = (\mu_Q)_a \epsilon(v)$, and $\epsilon(T x) \neq 0$ for some $T$ in the algebra of the $k_{ab}$, then $\nu - \mu_Q$ lies in the cone of $\mathrm{lev}$. $\square$

The three steps of this proof each use an input statement: a central element that commutes with the generators of a cyclic, locally finite module and has a constant value under $\epsilon$ acts by a scalar; the evaluation of the Gelfand invariants on a vector of lowest weight type; and the cone statement for highest weights. These are named in the Remark.

### The induced function

Let $M_n$ be the space of complex $n \times n$ matrices and $G = \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C}) \subset M_n$. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the space of functions on $M_n$ that are smooth on $G$ and zero outside $G$. For $X \in M_n$ let $D_X$ be the right derivative, $D_X h(g) = \frac{d}{dt} h(g \exp(tX))|_{t=0}$. Put
$$F^1_{ab} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(D_{E_{ab}} - i D_{i E_{ab}}\bigr), \qquad F^2_{ab} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(D_{E_{ba}} + i D_{i E_{ba}}\bigr).$$
These are the holomorphic and the antiholomorphic parts of the right regular action of $\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{C})$, the second written with transposed indices and a sign.

For $j = 0, \dots, n-1$ let $\Delta_j(g)$ be the leading principal minor of size $j+1$ and $\Gamma_j(g) = \det(g' g'^*)$, where $g'$ is the matrix of the first $j+1$ rows of $g$. For $v \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ put $\delta v_j = v_j - v_{j+1}$, with $v_n = 0$. Let $\mu^Z = 2\lambda + 2\rho^U \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ (so $\mu^Z = \mu_Q$) and $y = \lambda - \mu^Z$. Define
$$F(g) = \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} \Delta_j(g)^{\delta \mu^Z_j}\, \Gamma_j(g)^{\delta y_j} \quad (g \in G), \qquad F = 0 \text{ off } G .$$
Let $P$ be the group of invertible $p$ with $p_{ab} = 0$ whenever $\mathrm{lev}(a) < \mathrm{lev}(b)$. Since $\mathrm{lev}$ is monotone, $P$ is a block lower triangular parabolic subgroup. We will use that $F(p g) = F(p) F(g)$ for $p \in P$ and $g \in G$, so $F$ is a vector of the representation induced from the character $F|_P$ of $P$. The exponents $\delta\mu^Z_j$ and $\delta y_j$ vanish when $j$ and $j+1$ lie in the same block, since $\mu^Z$ and $y$ are constant on blocks.

**Theorem 1.3.4 (Existence of a model).** For every $n$, every decreasing $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ and every level function $\mathrm{lev}$ with $(\lambda, \mathrm{lev})$ adapted, there is a model for $(\lambda, \mathrm{lev})$.

*Proof.* Let $W \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ be the span of all $T F$, with $T$ in the algebra generated by the $F^1_{ab}$ and $F^2_{ab}$. We check the hypotheses of the criterion above with $f = F$ and $\epsilon(h) = h(1)$. We use the following facts about $F$ and $\mathcal{S}$, which are input statements of the proof.

1. $F$ is smooth on $G$, $F(p g) = F(p) F(g)$ for $p \in P$ and $g \in G$, and $F(1) = 1$.
2. Right derivatives preserve this left equivariance. So every element of $W$ satisfies $h(p g) = F(p) h(g)$.
3. There is a finite, right invariant measure $\kappa$ on $U(n)$ that is positive on nonempty open sets. For it, $\langle x, y \rangle = \int_{U(n)} \overline{x(k)}\, y(k)\, d\kappa(k)$ is a positive semidefinite hermitian form on $\mathcal{S}$, and $\langle x, x \rangle = 0$ implies that $x$ vanishes on $U(n)$.
4. Every $g \in G$ is $g = p k$ with $p \in P$ and $k \in U(n)$.
5. The right derivatives satisfy the relations of $\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{C})$ as a real Lie algebra, so $F^1$ and $F^2$ form a pair module on $\mathcal{S}$. The operators $k_{ab}$ are skew-adjoint up to the transpose for the form of fact 3, because the integral of a right derivative over $U(n)$ vanishes.
6. $F$ lies in a finite-dimensional subspace stable under the $k_{ab}$, and $F$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\mu^Z$ for them.
7. For $\mathrm{lev}(b) \leq \mathrm{lev}(a)$: $(F^1_{ab} F)(1) = \lambda_a \delta_{ab}$ and $(E'_{ab} F)(1) = y_a \delta_{ab}$.
8. A finite-dimensional space of functions in $W$, stable under the $k_{ab}$ and vanishing at $1$, vanishes on $U(n)$.

The form of fact 3 is definite on $W$: if $\langle w, w \rangle = 0$ then $w = 0$ on $U(n)$, and by facts 2 and 4, $w(p k) = F(p) w(k) = 0$ for every $g = p k \in G$; off $G$, $w = 0$ by definition. So $W$ is an inner product space. It is stable under $F^1$ and $F^2$ by construction, and the restricted operators form a pair module by fact 5. Condition 1 holds by fact 5, and hypothesis 1 of the criterion holds by the definition of $W$.

Hypothesis 2 is fact 6, together with $\epsilon(F) = F(1) = 1$. For hypothesis 3, let $\mathrm{lev}(b) \leq \mathrm{lev}(a)$. Then $E_{ab}$ and $i E_{ab}$ lie in the Lie algebra of $P$, and for $h \in W$ the equivariance of fact 2 gives $(D_X h)(1) = (D_X F)(1)\, h(1)$ for $X = E_{ab}$ and $X = i E_{ab}$. So $\epsilon(F^1_{ab} h) = (F^1_{ab} F)(1)\, \epsilon(h)$, and fact 7 gives the values. The same holds for $E'_{ab} = \tfrac{1}{2}(D_{E_{ab}} + i D_{i E_{ab}})$. Hypothesis 4 follows from fact 8 and the definiteness argument above. The criterion gives the model. $\square$

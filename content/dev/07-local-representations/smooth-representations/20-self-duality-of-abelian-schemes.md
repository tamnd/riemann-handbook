## Self-duality of abelian schemes

This section proves that an abelian scheme over an arbitrary commutative ring is its own dual when it carries a line bundle with trivial kernel, and that the Mumford bundle of that line bundle is the duality. It is the only result of the chapter that is not about representations. It belongs to the geometric side of the step [EveryCharacterOfLiterature](/step/every-character-of-literature/), where self-dual abelian schemes over rings that are not noetherian occur. The proof reduces to a noetherian base, proves there that a pairing with small kernels is a duality, and then returns by base change.

### Setting

Let $S$ be a scheme. All schemes over $S$ are taken in the category of $S$-schemes, with fibre products written $\times$. Let $A$ be an abelian scheme over $S$, with group law $m$, unit $e$ and structure map $\pi$, smooth of relative dimension $g$. For $S$-schemes $Y$ and $T$, a line bundle $M$ on $A \times T$, and points $a \in A(Y)$ and $t \in T(Y)$, write $M(a, t) = (a, t)^*M$, a line bundle on $Y$. A line bundle is *trivial* if it has a nowhere vanishing global section. For a group scheme $B$ over $S$ we write $0$ for its unit point and use the group law of $B(Y)$ multiplicatively.

**Definition 1.20.1 (Additive line bundle).** A line bundle $M$ on $A \times T$ is *additive* if for every $Y$, all $a, a' \in A(Y)$ and all $t \in T(Y)$ there is an isomorphism $M(aa', t) \cong M(a, t) \otimes M(a', t)$.

**Definition 1.20.2 (Duality).** Let $B$ be a group scheme over $S$ and $P$ a line bundle on $A \times B$. Then $P$ is a *duality between $A$ and $B$* if $P$ is additive, $P(\mathrm{id}_A, 0)$ is trivial on $A$, and for every $S$-scheme $T$ and every additive line bundle $M$ on $A \times T$ there is exactly one $x \in B(T)$ with $M \cong (\mathrm{id}_A \times x)^*P$.

The definition does not rigidify $M$ along the unit section, and uniqueness is asked for the point $x$, not for the isomorphism.

**Definition 1.20.3 (Trivial kernel).** For a line bundle $L$ on $A$, an $S$-scheme $T$ and $x \in A(T)$, the *translation difference* is the line bundle $D_x(L) = (m \circ (x \times \mathrm{id}_A))^*L \otimes p_2^*L^{-1}$ on $T \times A$. The line bundle $L$ has *trivial kernel* if the following holds for every ring $R'$, every morphism $\operatorname{Spec} R' \to S$ and every $x \in A(R')$. If every point of $\operatorname{Spec} R'$ has an open neighbourhood $V$ such that $D_x(L)$ is trivial on $V \times A$, then $x = 0$.

**Definition 1.20.4 (Mumford bundle).** For a line bundle $L$ on $A$, the *Mumford bundle* is
$$\Lambda(L) = m^*L \otimes p_1^*L^{-1} \otimes p_2^*L^{-1} \otimes (e \circ \pi)^*L$$
on $A \times A$. Equivalently, it is the alternating product of the pullbacks of $L$ along the partial sums $\sum_{i \in I} x_i$ for $I \subseteq \{1, 2\}$.

### The theorem

**Theorem 1.20.5 (Self-duality).** Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $A$ an abelian scheme over $\operatorname{Spec} R$, smooth of relative dimension $g$. Let $L$ be a line bundle on $A$ with trivial kernel. Then $\Lambda(L)$ is a duality between $A$ and $A$.

Lean: `CompanionTheory.PfDual.kTrivDual`.

The proof uses the following inputs.

**Theorem 1.20.6 (Inputs on abelian schemes).**
1. (First cohomology.) Let $k$ be a field and $A$ an abelian scheme over $k$ of dimension $g$. Then $H^1(A, \mathcal{O}_A)$ is finite-dimensional of dimension at most $g$.
2. (Trivial locus.) Let $Y$ be locally noetherian and $N$ a line bundle on $A \times Y$ with $N(0, \mathrm{id}_Y)$ trivial. There is a closed subscheme $Z \subseteq Y$ such that for every $y : T \to Y$, the bundle $N(p_1, y \circ p_2)$ on $A \times T$ is trivial if and only if $y$ factors through $Z$.
3. (Few equations.) Let $R$ be a noetherian local ring and $M$ a line bundle on $A$ over $\operatorname{Spec} R$. Let $I \neq R$ be an ideal such that, for every ideal $J$, the restriction of $M$ to $A \otimes R/J$ is trivial if and only if $I \subseteq J$. If item 1 holds, then $I$ is generated by $g$ elements.
4. (Flatness of complete intersections.) Let $R$ be noetherian, $C$ a standard smooth $R$-algebra of relative dimension $g$, and $f_1, \dots, f_g \in C$. If $C/(f_1, \dots, f_g)$ is quasi-finite over $R$, it is flat over $R$.
5. (Acyclicity.) Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $M$ a nontrivial additive line bundle on $A$ over $k$. The Čech complex of $M$ for an affine open cover is acyclic.
6. (Projectivity.) An abelian scheme over a field is projective.
7. (Base change.) Dualities are stable under base change along $\operatorname{Spec} R \to \operatorname{Spec} R_0$, and $\Lambda(L)$ commutes with base change.

Sources: Mumford, Abelian Varieties, Section 6 for item 6, Section 8 for item 5, Section 10 for item 2 and Section 13 for item 1; Matsumura, Commutative Ring Theory, Theorem 23.1, for item 4; for item 3 the tangent space argument of Mumford, Section 13. Item 7 follows from the definitions.

**Definition 1.20.7 (Pre-duality).** Let $B$ be a group scheme over $S$. A line bundle $P$ on $A \times B$ is a *pre-duality* if $P$ is additive in both variables, every $y \in B(T)$ with $P(p_1, y \circ p_2)$ trivial is $0$, and for every algebraically closed field $k$ and every $\operatorname{Spec} k \to S$, the set of $x \in A(k)$ with $P(x, \cdot)$ trivial is finite.

*Proof of the theorem.* Step 1: flat trivial loci. Let $S$ be locally noetherian, $Y$ smooth of relative dimension $g$ over $S$, $N$ as in item 2, and $Z$ its trivial locus. If $Z$ is locally quasi-finite over $S$, then $Z$ is flat over $S$. Indeed, locally $Z$ is cut out in $Y$ by an ideal that item 3 generates by $g$ elements, since item 1 holds. Item 4 then gives flatness.

Step 2: points of the dual. Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $A$ and $B$ abelian over $k$, and $P$ on $A \times B$ additive in both variables with finite left kernel. Then every additive line bundle $M$ on $A$ is $P(\cdot, y)$ for some $y \in B(k)$. Suppose not. Then for every $y$ the bundle $M \otimes P(\cdot, y)^{-1}$ is additive and nontrivial, so by items 5 and 6 its cohomology vanishes. A cohomology count on $A \times B$ with the finite left kernel then gives a contradiction.

Step 3: pre-dualities are dualities over a noetherian base. Let $R_0$ be noetherian and $P$ a pre-duality between abelian schemes $A_0$ and $B_0$ over $R_0$ of relative dimension $g$. Let $T$ be an $R_0$-scheme and $M$ additive on $A_0 \times T$. First let $T$ be the spectrum of a noetherian ring. The bundle $M \otimes P^{-1}$ on $A_0 \times T \times B_0$ has a trivial locus $Z \subseteq T \times B_0$ by item 2. The right kernel condition makes $Z \to T$ a monomorphism, and the finite left kernel makes it quasi-finite, so by Step 1 it is flat. By Step 2 it is onto on geometric points. So $Z \to T$ is an isomorphism, and its inverse composed with the projection to $B_0$ is the required unique point $x$. For general $T$ one passes to a limit over noetherian rings and glues by uniqueness.

Step 4: a noetherian model. By item 2 and the methods of limits of EGA IV_3, Section 8, there are a noetherian ring $R_0$, a map $R_0 \to R$, an abelian scheme $A_0$ over $R_0$ of relative dimension $g$, and a line bundle $L_0$ on $A_0$ with trivial kernel, such that $A$ is the base change of $A_0$ and $L$ is isomorphic to the pullback of $L_0$.

Step 5: conclusion. The bundle $\Lambda(L_0)$ is trivial on $A_0 \times 0$ and on $0 \times A_0$, and its left and right kernels are the kernel of $L_0$, so they are trivial. By the theorem of the cube, $\Lambda(L_0)$ is additive in both variables. So $\Lambda(L_0)$ is a pre-duality, hence a duality by Step 3. By item 7, its pullback, which is $\Lambda(L)$, is a duality between $A$ and $A$. $\square$

### Classical form

For an abelian variety $A$ over a field, a line bundle $L$ defines $\varphi_L : A \to \hat{A}$, $x \mapsto t_x^*L \otimes L^{-1}$, and $\Lambda(L) = (\mathrm{id} \times \varphi_L)^*\mathcal{P}$ for the Poincaré bundle $\mathcal{P}$. The kernel of $\varphi_L$ is the group scheme $K(L)$. The theorem says that $\varphi_L$ is an isomorphism when $K(L)$ is trivial, now over any base ring. Ampleness of $L$ is not assumed. Over a field, a trivial $K(L)$ makes $L$ nondegenerate, and the degree of $\varphi_L$ is the order of $K(L)$.

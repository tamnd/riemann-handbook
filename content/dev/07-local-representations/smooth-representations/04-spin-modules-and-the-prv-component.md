## Spin modules and the PRV component

Dirac operator methods for $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-modules tensor a module with a spin module and look for a specific $K$-type in the product. This section proves the one fact of this kind that the base change argument of Volume 8 needs: if a unitary $\mathfrak{gl}_n$-module $V$ has a $K$-type of highest weight $\nu$, then $V \otimes S$, with $S$ a spin module, contains a highest weight vector of weight $\nu - \rho$ moved to the dominant chamber. This is the case $\mu = \rho$ of the theorem of Parthasarathy, Ranga Rao and Varadarajan on the component of extreme weight in a tensor product. It is used in [Theorem 7.23.6 of Volume 8](/entry/core-g/) of Volume 8. For highest weights of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ see [highest weights and branching](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching).

### Highest weight vectors

Throughout, a *$\mathfrak{gl}_n$-module* is a complex vector space $V$ with operators $E_{ab}$ ($1 \leq a, b \leq n$) that satisfy $[E_{ab}, E_{cd}] = \delta_{bc} E_{ad} - \delta_{da} E_{cb}$. It is *unitary* if $V$ is an inner product space and $\langle E_{ab} x, y \rangle = \langle x, E_{ba} y \rangle$. A vector $v$ is a *highest weight vector of weight* $\nu \in \mathbb{R}^n$ if $E_{aa} v = \nu_a v$ for all $a$ and $E_{ab} v = 0$ for all $a < b$. We write $\rho_a = \frac{n-1}{2} - a$ for $a = 0, \dots, n-1$; this is half the sum of the positive roots.

The classical theorem is the following.

**Theorem 1.4.1 (Parthasarathy, Ranga Rao and Varadarajan).** Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and let $V_\lambda$, $V_\mu$ be irreducible finite-dimensional modules of highest weights $\lambda$ and $\mu$. Let $w_0$ be the longest element of the Weyl group. Then $V_\lambda \otimes V_\mu$ contains, with multiplicity one, the irreducible module whose highest weight is the dominant conjugate of $\lambda + w_0 \mu$.

This is in K. R. Parthasarathy, R. Ranga Rao and V. S. Varadarajan, *Representations of complex semi-simple Lie groups and Lie algebras*, Ann. of Math. 85 (1967). The result below is the existence part, for $\mathfrak{gl}_n$, with $V_\mu$ the spin module and $V_\lambda$ replaced by any unitary module that contains a finite-dimensional stable subspace with a highest weight vector.

### The spin module

**Definition 1.4.2 (Clifford family).** Let $\iota$ be a finite set. A *Clifford family* is a family of matrices $c_{ab} \in M_\iota(\mathbb{C})$, $1 \leq a, b \leq n$, such that
$$c_{ab} c_{de} + c_{de} c_{ab} = 2\, \delta_{ae} \delta_{bd}, \qquad c_{ab}^* = c_{ba}.$$

The first condition says that $X_{ab} \mapsto c_{ab}$ is a representation of the Clifford algebra of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ with the trace form $\mathrm{tr}(XY)$, since $\mathrm{tr}(E_{ab} E_{de}) = \delta_{ae}\delta_{bd}$. The spin representation of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ is the composite of the adjoint map $\mathfrak{gl}_n \to \mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{gl}_n)$ with the quadratic elements of the Clifford algebra.

**Definition 1.4.3 (Spin operators).** For a Clifford family $c$ put
$$s_{ab} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{e} c_{ae} c_{eb} - \frac{n}{2}\, \delta_{ab}.$$

We use three facts about these operators, which we take as given.

1. For every $n$ there is a Clifford family $c$ and a vector $q \neq 0$ with $c_{ba} q = 0$ for all $a < b$.
2. If $c$ is a Clifford family, then the $s_{ab}$ satisfy the relations of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$, and $s_{ab}^* = s_{ba}$. So $\mathbb{C}^\iota$ is a unitary $\mathfrak{gl}_n$-module, the *spin module*.
3. If $c_{ba} q = 0$ for all $a < b$, then $s_{aa} q = -\rho_a q$ and $s_{ba} q = 0$ for all $a < b$. So $q$ is a lowest weight vector of weight $-\rho$.

The span of the $s_{ab}$-translates of $q$ is then the irreducible module with lowest weight $-\rho$, so with highest weight $-w_0\rho = \rho$. Its relations have a simple form, and this is the content of the next input.

**Theorem 1.4.4 (Presentation of the module of highest weight $\rho$).** Let $A$ and $Z$ be $\mathfrak{gl}_n$-modules, with operators $G_{ab}$ and $F_{ab}$. Let $t \in A$, $t \neq 0$, be a highest weight vector of weight $\rho$. Let $z \in Z$ satisfy $F_{i+1, i}^2 z = 0$ for all $i$. If a linear combination $\sum_l c_l\, G_{l} t$ of products $G_l$ of lowering operators $G_{ab}$, $a > b$, is zero, then $\sum_l c_l\, F_l z = 0$.

This says that the module generated by a highest weight vector of weight $\rho$ is the quotient of the Verma module by the relations $f_i^{\langle \rho, \alpha_i^\vee \rangle + 1} = f_i^2$, and that no other relation is needed. We take it as given.

### A map out of the spin module

**Theorem 1.4.5 (Spin map).** Let $c$ be a Clifford family and $q \neq 0$ a vector with $s_{aa} q = -\rho_a q$ and $s_{ba} q = 0$ for $a < b$. Let $Z$ be a $\mathfrak{gl}_n$-module, let $\nu \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and let $z \in Z$ satisfy
$$E_{aa} z = (\nu_a - \rho_a) z \quad (\text{all } a), \qquad E_{i, i+1}^2 z = 0 \quad (0 \leq i < n - 1).$$
Then there is a linear map $\varphi : \mathbb{C}^\iota \to Z$ with $\varphi(q) = z$ such that
$$\varphi(s_{ab} f) = E_{ab}\varphi(f) \quad (a < b), \qquad \varphi(s_{aa} f) = (E_{aa} - \nu_a)\varphi(f)$$
for all $f \in \mathbb{C}^\iota$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.RealCount.SpinMap.exists_spin_map` (also `GlobalTheory.T.spin_tensor_highest_weight`, the same statement).

*Proof.* Twist both modules by $X_{ab} \mapsto -X_{ba}$. This exchanges raising and lowering operators and changes the sign of weights, and it is again a $\mathfrak{gl}_n$-module. After the twist, $q$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\rho$ (fact 3), and $z$ satisfies the hypothesis of the presentation theorem, because the twisted $F_{i+1, i}$ is $-E_{i, i+1}$.

Let $R \subseteq \mathbb{C}^\iota$ be the span of the vectors $s_{l} q$, where $s_l$ runs over products of operators $s_{ab}$ with $a < b$. The presentation theorem says exactly that $s_l q \mapsto E_l z$ is a well defined linear map $\varphi_R : R \to Z$. Extend it by zero on the orthogonal complement $R^\perp$.

The space $R$ is stable under all $s_{ab}$, not only the raising ones. For the diagonal operators this holds because $s_l q$ is a weight vector; for the lowering operators one moves $s_{ba}$ to the right through $s_l$ with the commutation relations, and uses $s_{ba} q = 0$. Since $s_{ab}^* = s_{ba}$ (fact 2), $R^\perp$ is also stable under all $s_{ab}$. Hence it suffices to check the two identities on $R$ and on $R^\perp$. On $R^\perp$ both sides vanish. On $R$, for $a < b$, $\varphi(s_{ab} s_l q) = E_{ab} E_l z$ by definition. For the diagonal, $s_l q$ has weight $-\rho + \beta$ and $E_l z$ has weight $\nu - \rho + \beta$ for the same sum of roots $\beta$, so $\varphi(s_{aa} s_l q) = (-\rho_a + \beta_a) E_l z = (E_{aa} - \nu_a) E_l z$. Finally $\varphi(q) = z$ is the case of the empty product. $\square$

Only the condition $E_{i, i+1}^2 z = 0$ and the diagonal action on $z$ enter. In particular $z$ need not be a highest weight vector.

### The PRV component

**Theorem 1.4.6 (PRV component in the spin tensor product).** For every $n$ there are a finite set $\iota$ and a Clifford family $c$ on $\mathbb{C}^\iota$ with the following property. Let $V$ be a unitary $\mathfrak{gl}_n$-module with operators $K_{ab}$, let $S \subseteq V$ be a finite-dimensional subspace stable under all $K_{ab}$, and let $v \in S$, $v \neq 0$, be a highest weight vector of weight $\nu \in \mathbb{Z}^n$. Then there is a permutation $w$ of $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ such that $a \mapsto \nu_{w(a)} - \rho_{w(a)}$ is decreasing, and a nonzero $x \in V^\iota = V \otimes \mathbb{C}^\iota$ that is a highest weight vector of weight $(\nu - \rho) \circ w$ for the operators
$$Y_{ab} = K_{ab} \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes s_{ab}.$$

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.SpinPRV.spin_parthasarathy_rao_varadarajan` (also `GlobalTheory.T.parthasarathy_rao_varadarajan`, the same statement).

*Proof.* Take $c$ and $q$ from fact 1. By fact 3, $q$ is a lowest weight vector of weight $-\rho$. The theorem is then the following lemma, applied to $c$ and $q$. $\square$

**Lemma 1.4.7 (Assembly).** Let $c$ be a Clifford family and $q \neq 0$ a vector with $s_{aa} q = -\rho_a q$ and $s_{ba} q = 0$ for $a < b$. Let $V$, $S$, $v$ and $\nu$ be as in the theorem. Then the conclusion of the theorem holds for $c$.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.SpinPRV.assemble`.

We use three more inputs. (a) A nonzero highest weight vector in a finite-dimensional stable subspace has a decreasing weight. (b) For every decreasing $\kappa \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with $\kappa_a - s \in \mathbb{Z}$ for some fixed $s$, and every permutation $\sigma$, there is a finite-dimensional unitary irreducible module $Z$ with a highest weight vector $u \neq 0$ of weight $\kappa$ and a vector $a_0 \neq 0$ that is extremal for $\sigma$: $E_{\sigma(x)\sigma(x)} a_0 = \kappa_x a_0$, and $E_{\sigma(x)\sigma(y)} a_0 = 0$ for $x < y$. (c) In a unitary module, if $a_0 \neq 0$ is a weight vector for $E_{ii}$ and $E_{jj}$ with eigenvalues $p, r$, with $p - r \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $p - r \geq -1$, and $E_{ij} a_0 = 0$ or $E_{ji} a_0 = 0$, then $E_{ij}^2 a_0 = 0$. (d) Let $T$ be a finite-dimensional unitary module with a nonzero highest weight vector $t$ of weight $\nu$, and $V$, $S$, $v$ as above with the same weight. Then there is a linear map $\psi : T \to V$ with $\psi(t) = v$ that intertwines all $E_{ab}$. We take (b), (c) and (d) as given.

*Proof.* By (a), $\nu$ is decreasing. Choose $\sigma$ that sorts $\nu - \rho$ in decreasing order and put $\kappa = (\nu - \rho) \circ \sigma$. The entries of $\kappa$ differ from $-\frac{n-1}{2}$ by integers. Take $Z$, $u$ and $a_0$ from (b) for $\kappa$ and $\sigma$. Then $E_{yy} a_0 = (\nu_y - \rho_y) a_0$ for all $y$.

For $i$ and $j = i+1$, the eigenvalues of $E_{ii}$ and $E_{jj}$ on $a_0$ differ by $p - r = \nu_i - \nu_{i+1} - 1 \geq -1$, an integer. Since $a_0$ is extremal for $\sigma$, either $E_{ij} a_0 = 0$ or $E_{ji} a_0 = 0$, depending on the order of $\sigma^{-1}(i)$ and $\sigma^{-1}(j)$. By (c), $E_{i, i+1}^2 a_0 = 0$. So the Spin map theorem applies to $z = a_0$ and gives $\varphi : \mathbb{C}^\iota \to Z$ with $\varphi(q) = a_0$.

Let $T = Z \otimes (\mathbb{C}^\iota)^*$, a unitary module for $E_{ab} \otimes 1 - 1 \otimes s_{ab}^{\mathsf{T}}$, the tensor product with the dual of the spin module. The map $\varphi$ is an element $t = \sum_i \varphi(e_i) \otimes e_i^*$ of $T$. For $a < b$ the action of the $(a,b)$ operator on $t$ is $\sum_i (E_{ab}\varphi(e_i) - \varphi(s_{ab} e_i)) \otimes e_i^*$, which is $0$ by the Spin map theorem. For $a = b$ it is $\sum_i \nu_a \varphi(e_i) \otimes e_i^* = \nu_a t$. So $t$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\nu$, and $t \neq 0$ because $\varphi(q) = a_0 \neq 0$. By (d) there is an intertwiner $\psi : T \to V$ with $\psi(t) = v$.

Put $x_i = \psi(u \otimes e_i^*)$ and $x = (x_i)_{i \in \iota} \in V^\iota$. Since $\psi$ intertwines, $\psi(E_{ab} z \otimes e_i^*) = K_{ab}\psi(z \otimes e_i^*) + \sum_j (s_{ab})_{ij}\, \psi(z \otimes e_j^*)$ for all $z \in Z$. Applied to $z = u$, this says $Y_{ab} x = (\psi(E_{ab} u \otimes e_i^*))_i$. Hence $x$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\kappa$ for the $Y_{ab}$, because $u$ is one for the $E_{ab}$.

It remains to see $x \neq 0$. Suppose $x = 0$. Let $W = \{z \in Z : \psi(z \otimes e_i^*) = 0 \text{ for all } i\}$. By the identity above, $W$ is stable under all $E_{ab}$, and $u \in W$. Since $Z$ is irreducible, $W = Z$. Then $v = \psi(t) = \sum_i \psi(\varphi(e_i) \otimes e_i^*) = 0$, a contradiction. $\square$

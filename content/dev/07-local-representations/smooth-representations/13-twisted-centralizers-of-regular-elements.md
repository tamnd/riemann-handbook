## Twisted centralizers of regular elements

This section studies the twisted centralizer of a regular element in $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over a CM field. It proves that this centralizer is commutative and that it has a homomorphism to $\mathbb{R}^m$ which records how it moves the degrees of the isotropic stable subspaces. The kernel of this map is compact modulo rational points. The result is global and adelic. It is the torus input to [Theorem 7.15 of Volume 8](/entry/class-vanish/), where the truncated twisted trace is computed one twisted conjugacy class at a time. Classically the twisted centralizer is the group of adelic points of a torus $T_d$, and the map is the Harish-Chandra map $H_{T_d}$ of Arthur's truncation. We give a proof by linear algebra in the commutative algebra generated by the norm of $d$.

### The setting

Let $E$ be a CM field with complex conjugation $x \mapsto \bar{x}$, and let $\mathbb{A}$ be its adele ring. Conjugation acts on $\mathbb{A}$ place by place. On $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ and on $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$ let
$$\theta(g) = {}^t\bar{g}^{-1}.$$
This is an automorphism of order two. We view $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ as a subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$.

**Definition 1.13.1 (Norm, twisted centralizer).** For $d \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ the *norm* of $d$ is $N(d) = d\,\theta(d)$. We call $d$ *regular* if the characteristic polynomial of $N(d)$ is separable. The *twisted centralizer* of $d$ is
$$G_d(\mathbb{A}) = \{t \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}) : d\,\theta(t) = t\,d\},$$
and $G_d(E) = G_d(\mathbb{A}) \cap \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$.

For $t \in G_d(\mathbb{A})$ we have $t\,N(d) = d\,\theta(t)\,\theta(d) = d\,\theta(t d) = d\,\theta(d\,\theta(t)) = N(d)\,t$. So $t$ commutes with $N(d)$.

**Definition 1.13.2 (Isotropic stable subspace).** For a subspace $W \subseteq E^n$ let $W^\perp = \{y : \sum_i \bar{x}_i y_i = 0 \text{ for all } x \in W\}$. A subspace $W$ is *isotropic stable* for $d$ if $N(d) W = W$ and $W \subseteq d\,W^\perp$.

**Definition 1.13.3 (Degree).** Let $x \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$ and let $M$ be an $n \times k$ matrix over $E$. Put $F = x^{-1} M$, a matrix over $\mathbb{A}$. The *degree* of $M$ at $x$ is
$$\deg_x M = -\sum_{v \mid \infty} \log \det\big(F_v^{*} F_v\big) - \sum_{w < \infty} \log \max_{s} \big|\det F_{w,s}\big|_w,$$
where $s$ runs over the ways to choose $k$ rows, and $F_{w,s}$ is the $k \times k$ minor. For a subspace $W$ we put $\deg_x W = \deg_x M_W$, with $M_W$ the matrix of a fixed basis of $W$. For $u \in \mathbb{A}$ let $|u| = \prod_{v \mid \infty} |u_v|^2 \prod_{w < \infty} |u_w|_w$.

The degree of $W$ at $x$ is the arithmetic degree of the adelic vector bundle $W$ with the metrics given by $x$. It is the function behind the slopes of Stuhler and Grayson.

**Definition 1.13.4 (Sign vectors).** Let $\Sigma_m = \{-1, 0, 1\}^m$. For $e, e' \in \Sigma_m$ and $a \in \mathbb{R}^m$ put $e \cdot a = \sum_i e_i a_i$. Write $e \preceq e'$ if $e'_i = e_i$ whenever $e_i \neq 0$. Let $e \wedge e'$ have entry $e_i$ where $e_i = e'_i$, and $0$ elsewhere. Let $\delta_j \in \Sigma_m$ have entry $1$ in place $j$ and $0$ elsewhere.

For an idempotent matrix $\pi$ over $E$ and $t \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$ put
$$\chi_\pi(t) = \det\big(1 + \pi(t - 1)\big) \in \mathbb{A}, \qquad \lambda_\pi(t) = \log |\chi_\pi(t)|.$$
If $t$ commutes with $\pi$, then $1 + \pi(t-1)$ acts as $t$ on the image of $\pi$ and as the identity on its kernel. So $\chi_\pi(t)$ is the determinant of $t$ on the image of $\pi$. For $X$ a matrix over $E$ let $X^* = d\,{}^t\bar{X}\,d^{-1}$.

### Inputs

**Theorem 1.13.5 (Adelic degree formula).** Let $x, t \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$, $s \in \mathrm{GL}_k(\mathbb{A})$, and let $M$ be an $n \times k$ matrix over $E$ with linearly independent columns. If $t M = M s$, then
$$\deg_{tx} M = \deg_x M + \log |\det s|.$$
This is the rule for the degree of an adelic vector bundle under a change of metric. See Stuhler (1976) and Grayson (1984), Section 1.

**Theorem 1.13.6 (Inputs on regular matrices).** Let $K$ be a field, let $\gamma \in M_n(K)$, and let $d \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$.

1. If the characteristic polynomial of $\gamma$ is separable, there is $v \in K^n$ such that every $y \in K^n$ is $h(\gamma) v$ for some polynomial $h$.
2. If $\gamma$ has such a cyclic vector, $R$ is a commutative $K$-algebra, and $X, Y \in M_n(R)$ both commute with $\gamma$, then $XY = YX$.
3. If the characteristic polynomial of $\gamma$ is separable and $\gamma W \subseteq W$, then $W$ is the image of an idempotent which is a polynomial in $\gamma$.
4. If the characteristic polynomial of $\gamma$ is separable, there is a finite set $S$ of primitive idempotents of $K[\gamma]$ such that for every nonzero row vector $x$ some $e \in S$ has $x e \neq 0$.
5. Every $t \in G_d(\mathbb{A})$ commutes with every polynomial in $N(d)$.
6. The map $X \mapsto X^*$ is an involutive ring automorphism of $E[N(d)]$.
7. For a matrix $\pi$ over $E$, the image of $\pi$ is contained in $d$ times its own orthogonal if and only if $\pi^* \pi = 0$.
8. Let $R$ be a commutative ring with an involutive automorphism $\sigma$. Let $P \colon \Sigma_m \to R$ be injective, with idempotent values, $P(e)\sigma(P(e)) = 0$, $P(0) = 0$, $P(e \wedge e') = P(e)P(e')$, and $P(e)P(e') = P(e)$ when $e \preceq e'$. Assume every idempotent $p$ with $p\,\sigma(p) = 0$ is some $P(e)$. Then $P(e) = \sum_j P(e_j \delta_j)$ for all $e$, and $P(-\delta_j) = \sigma(P(\delta_j))$ for all $j$.
9. If $\pi$ is an idempotent in $E[N(d)]$ and $t \in G_d(\mathbb{A})$, then $\lambda_{\pi^*}(t) = -\lambda_\pi(t)$.
10. Assume the adelic degree formula. If $\pi$ is an idempotent over $E$ and $t$ commutes with $\pi$, then $\deg_{tx}(\operatorname{im}\pi) = \deg_x(\operatorname{im}\pi) + \lambda_\pi(t)$.
11. The function $|\cdot|$ on ideles is the idele norm. For an idempotent $\pi$, the function $\lambda_\pi$ is continuous on every subgroup of elements which commute with $\pi$.
12. Let $e$ be a primitive idempotent of $E[\gamma]$ with $\gamma$ separable, and let $c > 0$. There is $\varepsilon > 0$ such that for every $g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$ commuting with $\gamma$ with $|\chi_e(g)| \geq c$, and every $x \in E^n$ with $x e \neq 0$, we have $\|x g\|_{\mathbb{A}} \geq \varepsilon$.
13. (Mahler's criterion.) For $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a compact $C \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$ such that every $g$ with $|\det g| = 1$ and $\|x g\|_{\mathbb{A}} \geq \varepsilon$ for all nonzero $x \in E^n$ lies in $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)\,C$.
14. For every compact $C$ there is a compact $C'$ such that $G_d(\mathbb{A}) \cap \mathrm{GL}_n(E)\,C \subseteq G_d(E)\,C'$.

Items 1 to 4 are linear algebra over a field; see Lang, *Algebra*, Chapter XIV, Section 2. Items 5 to 11 are direct computations. Item 12 is a height bound of the kind used in reduction theory, item 13 is Mahler's criterion in the adelic form of Godement (1963), and item 14 is the closedness of $G_d(E)\backslash G_d(\mathbb{A})$ in $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$; see Borel and Harish-Chandra (1962), Section 5.

### The theorem

**Theorem 1.13.7 (Characters of twisted centralizers).** Assume the adelic degree formula. Let $d \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ be regular. Let $m \geq 0$ and let $W_e \subseteq E^n$, for $e \in \Sigma_m$, be subspaces with these properties:

1. every $W_e$ is isotropic stable for $d$;
2. every isotropic stable subspace is $W_e$ for exactly one $e$;
3. $W_0 = 0$;
4. $W_{e \wedge e'} = W_e \cap W_{e'}$ for all $e, e'$;
5. $W_e \subseteq W_{e'}$ when $e \preceq e'$.

Then $G_d(\mathbb{A})$ is commutative. There is a map $a \colon \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}) \to \mathbb{R}^m$ such that:

1. $\deg_{tx} W_e = \deg_x W_e + e \cdot a(t)$ for $t \in G_d(\mathbb{A})$, all $x \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$ and all $e$;
2. $a(tt') = a(t) + a(t')$ for $t, t' \in G_d(\mathbb{A})$;
3. $a(t) = 0$ for $t \in G_d(E)$;
4. $a$ is continuous on $G_d(\mathbb{A})$;
5. for every $M \in \mathbb{R}$ there is a compact $C \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A})$ such that every $t \in G_d(\mathbb{A})$ with $|a_i(t)| \leq M$ for all $i$ lies in $G_d(E)\,C$.

Lean: `torusChar`.

The hypotheses on the $W_e$ have a simple meaning. The algebra $E[N(d)]$ is a product of fields, and $X \mapsto X^*$ permutes the factors. Let $m$ be the number of pairs of factors exchanged by the involution. An isotropic stable subspace takes, from each such pair, the first factor, the second factor, or neither, and it takes nothing from a fixed factor. This choice is the sign vector $e$.

*Proof.* Put $\gamma = N(d)$ and $A = E[\gamma]$. The ring $A$ is commutative.

Step 1: commutativity. By input 5 every $t \in G_d(\mathbb{A})$ commutes with $\gamma$. By input 1, $\gamma$ has a cyclic vector. By input 2 with $R = \mathbb{A}$, any two elements of $G_d(\mathbb{A})$ commute.

Step 2: the idempotents. By input 3, for each $e$ there is an idempotent $\pi_e \in A$ with image $W_e$. As $W_e$ is isotropic stable, input 7 gives $\pi_e^* \pi_e = 0$. We check the hypotheses of input 8 for $P(e) = \pi_e$ and $\sigma(X) = X^*$, which is an involution of $A$ by input 6. Let $p \in A$ be idempotent with $p\,p^* = 0$. Its image is stable under $\gamma$, since $p$ commutes with $\gamma$, and it is isotropic by input 7. By hypothesis 2 the image is some $W_e$. Two idempotents of the commutative ring $A$ with the same image are equal, so $p = \pi_e$. If $\pi_e = \pi_{e'}$ then $W_e = W_{e'}$, and hypothesis 2 gives $e = e'$. Hypothesis 3 gives $\pi_0 = 0$. The product $\pi_e \pi_{e'}$ is an idempotent of $A$ with image $W_e \cap W_{e'} = W_{e \wedge e'}$, so it equals $\pi_{e \wedge e'}$. If $e \preceq e'$ then $W_e \subseteq W_{e'}$, so $\pi_e \pi_{e'} = \pi_e$. Input 8 now gives
$$\pi_e = \sum_j \pi_{e_j \delta_j}, \qquad \pi_{-\delta_j} = \pi_{\delta_j}^*.$$

Step 3: the map $a$. Put $a_j(t) = \lambda_{\pi_{\delta_j}}(t)$. Let $t \in G_d(\mathbb{A})$. Then $t$ commutes with every $\pi_e$. For $j \neq k$ we have $e_j\delta_j \wedge e_k\delta_k = 0$, so the idempotents $\pi_{e_j \delta_j}$ are pairwise orthogonal. For orthogonal idempotents that commute with $t$, the determinant on the sum of the images is the product of the determinants. So
$$\lambda_{\pi_e}(t) = \sum_j \lambda_{\pi_{e_j \delta_j}}(t).$$
The term for $e_j = 0$ is $\lambda_0(t) = 0$. The term for $e_j = 1$ is $a_j(t)$. The term for $e_j = -1$ is $\lambda_{\pi_{\delta_j}^*}(t) = -a_j(t)$ by input 9. So $\lambda_{\pi_e}(t) = e \cdot a(t)$. Claim 1 now follows from input 10 with $\pi = \pi_e$. For claim 2, $\chi_\pi(tt') = \chi_\pi(t)\chi_\pi(t')$ when $t$ and $t'$ commute with $\pi$, since both sides are determinants on the image of $\pi$. For claim 3, if $t \in G_d(E)$ then $\chi_\pi(t)$ is a principal idele, and the product formula gives $|\chi_\pi(t)| = 1$. Claim 4 is input 11.

Step 4: compactness. Fix $M$ and let $S$ be as in input 4. Let $e \in S$ and $t \in G_d(\mathbb{A})$ with $|a_i(t)| \leq M$ for all $i$. The idempotent $e^*$ is primitive, so either $e\,e^* = 0$ or $e^* = e$. In the first case $e = \pi_s$ for some $s$ by Step 2, and $|\lambda_e(t)| = |s \cdot a(t)| \leq m|M|$. In the second case input 9 gives $\lambda_e(t) = -\lambda_e(t)$, so $\lambda_e(t) = 0$. In both cases $|\chi_e(t)| \geq \exp(-m|M|)$. Input 12 gives $\varepsilon_e > 0$, and we let $\varepsilon$ be the least of the $\varepsilon_e$ for $e \in S$. Input 9 with $\pi = 1$, for which $1^* = 1$, gives $\log|\det t| = 0$. Now let $x \in E^n$ be nonzero. Choose $e \in S$ with $x e \neq 0$. Then $\|x t\|_{\mathbb{A}} \geq \varepsilon_e \geq \varepsilon$. Input 13 gives a compact $C$ with $t \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)\,C$, and $C$ does not depend on $t$. Input 14 gives a compact $C'$ with $t \in G_d(E)\,C'$. $\square$

Claim 5 says that $G_d(E)\backslash G_d(\mathbb{A})^1$ is compact, where $G_d(\mathbb{A})^1$ is the kernel of $a$. This is the theorem of Borel and Harish-Chandra for the torus $T_d$. Claim 1 says that $a$ is the Harish-Chandra map of $T_d$ in the coordinates given by the isotropic stable subspaces. In [Theorem 7.15 of Volume 8](/entry/class-vanish/) the orbital term of the truncated twisted trace is integrated first over $G_d(E)\backslash G_d(\mathbb{A})$, and claims 1 and 5 turn the truncation into a function of $a(t)$.

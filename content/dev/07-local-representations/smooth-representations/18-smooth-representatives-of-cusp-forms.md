## Smooth representatives of cusp forms

An element of $L^2$ is a class of functions, not a function. This section proves that a $K$-finite vector in an irreducible cuspidal representation has a representative that is a smooth function, invariant on the left under $\mathrm{GL}_a(E)$, $K_\infty$-finite on the right, and finite under the centre of the enveloping algebra. In other words, it is an automorphic form in the sense of Harish-Chandra. The tool is convolution with a test function that is injective on a finite-dimensional space of $K$-types, which Section 17 provides. The result turns cuspidal $L^2$ data into the cusp forms used in the base change argument of the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/). For background see [representations of real groups](/learn/representations/#representations-of-real-groups).

### Setting

We keep the notation of Section 17: $E$ totally complex with $r$ complex places, $G = G_\infty \times G_f$ with $G = \mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A})$, $K_\infty = \mathrm{U}(a)^r$, $z(t)$, $\Gamma = \mathrm{GL}_a(E)\cdot z(1)^{\mathbb{Z}}$, $\mu$, $L^2 = L^2(\Gamma\backslash G)$, $R$ and $L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}$. For $F \in L^2$ let $\tilde F$ be its lift, a function on $G$ that is left $\Gamma$-invariant and defined up to $\mu$-null sets. Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathrm{M}_a(E_\infty)$, viewed as a real vector space.

**Definition 1.18.1 (Smooth function, Lie derivative).** A function $\varphi$ on $G$ is *smooth* if for every $g \in G$ the function $x \mapsto \varphi(gx)$ is $C^\infty$ on the open set $G_\infty \subseteq \mathfrak{g}$. For $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ the *Lie derivative* $D_X\varphi(g)$ is the derivative at $x = 1$ of $x \mapsto \varphi(gx)$ in the direction $X$.

**Definition 1.18.2 (Centre).** For each embedding $\tau : E \to \mathbb{C}$ and $1 \leq i, j \leq a$ let $E^\tau_{ij}$ be the complex combination of Lie derivatives that acts as the elementary matrix at the place of $\tau$. For $u \in \mathbb{C}$ let $C_\tau(u)$ be the Capelli operator: the quantum determinant of the matrix $(E^\tau_{ij})$, with spectral parameter $u + (a-1)/2$. Let $\mathcal{Z}$ be the algebra of operators on smooth functions generated by all $C_\tau(u)$.

The operators $C_\tau(u)$ commute with all $E^\tau_{ij}$, and their coefficients generate the centre of the enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak{gl}_a(\mathbb{C})$ at the place of $\tau$. This is the theorem of Capelli; see Howe and Umeda (1991).

**Definition 1.18.3 (K-finite element).** An element $F \in L^2$ is *$K$-finite* if its stabilizer in $G_f$ is open and the translates $R(k)F$, $k \in K_\infty$, span a finite-dimensional space.

**Definition 1.18.4 (Test function).** A *test function* is a $C^\infty$ function $\beta$ on $\mathfrak{g}$ with compact support inside $G_\infty$. It is *conjugation invariant* if $\beta(kxk^{-1}) = \beta(x)$ for $k \in K_\infty$. For a compact open subgroup $U \subseteq G_f$ put $f_{U,\beta}(y) = \beta(y_\infty^{-1})\mathbf{1}_U(y_f^{-1})$ and, for a locally integrable $\Phi$ on $G$,
$$(\Phi * f_{U,\beta})(x) = \int_G f_{U,\beta}(y)\,\Phi(xy)\,d\mu(y).$$
On $L^2$ the same integral defines the operator $R(f_{U,\beta})$, and the lift of $R(f_{U,\beta})F$ is $\tilde F * f_{U,\beta}$ almost everywhere.

### Inputs

**Theorem 1.18.5 (Inputs on convolution).** Let $H \subseteq L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ be closed, $G$-stable and irreducible, and let $U \subseteq G_f$ be a compact open subgroup.

1. For each $K$-finite $F \in H$ there are a compact open subgroup $U_F$ and a finite-dimensional subspace $X \subseteq H$ with $F \in X$, $X$ stable under $R(K_\infty)$ and fixed by $R(U_F)$, such that $B(X) \subseteq X$ for every linear $B$ on $L^2$ that commutes with $R(K_\infty)$ and maps $H^{U_F}$ into $H^{U_F}$.
2. For every finite-dimensional $X \subseteq L^2$ fixed by $R(U)$ there is a conjugation invariant test function $\beta$ such that $R(f_{U,\beta})$ is injective on $X$.
3. For a test function $\beta$ and a locally integrable $\Phi$, $\Phi * f_{U,\beta}$ is smooth, and $D_X(\Phi * f_{U,\beta}) = \Phi * f_{U,\beta_X}$, where $\beta_X(w) = d\beta_w(wX)$ is again a test function. The function $f_{U,\beta}$ is continuous with compact support.
4. $\Phi * f_{U,\beta}$ is right invariant under $U$.
5. If $\beta$ is conjugation invariant, then $(\widetilde{R(k)F} * f_{U,\beta})(x) = (\tilde F * f_{U,\beta})(xk)$ for $k \in K_\infty$.
6. For $F \in L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ and every $f$, $\tilde F * f$ is left invariant under all $z(t)$, $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
7. Every $z \in \mathcal{Z}$ commutes with all $D_X$. Right translation by $g \in G_\infty$ carries $D_X$ to $D_{gXg^{-1}}$. Every $k \in K_\infty$ is $c(1)$ for a smooth path $c$ with $c(0) = 1$ and $c'(s) = c(s)Y$ for a fixed $Y$. An operator in $\mathcal{Z}$ commutes with right translation by the end point of such a path.
8. Lifts of elements of $L^2$ are locally integrable. A function that is smooth and has open stabilizer in $G_f$ is continuous.

Item 1 follows from the finiteness of $K$-types in Section 17, by taking $X$ to be the sum of the type spaces in $H^{U_F}$ of the $K_\infty$-types of $F$. Items 2 to 6 are the convolution arguments of Harish-Chandra (1968), Chapter I. Item 7 is the action of the centre on smooth vectors; see Knapp, *Representation Theory of Semisimple Groups*, Chapter VIII, Section 1.

### The theorem

**Theorem 1.18.6 (Smooth representatives).** Let $H \subseteq L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ be a closed $G$-stable subspace on which $R$ is irreducible, and let $F \in H$ be $K$-finite. There is a smooth function $\varphi$ on $G$ such that:

1. $\tilde F = \varphi$ almost everywhere;
2. $\varphi$ is left $\Gamma$-invariant and square integrable on $\Gamma\backslash G$;
3. $\varphi(\gamma g) = \varphi(g)$ for $\gamma \in \mathrm{GL}_a(E)$;
4. $\varphi(z(t)g) = \varphi(g)$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$;
5. the stabilizer of $\varphi$ in $G_f$ under right translation is open;
6. the right translates of $\varphi$ by $K_\infty$ span a finite-dimensional space;
7. the space $\mathcal{Z}\varphi$ is finite-dimensional.

Lean: `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.SmoothRep.Rep.smooth_rep` (also `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.SmoothRep.smoothRep`).

*Proof.* Step 1: a preimage under convolution. By input 1 choose $U = U_F$ and $X$. By input 2 choose a conjugation invariant test function $\beta$ with $R(f_{U,\beta})$ injective on $X$, and write $f = f_{U,\beta}$. The operator $R(f)$ maps $H$ into $H$, since $H$ is closed and $G$-stable and $f$ is integrable by input 3. By input 4 it maps every vector into the $U$-fixed vectors. By input 5 it commutes with $R(K_\infty)$. So $R(f)(X) \subseteq X$ by input 1. Since $X$ is finite-dimensional, the injective map $R(f) : X \to X$ is onto, and there is $G_0 \in X$ with $R(f)G_0 = F$.

Step 2: the representative. Put $\varphi = \tilde G_0 * f$. By inputs 3 and 8, $\varphi$ is smooth. Since $\tilde F$ is the lift of $R(f)G_0$, it equals $\varphi$ almost everywhere, which is item 1. Item 3 holds because $\tilde G_0$ is left invariant under $\mathrm{GL}_a(E)$ and $f$ acts on the right. Item 2 follows from item 1 and item 3. Item 4 is input 6, because $G_0 \in H \subseteq L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}$. By input 4 the stabilizer of $\varphi$ contains $U$, which is open; this is item 5.

Step 3: a finiteness lemma. Let $S$ be a set of continuous left $\Gamma$-invariant functions $\psi$ on $G$, each equal almost everywhere to the lift of some element of $X$. Two continuous functions that agree almost everywhere are equal, because $\mu$ is positive on open sets. So $\psi \mapsto$ (its class in $L^2$) is injective on the span of $S$ and takes values in $X$. Hence $S$ spans a finite-dimensional space.

Step 4: $K_\infty$-finiteness. For $k \in K_\infty$, input 5 gives that the right translate of $\varphi$ by $k$ is $\widetilde{R(k)G_0} * f$. Here $R(k)G_0 \in X$, so this function is continuous by input 8 and equals almost everywhere the lift of $R(f)R(k)G_0 \in X$. Step 3 gives item 6.

Step 5: $\mathcal{Z}$-finiteness. Each $C_\tau(u)$ is a polynomial in the operators $D_X$. By input 3, applied once for each factor, for every $z \in \mathcal{Z}$ there is a test function $\beta'$ with $z(\Phi * f) = \Phi * f_{U,\beta'}$ for all locally integrable $\Phi$. By input 7, $z$ commutes with right translation by $K_\infty$. Combined with input 5 for $\beta$, this gives $(\widetilde{R(k)F'} * f_{U,\beta'})(x) = (\tilde F' * f_{U,\beta'})(xk)$ for all $F' \in L^2$ and $k \in K_\infty$. So $R(f_{U,\beta'})$ commutes with $R(K_\infty)$, and as in Step 1 it maps $X$ into $X$. Hence $z\varphi = \tilde G_0 * f_{U,\beta'}$ is continuous and equals almost everywhere the lift of an element of $X$. Step 3 gives item 7. $\square$

The test function $\beta'$ depends on $z$, but all the functions $z\varphi$ lie in one finite-dimensional space, the space of continuous representatives of elements of $X$. That is why the proof does not need a bound on the degree of $z$.

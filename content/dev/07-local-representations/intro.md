This volume proves the results on representations of local reductive groups that the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis uses. The groups are $\mathrm{GL}_n$ and unitary groups over $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, $p$-adic fields and finite fields. On the $p$-adic side the volume proves the theory of Bernstein and Zelevinsky for $\mathrm{GL}_n$, the uniqueness of Whittaker functionals, Bernstein's theorems on the unitary dual of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ and Zelevinsky's classification of generic representations by segments. On the real side it proves facts on the discrete series of $U(p, q)$: formal degrees, decay and pointwise bounds of matrix coefficients, branching, and the uniqueness of Bessel maps. Volume 1 gives the background: [smooth representations of p-adic groups](/learn/representations/#smooth-representations-of-p-adic-groups), [parabolic induction and the Jacquet module](/learn/representations/#parabolic-induction-and-the-jacquet-module), [cuspidal representations and segments](/learn/representations/#cuspidal-representations-and-segments), [generic representations](/learn/representations/#generic-representations-and-local-factors), [discrete series and formal degrees](/learn/real-groups/#discrete-series-and-formal-degrees), [asymptotics of matrix coefficients](/learn/real-groups/#asymptotics-of-matrix-coefficients) and [Bessel periods](/learn/periods/#bessel-and-fourier-jacobi-periods).

The proof uses the volume in three places. The first is the step [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/). There the local components of unitary cuspidal representations of $\mathrm{GL}_N$ are written as irreducible products of segment representations with bounded exponents. Chapters 1, 2, 4 and 7 supply these inputs, and the theorem of Clozel in Chapter 7 enters the step [EveryCharacter from Row0b](/step/every-character-of-row/). The second place is the step [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) of [the literature](/proof/literature/), It needs the discrete series with a given parameter and their formal degrees, the local results for theta lifts and the least value of theta generators. Chapters 2, 3 and 5 supply these. Chapter 3 also feeds the steps [theta local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/) and [theta integrals agree and local matching](/step/theta-integrals-agree-and-local-matching-of-literature/). The third place is the step [HypsAlt is nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/) of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/). It needs the local bounds at the real places: decay of coefficients, the majorant, pointwise bounds, and the local inputs of the closed bound on Bessel periods. Chapters 2 to 6 supply these. Chapters 1 and 4 also feed the base change argument of Volume 8.

Chapter 1 proves facts on $\mathrm{GL}_n$ and unitary groups that Volume 8 and Chapter 7 use. Sections 1 to 4 treat $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ as a real group. They prove a form of the Casselman-Osborne theorem for the top compact class, [Theorem 1.1.10](/entry/action-unipotent-radical-real/), the injectivity of the top class, [Theorem 1.2.5](/entry/injective-unipotent-of-restriction/), the existence of a model built from an induced function, [Theorem 1.3.4](/entry/real-model-exists/), and the case $\mu = \rho$ of the theorem of Parthasarathy, Ranga Rao and Varadarajan, [Theorem 1.4.6](/entry/parthasarathy-rao-varadarajan/). Sections 5 to 9 develop the theory of Bernstein and Zelevinsky over a $p$-adic field: the exact sequence for $\Phi(\tau \times \sigma)$, [Theorem 1.8.2](/entry/phi-pg/) and the Leibniz rule with ordered layers, [Theorem 1.9.3](/entry/leibniz-order-of-exact-sequences/). Section 10 proves the swap and inverse reductions for unramified principal series of unitary groups, [Theorem 1.10.1](/entry/unitary-swap-reduction/) and [Theorem 1.10.2](/entry/unitary-inverse-reduction/). The later sections prove that spherical vectors survive in the Jacquet module, [Theorem 1.12.8](/entry/unitary-jacquet-nonzero/), finite $K$-types in cuspidal representations, [Theorem 1.17.5](/entry/cuspidal-k-type-finite/), multiplicity one at bad places, [Theorem 1.19.11](/entry/ramified-multiplicity-one-of-bernstein/), the polynomial ring structure of the Grothendieck ring of $\mathrm{GL}_n$, [Theorem 1.21.11](/entry/companion-theory-groth-grothendieck-ring-polynomial-of-seg/), and the Iwahori factorization, [Theorem 1.23.2](/entry/iwahori-statement/).

Chapter 2 collects facts on real and $p$-adic groups in explicit form. For $U(p) \times U(q)$ it proves the Weyl dimension formula, [Theorem 2.1.5](/entry/weyl-for-k-closed/). For $\mathrm{GL}_N$ it proves the branching rule of Gelfand and Tsetlin with integral branching maps, [Theorem 2.2.7](/entry/branching-interlacing-pairs/), and the polarization words for products in the Fock model, [Theorem 2.3.5](/entry/tensor-operators-holds/). For the discrete series of $U(p, q)$ it proves that the lowest $K$-type has the predicted infinitesimal character, [Theorem 2.4.5](/entry/h-alg-core/), and computes the norm of the lowest $K$-type coefficient through the formal degree, [Theorem 2.5.4](/entry/psi-norm/). On the $p$-adic side it proves a uniform admissibility theorem through a polynomial identity, [Theorem 2.6.5](/entry/loc-pi/), Flath's tensor product theorem, [Theorem 2.6.9](/entry/flath-tensor-product/), and Zelevinsky's segment representations, [Theorem 2.7.6](/entry/companion-theory-rg-gu-segment-representation/). It ends with Harish-Chandra's finiteness theorem for automorphic forms, [Theorem 2.8.3](/entry/automorphic-forms-finite-dimensional/), an unfolding identity over a finite field, [Theorem 2.9.6](/entry/residue-field-unfolding-claims-hold/), and a slope bound of Bost type for glued lattices, [Theorem 2.10.3](/entry/functional-lower-bound-of-conditions/).

Chapter 3 proves the local facts on discrete series of $U(p, q)$ and on $p$-adic induced representations that the comparison of theta lifts needs. It identifies the normalized polar measure with the compact dual measure, [Theorem 3.3.7](/entry/mu-cd-eq-cd-vol/). It computes the Gelfand eigenvalues of the coefficient $\Psi$, [Theorem 3.4.9](/entry/psi-m-gelfand/), and proves a supertrace formula through a Dirac type operator, [Theorem 3.5.6](/entry/sup-trace/). Matrix coefficients decay like a fixed power of $\|g\|$ when the parameter has a large gap, [Theorem 3.6.2](/entry/dec-sm-j/). A shift of the parameter is a summand of the tensor product with the standard representation, [Theorem 3.7.4](/entry/ds-cc-gk/). Every irreducible square integrable unitary representation is a discrete series with a Harish-Chandra parameter, [Theorem 3.9.14](/entry/ds-exhaust/). Section 10 builds a majorant that is integrable with exponent $2$, [Theorem 3.10.11](/entry/xi-exists/), and Section 11 proves theta interpolation on tori with complex multiplication, [Theorem 3.11.4](/entry/prop-a/). Section 12 proves Bernstein's irreducibility theorem for $\mathrm{GL}_{a+b}$ induced from two unitary characters. From it come local theta data at split places, [Theorem 3.12.5](/entry/fin-split/), and multiplicity one at ramified places, [Theorem 3.12.7](/entry/ramified-multiplicity-one/).

Chapter 4 is about Whittaker and Kirillov models of $\mathrm{GL}_n$. It proves the theorem of Gelfand and Kazhdan for Whittaker distributions, [Theorem 4.1.6](/entry/distribution-sigma-invariant/), and the uniqueness of Whittaker functionals, [Theorem 4.2.6](/entry/rank-whittaker-le-one/). A representation that contains a principal series type is a subquotient of a principal series, [Theorem 4.3.9](/entry/subquotient-prin-series-of-has-vec/). Over finite fields it bounds the share of bad rows through ranks of Whittaker idempotents, [Theorem 4.4.14](/entry/bad-rows-share-bound-two-primes/). Theorem M gives multiplicity one for the Bessel integral of a discrete series, [Theorem 4.5.6](/entry/thm-m/). The archimedean part starts with a Dixmier-Malliavin lemma on the line, [Theorem 4.6.1](/entry/exists-dixmier-malliavin-line/), and proves the Kirillov theorem at the complex places, [Theorem 4.7.2](/entry/kirillov-archimedean-totally-complex-mirabolic/), and its semi-local form, [Theorem 4.8.7](/entry/kirillov-semilocal-smooth-bump/). The global part proves Flath's tensor product theorem for $\mathrm{GL}_n$, [Theorem 4.9.5](/entry/nonempty-adelic-decomposition/), and the functional equation of the Godement-Jacquet zeta integral, [Theorem 4.10.5](/entry/global-zeta-entire-functional-equation/). The last two sections prove good reduction from many rational torsion points, [Theorem 4.11.6](/entry/has-good-reduction-of-many-rational-torsion-points/), and an index bound under local rank conditions, [Theorem 4.12.4](/entry/on-core-at-places/).

Chapter 5 bounds the matrix coefficients of a discrete series of $U(p, q)$ pointwise on a big cell, by the $L^2$ norm times an explicit power of the distance to the boundary. It bounds derivatives along words of operators in normal form, [Theorem 5.3.5](/entry/word-bound-smooth-decaying-coefficients/), and removes a cutoff near the boundary by domination, [Theorem 5.4.7](/entry/top-density-cut-dominated-all/). A bound at three linked places, [Theorem 5.5.5](/entry/eigen-three-places-main/), gives the bound for Capelli eigenfunctions, [Theorem 5.6.5](/entry/pointwise-bound-capelli-eigenfunction/), and for discrete series coefficients without hypotheses, [Theorem 5.6.10](/entry/discrete-series-coef-pointwise-bound/). At a finite place it bounds the least value of theta generators through characters of formal groups, [Theorem 5.7.7](/entry/least-value-le/). The last section shows that a locally uniform limit of finite sums of matrix coefficients is a convergent series of them, [Theorem 5.8.11](/entry/closure-model/).

Chapter 6 collects the local results on Bessel models. At a real place a bounded Bessel map on a discrete series of $U(p, q)$ is unique up to a scalar when the gaps of the parameter are large, [Theorem 6.2.7](/entry/bessel-map-uniq-gap/). The chapter bounds the convolution integrals, [Theorem 6.3.5](/entry/real-obj-in-infinity-x-jsz/), and the amplitudes uniformly in the parameter, [Theorem 6.3.7](/entry/amp-unif-gap/). Over a finite field the restriction of a cuspidal representation of $\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}$ to the mirabolic subgroup is induced from a generic character, [Theorem 6.4.10](/entry/cuspidal-mirabolic/). The chapter then gives the ingredients of Zuckerman tensoring, [Theorem 6.5.5](/entry/zuckerman-ingredients/), the moments of Bessel functionals from the Pieri rule, [Theorem 6.6.5](/entry/moment-first-length/), and the domination of the normal form near the boundary, [Theorem 6.7.6](/entry/normal-form-top-domination/). These give the weighted bound on rotated points, [Theorem 6.8.3](/entry/weighted-bound-rotated-uniform/). The last section proves that unitary groups over a CM field have finite covolume, [Theorem 6.9.5](/entry/fin-vol/).

Chapter 7 is about the unitary dual of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over a nonarchimedean local field. From Frobenius reciprocity for distributions, [Theorem 7.1.1](/entry/frobenius-reciprocity/), it proves Bernstein's vanishing theorem, Theorem T, in characteristic zero and in characteristic $p$, [Theorem 7.4.1](/entry/all-thm-t/). Theorem A follows: every $P$-invariant distribution on $G$ is $G$-invariant, [Theorem 7.4.2](/entry/thm-a/). So does Theorem B on $P$-invariant forms, [Theorem 7.4.5](/entry/thm-b/). The chapter then proves the Leibniz rule for derivatives, [Theorem 7.5.2](/entry/leibniz-ord/), the exponent bound $|s| < \frac12$ for irreducible unitarisable generic representations, [Theorem 7.6.6](/entry/exp-bound/), and Bernstein's theorem that the product of two irreducible unitarisable representations is irreducible, [Theorem 7.7.1](/entry/bern-at/). In characteristic zero, every irreducible generic representation is a product of segment representations of pairwise unlinked segments, [Theorem 7.8.6](/entry/generic-unlinked-gl-of-seg-rep2/). At the real places it proves He's branching theorem for $U(p, q)$, [Theorem 7.9.1](/entry/occ/), and Harish-Chandra's formula for formal degrees, [Theorem 7.9.4](/entry/formal-degree-compact-dual/). The last section proves Clozel's theorem that regular algebraic cuspidal representations are cohomological, [Theorem 7.10.1](/entry/clozel-cohomological/), the global Cauchy identity, [Theorem 7.10.4](/entry/cauchy-all/), and the base change fundamental lemma for the unit, [Theorem 7.10.5](/entry/bcfl-unit/).

Chapter 1 supplies inputs to three other chapters. Its Sections 5 to 9 give the Leibniz rule of Theorem 7.5.2 and Lemma 7.5.3. Its Section 19 gives inputs of Theorems 3.12.4 and 3.12.6, and its Section 22 gives the geometric facts behind the decay estimate of Chapter 3. Its Section 23 gives a condition of the Cartan structure in Theorem 2.6.4. Chapters 2, 3 and 7 use no other chapter of this volume except Chapter 1. The majorant of Theorem 3.10.11 is an input of Chapter 5 and of Section 5 of Chapter 4. Theorem M of Chapter 4 is an input of the uniqueness of Bessel maps in Chapter 6. So a reader can take Chapter 1 first, then Chapters 2, 3 and 7 in any order, then Chapter 4, and Chapters 5 and 6 last.

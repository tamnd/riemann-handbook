## The polynomial radial relation

At a real place the vectors of the model are functions on a real group that transform on the left and on the right by two unitary representations of compact subgroups. Along a one parameter section $\zeta \mapsto \mu_0(\zeta)$ such a function is determined by its values on an annulus, and the operators of the model act on these values by differential operators in $\zeta$ and $\bar\zeta$. This section proves that the second shift operator, after projection to one type, is a polynomial in $\sqrt{|\zeta|^2 - \tau}$ times a fixed operator, up to a power of $|\zeta|^2 - \tau$. It is the input "polynomial radial" of [the second shift representation](/entry/ssn-rep-of-poly-s/), and through it of [the model places](/entry/model-place-built-holds/). For the background on real groups and their Lie algebras see [real reductive groups](/learn/real-groups/#real-reductive-groups) and [Lie groups and Lie algebras](/learn/analysis/#lie-groups-and-lie-algebras).

**Definition 3.7.1 (Real place data).** Let $k \geq 1$ and $r \geq 0$, and let $W_p, W_q$ be finite dimensional Hilbert spaces. *Real place data* consist of two vectors $z, z' \in \mathbb{C}^{2r + k + 1}$ in the admissible set with $T(z), T(z') > 0$; continuous unitary representations $\pi$ of the stabilizer group $U_W(z)$ on $W_p$ and $\pi'$ of $U_W(z')$ on $W_q$; Haar measures on the groups in question; a section $\mu : \mathbb{C} \to M_{k+1}(\mathbb{C})$; and a function $\chi$. We write $\mu_0(\zeta)$ for the image of $\mu(\zeta)$ in the large matrix group, and $\tau = T(z) T(z')$.

For $\zeta$ in the annulus $A = \{\zeta \in \mathbb{C} : |\zeta|^2 > \tau\}$ the *stabilizer* of $\zeta$ is the set of pairs $(p_1, p_2) \in U_W(z) \times U_W(z')$ with $p_1 \mu_0(\zeta) p_2 = \mu_0(\zeta)$, and the *fibre* $\mathrm{Fib}(\zeta)$ is the space of linear maps $X : W_q \to W_p$ with $\pi(p_1) X \pi'(p_2) = X$ for all pairs in the stabilizer. A function $F$ with $F(h g h') = \pi(h) F(g) \pi'(h')$ takes values in $\mathrm{Fib}(\zeta)$ at $\mu_0(\zeta)$.

**Definition 3.7.2 (The class PS).** Let $\tau > 0$, and put $g(\zeta) = |\zeta|^2 - \tau$ and $\sigma(\zeta) = \sqrt{g(\zeta)}$ on $A$. A function $f : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ is in $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$ if there are $m \in \mathbb{N}$ and a polynomial $p \in \mathbb{C}[X_1, X_2, X_3]$ such that
$$g(\zeta)^m f(\zeta) = p\big(\zeta, \bar\zeta, \sigma(\zeta)\big) \quad \text{for all } \zeta \in A.$$
A function $f$ with values in a vector space $V$ is in $\mathrm{PSV}_\tau$ if $f = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i\, v_i$ on $A$ for some $a_i \in \mathrm{PS}_\tau$ and $v_i \in V$.

So $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$ is the ring of polynomials in $\zeta, \bar\zeta, \sigma$ with the function $g$ inverted, restricted to $A$; see [polynomials](/learn/algebra/#polynomials-and-power-series).

**Lemma 3.7.3 (Closure of PS).** Let $\tau > 0$.

1. Constants, $\zeta$, $\bar\zeta$, $\sigma$ and $g^{-1}$ are in $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$, and $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$ is closed under sums and products.
2. $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$ is closed under $\partial_\zeta$ and $\partial_{\bar\zeta}$.
3. $\mathrm{PSV}_\tau$ is closed under sums, under multiplication by elements of $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$, under linear maps, and under $\partial_\zeta$ and $\partial_{\bar\zeta}$.

*Proof.* For part 1, the first four functions take $m = 0$, and $g \cdot g^{-1} = 1$ gives $g^{-1}$ with $m = 1$. If $g^m f = p$ and $g^{m'} f' = p'$, then $g^{m + m'}(f f') = p p'$ and $g^{m + m'}(f + f') = g^{m'} p + g^m p'$, and $g = \zeta \bar\zeta - \tau$ is a polynomial in $\zeta$ and $\bar\zeta$. For part 2, write $f = g^{-m} p(\zeta, \bar\zeta, \sigma)$. On $A$ we have $\partial_\zeta g = \bar\zeta$ and $\partial_\zeta \sigma = \bar\zeta / (2\sigma) = \bar\zeta \sigma g^{-1} / 2$. By the chain rule $\partial_\zeta f$ is a sum of products of $g^{-1}$, $\bar\zeta$, $\sigma$ and partial derivatives of $p$, so it is in $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$ by part 1. The same holds for $\partial_{\bar\zeta}$. Part 3 follows from parts 1 and 2: concatenate the lists for sums, and apply the operation to each coefficient $a_i$ or to each vector $v_i$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.7.4 (Radial elements of PS).** Let $f \in \mathrm{PS}_\tau$ with $f(e^{i\theta}\zeta) = f(\zeta)$ for all real $\theta$ and $\zeta \in A$. Then there are $m$ and a polynomial $q \in \mathbb{C}[X]$ with $g(\zeta)^m f(\zeta) = q(\sigma(\zeta))$ on $A$.

*Proof.* Write $g^m f = p(\zeta, \bar\zeta, \sigma)$ with $p = \sum c_{abc} X_1^a X_2^b X_3^c$. The functions $g$ and $\sigma$ are invariant under rotation, so $g^m f$ is invariant. Fix $\zeta \in A$ and take the mean over $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$ of $p(e^{i\theta}\zeta, e^{-i\theta}\bar\zeta, \sigma)$. The mean of $e^{i(a - b)\theta}$ is $0$ for $a \neq b$, so
$$g(\zeta)^m f(\zeta) = \sum_{a, c} c_{aac}\, |\zeta|^{2a} \sigma^c = \sum_{a, c} c_{aac}\, (\sigma^2 + \tau)^a \sigma^c,$$
which is a polynomial in $\sigma$. $\square$

Let $\eta(\zeta) = \mu_0(\zeta)^{-1} \partial_\zeta \mu_0(\zeta)$ and $\bar\eta(\zeta) = \mu_0(\zeta)^{-1} \partial_{\bar\zeta} \mu_0(\zeta)$. For a complex matrix $Z$ with Hermitian parts $Z_1, Z_2$ in the Lie algebra, the right derivative is $R_Z F = R_{Z_1} F + i R_{Z_2} F$, and $R_w F$ for a word $w = (Z_1, \dots, Z_n)$ is $R_{Z_1} \cdots R_{Z_n} F$. Let $E_{ab}$ be the elementary matrix at the entry of the embedded $(k+1)$-block given by the indices $a, b \in \{0, \dots, k\}$.

**Theorem 3.7.5 (Radial decomposition).** Let $k \geq 1$, let $z, z'$ be admissible with $T(z), T(z') > 0$, let $\mu_0$ be the standard section, and let $a, b \in \{0, \dots, k\}$. There are matrices $B^1_t$ in the complexified Lie algebra of $U_W(z')$, matrices $B^2_t$ in that of $U_W(z)$, and functions $b^1_t, b^2_t, \beta_1, \beta_2 \in \mathrm{PS}_\tau$ such that for all $\zeta \in A$
$$\begin{aligned} E_{ab} = {} & \sum_t b^1_t(\zeta)\, B^1_t + \mu_0(\zeta)^{-1} \Big(\sum_t b^2_t(\zeta)\, B^2_t\Big) \mu_0(\zeta) \\ & + \beta_1(\zeta)\, \eta(\zeta) + \beta_2(\zeta)\, \bar\eta(\zeta). \end{aligned}$$

*Proof.* We give the shape of the proof. The standard section is a conjugate $\mu_0(\zeta) = g_A\, \nu(\zeta)\, g_B$ of a matrix $\nu(\zeta)$ with entries in $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$, where $g_A$ and $g_B$ are fixed matrices that bring the Hermitian forms of $z$ and $z'$ to a normal form. Under this conjugation the Lie algebras of the two stabilizers become fixed block subalgebras, and $\eta(\zeta)$ and $\bar\eta(\zeta)$ become conjugates $g_B^{-1} H_1(\zeta) g_B$ and $g_B^{-1} H_2(\zeta) g_B$ of explicit matrices with entries in $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$. The proof then treats the cases of $a$ and $b$ (each equal to $0$, to $k$, or in between) and writes down the coefficients in each case. They are rational in $\zeta, \bar\zeta, \sigma$ with denominators that are powers of $g$, so they lie in $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$ by the closure lemma. $\square$

The decomposition is an infinitesimal form of the decomposition $G = U_W(z)\, \mu_0(A)\, U_W(z')$ of the open set where $\mu_0$ is defined. The first sum acts on a covariant function by $\pi'$ on the right, the second by $\pi$ on the left, and the last two terms act by $\partial_\zeta$ and $\partial_{\bar\zeta}$ along the section.

**Proposition 3.7.6 (Words along the section).** Let $\Phi$ be a smooth function in the radial space of the place data, and assume that $\Phi_c(\mu_0(\zeta)) = X_0$ for all $c$ and all $\zeta \in A$. Then for each $c$ and each word $w$ of matrices whose Hermitian parts lie in the Lie algebra of the last level, the function $\zeta \mapsto R_w \Phi_c(\mu_0(\zeta))$ is in $\mathrm{PSV}_\tau$.

*Proof.* We prove a stronger claim by induction on the length of $w$: for every linear map $L$, the function $\zeta \mapsto R_w (L \circ \Phi_c)(\mu_0(\zeta))$ is in $\mathrm{PSV}_\tau$. For the empty word the function is the constant $L(X_0)$. Let $w = (Z) w'$. Since $R_Z$ at a point is linear in $Z$, it is enough to take $Z = E_{ab}$, and we use the decomposition of the theorem at $\zeta$. Put $G = R_{w'}(L \circ \Phi_c)$. Right derivatives keep left covariance, so the term $\mu_0^{-1} B^2_t \mu_0$ gives $d\pi(B^2_t) \circ G(\mu_0(\zeta))$, a linear image of a function in $\mathrm{PSV}_\tau$. The term $\beta_1 \eta + \beta_2 \bar\eta$ gives $\beta_1 \partial_\zeta + \beta_2 \partial_{\bar\zeta}$ of $\zeta \mapsto G(\mu_0(\zeta))$. For the term $B^1_t$, move $R_{B^1_t}$ to the right of $R_{w'}$. Each move adds a word of length $|w'|$ with one entry replaced by a commutator. At the right end, right covariance gives $R_{B^1_t} \Phi_c = \Phi_c \circ d\pi'(B^1_t)$, which is a linear map applied to $\Phi_c$. All the words that remain are shorter than $w$ or have the form $R_{w'}(L' \circ \Phi_c)$. The induction hypothesis and the closure lemma end the proof. $\square$

**Proposition 3.7.7 (Core polynomial).** Let the place data be a place, with $k \geq 2$ and the standard section, and let $\Phi$ and $X_0$ be as in the previous Proposition. Then for each $c$ and each $Y \in \mathbb{C}$, the function $\zeta \mapsto (t_L(Y) \Phi_c)(\mu_0(\zeta))$ is in $\mathrm{PSV}_\tau$, where $t_L(Y)$ is the operator of Capelli type of the last level.

*Proof.* The operator $t_L(Y)$ is built from lower unipotent matrices. At the point $\mu_0(\zeta)$ it equals $\sum_i \kappa_i(\zeta)\, R_{w_i}$, where the $\kappa_i$ are in $\mathrm{PS}_\tau$ and each $w_i$ is a word of elementary matrices $E_{ab}$. Each $R_{w_i} \Phi_c(\mu_0(\zeta))$ is in $\mathrm{PSV}_\tau$ by the previous Proposition, and the closure lemma gives the sum. $\square$

**Theorem 3.7.8 (Polynomial radial relation).** Let real place data have $k \geq 2$, let $X$ be actual operators with normal form data $d$ that satisfy the standing hypotheses, let $1 \leq l \leq k - 1$, and let $S$ be a second shift at the level $l$. Let $\mathrm{Pr}$ be a family of type projections, let $B_w, B_w'$ be frames of $z$ and $z'$, and let $\pi, \rho$ be types with $\rho_i = \pi_i + [i + 1 = l]$. Then there are $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $q \in \mathbb{C}[X]$ such that for all $\zeta \in A$ and all $X_f \in \mathrm{Fib}(\zeta)$ with $\mathrm{Pr}_\pi(\nu_0(\zeta)) X_f = X_f$
$$\begin{aligned} & (|\zeta|^2 - \tau)^n\, \mathrm{Pr}_\rho(\nu_0(\zeta))\, S(\zeta) X_f \\ & \quad = q\big(\sqrt{|\zeta|^2 - \tau}\big)\, \mathrm{Pr}_\rho(\nu_0(\zeta))\, \theta(\zeta) X_f. \end{aligned}$$
Here $\nu_0(\zeta)$ is the unit vector of the projection of $\mu_0(\zeta) z'$, and $\theta(\zeta)$ is the shift operator built from the frames.

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.CutMP.JA.SSn.Rep.Core.TC.PR.polynomial_radial_holds` (also `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.CutMP.JA.SSn.Rep.Core.TC.PR.polyRadialS_of`, the form with the decomposition and the word statement as hypotheses).

*Proof.* We give the plan. First, the relation holds at the standard section with a polynomial in $\sqrt{|\zeta|^2 - \tau}$, and a transfer step moves it to the section of the data, since the data are constant along the relevant line and the fibres are stable. At the standard section, the projected shift $\mathrm{Pr}_\rho S(\zeta) X_f$ is computed from the core functions of the previous Proposition by an algebraic shift identity, so its coefficients are in $\mathrm{PSV}_\tau$. The projected image of the type $\pi$ is a line on which $\mathrm{Pr}_\rho \theta(\zeta)$ does not vanish, so the projected shift is a scalar $f(\zeta)$ times $\mathrm{Pr}_\rho \theta(\zeta) X_f$, with $f \in \mathrm{PS}_\tau$. Both sides transform in the same way under the rotation $\zeta \mapsto e^{i\theta}\zeta$, so $f$ is invariant under rotation. The Lemma on radial elements gives $g^n f = q(\sigma)$. $\square$

The power $(|\zeta|^2 - \tau)^n$ is the price of the boundary of the annulus: the coefficients of the decomposition have poles there, and the relation clears them. In [the second shift representation](/entry/ssn-rep-of-poly-s/) the polynomial $q$ fixes the growth of the shift near the boundary, which feeds the bounds of [the bounded operator](/entry/j-abdd-op-qq-of-ssn-rep/).

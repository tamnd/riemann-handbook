## The energy limit in the model

This section applies [Lemma E](/entry/lemma-e-closed/) to the model at one infinite place. There the energy is an inner product of smooth vectors on an open set of $\mathbb{C}^{r+1}$, the operators of the steps are differential operators of Capelli type, and the cutoff is a function of one radial coordinate. The result says that, as the cutoff is removed, the energy of a word tends to the size of the word times the $L^2$ norm of the vectors. It is the input "energy limit" of [the model places](/entry/model-place-built-holds/) and through them of [the built bounds](/entry/built-bounds-compact/).

**Definition 3.6.1 (The model domain).** Let $r \geq 0$ and $T, T' > 0$, and put $\tau = TT'$. The *model domain* is the open set
$$Q = \{(z_1, \dots, z_r, \zeta) \in \mathbb{C}^{r+1} : z_l \neq 0 \text{ for all } l, \ |\zeta|^2 > \tau\},$$
with the measure $d\mu_0 = \prod_l |z_l|^{-2}\, d\lambda$, where $\lambda$ is Lebesgue measure. The *radial coordinate* is $G(z, \zeta) = \prod_l |z_l|^2 \cdot (|\zeta|^2 - \tau)$, a positive smooth function on $Q$.

The function $G$ tends to $0$ at the boundary $|\zeta|^2 = \tau$ and on the coordinate hyperplanes. A function $\chi_\varepsilon(G)$ for a cutoff $\chi$ therefore vanishes near the boundary and equals $1$ away from it.

**Definition 3.6.2 (Model data).** *Model data* consist of: parameters $(k, r, T, T', u, u')$ with $u, u'$ sequences of reals; an integer $l$ with $1 \leq l \leq k$; a *level datum* $\mathcal{L}$, a finite list of differential operators on $Q$ that contains a set $\widehat{\mathcal{A}}$ of operators and a set $\mathcal{B}$ of bounded operators; a finite set $\Pi$ of *types*; smooth functions $j_\pi$ on $Q$ for $\pi \in \Pi$; and reals $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_{k + 2r + 1}$ and $\alpha < \beta$. For $Y \in \mathbb{C}$ there is a differential operator $C(Y)$ of Capelli type, a polynomial in $Y$ with operator coefficients. We use the following conditions, for an integer $m$.

1. (*Setting*) The parameters are admissible, $\mathcal{L}$ is a model level datum, the $\lambda_s$ interlace with $u$ and with $u'$, and each $Y \in [\alpha, \beta]$ lies on the cut of $(u, u', l)$.
2. (*Decay of order $m$*) For each product $a_1 \cdots a_n$ of at most $m$ operators in $\widehat{\mathcal{A}}$, $\|a_1 \cdots a_n j_\pi\|^2$ is $\mu_0$-integrable.
3. (*Adjoints of order $m$*) For $X \in \widehat{\mathcal{A}}$, $b$ in the monoid generated by $\mathcal{B}$, products $v, w$ of operators in $\widehat{\mathcal{A}}$ of total length at most $m - 1$, and $\varphi$ smooth in $\log x$, vanishing on $(-\infty, \varepsilon]$ and with bounded logarithmic derivatives,
$$\langle X(\varphi(G)\, b v j), w j \rangle = \langle \varphi(G)\, b v j, X^* w j \rangle.$$
4. (*Eigenvalue*) $C(Y) j_\pi = \prod_s (Y + \lambda_s)\, j_\pi$ for all real $Y$ and all $\pi$.

The inner product is $\langle f, g \rangle = \sum_\pi \int_Q f_\pi \overline{g_\pi}\, d\mu_0$. The adjoint condition is integration by parts against functions that vanish near the boundary. The eigenvalue condition is what makes $P(Y) = \prod_s (Y + \lambda_s)$ appear in the energy.

**Definition 3.6.3 (Model energy).** Let $\chi$ be a cutoff. For $\varepsilon > 0$ and a word $w = ((b_1, Y_1), \dots, (b_n, Y_n))$ put
$$\mathcal{E}_\varepsilon(w) = \mathrm{Re}\, \big\langle C_l(Y_1) \cdots C_l(Y_n)\, \chi_\varepsilon(G) j,\; \chi_\varepsilon(G) j \big\rangle,$$
where $C_l(Y)$ is the Capelli type operator of the starting level $l$. The entries $b_i$ do not enter.

**Proposition 3.6.4 (Model steps).** There are $C_R, C_d \geq 0$ with the following property, for $C_P = e^3$. Let model data satisfy the conditions above with decay and adjoints of order $2N + 2$, and assume $r + 1 \leq N$, $N \leq L$, $2 < L$, $(k + 2r + 1) L \leq \beta - \alpha$ and $6 N L \leq \beta - \alpha$. Then in the energy system of the model data with box length $L$, each step of a list on the boxes of length less than $N$ is admissible, in the sense of [the step conditions](/entry/lemma-e-closed/), for $\sigma = 4N$ and the constants $C_R, C_d, C_P$.

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.induction_imports`.

*Proof.* Admissibility is a list of twelve conditions, and each is proved for all model steps at once. Measurability holds since all data are continuous on $Q$. The weights are positive. The polynomial $P$ is continuous and does not vanish on the boxes, and the ratio bound $|P(Y + s)| \leq e^3 |P(Y)|$ holds, since the roots $-\lambda_s$ lie outside $[\alpha, \beta]$ and the boxes lie in its middle third. The norm identity comes from a signed identity, $g^*(Y)(d(Y) g(Y) X) = (-1)^{k - l} P(Y)\, d_0 X$, together with the fact that the sign of $(-1)^{k-l} P$ is positive on the cut. The adjoint condition comes from condition 3, and locality and polynomiality from the structure of the operators. The shift formula is an identity of differential operators, and the residue and weight bounds give $C_R$ and $C_d$. $\square$

**Proposition 3.6.5 (Factorization).** Let model data satisfy the hypotheses of the previous Proposition, and let $n \leq N$. Then there are fields $m_y$ ($y \in \mathbb{R}^n$) and matrices $B_Y$ such that the family of length $n$ built from them is measurable, factors as $(g_Y)_s j = B_Y\, m_{Y + s}$, has $B_Y$ and $B_Y^{-1}$ bounded and measurable, and has $m_y$ polynomial of degree at most $r + 1$ in each variable $y_a$.

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.factor_exists`.

*Proof.* Take for $m_y$ the vector of monomial terms of the word $y$ applied to $j$, and for $B_Y$ the point matrix of the frame of the word $Y$. The polynomial form of $m_y$ in $y$, with coefficients that are continuous, comes from the structure of the monomials. Measurability follows from continuity. The factorization is an identity between the conjugated operator and the monomials at the shifted point $Y + s$. Boundedness of $B_Y$ and of its inverse holds because both have entries in a ring of bounded functions on $Q$, which uses $\tau > 0$ and the model level datum. $\square$

**Corollary 3.6.6 (Model families).** Under the same hypotheses, each family of length at most $N$ of the model energy system is admissible.

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.family_imports`.

*Proof.* The family of length $n$ is defined with a factorization chosen among those that the Factorization proposition provides, so it has the first four properties. Positivity of its weights and locality of its operators are proved separately. $\square$

**Theorem 3.6.7 (Lemma E in the model).** There are $C_2, C_6 > 0$ with the following property. Let model data satisfy the setting condition, decay and adjoints of order $2N + 2$ and the eigenvalue condition, with $r + 1 \leq N$ and
$$C_6\big(k N (r + 1)^2 + N^2 (r + 1)^2\big) \leq \beta - \alpha.$$
Let $\chi$ be a cutoff, and let $w$ be a word of length at most $N$ with entries $Y_i \in [\alpha, \beta]$ and $|Y_i - Y_j| > 2$ for $i \neq j$. Then
$$\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^+} \mathcal{E}_\varepsilon(w) = \prod_i \Big|\prod_s (Y_i + \lambda_s)\Big| \cdot \sum_{\pi \in \Pi} \int_Q |j_\pi|^2\, d\mu_0.$$

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.lemma_model`.

*Proof.* Let $C_R, C_d$ be the constants of the Proposition on model steps, $C_P = e^3$, $C_7 = C_d C_P (1 + C_R)^2$, and put $C_2 = 64(1 + 2e\sqrt{C_7})$ and $C_6 = 6 C_2$. Consider the model energy system with box length $L = C_2 N (r + 1)^2$. Then $L \geq 64 N > 2$, $N \leq L$, and the bound on $\beta - \alpha$ gives $(k + 2r + 1) L \leq \beta - \alpha$ and $6 N L \leq \beta - \alpha$. So the Proposition on model steps and the Corollary apply. The other hypotheses of [Lemma E](/entry/lemma-e-closed/) hold as well. The roots $-\lambda_s$ lie outside $[\alpha, \beta]$, by interlacing. The energy is a polynomial of degree at most $k + 2r + 1$ in each $Y_i$ on admitted words, since $C_l(Y)$ is a polynomial in $Y$. The base density is integrable, by decay. Theorem P holds: the energy of $Y^+$ is a form $\langle F^* D F \chi_\varepsilon j, \chi_\varepsilon j\rangle$, which by adjointness equals $\sum_e \langle D F \chi_\varepsilon j, F \chi_\varepsilon j \rangle$, and this is the integral of the family density with $j$ replaced by $\chi_\varepsilon(G) j$. Finally, the base energy $j = \int \rho(())\, d\mu_0$ of the system is $\sum_\pi \int |j_\pi|^2\, d\mu_0$. Lemma E gives the limit. $\square$

The polynomial $\prod_s (Y + \lambda_s)$ has constant sign on $[\alpha, \beta]$, so the limit is, up to a sign, a polynomial in the $Y_i$. This is the form in which [the model place](/entry/model-place-built-holds/) uses it: the energy of the cut is a constant times a cutoff energy, and its limit is a multiple of the size of the word.

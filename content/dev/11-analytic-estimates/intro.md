This volume proves the elementary but long estimates of the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. They are of three kinds. The first is analytic number theory: zero density bounds, the explicit formula, primes in progressions with a relative error, and primes represented by norms in short intervals. The second is analysis on unitary groups: normal forms for hermitian curvature operators, stationary phase, and energy bounds at the infinite places. The third is lattice combinatorics over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and over discrete valuation rings: Pieri rules, path polynomials, growth bounds for projections of lattices, and bounds for formal groups. Volume 1 gives the background: [Dirichlet L-functions](/learn/l-functions/#dirichlet-l-functions), [the explicit formula](/learn/analytic-number-theory/#the-explicit-formula), [primes represented by norm forms](/learn/analytic-number-theory/#primes-represented-by-norm-forms), [Haar measure](/learn/analysis/#haar-measure), [highest weights and branching](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching), [the Pieri rule](/learn/combinatorics/#the-pieri-and-littlewood-richardson-rules), [the Fock model](/learn/theta/#the-fock-model) and [formal group laws](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws). This volume proves them.

The proof uses the volume in four places. The first is the step [HypsAlt is nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/) of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/). Chapter 1 gives it the germ rate, the head formula and the compact form of Theorem P, Chapter 3 the model places and the built bounds, and Chapter 4 inputs to its rows and frames. The second place is the step [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/). It uses the short interval norm theorem and the primes in progressions of Chapter 2, and Property W of Chapter 6. The volume ratio of Chapter 2 is the volume clause of [the record of hypotheses](/step/hyps-alt-def/). The third place is the step [ZeroFreeAll from Green, VWin and HypsAlt](/step/zero-free-all-of-green-v-win/), which takes the local estimates of Chapter 5. The fourth is the step [EveryCharacter from theta integrals and local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/). It takes the admissible words, column systems and pre-systems of Chapter 7, the symbol form of Chapter 5, and Sections 1, 4, 5 and 6 of Chapter 8.

Chapter 1 puts the hermitian curvature operators of a place in normal form. A model algebra of shift operators has a matrix that satisfies the RTT relation of the Yangian, [Theorem 1.1.9](/entry/q-qrtt/), with quantum determinant $q$, [Theorem 1.2.2](/entry/q-qdet/). A start gives the curvature relation, [Theorem 1.3.7](/entry/curv/), and gauge frames put the actual operators in the model form, [Theorem 1.4.11](/entry/second-shift-gauge-holds/). Theorem S, [Theorem 1.8.3](/entry/theorem-s-ring/), says that a start with a few commutation rules is the matrix of a normal form datum. This gives the normal form of the actual operators, [Theorem 1.8.5](/entry/normal-form-from-module-statement/). The analytic part proves a germ expansion with a rate by holomorphic stationary phase, [Theorem 1.5.8](/entry/germ-rate/), the head formula, [Theorem 1.6.7](/entry/pb-head-top-of-mk-top/), and the compact form of Theorem P, [Theorem 1.7.13](/entry/actual-compact-theorem/). The chapter also proves Theorem U, [Theorem 1.13.8](/entry/theorem-u/), and the dual kernel inequality, [Theorem 1.15.7](/entry/dk-radial/).

Chapter 2 has two groups of results. The first is analytic. For a prime $d \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ and a modulus $M$, the interval $(X, X(1+h)]$ contains at least $c'hX/((4dM^2)^A \log X)$ primes represented by a norm form of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ with a congruence condition modulo $M$, for $h$ and $dM^2$ in a suitable range, [Theorem 2.3.4](/entry/norm-primes-short-interval-lower/). The proof combines a ray class family, [Theorem 2.2.4](/entry/exists-l-fam-ray/), with a bound for the trivial class, [Theorem 2.1.11](/entry/trivial-class-short-interval/). A log-free zero density estimate, [Theorem 2.4.11](/entry/zero-density/), then gives $\varphi(M)\psi(u; M, b)$ as $u - \theta u^\beta/\beta$ up to a small relative error, where $\beta$ is a possible exceptional zero, [Theorem 2.6.9](/entry/psi-ap-relative/). The same holds for the number of primes, [Theorem 2.6.10](/entry/primes-rel-holds/). The second group is about volumes. With the measures at the real places and an unfolding identity, [Theorem 2.8.6](/entry/unfolding/), the chapter computes $\mathrm{vol}(x^\perp)/\mathrm{vol}(W)$ for a hermitian space $W$ over a CM field and a vector $x$ of length $\pm 1$, [Theorem 2.9.7](/entry/volume-ratio/). It assumes a one-vector Siegel-Weil formula.

Chapter 3 proves energy bounds for curvature operators at the places of a CM field. The local cores, [Theorem 3.1.4](/entry/local-cores/), include the Gram and support property of the block kernel of a local field, [Theorem 3.2.6](/entry/second-gram-and-vanishing/). Theorem A for towers of kernels, [Theorem 3.3.9](/entry/theorem-a-after/), bounds a sum of squared norms by a product of local factors, and the global cores, [Theorem 3.4.6](/entry/global-cores/), add the bound $2$ for the density. Lemma E, [Theorem 3.5.10](/entry/lemma-e-closed/), gives the limit of an energy along a cutoff, and [Theorem 3.6.7](/entry/lemma-model/) computes it in the model. At the infinite places the chapter proves the polynomial radial relation, [Theorem 3.7.8](/entry/polynomial-radial-holds/), shows that each built place is a model place, [Theorem 3.8.3](/entry/model-place-built-holds/), and proves the top degree identity, [Theorem 3.9.6](/entry/degree-top/). Section 10 assembles the built bounds, [Theorem 3.10.8](/entry/built-bounds-compact/).

Chapter 4 has two groups of results. The first is about the Weyl lattice $\Delta$ and its dual $\Delta^\vee$ in a representation of $\mathfrak{gl}_{p+q}$. A vector of $\Delta^\vee$ of a given weight lands in $\Delta$ after multiplication by an explicit power of $l$, read off from the Young diagrams that the Pieri rule allows. This is the depth skeleton, [Theorem 4.1.4](/entry/depth-skeleton-second-form-holds/), and Lemma D, [Theorem 4.2.7](/entry/h-g/). The weight theorem at the top, [Theorem 4.3.8](/entry/weight-theorem-top/), turns this into an inclusion of spaces of forms. For the weights of the proof the inclusion holds with a short exponent, [Theorem 4.4.7](/entry/down-inclusion-w-real-holds/), and a coarse exponent, [Theorem 4.4.8](/entry/down7-inclusion-w-seven-real-holds/), with weight inputs from lattice links, [Theorem 4.5.8](/entry/weight-inputs-of-linkage-holds/). The second group is a row bound. If a map of corank one glues two terms of a pair, one term is small, [Theorem 4.6.3](/entry/small-term-of-corank-one-pair/). A weighted count over fibres then bounds the sum over all such pairs, [Theorem 4.6.8](/entry/corank-one-bound-of-injective-terms/).

Chapter 5 proves upper bounds on valuations of pairings and lattice functions at an unramified place, and counting statements with explicit constants. The plane Gram theorem, [Theorem 5.2.5](/entry/stmt/), is a lift bound for polynomials over a discrete valuation ring. Statement E, a local lattice estimate, follows from a test bound, [Theorem 5.3.9](/entry/theorem-ii-closed-of-test-bound/). With Proposition 5, [Theorem 5.4.11](/entry/proposition5-holds/), the chapter removes the test bound, [Theorem 5.5.12](/entry/stmt-e/), and one constant serves at all data, [Theorem 5.9.7](/entry/second-theorem-absolute-constant/). Other sections prove the packet statements for formal group systems, [Theorem 5.7.9](/entry/packet-statements-hold/), and the second part of the selection by a count of paths of weights, [Theorem 5.8.10](/entry/second-part-closed/). Sections 11 to 16 prove the closed forms of Theorem A, [Theorem 5.11.3](/entry/theorem-a-closed-form-holds/), and of Theorem I, [Theorem 5.14.10](/entry/theorem-i-closed-form-holds/), the alternative mean value theorem, [Theorem 5.11.5](/entry/alternative-mean-value-theorem/), and the first theorem with constants, [Theorem 5.16.6](/entry/first-theorem-with-constants/).

Chapter 6 proves the estimates on Dirichlet characters and their L-functions that the proof takes from the literature. It gives a log zero density bound, [Theorem 6.1.3](/entry/log-density-bound/), a weak form of the Deuring-Heilbronn phenomenon, [Theorem 6.1.4](/entry/deuring-heilbronn-lossy/), and the prime number theorem for progressions with a relative error, [Theorem 6.2.3](/entry/pnt-progressions-relative-weak-holds/). It proves the truncated explicit formula for $\psi(u, \chi)$, [Theorem 6.3.8](/entry/explicit-formula-closed/), the thin bound for a host, [Theorem 6.4.6](/entry/thin-h/), a mean square bound over primes, [Theorem 6.5.4](/entry/mean-sq-k/), and the existence of good pairs of primes, [Theorem 6.6.9](/entry/pair-ex/). Section 7 bounds the mean number of bad $p$-adic invariant factors on a progression grid, [Theorem 6.7.12](/entry/mc-theorem-mv7/). Section 8 bounds jets at a ramified prime, [Theorem 6.8.4](/entry/jet-bound-ramified-prime/), and proves weighted square integrability on $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$, [Theorem 6.8.5](/entry/square-integrable-of-lipschitz/). It ends with Property W, [Theorem 6.8.8](/entry/property-w-of-estimates/), which bounds the probability that a tuple of random polynomials over a finite field is not good.

Chapter 7 proves integrality and lattice estimates for polynomials in several vector and covector letters for $\mathfrak{gl}_r$. For odd $p$, a lattice over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ with a symmetric pairing has an orthogonal family that misses its rank only by the number of bad vectors, Theorem 7.1.7. The chapter computes the leading term of the Fock pairing, [Theorem 7.2.8](/entry/gamma-leading-term/), and splits the standard lattice of a block into Fock blocks, [Theorem 7.3.6](/entry/fock-blocks-of-weights/). From these it proves the block valuation bound, [Theorem 7.4.4](/entry/block-valuation-over-zp-holds/), an operator at one tie, [Theorem 7.5.4](/entry/tie-core/), and the box bound, [Theorem 7.6.6](/entry/rest-tm-closed/). It reduces the closed form of Theorem A to a window condition and two facts on lattices, [Theorem 7.8.9](/entry/closed-of-minimal-vector/). The second part describes the span of the path polynomials by weights, [Theorem 7.9.7](/entry/admissible-word-basis/), and the integral span of the bracket polynomials, [Theorem 7.9.11](/entry/bracket-polynomial-span/). It ends with the column systems, [Theorem 7.10.6](/entry/rest-cols/), and the pre-systems, [Theorem 7.10.10](/entry/rest-pre/).

Chapter 8 proves lattice, valuation and formal group bounds for sharp restriction. Over a local domain, a power of $p$ moves the dual Weyl lattice of a representation of $\mathrm{GL}_r$ into the Weyl lattice, [Theorem 8.1.3](/entry/sandwich-local/). Section 2 proves restricted saturation, [Theorem 8.2.2](/entry/restricted-saturation-holds/), and compares level lattices, [Theorem 8.2.5](/entry/level-lattice/). Binomial coefficients bound the weight of a branching path, [Theorem 8.3.6](/entry/lemma-t-one/). The chapter classifies the bilinear cocycles of a Lubin-Tate formal group of height $2$, [Theorem 8.4.3](/entry/beta-is-g/), bounds the jets of theta kernels, [Theorem 8.4.7](/entry/kernel-bounds-generic/), and puts these bounds together over Frobenius orbits, [Theorem 8.5.10](/entry/orbit-entry-bounds-hold/). Theorem B, [Theorem 8.6.6](/entry/lattice-theorem-b/), is an inequality between the norms of two flags in a unimodular Hermitian lattice. Section 7 builds the CM host field, [Theorem 8.7.14](/entry/host-der/), and Section 8 proves a $p$-adic theorem of the cube for the sigma function, [Theorem 8.8.12](/entry/theorem-of-cube-of-addition-formulas/).

Chapter 2 uses no other chapter of this volume. Chapter 3 uses the compact form of Theorem P, the head formula, the gauge transfer and the chart equations of Chapter 1. Chapters 4 to 8 depend on each other in both directions. Chapter 8 restates Theorem 6.7.12 as Theorem 8.2.6 and uses Theorem 6.8.4. Its Theorem B gives Theorem 4.6.3, and its path weights feed Chapter 5. Theorem 5.7.9 feeds Theorem 8.5.11. The box bound 7.6.6 uses Theorem 5.11.5, and Theorem 5.11.3 uses Theorem 7.8.9. Section 7 of Chapter 6 uses Sections 2 and 5 of Chapter 7. So a reader can take Chapter 1 before Chapter 3, and Chapter 2 alone. For the rest, a reader can take Chapter 6 first, with Sections 2 and 5 of Chapter 7 before its Section 7. Then come Chapters 8 and 4, with Section 7 of Chapter 5 before Section 5 of Chapter 8. Chapters 7 and 5 come last, read together.

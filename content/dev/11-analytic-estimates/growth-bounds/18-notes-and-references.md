## Notes and references

The chapter feeds the local estimates of Volume 15. The plane Gram theorem of Sections 1 and 2 goes into [the concrete local bound](/entry/link-g-concrete5/), and [Statement E](/entry/stmt-e/) of Sections 3 to 5 into [the proof of the local bound](/entry/loc-proved/). The Hecke counting statement of Section 6 feeds [the admissible family without kernels](/entry/admissible-family-no-kernel/), the packet statements of Section 7 feed [the service bound for sums over primes](/entry/sum-over-primes-service/), and the second part of the selection of Sections 8 and 10 feeds [the local model results](/entry/local-model-results/). Section 9 gives [the link without kernels](/entry/link-g-without-kernels/) and [the closed local link](/entry/link-of-local-closed/). Sections 11 to 16 close [Theorem A](/entry/theorem-a-closed-form-holds/), [Theorem I](/entry/theorem-i-closed-form-holds/) and [the tool statements](/entry/tool-statements-hold/), which together give [the first theorem with constants](/entry/first-theorem-with-constants/). Section 17 gives the symbol form, an input of the extended closed form on column systems in the local record of that theorem, which is used below the step [EveryCharacter from theta integrals and local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/). Through these entries the chapter serves the step [ZeroFreeAll from Green, VWin and HypsAlt](/step/zero-free-all-of-green-v-win/).

The plane Gram theorem of Section 1 bounds a Fock valuation from above. The Fock pairing, for which differentiation is adjoint to multiplication, is the Fischer inner product on polynomials, and its role in the invariant theory of several vector and covector variables is the subject of Howe's paper. Property DIG of Section 1 and the factorial unit decomposition of Section 17 rest on the same arithmetic: by Legendre's formula $v_p(n!) = (n - s_p(n))/(p - 1)$, the factorial of $\rho + qe$ with $q = p^g$ and $\rho < q$ has the valuation of $\rho!\,e!\,(q!)^e$, and the quotient is a $p$-adic unit. That such a unit depends on the high digits $e$ through a function that is close, modulo $p^c$, to a polynomial of low degree is a property of the same kind as the continuity of Morita's $p$-adic gamma function, and the approximation by binomial coefficients $\binom{e}{m}$ is a truncated Mahler expansion. Kummer's theorem on the valuation of binomial coefficients by carries is the form of the digit count that the window condition of Section 11 and the integrality steps of Section 15 use. Property DER, the congruence for divided derivatives of $p$-power substitutions, is at precision $c = 1$ Lucas's congruence $\binom{\rho + q\beta}{\mu + q\kappa} \equiv \binom{\rho}{\mu}\binom{\beta}{\kappa}$ modulo $p$ for $\rho, \mu < q$. The Frobenius step of Section 10 uses the identity $(x + y)^{p^e} \equiv x^{p^e} + y^{p^e}$ modulo $p$.

Statement E of Section 3 and Proposition 5 of Section 4 are local lattice estimates at an unramified place for the specific modules of this proof, and we know no classical source for them. Their setting is classical: the unramified quadratic extension $\mathbb{Q}_q$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and its ring of integers, which is the ring of Witt vectors of $\mathbb{F}_q$ (Serre, *Local Fields*, Chapter II, §6); lattices in $\mathbb{Q}_q^{m+1}$ and the edges $pL \subseteq L'' \subseteq L$; and the dual Weyl modules of $\mathrm{GL}_{m+1}$ realized in polynomial rings (Jantzen, Part II, Chapter 2). The reductions of Section 5 are bookkeeping that removes the test bound step by step.

The Hecke operators of Section 6 are the classical Hecke operators written as correspondences on lattices: $y_v$ sums a function over the sublattices of a given index, as $T(n)$ does for lattices in $\mathbb{C}$ (Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Chapter VII, §5) and as the double coset operators of Shimura, Chapter 3, do in general. The commutativity of the $y_v$ at distinct places, which Section 6 uses to remove one admissibility condition, is the commutativity of local Hecke algebras at distinct primes. The counting statement, a colength bound for a product ideal of Hecke monomials, is specific to the proof.

The formal group systems of Section 7 build theta series of formal groups along a chain of kernel maps. Formal groups with complex multiplication by the integers of a local field are due to Lubin and Tate, and the pending data of Section 7 use the Witt vectors and an embedding of the coefficient field as in Serre, *Local Fields*, Chapter II, §6. The eleven packet statements and the kernel commutation up to units have no classical counterpart that we know of.

The selection of Sections 8 and 10 counts paths of weights. A path in the sense of Section 8 is a sequence of dominant weights in which each step is a horizontal strip, which is Pieri's rule (Macdonald, Chapter I, §5; Fulton and Harris, Lecture 6), and the sequences of all restrictions $\mathrm{GL}_n \supset \mathrm{GL}_{n-1} \supset \cdots$ are the patterns of Gelfand and Tsetlin. The weight of a path is the number of semistandard tableaux of its last shape, which by the Weyl dimension formula (Fulton and Harris, Lecture 6) is the product $\prod_{i<j}(\nu_i - \nu_j + j - i)/(j - i)$ of Section 10. The typicality and the bound on atypical paths are the large deviation part of the count and are specific to the proof.

Theorem A of Section 11 is an averaged count of bad vectors in lattices with a symmetric pairing over $\mathbb{Z}_p$. The underlying fact, that a lattice over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ with $p$ odd has an orthogonal basis obtained by splitting off a vector of minimal valuation, is the Jordan splitting of O'Meara, §91 and §92. The mean value theorem of Section 11 is the mean bad count of the chapter on character sums on a grid.

Theorem I of Sections 12 to 14 gives property $\mathrm{LP}'$ for edges of rank $1$ with a small mean defect. Its proof models the two-letter spaces by bihomogeneous polynomials in two sets of variables and splits them into harmonic parts, a decomposition of the type studied in Howe's paper, and then works with an invariant pairing between dual Weyl modules of complementary weights. The bracket polynomials of Section 16, products of minors in the first rows, are the bideterminants of De Concini, Eisenbud and Procesi, and their span in a fixed weight is the Weyl module in the realization of Green, Chapter 4, and of Fulton, Chapter 8. The contraction maps and the two letter clauses of Section 14 are specific to the proof.

The extremal projector of Section 15 is the projector of Asherova, Smirnov and Tolstoy, written for $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ as a normally ordered product of the factors $p_{ab}$. Its denominators $h_{ab} + k$ are what the admissible discrete valuation rings must invert. The integral forms are Kostant's: the divided powers $E^{(k)}$ generate the $\mathbb{Z}$-form $U_{\mathbb{Z}}$ (Kostant; Humphreys, §26), and admissible lattices are the $U_{\mathbb{Z}}$-stable lattices of Humphreys, §27. The isotypic projectors come from complete reducibility in characteristic $0$ (Humphreys, §6.3), and the denominator bound uses Kummer's theorem and Gauss's lemma on contents of products of polynomials (Lang, Chapter IV, §2). The first theorem with constants of Section 16 assembles these inputs; its local record and its threshold functions are bookkeeping.

The block and symbol forms of Section 17 say that symbols of global theories are orthogonal for the Fock pairing modulo $w^c$, and that the pairing of a symbol with itself factors over the segments. Over a field of characteristic $0$ the first statement is the orthogonality of distinct isotypic components for an invariant pairing; the content of Section 17 is the control of the powers of $p$, through the $\kappa$-numerics and the factorial unit decomposition.

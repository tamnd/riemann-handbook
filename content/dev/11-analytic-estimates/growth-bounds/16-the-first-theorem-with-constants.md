## The first theorem with constants

This section assembles the first theorem of the chapter: Theorem I with constants, in the sense of Section 12, holds for large constants, with thresholds that depend neither on the datum nor on anything but $C_0$. The proof collects the tools of Section 15, the span of bracket polynomials, the closed form of Theorem A of Section 11 and the closed form of Theorem I of Section 14, and passes them through the definition of admissibility for Theorem I. The result is used below the step [EveryCharacter from theta integrals and local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/).

### Bracket polynomials

Let $n \geq 1$ and let $X = (X_{ab})$ be an $n \times n$ matrix of variables. For a dominant weight $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ and an integer $c$ with $\mu_n < c \leq \mu_1$, the *column height* $h_\mu(c)$ is the number of $a$ with $\mu_a \geq c$. A *tableau index* $I$ of shape $\mu$ assigns to each such $c$ a set $I(c) \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$ with $h_\mu(c)$ elements; the *canonical* one has $I(c) = \{1, \dots, h_\mu(c)\}$.

**Definition 5.16.1 (Bracket polynomial).** The *bracket polynomial* of shape $\mu$ and index $I$ is
$$[\mu, I] = (\det X)^{\max(\mu_n, 0)}\prod_{\mu_n < c \leq \mu_1}\det\big(X_{a,b}\big)_{a \leq h_\mu(c),\ b \in I(c)},$$
the product over the columns of the minors with the first $h_\mu(c)$ rows and the columns in $I(c)$.

The group $\mathrm{GL}_n$ acts on the rows of $X$, and the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ acts on polynomials by the corresponding derivations. The canonical bracket polynomial $[\mu, I_0]$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\mu^+ = \mu + \max(-\mu_n, 0)$, the shift making all entries nonnegative when $\mu_n < 0$. For a highest weight vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$, $\nabla(v)$ is as in Section 15: the set of $x$ such that for each $u$ in the Kostant monoid the $\lambda$-weight component of $ux$ lies in $\mathbb{Z}v$.

**Theorem 5.16.2 (Span of bracket polynomials).** For every datum, every $n \geq 1$ and every dominant $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}^n$, the $\mathbb{Z}$-span of the bracket polynomials $[\mu, I]$ in $\mathbb{Q}[X]$ is the set of elements of their $\mathbb{Q}$-span that lie in $\nabla([\mu, I_0])$ for the weight $\mu^+$.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.G.ColumnFamilies.Top.b10`.

*Proof.* This is [the second corollary on highest weights](/entry/highest-weight-corollary-two/), which does not depend on the datum. $\square$

So the bracket polynomials span the largest lattice that meets the highest weight line in $\mathbb{Z}[\mu, I_0]$: the integral form generated by products of minors is as large as the Kostant monoid allows.

### The local record

The first theorem is proved from two hypotheses. The first is the *extremal projector hypothesis*: the conclusion of the Theorem (Extremal projector) of Section 15 holds for every datum. The second is a record of statements on local models.

**Definition 5.16.3 (Local record).** The *local record* is the conjunction of the following statements, each for every datum where a datum enters:

1. the closed form of Theorem I of Section 12 and the closed form of Theorem A of Section 11;
2. the extended closed form on column systems: from the inequality admissions of a datum, a nest of thresholds in $C_c$, then $C_0$, then $C_{E1}, C_{E2}, C_m$, then five constants $C_{Yb}, C_{de}, C_k, C_D, C_b$, giving $C_\kappa, C_t, C_s > 0$ and ten window statements;
3. two window clauses on column systems and the Weyl dimension formula;
4. the Witt identity, two flatness statements and four statements on dual heights: a basis, the dual height inclusion, a perfectness statement and a span statement;
5. the test side statement and a span statement for lower sets;
6. the two Cartan statements of Section 14;
7. four branching statements on spaces of sections of a weight: division by a fixed minor stays among the sections, a lifting statement modulo that minor, a dimension count for the quotient by it, and an injective restriction map;
8. two threshold clauses, nests of thresholds after which the first and third admissibility conditions of an instance hold, and an eighth condition on every instance.

We do not unfold the statements of items 2 to 5, 7 and 8 beyond these descriptions.

**Theorem 5.16.4 (Tools from the local record).** Assume the extremal projector hypothesis and the local record. Then every datum has the twelve tools of the Definition (Admissibility for Theorem I).

*Proof.* The Theorem (Tool statements) of Section 15 gives the projection package, the seven statements on path polynomials and the rank statement on the polynomials $\Psi$. The twelve tools come from these and from the record:

1. the projection criterion, from the comparison of $\Delta(v)$ and $\nabla(v)$ in the projection package;
2. the closure of column families, from the isotypic projector theorem and the corollary on filtered products;
3. the extremal projector, from the hypothesis;
4. the branching statements, from item 7 of the record;
5. the Schur property of global sections, from the first two statements on path polynomials and the rank statement;
6. the span of bracket polynomials, which bundles four statements on column families: the global sections over a field, a discrete valuation ring or $\mathbb{Z}$ are spanned by the brackets, the Theorem (Span of bracket polynomials), a stability statement for lattices of an isotypic representation, and a duality statement for lattices; all four are proved for every datum;
7. the Cartan statements, from item 6;
8. the nested type bound, from the Witt identity and the two flatness statements;
9. the commutation of type decompositions, from the four statements on dual heights;
10. the test side, and 11. the span of saturated lower sets, from item 5;
12. the paired path independence, from the third window clause and the Weyl dimension formula. $\square$

### Admissibility and the first theorem

**Theorem 5.16.5 (Admissibility for large constants).** Assume the extremal projector hypothesis and the local record. Then every datum is admissible for Theorem I for large constants.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.G.first_theorem_admitted_closed`.

*Proof.* Three nests of thresholds are merged. The first is the extended closed form of item 2, applied to the inequality admissions of the datum, which are proved from the two hypotheses. The second joins the closed form of Theorem A with the two threshold clauses of item 8; it has the levels $C_c$, then $C_0$, then $C_{E1}, C_{E2}$, then the five constants. At each level take the maximum of the thresholds of the two nests, and set all five constants $C_{Yb}, \dots, C_b$ equal to $C_H$; this is allowed, since the nests only ask for constants above a threshold.

Fix $C_c \geq 0$, then $C_0$, then $C_{E1}, C_{E2}, C_m$, then $C_H$ above the merged thresholds. The extended closed form gives $C_\kappa, C_t, C_s > 0$ and ten statements, of which six are used. The closed form of Theorem A, at $C_\kappa$ and $C_s + C_\kappa$, gives $C_A > 0$ such that the mean bad share of every instance $X$ is bounded, provided $X$ is admitted and satisfies the window hypotheses. These eleven conditions on $X$ come from the two threshold clauses, from the comparison of $\Delta(v)$ and $\nabla(v)$, from the four branching statements, from the eighth instance condition and from three of the window statements.

The constants of admissibility are $(C_A, C_\kappa, C_t, C_s + C_\kappa)$. The path pair correspondence follows from the bound on the mean bad shares; the edge bound, the rank bound and the lower set bound follow from the remaining window statements; and the twelve tools are the Theorem (Tools from the local record). $\square$

**Theorem 5.16.6 (First theorem with constants).** Assume the extremal projector hypothesis and the local record. Then for every datum there is $T_0 > 0$ such that for every $C_0 \geq T_0$ there are $T_2, C > 0$ with Theorem I with constants $(C_0, C_H, C)$ for every $C_H \geq T_2$.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.G.first_theorem_with_constants`.

*Proof.* The closed form of Theorem I, which is part of the local record, says that admissibility for large constants implies this conclusion. The Theorem (Admissibility for large constants) gives the admissibility. $\square$

**Theorem 5.16.7 (Uniform thresholds).** Assume the extremal projector hypothesis and the local record. Then there is $T_0 > 0$ such that for every $C_0 \geq T_0$ there are $T_2, C > 0$ with Theorem I with constants $(C_0, C_H, C)$ for every $C_H \geq T_2$ and every datum.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.G.first_theorem_uniform_thresholds`.

*Proof.* If there is no datum, take $T_0 = T_2 = C = 1$. Otherwise fix a datum $\mathcal{D}_0$ and take $T_0$, $T_2$ and $C$ from the Theorem (First theorem with constants) at $\mathcal{D}_0$. Theorem I with constants $(C_0, C_H, C)$ is a statement about parameters and instances that does not involve the datum, so the same numbers serve every datum. $\square$

**Theorem 5.16.8 (Thresholds as functions).** Assume the extremal projector hypothesis and the local record. Then there are $T_0 > 0$ and functions $T_2, C\colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ such that for every $C_0 \geq T_0$ we have $T_2(C_0) > 0$, $C(C_0) > 0$, and Theorem I with constants $(C_0, C_H, C(C_0))$ for every $C_H \geq T_2(C_0)$ and every datum.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.G.first_theorem_threshold_functions`.

*Proof.* Apply the Theorem (Uniform thresholds) and choose, for each $C_0 \geq T_0$, numbers $T_2(C_0)$ and $C(C_0)$; extend the functions arbitrarily below $T_0$. $\square$

The two hypotheses are not open in substance. The extremal projector hypothesis is the Theorem (Extremal projector) of Section 15. The first item of the local record is the Theorem (Closed form of Theorem I) of Section 14 and the Theorem (Closed form of Theorem A) of Section 11, The other items are inputs of this section.

The functions $T_2$ and $C$ are used where the first theorem is applied to a family of data with a list of constants $c$: the bound needs $\max(2T_2(c), 2C(c))$ over the list, which requires the thresholds as functions of $C_0$.

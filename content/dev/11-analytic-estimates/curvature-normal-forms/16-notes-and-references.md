## Notes and references

The chapter feeds the analytic inputs at the real places of the step [HypsAlt is nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/) of [the unitary hypotheses](/proof/unitary-hypotheses/). The algebra of Sections 1 to 4 goes in through [the start data at a model level](/entry/start-ok/), [the second shift phase](/entry/second-shift-phase-holds/) and [the second shift gauge](/entry/second-shift-gauge-holds/). The analysis of Sections 5, 6 and 13 gives [the germ expansion with a rate](/entry/germ-rate/), [the head formula](/entry/pb-head-top-of-mk-top/) and [Corollary U2](/entry/corollary-u2-germ-hypothesis/). Section 7 gives [the compact form of Theorem P](/entry/actual-compact-theorem/), Section 8 [the normal form of the actual operators](/entry/normal-form-from-module-statement/), and Sections 10, 14 and 15 give [the word frames](/entry/plus-letter-maps-frames/), [the top lift](/entry/top-lift-exists/) and [the dual kernel inequality](/entry/dk-radial/), which together control the action of words of letters on the radial space. The measure theory of Sections 11 and 12, with [the Bessel lemma](/entry/lemma-b-of-components/) and [the slice induction](/entry/slice-induction-level/), supplies the integrability that all of these use.

The Yangian $\mathrm{Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_2)$ of Section 1, with its defining relation in the RTT form, goes back to Drinfeld and to Faddeev, Reshetikhin and Takhtajan; the standard account is Molev's book, Chapter 1, and the survey of Molev, Nazarov and Olshanski. The proof in Section 1 is not the usual one. Classically one starts from the relation and derives the Gauss decomposition of $T(u)$ into triangular factors; here the matrix $Q(Y)$ is given by explicit formulas in the shift operators, its Gauss factors can be written down, and the relation is checked on the factors at generic points and extended by polynomiality. The interlacing condition on the coordinates of a parameter is the Gelfand-Tsetlin condition for one step of the branching $U(n) \supset U(n - 1)$ (Gelfand and Tsetlin; Knapp, Chapter IX; Molev, Chapter 5). See also [highest weights and branching](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching).

The quantum determinant of Section 2 is the central element of the Yangian of Molev, Chapter 1, which for $\mathfrak{gl}_2$ is the expression $T_{11}(u) T_{22}(u - 1) - T_{21}(u) T_{12}(u - 1)$ in one of its equivalent orderings. In Section 9 the quantum determinant of the actual operators comes from the factorization of a quantum determinant of Capelli type into a left and a right Capelli determinant. Capelli's identity is Capelli's paper of 1887; its modern form, and its relation to multiplicity free actions, is in Howe and Umeda, and its relation to the Yangian is in Molev, Chapter 7. A start, which joins the RTT relation, the quantum determinant and a hermitian symmetry at real points, has no classical name.

Section 3 reverses the direction of Sections 1 and 2: from a start it recovers the relations of the model. The scalar identity it needs is computed in a ring of Hahn series, the series with well ordered support in an ordered group introduced by Hahn in 1907, chosen because it is a field on which the Euler operators act coefficient by coefficient. The gauge frames of Section 4 are a normal form for a connection with hermitian structure in a trivialized bundle, found by successive changes of phase; we know no source for the specific statement. The same holds for Theorem S of Section 8, which says that a start with a few commutation rules is the matrix of a normal form datum.

The stationary phase argument of Section 5 follows the pattern of Hörmander, Section 7.7: nonstationary phase away from the critical points (Theorem 7.7.1) and the expansion at a nondegenerate critical point (Theorem 7.7.5). The phase in Section 5 is holomorphic in several complex variables, and the derivative bounds that Hörmander assumes are replaced by bounds on the size of the function on a complex ball, through the Cauchy estimates. This is the saddle point method of asymptotic analysis (de Bruijn, Chapter 5) in the form of a uniform estimate with a rate. The germ expansion at the long Weyl points has no classical counterpart that we know of.

The top unipotent integral of Section 6 and the fibre integral of Section 12 are twisted integrals over unipotent subgroups of the type of Jacquet's integrals for Whittaker functions (Jacquet). The head formula and the head Jacquet identity of Section 14 are the identities of that type that the level tower needs. The chart equations of Section 14 rest on a fact of Harish-Chandra's theory of invariant eigendistributions: the principal symbols of the invariant differential operators vanish together only on the nilpotent elements. In the language of wave front sets, Hörmander, Theorem 8.3.1, then puts the wave front set of a joint eigenfunction inside the set of nilpotent covectors.

The Haar measure facts of Section 11 are standard: existence and uniqueness of Haar measure and the integration formula for a quotient by a closed subgroup are in Folland, Chapter 2, and the integration formulas for reductive groups, among them the one for the Cartan decomposition, are in Knapp, Chapter VIII. Section 11 does not use the Cartan decomposition for the volume of norm balls. It gets the exact power $\rho^{2(k + r)(r + 1)}$ for $U(r + k, r + 1)$ from a double integral over the group and over frames of the negative space, and a scaling in the off diagonal block $E_+^* g E_-$, which has $(k + r)(r + 1)$ complex entries. The Bessel lemma of Section 11 is a statement about the pairing of a Bessel function of the place with a decaying coefficient, and it is not the Bessel inequality of Hilbert space theory. See [Haar measure](/learn/analysis/#haar-measure) and [Lie groups and Lie algebras](/learn/analysis/#lie-groups-and-lie-algebras).

Theorem U of Section 13 uses calculus with values in a Banach space (Dieudonné, Chapter VIII) and the passage from weak to strong regularity for vector valued functions, of which the holomorphic case is Rudin, *Functional Analysis*, Chapter 3. The derivative cost, the price in decay of one derivative along the Lie algebra, is specific to the proof.

The compact form of Theorem P in Section 7 gives positive forms with [Fourier](/learn/analysis/#the-fourier-transform) decay on a chain of subspaces; its proof is a chain of reductions within the chapter and has no classical model. The chain step of Section 10 restricts the operators of one level to a slice through a twisted Weyl element and finds the operators of the next level conjugated by a matrix of the model form. It is how the RTT relation passes down the levels, in the way that the Yangian acts along the chain $\mathfrak{gl}_n \supset \mathfrak{gl}_{n-1} \supset \cdots$ in the theory of Gelfand-Tsetlin bases (Molev, Chapter 5). The plus and minus letters are products with one site $R$-matrices, as in the fusion of evaluation modules (Molev, Chapter 1).

The duality of Section 15 rests on the identity $T(Y) J T(Y + 1)^{\mathsf T} = q(Y) J$, which for $\mathfrak{gl}_2$ expresses the inverse of $T(Y)$ through its quantum comatrix and the quantum determinant (Molev, Chapter 1). It is what makes a plus letter at $Y$ and a minus letter at $Y + 1$ dual to one another. The analytic part of Section 15 is the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for a positive semidefinite hermitian form (Rudin, *Real and Complex Analysis*, Chapter 4), applied to the form of a word.

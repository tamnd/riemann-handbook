## Haar measure and volumes

The analytic estimates of this chapter are integrals over the group $G_V = U(H)$ and over its unipotent subgroups. This section collects the facts about these measures. The actual operators are hermitian for the Haar measure. The Haar measure of a level splits along one step of the level tower. The Haar measure of a norm ball grows like a fixed power of the radius, with the exact exponent $2(k + r)(r + 1)$. The unipotent measure splits into radical coordinates, an upper triangular block and a smaller unipotent group. Orbits of the unipotent group are controlled by the size of the orbit point. The section ends with the Bessel lemma, whose growth condition $A \ge 2(k + r)(r + 1) + 1$ is set by the volume exponent. All results are used in the step [HypsAlt is nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/).

Background on Haar measure is in [Haar measure](/learn/analysis/#haar-measure), and on unitary groups in [unitary groups](/learn/groups/#unitary-groups).

### The actual operators are hermitian

For a level $i \le r$ let $G_i \subseteq U(H)$ be the subgroup of $g$ with $g e_a = e_a$ and $g e_{\bar a} = e_{\bar a}$ for all $a < i$. It is a unitary group of the same kind, of rank $r - i$. Let $t_{ab}(Y)$ be the actual operators at level $i$.

**Theorem 1.11.1 (Hermitian property of the actual operators).** Let $\mu$ be a Haar measure on $G_i$, let $z, z'$ lie in the middle block, and let $f_1, f_2$ be smooth functions with compact support on $G_i$. Then for every $Y \in \mathbb C$ and all $a, b \in \{1, 2\}$,
$$\int_{G_i} \bigl(t_{ab}(Y) f_1\bigr)\, \overline{f_2}\; d\mu = \int_{G_i} f_1\; \overline{t_{ba}(\bar Y) f_2}\; d\mu.$$

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.hermitian_haar_invariance`

*Proof.* The operators $t_{ab}(Y)$ are polynomials in the right invariant derivatives $R_X$, $X \in \mathfrak g_i$, with coefficients that are functions of $g$. First, each $R_X$ is skew for $\mu$: $\int (R_X f_1) \overline{f_2}\, d\mu = -\int f_1 \overline{R_X f_2}\, d\mu$, because the flow of $R_X$ is translation by $\exp(tX)$ and $\mu$ is invariant. Second, the algebra generated by the $R_X$ and the multiplication operators is closed under this formal adjoint, and the adjoint of a quantum minor is the quantum minor with rows and columns exchanged and the spectral parameter conjugated. This uses the generation of the hermitian algebra, the star rule for quantum minors, the covariance of the contractions with $z, z'$, the rule that moves a quantum minor past a function, the Leibniz rule in $Y$, and the commutation relations and level preservation of the generators $E_{ab}$. Applying these rules to the bordered minor $t_{ab}(Y)$ gives $t_{ba}(\bar Y)$. $\square$

### One step of the level tower

Let $r' \ge 0$, let $G = G_V(1 + r')$ be the group of rank $1 + r'$ and $G_1 = G_V(r')$ the group of rank $r'$, embedded as the first level. For a Heisenberg parameter $h$ let $\eta(h)$ be the corresponding element of the Heisenberg unipotent group of the first step. For $c \in \mathbb C^\times$ let $w_0(c)$ be the twisted Weyl element of the first step. Put
$$\operatorname{os}(h_1, c, g_1, h_2) = \eta(h_1)^{-1}\, w_0(c)\, \iota(g_1)\, \eta(h_2).$$

**Theorem 1.11.2 (Stabilizer integration formula).** Let $\mu_G$ and $\mu_{G_1}$ be Haar measures on $G$ and $G_1$. Then there is $C > 0$ such that for every integrable $f$ on $G$,
$$\int_G f\, d\mu_G = C \int_{\mathbb C} |c|^{2(2r' + k + 1)} \iiint f\bigl(\operatorname{os}(h_1, c, g_1, h_2)\bigr)\, dh_1\, dh_2\, d\mu_{G_1}(g_1)\, dc,$$
where $dh_1$, $dh_2$ and $dc$ are Lebesgue measures.

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.stabilizer_integration_formula`

*Proof.* The map $\operatorname{os}$ sends the parameters into $G$, and it is a diffeomorphism from the parameter space onto an open subset of $G$ whose complement is a null set: it parametrizes the open Bruhat cell of the first step. Left translation by the Heisenberg group and right translation by $G_1$ act on the parameters by translations in $h_1$, $h_2$ and $g_1$. So the pull back of $\mu_G$ is invariant in these variables, and it equals Lebesgue measure in $h_1$, $h_2$ times $\mu_{G_1}$ times a measure in $c$. The density of this measure in $c$ is the Jacobian of the map in the variable $c$, which is the power $|c|^{2(2r' + k + 1)}$ up to the constant $C$. $\square$

### Volumes of norm balls

For a matrix $g$ let $\|g\|$ be its Frobenius norm. The ball volume uses a frame for $H$.

**Definition 1.11.3 (Frame for the form).** A *frame* for $H$ is a pair of matrices $(E_+, E_-)$ of sizes $n \times |P|$ and $n \times |Q|$ such that
$$E_\pm^* E_\pm = 1, \qquad E_+^* E_- = 0, \qquad E_+ E_+^* + E_- E_-^* = 1, \qquad E_+ E_+^* - E_- E_-^* = H.$$
So the columns of $E_+$ and $E_-$ are orthonormal bases of the positive and the negative space of $H$. For $\rho \ge 0$ put
$$B_\rho = \{ g : \operatorname{tr}\bigl((E_+^* g E_-)(E_+^* g E_-)^*\bigr) \le \rho^2 \}.$$

**Lemma 1.11.4 (Scaling of the ball volume).** Let $(E_+, E_-)$ be a frame for $H$, and let $\mu$ be a measure on the matrices that is concentrated on $U(H)$ and is left and right invariant under $U(H)$. Then for every $\rho \ge 0$,
$$\mu(B_\rho) = \mu\bigl(\operatorname{inv}^{-1}(B_1)\bigr)\, \rho^{2 |P| |Q|},$$
where $\operatorname{inv}(g) = g^{-1}$.

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.HermitianFormFactor.vol_core`

*Proof.* For a matrix $Y$ of size $n \times |Q|$ put $T(Y) = -Y^* H Y$ and $B(Y) = E_+^* Y$. Let $K_\rho$ be the set of $Y$ with $T(Y)$ positive definite, $\operatorname{tr} T(Y) \le 1$ and $\operatorname{tr}(B T^{-1} B^*) \le \rho^2$. Consider the double integral
$$I(\rho) = \iint 1_{K_1}(Y)\, 1_{K_\rho}(g Y)\, dY\, d\mu(g).$$
Since $T(gY) = T(Y)$ for $g \in U(H)$, the condition on $gY$ is a condition on $E_+^* g Y$. Integrate first over $g$ with $Y$ fixed: right invariance of $\mu$ moves $Y$ to the base point $E_-$, and $I(\rho) = L(1)\, \mu(B_\rho)$, where $L(\rho)$ is the Lebesgue measure of $K_\rho$. Integrate first over $Y$ with $g$ fixed: left invariance and inversion give $I(\rho) = L(\rho)\, \mu(\operatorname{inv}^{-1} B_1)$. A linear change of coordinates gives $L(\rho) = c\, M(\rho)$, where $M(\rho)$ is the volume of the model set in the coordinates $(T, B)$, and the scaling $B \mapsto \rho B$ gives $M(\rho) = \rho^{2|P||Q|} M(1)$, since $B$ has $|P||Q|$ complex entries. Finally $M(1) > 0$, since the model set contains an open slice. Dividing by $L(1) = c M(1) > 0$ gives the claim. $\square$

**Theorem 1.11.5 (Exact ball volume).** Let $\mu$ be a Haar measure on $G_V$ of rank $r$. There is $C > 0$ such that for every $\rho \ge 0$,
$$\mu\{ g \in G_V : \|g\|^2 \le n + 4\rho^2 \} = C\, \rho^{2(k + r)(r + 1)}.$$

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.HermitianFormFactor.volExact`

*Proof.* The form $H$ has a frame with $|P| = k + r$ and $|Q| = r + 1$. For $g \in U(H)$, write $g$ in the frame as a block matrix. The relations $g^* H g = H$ give $\|g\|^2 = n + 4 \operatorname{tr}\bigl((E_+^* g E_-)(E_+^* g E_-)^*\bigr)$. So the set in the claim equals $B_\rho$ up to a null set. Apply the [scaling of the ball volume](/entry/vol-core/), with $C = \mu(\operatorname{inv}^{-1} B_1)$, which is positive since this set has nonempty interior in $G_V$. $\square$

**Corollary 1.11.6 (Ball volume bound).** Let $\mu$ be a Haar measure on $G_V$. There is $C$ such that $\mu\{ g : \|g\| \le R \} \le C R^{2(k + r)(r + 1)}$ for every $R \ge 1$.

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.HermitianFormFactor.volSharp` (also `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.norm_ball_volume_sharp`, a second name).

*Proof.* For $R \ge 1$ the ball $\{\|g\| \le R\}$ lies in the set of the [exact ball volume](/entry/vol-exact/) with $\rho = R$, since $\|g\|^2 \le R^2 \le n + 4R^2$. $\square$

### The unipotent measure

Let $N_\rho$ be the maximal unipotent group of $G_V$ of rank $\rho$, with its standard measure $\mu_N(\rho)$. For $j \ge 0$ let $\mathrm{RC}(j, \rho)$ be the space of radical coordinates of the step from rank $\rho$ to rank $j + \rho$, with Lebesgue measure $\mu_{RC}$, and let $\mathrm{UT}_j$ be the coordinates of the strictly upper triangular matrices of size $j$. Define
$$\Psi(y, u, n) = R(y)\, \mu_P(\operatorname{upper}(u))\, \iota(n),$$
where $R(y)$ is the radical element with coordinates $y$, $\operatorname{upper}(u)$ the upper unipotent matrix with entries $u$, $\mu_P$ its embedding in the Levi factor, and $\iota$ the embedding of $N_\rho$.

**Proposition 1.11.7 (Splitting of the unipotent measure).** For all $j, \rho \ge 0$,
$$\mu_N(j + \rho) = \Psi_*\bigl(\mu_{RC}(j, \rho) \times \mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{UT}_j) \times \mu_N(\rho)\bigr).$$

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.Dx2.SplitF.split_all`

*Proof.* By induction on $j$. For $j = 0$ both sides are $\mu_N(\rho)$. For the step from $j$ to $j + 1$, write $N_{j + 1 + \rho}$ as the semidirect product of its radical with the unipotent group of rank $j + \rho$, apply the case $j$ to the second factor, and regroup the coordinates. Each map in the chain is a unipotent change of coordinates, so it preserves Lebesgue measure. $\square$

### Orbits of the unipotent group

Let $\Omega$ be the set of $g \in G_V$ with all leading minors $D_l(g) \ne 0$ for $l \le r$ and $G_f(g) > 0$.

**Proposition 1.11.8 (Orbit bound).** Let $K \subseteq \Omega$ be compact. Then there is $C \ge 0$ such that for every $g \in K$ and all $n, n' \in N$,
$$\|n\| \le C\, \|n^{-1} g n'\|^{2r + 1}, \qquad \|n'\| \le C\, \|n^{-1} g n'\|^{2r + 1}.$$

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.WeakDominationMajorants.Orb.orbit`

*Proof.* Put $x = n^{-1} g n'$. The entries of $n$ and $n'$ are recovered from $x$ by Cramer's rule: they are quotients of minors of $x$ by leading minors that are the same for $x$ and $g$, because $n$ and $n'$ are unipotent. The numerators are polynomials of degree at most $2r + 1$ in the entries of $x$, so they are bounded by a constant times $\|x\|^{2r+1}$ when $\|x\| \ge 1$, and $\|x\|$ is bounded below on $K$ since $x \in G_V$. The denominators are leading minors of $g$ and are bounded below on $K$ by compactness, since they do not vanish on $\Omega$. $\square$

### The Bessel lemma

Let $S$ be real place data of rank $r$, let $\Xi$ be a parameter set, and let $A \ge 2(k + r)(r + 1) + 1$. The Bessel lemma concerns the pairing of a Bessel function with a coefficient of class $CC$ of order $A$ (the functions whose derivatives up to a fixed order decay like $\|g\|^{-A}$).

**Theorem 1.11.9 (Bessel lemma).** Assume an integration formula for the Haar measure, the invariance of the pairing under conjugation, a Jacobian formula for dilations, the integrability of functions of class $CC$ of order $A$, a bound for the coefficients, and the integrability in $\Xi$. Then the pairing satisfies a continuity bound, equals the limit of its truncations, and satisfies the adjoint relation.

Lean: `Formalism.FourierDecayAlt.P.lemma_B_of_components`

*Proof.* The three conclusions are reduced, by fifteen separate steps, to the six hypotheses. The continuity bound comes from the coefficient bound and the integrability of functions of class $CC$ of order $A$, by dominated convergence. The same domination gives the equality of the pairing with the limit of its truncations. The adjoint relation comes from the conjugation invariance and the Jacobian formula, by a change of variables in the integration formula. $\square$

The threshold for $A$ is the one that the [ball volume bound](/entry/vol-sharp/) suggests: on the shell $2^m \le \|g\| < 2^{m+1}$ a function bounded by $\|g\|^{-A}$ is at most $2^{-mA}$, the shell has volume at most $C\, 2^{(m+1) \cdot 2(k + r)(r + 1)}$, and the sum over $m$ converges when $A > 2(k + r)(r + 1)$.

**Remark 1.11.10 (Formalization).** The hermitian property is proved by a lemma that takes eight hypotheses and ignores all of them, returning the result proved earlier ([OperatorPreservesFusionForms.lean](lean:Formalism/HermitianCurvature/HermitianCurvatureProofs/OperatorPreservesFusionForms.lean#L60)); the theorem itself is declared with its full name outside any namespace. The stabilizer formula is assembled from eleven inputs, and the Bessel lemma from fifteen; the six hypotheses and three conclusions of the Bessel lemma are named statements that we did not unfold, so its statement above gives only their content as their names describe it. The ball volume bound is stated for $R \ge 1$; its constant is not claimed to be positive.

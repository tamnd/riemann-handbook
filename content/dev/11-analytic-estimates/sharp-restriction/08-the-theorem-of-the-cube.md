## The theorem of the cube

This section proves a $p$-adic theorem of the cube for the logarithm of a normalized sigma function of a CM elliptic curve with good reduction. The result says that the function $\phi$ built from the sigma function is a quadratic function on the formal group, up to a term whose exponential is integral. It is proved from two classical identities for the sigma function, written in the formal parameter: the addition formula and the three-point formula of Frobenius and Stickelberger. These two identities are hypotheses. The theorem feeds [Theorem 1.8.3 of Volume 13](/entry/cube-add-int-be-biadd/), which uses it to make the pairing $\phi(a + b) - \phi(a) - \phi(b)$ biadditive up to integral terms.

### The curves

Let $D$ be a datum of the kind used in the frame constructions. It contains host axioms in the sense of [Corollary 8.7.15](/entry/host-ax/), so it has the CM field $E$, the imaginary quadratic field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d}) \subseteq E$, and a complex embedding $\varphi_0$ of $E$ in the CM type. For a lattice $\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ let $g_2(\Lambda)$ and $g_3(\Lambda)$ be the usual invariants and $\Delta(\Lambda) = g_2^3 - 27 g_3^2$; see [lattices and the Weierstrass function](/learn/elliptic-curves/#lattices-and-the-weierstrass-function).

**Definition 8.8.1 (Curve ideals and their lattices).** A curve ideal is a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{c}$ of $\mathcal{O}_K$ whose norm is prime to $2d$. For $\delta \in \mathbb{C}$ let $\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}, \delta}$ be the subgroup of $\mathbb{C}$ generated by the numbers $\delta \varphi_0(c)$, $c \in \mathfrak{c}$. We say that $\delta$ is a twelfth root for $\mathfrak{c}$ when $\delta^{12} = \Delta(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}, 1})$.

Since $\Delta(t\Lambda) = t^{-12} \Delta(\Lambda)$, the lattice $\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}, \delta}$ has $\Delta = 1$ when $\delta$ is a twelfth root for $\mathfrak{c}$. The curve $\mathbb{C}/\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c},\delta}$ has complex multiplication by $\mathcal{O}_K$; see [complex multiplication](/learn/elliptic-curves/#complex-multiplication).

Fix a prime $p$ and an embedding $\lambda$ of the algebraic numbers $\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ into $\mathbb{C}_p$. For a complex number $x$ we write $\lambda(x)$ for its image when $x$ is algebraic, and $\lambda(x) = 0$ otherwise. A number $x \in \mathbb{C}_p$ is integral when $|x|_p \le 1$, and a power series is integral when all its coefficients are integral.

**Definition 8.8.2 (The sigma function).** For a lattice $\Lambda$ with covolume $A$, the normalized sigma function $\sigma^*_\Lambda$ is an entire odd function $G$ with $G'(0) = 1$ such that for all $\omega \in \Lambda$ and $z \in \mathbb{C}$,
$$G(z + \omega) \exp\Big(-\frac{\pi z \bar{\omega}}{A} - \frac{\pi \omega \bar{\omega}}{2A}\Big) = \pm G(z),$$
with the sign $+$ when $\omega/2 \in \Lambda$ and $-$ otherwise. We write $\sigma^*_\lambda$ for the power series in $\mathbb{C}_p[[T]]$ whose coefficients are the images under $\lambda$ of the Taylor coefficients of $\sigma^*_\Lambda$ at $0$.

**Definition 8.8.3 (Formal data).** A formal datum $g$ at $p$ consists of an embedding $\lambda$, a lattice $\Lambda$, a change of variables $C$ over $\mathbb{C}_p$ with scale $u$, and power series $w(T)$ and $e(T)$ over $\mathbb{C}_p$. Let $W$ be $C$ applied to the curve
$$y^2 = x^3 - \lambda\big(g_2(\Lambda)/4\big) x - \lambda\big(g_3(\Lambda)/4\big).$$
The datum is good when:

- $W$ has integral coefficients $a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4, a_6$ and its discriminant is a unit;
- $w$ satisfies $w = 1 + a_1 T w + a_2 T^2 w + a_3 T^3 w^2 + a_4 T^4 w^2 + a_6 T^6 w^3$;
- $e$ has constant term $0$ and $\ell(e(T)) = T$, where $\ell$ is the formal logarithm of $W$ built from $w$.

The series $T^3 w(T)$ is the series $w(z)$ of the formal group of $W$ in [Silverman, Ch. IV, §1], and $\ell$ is the integral of a series $\omega_W$ built from $w$, $a_1$ and $a_3$, which is the invariant differential in this parameter. On power series $a, b$ with constant term $0$ the group law and its difference are
$$a \oplus b = e\big(\ell(a) + \ell(b)\big), \qquad a \ominus b = e\big(\ell(a) - \ell(b)\big).$$
See [formal group laws](/learn/formal-groups/#formal-group-laws) and [the logarithm](/learn/formal-groups/#the-invariant-derivation-and-the-logarithm).

**Definition 8.8.4 (The function $\phi$).** Let $g$ be a formal datum and $\varepsilon = u^{-1}$. Let $E_\Phi(T)$ be the power series whose coefficient of $T^n$ is $\varepsilon^{-1}$ times the coefficient of $T^{n+1}$ in $\sigma^*_\lambda(\varepsilon \ell(T))$, so that $E_\Phi(T) = \sigma^*_\lambda(\varepsilon\ell(T))/(\varepsilon T)$. Put
$$\phi(T) = -\log E_\Phi(T),$$
where $\log F = \sum_{n \ge 1} (-1)^{n+1} (F - 1)^n / n$. The constant term of $E_\Phi$ is $1$ for every good datum at a curve ideal, so $\phi$ is defined.

**Definition 8.8.5 (Congruence up to units).** For power series $f, h$ in several variables over $\mathbb{C}_p$, we write $f \simeq h$ when $f - h$ has constant term $0$ and $\exp(f - h)$ is integral.

So $f \simeq h$ says that $f$ and $h$ differ by the logarithm of an integral series with constant term $1$.

**Definition 8.8.6 (Admissible point).** A formal datum $g$ at $p$ is admissible for $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$ when $\mathfrak{c}$ is a curve ideal with norm prime to $p$, $\delta$ is a twelfth root for $\mathfrak{c}$, the lattice of $g$ is $\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}, \delta}$, and $g$ is good.

### The two classical identities

For the Weierstrass functions of a complex lattice, the addition formula reads
$$\wp(u) - \wp(v) = -\frac{\sigma(u + v)\,\sigma(u - v)}{\sigma(u)^2 \sigma(v)^2}.$$

**Theorem 8.8.7 (Addition formula for the sigma function).** [Whittaker and Watson, Ch. XX] For every lattice, the sigma function and the Weierstrass function satisfy the addition formula above.

In the formal parameter, with $x_i$ the variables and $w_i = w(x_i)$, this gives the following property of $D$.

**Definition 8.8.8 (The $p$-adic addition formula).** The $p$-adic addition formula holds for $D$ when for every prime $p$ and every formal datum $g$ admissible for some $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$,
$$\begin{aligned} &(x_0 \oplus x_1)(x_0 \ominus x_1)\, E_\Phi(x_0 \oplus x_1)\, E_\Phi(x_0 \ominus x_1) \\ &\quad = \big(x_0^2 w_1^{-1} - x_1^2 w_0^{-1}\big) E_\Phi(x_0)^2 E_\Phi(x_1)^2. \end{aligned}$$

**Theorem 8.8.9 (Frobenius and Stickelberger).** [Whittaker and Watson, Ch. XX] For three complex numbers, the determinant with rows $(1, \wp(u_i), \wp'(u_i))$ is a constant multiple of $\sigma(u_0 + u_1 + u_2) \prod \sigma(u_i - u_j) / \prod \sigma(u_i)^3$, where the product in the numerator is over the three cyclic differences.

**Definition 8.8.10 (The $p$-adic Frobenius and Stickelberger formula).** Let
$$R = x_0^3 w_0 (x_2 - x_1) + x_1^3 w_1 (x_0 - x_2) + x_2^3 w_2 (x_1 - x_0)$$
in three variables, and let $s = x_0 \oplus x_1 \oplus x_2$ and $P(a) = a\, E_\Phi(a)$. The $p$-adic Frobenius and Stickelberger formula holds for $D$ when for every prime $p$ and every formal datum $g$ admissible for some $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$,
$$\begin{aligned} &R \cdot E_\Phi(x_0)^3 E_\Phi(x_1)^3 E_\Phi(x_2)^3 \\ &\quad = w_0 w_1 w_2 \, P(s)\, P(x_0 \ominus x_1)\, P(x_1 \ominus x_2)\, P(x_2 \ominus x_0). \end{aligned}$$

### The cube identities

**Theorem 8.8.11 (Second cube identity).** Let $D$ be a datum for which the $p$-adic Frobenius and Stickelberger formula holds. Then for every prime $p$ and every formal datum admissible for some $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$,
$$\begin{aligned} &\phi(x_0 \oplus x_1 \oplus x_2) + \phi(x_0 \ominus x_1) + \phi(x_1 \ominus x_2) + \phi(x_2 \ominus x_0) \\ &\quad \simeq 3\big(\phi(x_0) + \phi(x_1) + \phi(x_2)\big). \end{aligned}$$

*Proof.* Fix $p$ and an admissible datum. The constant term of $E_\Phi$ is $1$, and the series $x_0 \oplus x_1$ is integral. From this, a factorization lemma for the formal group gives an integral series $n$ with constant term $1$ and
$$R = s\,(x_0 \ominus x_1)(x_1 \ominus x_2)(x_2 \ominus x_0)\, n.$$
Put this into the formula of Frobenius and Stickelberger. The factors $s$ and $x_i \ominus x_j$ appear on both sides and cancel, and what is left is
$$\frac{E_\Phi(s) \prod E_\Phi(x_i \ominus x_j)}{\prod_i E_\Phi(x_i)^3} = \frac{n}{w_0 w_1 w_2}.$$
Each $w_i$ is integral with constant term $1$, by its recursion, so $w_0 w_1 w_2 / n$ is integral. Taking $-\log$, the left side of the identity minus the right side is $-\log(n / (w_0 w_1 w_2))$, and its exponential is $w_0 w_1 w_2 / n$. $\square$

**Theorem 8.8.12 (Theorem of the cube).** Let $D$ be a datum for which the $p$-adic addition formula and the $p$-adic Frobenius and Stickelberger formula hold. Then for every prime $p$ and every formal datum admissible for some $(\mathfrak{c}, \delta)$,
$$\begin{aligned} &\phi(x_0 \oplus x_1 \oplus x_2) - \phi(x_0 \oplus x_1) - \phi(x_1 \oplus x_2) - \phi(x_2 \oplus x_0) \\ &\quad + \phi(x_0) + \phi(x_1) + \phi(x_2) \simeq 0. \end{aligned}$$

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.Ext.CutA.theorem_of_cube_of_addition_formulas`

*Proof.* First, the addition formula gives the parallelogram identity
$$\phi(x_0 \oplus x_1) + \phi(x_0 \ominus x_1) \simeq 2\phi(x_0) + 2\phi(x_1).$$
Indeed, since $x_0 \oplus x_1$ is integral, a second factorization lemma writes $x_0^2 w_1^{-1} - x_1^2 w_0^{-1} = (x_0 \oplus x_1)(x_0 \ominus x_1)\, n_2$ with $n_2$ integral of constant term $1$. Put into the addition formula, this gives $E_\Phi(x_0 \oplus x_1) E_\Phi(x_0 \ominus x_1) = n_2 E_\Phi(x_0)^2 E_\Phi(x_1)^2$, so the difference of the two sides of the parallelogram identity is $-\log n_2$, with exponential $1/n_2$. Second, Theorem (Second cube identity) gives the three-variable identity. A general lemma on functions on a formal group shows that the parallelogram identity and the second cube identity together imply the cube identity. $\square$

The relation $\simeq$ is preserved by sums and differences, since the exponential turns them into products and quotients of integral series with constant term $1$. So the theorem says that
$$\beta(a, b) = \phi(a \oplus b) - \phi(a) - \phi(b)$$
satisfies $\beta(x_0 \oplus x_1, x_2) \simeq \beta(x_0, x_2) + \beta(x_1, x_2)$, since the difference of the two sides is the left side of the theorem. This is the form in which [Theorem 1.8.3 of Volume 13](/entry/cube-add-int-be-biadd/) uses it.

**Remark 8.8.13 (Formalization).** Both theorems are in [TheoremOfCubeOfAdditionFormulas.lean](lean:Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GlobalRankLatticeProofs/UnitTraceSumBounds/TheoremOfCubeOfAdditionFormulas.lean#L33). The two formulas are hypotheses of the theorems; they are defined in [AdditionIdentityOfSigmaFormula.lean](lean:Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GlobalRankLatticeProofs/UnitTraceSumBounds/AdditionIdentityOfSigmaFormula.lean#L11) and [AdditionIdentityOfSigmaFormulaUnit.lean](lean:Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GlobalRankLatticeProofs/UnitTraceSumBounds/AdditionIdentityOfSigmaFormulaUnit.lean#L575), and no proof of them is given. The normalized sigma function is defined by a choice: it is some function with the stated properties if one exists, and an arbitrary function otherwise ([IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean](lean:Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L238)). The map $\lambda$ sends every transcendental number to $0$ (line 178 of the same file), so the series $\sigma^*_\lambda$ and the curve $W$ see only the algebraic Taylor coefficients and invariants. The name of the second cube identity says "second", but it is the three-variable identity, and the parallelogram identity is called the first. We do not unfold the factorization lemmas, the integrality of the group law, the general lemma on functions on a formal group, or the full datum $D$ beyond its host field and its embedding.

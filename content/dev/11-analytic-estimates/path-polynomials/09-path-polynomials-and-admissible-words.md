## Path polynomials and admissible words

This section is about polynomials in several vector and covector letters for $\mathfrak{gl}_n$. A sequence of dominant weights that grows or shrinks by a horizontal strip at each letter is a path, and a path with a standard filling gives a product of column polynomials. Over every commutative ring these path polynomials form a basis. We prove two consequences. The first describes the span of the path polynomials whose weight after the first $i$ letters lies in a given dominant lower set, in terms of the weights of the module the polynomial generates; we call this the theorem on admissible words. The second turns it into a description of the polynomials with given partial valuations and partial top weights, over a discrete valuation ring and over its residue field. We also prove the integral form of the bracket polynomials, which is used by [the bracket lemma](/entry/b10/). With the path basis theorem and five statements imported from other chapters, the second result makes up the splitting package used by [the tool statements](/entry/tool-statements-hold/), and through them by the step [theta local matching](/step/theta-local-matching/).

We use [partitions and Young tableaux](/learn/combinatorics/#partitions-and-young-tableaux), [minors](/learn/combinatorics/#minors-and-determinant-identities), [polynomials on matrices](/learn/combinatorics/#polynomials-on-matrices) and [highest weights](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching). The dominance order on $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is the order of partial sums: $\nu \preceq \mu$ if $\nu_0 + \dots + \nu_k \leq \mu_0 + \dots + \mu_k$ for all $k$, with equality for $k = n - 1$.

**Definition 7.9.1 (Letters and their polynomials).** A *word* is a sequence of $N$ letters, each a *vector* or a *covector*. Over a commutative ring $R$ the letter $i$ has variables $x_{i,0}, \dots, x_{i,n-1}$, and $\operatorname{Pol}_R(B)$ is the module of polynomials of degree $B_i$ in the variables of letter $i$, for each $i$. The operator $E_{ab}$ acts on letter $i$ as $x_{i,a} \partial_{i,b}$ if $i$ is a vector and as $-x_{i,b} \partial_{i,a}$ if $i$ is a covector, and on a set $S$ of letters as the sum over $i \in S$. A monomial has weight $\sum_{i \in S} \pm \deg_{i}$, where $\deg_i \in \mathbb{N}^n$ is its degree in the variables of letter $i$ and the sign is $-$ for covectors. The divided powers $E^{(k)}_{ab} = E_{ab}^k / k!$ for $a \neq b$ and the binomials $\binom{E_{aa}}{k}$ preserve polynomials over $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Definition 7.9.2 (Paths).** For $\nu, \mu \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ and $b \geq 0$, $\mu / \nu$ is a *horizontal strip of size $b$* if $\nu_a \leq \mu_a$ for all $a$, $\mu_{a+1} \leq \nu_a$ for all $a$, and $|\mu| - |\nu| = b$. A *path* for the word and the degrees $B$ is a sequence $\mu_0, \dots, \mu_N$ of dominant weights with $\mu_0 = 0$ such that $\mu_{i+1} / \mu_i$ is a horizontal strip of size $B_i$ if letter $i$ is a vector and $\mu_i / \mu_{i+1}$ is one if it is a covector.

**Definition 7.9.3 (Column polynomials).** Fix a path $\mu$ and an integer column $c$. The *height* of $\mu_j$ at $c$ is $h_j(c) = \#\{m : c \leq \mu_j(m)\}$. For a vector $x = (x_0, \dots, x_{n-1})$ and a function $K$ on subsets of $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, put
$$(x \wedge K)(J) = \sum_{m \in J} (-1)^{\#\{j \in J : j > m\}} x_m K(J \setminus m), \qquad (x \lrcorner K)(J) = \sum_{m \notin J} (-1)^{\#\{j \in J : j > m\}} x_m K(J \cup m).$$
The *column polynomial* $K_c$ is defined by $K_c^{(0)}(J) = 1$ for $J = \emptyset$ and $0$ otherwise, and $K_c^{(j+1)} = x_j \wedge K_c^{(j)}$ or $x_j \lrcorner K_c^{(j)}$ or $K_c^{(j)}$, where $x_j$ is the vector of variables of letter $j$: the wedge is used if the height goes up at step $j$ and $c \geq 1$, or goes down and $c \leq 0$; the contraction in the two other cases; and nothing is done if the height does not change. Put $K_c = K_c^{(N)}$.

**Definition 7.9.4 (Path polynomials).** A *filling* of $\mu$ is a family $I = (I_c)_{c \in \mathbb{Z}}$ of subsets of $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$. The *path polynomial* is $\Psi(\mu, I) = \prod_{c} K_c(I_c)$, over $|c| \leq T$ with $T = \sum_{i, m} |\mu_i(m)|$. The filling is *standard* if $I_c = \emptyset$ for $|c| > T$, $|I_c| = h_N(c)$ for $c \geq 1$ and $|I_c| = n - h_N(c)$ for $c \leq 0$, and, writing $\tau_c = I_c$ for $c \geq 1$ and $\tau_c$ for the complement of $I_c$ for $c \leq 0$, we have $\#\{t' \in \tau_{c+1} : t' \leq t\} \leq \#\{t' \in \tau_c : t' \leq t\}$ for all $c$ and $t$. A *standard pair* is a path with a standard filling.

For $|c| \leq T$ with constant height the column polynomial is the indicator of $\emptyset$, and a standard filling has $I_c = \emptyset$ there, so only the columns where the height changes contribute. The input from other chapters is the path basis theorem: over every commutative ring $R$, the path polynomials of the standard pairs are linearly independent and span $\operatorname{Pol}_R(B)$.

**Definition 7.9.5 (Generated modules and partial weights).** For a polynomial $G$ over a field $F$ and a set $S$ of letters, $\langle G \rangle_S$ is the $F$-span of the images of $G$ under the monoid generated by the divided powers and binomials of the letters of $S$, and $\operatorname{wt}_S(G)$ is the set of weights $\lambda$ for which $\langle G \rangle_S$ has a nonzero weight vector of weight $\lambda$. After a generic change of coordinates on the letters of $S$, let $v_S(G)$ be the least last coordinate and $\lambda_S(G)$ the largest first coordinate of the $S$-weights of the monomials of $G$.

**Definition 7.9.6 (Dominant lower sets).** A set $\pi$ of dominant weights is a *dominant lower set* if $\nu \in \pi$ whenever $\nu$ is dominant and $\nu \preceq \mu$ for some $\mu \in \pi$.

**Theorem 7.9.7 (Admissible words).** Let $F$ be a field, $i \leq N$ and $\pi$ a dominant lower set. For $G \in \operatorname{Pol}_F(B)$ the following are equivalent:

1. $G$ lies in the span of the $\Psi(\mu, I)$ over the standard pairs with $\mu_i \in \pi$;
2. every dominant weight in $\operatorname{wt}_{\{j < i\}}(G)$ lies in $\pi$.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.G.ColumnFamilies.admissible_word_basis`.

*Proof.* (1) implies (2). The set of $G$ satisfying (2) is a subspace, because $\langle G + G' \rangle \subseteq \langle G \rangle + \langle G' \rangle$ and weights of a sum of modules are weights of a summand. So it suffices to take $G = \Psi(\mu, I)$ with $\mu_i \in \pi$. The factors $K_c$ restricted to the first $i$ letters are built from wedges and contractions with these letters, and the operators of the first $i$ letters move $\Psi(\mu, I)$ inside the span of products of the same shape. Every weight of a monomial of such a product is dominated by $\mu_i$. So each dominant weight of $\langle G \rangle_{\{j<i\}}$ is $\preceq \mu_i$ and lies in $\pi$.

(2) implies (1). Let $U$ be the span of the $\Psi(\mu, I)$ with $\mu_i \in \pi$, and let $W$ be the span of the products $m \cdot q$, where $q$ is a monomial in the letters $j \geq i$ and $m$ runs over a fixed set of products of path polynomials of the first $i$ letters, with end weight in $\pi$, and monomials. By independence, $\dim U$ is the number of standard pairs with $\mu_i \in \pi$. Counting, $\dim W$ is at most the number of paths of the first $i$ letters with end weight in $\pi$ times the number of exponents for the remaining letters, and a counting identity for paths, with binomial coefficients, shows that this product is the same number. Since $U \subseteq W$, we get $U = W$. Now let $G$ satisfy (2). Act on the first $i$ letters by the generic matrix and specialize: the components of $G$ of a highest weight for the first $i$ letters have dominant weights in $\pi$ by (2), and $G$ is recovered from them as an element of $W$. So $G \in W = U$. $\square$

**Definition 7.9.8 (The conditions A3).** Let $R$ be a commutative ring, and fix thresholds $\theta_i, M_i \in \mathbb{Z}$ for $i < N$. The condition *A3 over $R$* says: the set of $G \in \operatorname{Pol}_R(B)$ with $\theta_i \leq v_{\{j \leq i\}}(G)$ and $\lambda_{\{j \leq i\}}(G) \leq M_i$ for all $i$ is the $R$-span of the $\Psi(\mu, I)$ over the standard pairs with $\theta_i \leq \mu_{i+1}(n-1)$ and $\mu_{i+1}(0) \leq M_i$ for all $i$.

**Theorem 7.9.9 (Valuation and top weight conditions).** Let $p$ be a prime and $O$ a discrete valuation ring with a uniformizer over $p$, and let $k$ be its residue field. For every word, degrees and thresholds, A3 holds over $O$ and over $k$.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.G.ColumnFamilies.splitting_third_part_holds`.

*Proof.* Let $F$ be a field. For each $i$ the set $\pi_i = \{\nu \text{ dominant} : \theta_i \leq \nu(n-1),\ \nu(0) \leq M_i\}$ is a dominant lower set, since $\nu \preceq \mu$ gives $\nu(0) \leq \mu(0)$ and $\nu(n-1) \geq \mu(n-1)$. The word valuation bounds say that $\theta_i \leq v_{\{j \leq i\}}(G)$ and $\lambda_{\{j \leq i\}}(G) \leq M_i$ hold if and only if every dominant weight of $\langle G \rangle_{\{j \leq i\}}$ lies in $\pi_i$: the generic change of coordinates makes the extreme coordinates of the monomial weights equal to the extreme coordinates of the dominant weights of the generated module. By the [Theorem (Admissible words)](/entry/admissible-word-basis/) at $i + 1$, this holds if and only if $G$ lies in the span of the $\Psi(\mu, I)$ with $\mu_{i+1} \in \pi_i$. These path polynomials form a subset of a basis, so the intersection over $i$ of the spans is the span of the intersection of the subsets, which is A3 over $F$. Applied to $F = k$ this gives A3 over $k$.

For $O$, let $K$ be its fraction field. One inclusion is clear: the path polynomials in question satisfy the conditions, which are stable under $O$-linear combinations. Conversely let $G \in \operatorname{Pol}_O(B)$ satisfy the conditions. Its image over $K$ satisfies them, since they are read off from monomials, so by A3 over $K$ it lies in the $K$-span of the required path polynomials. By the path basis theorem over $O$, $G$ is also an $O$-combination of all path polynomials of standard pairs. These are linearly independent over $K$, so the two expansions agree, and the coefficients of the other path polynomials vanish. $\square$

**Definition 7.9.10 (Bracket polynomials).** Let $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ be dominant, and let $X = (x_{ab})$ be the generic $n \times n$ matrix over $\mathbb{Z}$. For $\mu_{n-1} < c \leq \mu_0$ put $h(c) = \#\{m : c \leq \mu_m\}$. For subsets $I_c$ with $|I_c| = h(c)$, the *bracket polynomial* is
$$[\mu, I] = \det(X)^{\max(\mu_{n-1}, 0)} \prod_{\mu_{n-1} < c \leq \mu_0} \det X_{\{0, \dots, h(c) - 1\}, I_c},$$
the product of the minors with the first $h(c)$ rows and the columns $I_c$. The *canonical* bracket polynomial $v_\mu$ is the one with $I_c = \{0, \dots, h(c) - 1\}$. For $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ and a vector $v$, let $\nabla(\lambda, v)$ be the set of rational polynomials $x$ such that for every $u$ in the monoid generated by the divided powers and binomials of the rows, the component of $u x$ outside the weight $\lambda$ differs from $u x$ by an integer multiple of $v$.

**Theorem 7.9.11 (Bracket polynomial span).** Let $\mu$ be dominant and $\lambda = \mu + \max(-\mu_{n-1}, 0)$. The $\mathbb{Z}$-span of the bracket polynomials $[\mu, I]$ is the set of $x$ in their $\mathbb{Q}$-span with $x \in \nabla(\lambda, v_\mu)$.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.G.ColumnFamilies.bracket_polynomial_span`.

*Proof.* The $\mathbb{Q}$-span $V$ of the bracket polynomials is a module for the row operators with highest weight $\lambda$, and its $\lambda$-weight space is the line through $v_\mu$. The value of $v_\mu$ at the identity matrix is $1$, since each factor is a leading principal minor. Let $x$ be in the $\mathbb{Z}$-span and $u$ in the monoid. Then $u x$ has integer coefficients, its $\lambda$-component is $c\, v_\mu$ with $c \in \mathbb{Q}$, and evaluating the weight components at the identity shows that $c$ is an integer. So $x \in \nabla(\lambda, v_\mu)$. Conversely, $V \cap \nabla(\lambda, v_\mu)$ is the dual Weyl lattice of $V$: the largest lattice whose images under the monoid have integral $\lambda$-component. Every bracket polynomial lies in it, and by the standard monomial theory of bracket polynomials it is contained in their $\mathbb{Z}$-span. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9.12 (Splitting package).** The following seven statements hold: the automorphic side conclusion; the conditions A3 over every discrete valuation ring and its residue field; the rotated sum bound; the description of the path weight components; the nested sets statement B3; the dependence of the coefficients; and the canonical translate jet identity.

Lean: `Formalism.MainNotions.G.ColumnFamilies.splitting_package_holds`.

*Proof.* The second statement is the [Theorem (Valuation and top weight conditions)](/entry/splitting-third-part-holds/). The first follows from the path basis theorem. The nested sets statement follows from the description of joint components of paths and from the independence of paired paths, both imported. The other four are imported from the chapters where they are proved. $\square$

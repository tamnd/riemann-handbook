## An operator at one tie

Let a word of letters carry admissible tie data, and fix one tie $i$. This section constructs two linear operators $T$ and $\pi$ on polynomials. The operator $T$ preserves the highest weight space $Y$ and is integral up to an explicit power of $p$. It moves the tie operator $\Phi_i$ to the other side of the normalized Fock pairing at the price of $p^{\lfloor E \rfloor}$ and of the replacement of $u$ by $\pi u$. The operator $\pi$ commutes with the later tie operators and is the identity on most of $Y$: the codimension of its fixed space is bounded by the dimensions of the pieces where a diagonal coefficient has small absolute value. This is the core of [the theorem on the tie operators](/entry/tie-ops-x-main/), which applies it tie by tie.

We use the letters, the operators $E^{(j)}_{cd}$, the Fock pairing $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ and the normalized pairing $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_L$ of Section 2, the spaces $Y(j, M)$, $Y(L)$, $Y_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)$ and the tie data of Section 3, and [highest weights](/learn/representations/#highest-weights-and-branching). Let $\Phi_i$ be the tie operator $\Phi_{o,n,z_i}$ of Section 2 at the letters $\mathrm{pos}_i$ (degree $o$) and $\mathrm{pos}_i + 1$ (degree $n$).

**Definition 7.5.1 (The cut space at a position).** Let $L$ be a word of $N$ letters with tie data, and $j_0 \leq N$. Let $\mathfrak{g}_{j_0}$ be the action of $\mathfrak{gl}_r$ on the first $j_0$ letters by the operators $E^{(j_0)}_{cd}$. A *highest weight vector* of weight $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^r$ for $\mathfrak{g}_{j_0}$ is a nonzero weight vector of weight $\lambda$ killed by the raising operators. The *cut space* is
$$C_{j_0} = \operatorname{Pol}(L_{\leq j_0}) \cap \bigcap_{k} Y(J_k, M^{\leq j_0}_k), \qquad M^{\leq j_0}_k = \begin{cases} M_k & J_k < j_0, \\ \infty & J_k \geq j_0, \end{cases}$$
where $L_{\leq j_0}$ is the word obtained from $L$ by a modification of the letters at and after position $j_0$. For a vector $v$, let $Y_v$ be the intersection of $Y(L)$ with the span of the products $g q$, where $g$ runs over the $\mathfrak{g}_{j_0}$-submodule generated by $v$ and $q$ over the polynomials in the variables of the letters at positions $\geq j_0$.

**Definition 7.5.2 (Partial thresholds).** For a tie $i$, the thresholds *up to* $i$ are $M^{(i)}_k = M_k$ if $J_k \leq \mathrm{pos}_i + 1$ and $M^{(i)}_k = \infty$ otherwise. We write $Y^{(i)}(L)$ for $Y(L)$ built with them, so $Y(L) \subseteq Y^{(i)}(L)$.

**Definition 7.5.3 (Projector and Weyl hypotheses).** Let $\rho < r$ and $e_1 \in \mathbb{N}$. The *projector hypothesis* says: for every $j \leq N$, every set of columns $C_s$ equal to $\{0, \dots, r - 1\}$ or to its complement of $\rho$, and every saturated set $T$ of weights, there is a type projector for $(j, C_s, T)$ that is self-adjoint for the Fock pairing and maps integral polynomials of multidegree $L$ to polynomials with $p^{e_1}$ times them integral; and two such projectors for $j \leq j'$ and $C'_s \subseteq C_s$ commute. The *Weyl hypothesis* says: for every $j \leq N$ and every weight $\lambda$, $p^{e_1}$ times an integral vector of the isotypic part of type $\lambda$ lies in the sum of the Weyl lattices generated by integral highest weight vectors of weight $\lambda$.

**Theorem 7.5.4 (Operator at one tie).** Let $L$ be a word of $N$ letters with admissible tie data, $i$ a tie with $\mathrm{pos}_i + 2 \leq N$, $\rho < r$, and $e_1 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that the projector and Weyl hypotheses hold. Put $j_0 = \mathrm{pos}_i + 2$, $C = C_{j_0}$, and let $E \geq 0$ be real. Let $I$ be a finite set, and let $h'_x, h''_x, v_x$ ($x \in I$) be highest weight vectors for $\mathfrak{g}_{j_0}$ of weights $\lambda_x$, all in $C$, such that:

1. the $h'_x$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and every integral highest weight vector of $C$ lies in the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-span of the $h'_x$;
2. the $h''_x$ are integral, and the $v_x$ are nonzero;
3. there are $d_x, c \in \mathbb{Q}_p$ with $c \langle \Phi_i h'_x, h''_y \rangle = \delta_{xy} d_x$ whenever $\lambda_x = \lambda_y$, and $|c \langle h'_x, h'_y \rangle|_p \leq 1$ for all $x, y$.

Then there are linear maps $T, \pi$ on $\mathbb{Q}_p[x]$ such that:

1. $T$ maps $Y(L)$ into $Y(L)$, and $p^{e_1 j_0 + 2 e_1} T y$ is integral for every $y \in Y_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)$;
2. $\langle \Phi_i u, T u' \rangle_L = p^{\lfloor E \rfloor} \langle \pi u, u' \rangle_L$ for all $u \in Y^{(i)}(L)$ and $u' \in Y(L)$;
3. $\pi \Phi_j = \Phi_j \pi$ for every tie $j > i$;
4. $\dim Y(L) - \dim \bigl(Y(L) \cap \ker(\pi - \mathrm{id})\bigr) \leq \sum_{x :\, |d_x|_p < p^{-E}} \dim Y_{v_x}$.

*Proof.* First, $C$ is a finite sum of weight spaces for $\mathfrak{g}_{j_0}$ and is stable under it. By hypothesis 1, after scaling by powers of $p$, every highest weight vector of $C$ lies in the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-span of the $h'_x$, so $C$ is contained in the sum of the submodules generated by the $h'_x$. The projector hypothesis gives a projection $\Pi$ onto $C$ built from type projectors.

Put $c' = p^{\lfloor E \rfloor}$. Define new highest weight vectors: $g_x = 0$ if $|d_x|_p < p^{-E}$ and $g_x = h'_x$ otherwise, and $\tau_y = \sum_z t_{zy} h''_z$ with coefficients $t_{zy}$, built from $c'$, $c$ and the $d_x$, with $|t_{zy}|_p \leq 1$. The $\tau_y$ are integral, and they lie in $C$. Extend the assignments $h'_x \mapsto g_x$ and $h'_x \mapsto \tau_x$ to $\mathfrak{g}_{j_0}$-equivariant maps $\varpi$ and $\tau$ on $C$; this is possible because the $h'_x$ are independent highest weight vectors that generate $C$. Then extend them to all polynomials through $\Pi$ and the variables of the letters at positions $\geq j_0$; this gives $\pi$ (from $\varpi$) and $T$ (from $\tau$).

Property 1 follows from the integrality of the $\tau_y$, from $|t_{zy}|_p \leq 1$, and from the projector and Weyl hypotheses: the type projectors cost $p^{e_1}$ at each of the $j_0$ positions, and the Weyl lattices cost $p^{2 e_1}$. Property 2 reduces, by equivariance and the adjointness of $\Phi_i$ for the Fock pairing, to the pairings of highest weight vectors; there it is the diagonal relation 3, rescaled by $c'$, which is how the $t_{zy}$ were chosen. Property 3 holds because the later ties act on letters after position $j_0$, where $\pi$ acts as the identity. For property 4, $\pi$ fixes every vector built from an $h'_x$ with $g_x = h'_x$, so the vectors of $Y(L)$ not fixed by $\pi$ lie in the sum of the spaces built from the $h'_x$ with $|d_x|_p < p^{-E}$, which have the dimensions $\dim Y_{v_x}$. $\square$

The theorem trades the tie operator for a factor $p^{\lfloor E \rfloor}$ at all diagonal coefficients that are not too small. The vectors where $d_x$ is small are lost, and property 4 counts them.

**Remark 7.5.5 (Formalization).** The words $L_{\leq j_0}$ (`Lz`), the action `GA`, highest weight vectors `IsHW`, the generated submodules `gS`, type projectors `IsTypeProj`, saturated sets `CsSaturated`, Weyl lattices `WeylLat` and isotypic parts `IsoC` were not unfolded; the definitions above say what we know of them. The proof of the theorem in [TieOps.lean](lean:Formalism/PolynomialDimension/MultinomialDepth/TieOps.lean#L3104) is about 300 lines (through `projC`, `GlX.span_hw`, `int_main`, `fock_ext_reduce` and `phi_ext_comm`); we read its outline, and the proof above follows that outline without the bookkeeping. Hypothesis 3 uses the plain Fock pairing, while conclusion 2 uses the pairing normalized by $\prod_i d_i!$. The vectors $v_x$ enter only through the dimensions in property 4; they need not be related to the $h'_x$.

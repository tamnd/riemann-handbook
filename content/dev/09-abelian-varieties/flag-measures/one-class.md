This theorem bounds the sum of the weights $\omega_I$ over inequivalent flags whose normal forms have the same rank $j$, unit $c$, integer $T$ and pairing matrix. It moves all the normal forms to one base normal form $N_0$ by unipotent isometries, and then applies the fixed form count [`sum_weights_inequivalent_hermFrameTerms_le`](/entry/sum-weights-inequivalent-herm-frame-terms-le/). [`sum_weights_per_datum`](/entry/sum-weights-per-datum/) uses it once for each sort of normal forms.

## Statement

The notation is that of [`sum_weights_per_datum`](/entry/sum-weights-per-datum/). For a normal form $N$, $R_N$ is the group generated by the isometries in the unipotent groups of both flags of $N$, and $\omega_N(\Lambda)$ is a ratio of two indices between the stabilizers in $R_N$ of $\Lambda$ and of a reference lattice (`NF.om`).

**Theorem.** Let $K$ satisfy `IsCMUnram`, $F$ a flag, $\mathfrak d$ an ideal, $g \ge 1$ and $\Lambda$ a lattice. Let $e \colon W \to V$ satisfy `IsEmb` for $F$. Let $N_0$ be a normal form adapted to $(F, G_0, \mathfrak d)$ that is a term for $\Lambda$. Let $(G_i, N_i)_{i \in s}$ be a finite family such that, for each $i$:

1. $N_i$ is adapted to $(F, G_i, \mathfrak d)$ and is a term for $\Lambda$;
2. $N_i$ and $N_0$ have the same $j$, $c$, $T$, and the same first $j \times j$ block of pairings $H(N.F.e_a, N.F'.e_b)$;
3. the datum of $(F, G_i, e, g, \Lambda)$ equals that of $(F, G_0, e, g, \Lambda)$;

and no two distinct $G_i$ are frame equivalent for $F$ and $\Lambda$. Then, with $\Delta, D_b, N_T$ the invariants of $N_0$,
$$\sum_{i \in s} \omega_I(F, G_i, \mathfrak d, \Lambda) \le N_T^2 \Bigl(\frac{\Delta}{\mathrm{gI}(N_0, \Lambda)}\Bigr)^{\dim W/2} \Delta^{c_f + 2} (D_b \Delta N_T)^{10000(r+1)^3}.$$

## Proof

1. By `hermFramePairNormalForm_unique_up_to_unipotent`, for each $i$ there is an isometry $\sigma_i$ in the unipotent group of $F$ that carries the frames of $N_0$ to those of $N_i$.
2. By `exists_hermFramePairNormalForm_moved_by_isometry`, $\sigma_i N_0$ is a normal form with $\omega_{\sigma_i N_0}(\Lambda) = \omega_{N_0}(\sigma_i^{-1}\Lambda)$. It is adapted to $(F, G_i, \mathfrak d)$, so `WeightInf.main` gives $\omega_I(F, G_i, \mathfrak d, \Lambda) \le \omega_{N_0}(\sigma_i^{-1}\Lambda)$. By `adapt_invariants`, $\sigma_i^{-1}\Lambda$ is a term for $N_0$.
3. `finrank_wt` gives $\dim W_t(N_0) = \dim W$.
4. Apply [`sum_weights_inequivalent_hermFrameTerms_le`](/entry/sum-weights-inequivalent-herm-frame-terms-le/) to $\Lambda_i = \sigma_i^{-1}\Lambda$. `embeddedDatum_invariant_under_unipotent` and hypothesis 3 show that the $\Lambda_i$ have the lattices $L$, the definite part lattice and the graph of $\Lambda$. If $\rho \in R_{N_0}$ and $\rho\Lambda_i = \Lambda_{i'}$, then $\sigma_{i'}\rho\sigma_i^{-1}$ fixes $\Lambda$ and carries $G_i$ to $G_{i'}$ modulo the flags (the relation `Carr`). So $G_i$ and $G_{i'}$ are frame equivalent, and $i = i'$.

The name "one class" does not mean one frame class: the sum is over many inequivalent flags. The "class" is the fibre of $(P_i, c_i)$ in [`sum_weights_per_datum`](/entry/sum-weights-per-datum/). We have not unfolded the reference lattice `Ref` in $\omega_N$.
